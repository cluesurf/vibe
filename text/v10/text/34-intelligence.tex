\section{Intelligence}

Intelligence is the same four base things read as a solver. Nothing is added to the substrate to obtain it. The
field, the arrow, the rule, and the geometry are simply asked a different question, namely how a configuration
gets from where it is to where it would prefer to be. Throughout this chapter intelligence stays sharply distinct
from consciousness. Intelligence is problem-solving capability, consciousness is integrated experience, and a
system can in principle have one without the other. The two will be developed separately, and the distinction
should be held firmly from the start.

\begin{definition}[Intelligence]
\emph{Intelligence} is the capacity to take a problem and produce a solution. A \emph{problem} is a gap
$P = D - C$ between a desired state $D$ and the current state $C$. When $P$ is zero there is nothing to solve. A
\emph{solution} is a path from $C$ to $D$ that drives $P$ to zero.
\end{definition}

This definition already names the four things any solver needs. It needs \emph{means}, paths it can compute. It
needs a \emph{goal}, the state $D$. It needs a \emph{value}, an ordering that measures progress along the gap. And
it needs \emph{selection}, a way to keep the moves that reduce $P$ and discard the rest. The remaining sections
show that all four are present in the substrate as it already stands, with the value and the selection supplied by
the arrow rather than bolted on as a separate reward machine.

\subsection{The means: the substrate computes}

Cellular automata on hyperbolic honeycombs are computationally universal, a result long established for tilings of
the hyperbolic plane and its higher analogues \cite{margenstern2008, margenstern2013}. The substrate confirms it
at toy scale. A two-counter Minsky machine, which is itself Turing-universal, runs directly on the mesh
\cite{pollard2026vibetest}. Registers are charged regions, increment is a create operation, decrement is an
annihilate operation, and test-for-zero is a count. With those primitives wired together the construction computes
$a \times b$ correctly. The universe on this reading is not merely like a computer. It is one, with no outside
machine driving it. Running the rule on the substrate is what existence does.

\begin{proposition}[The means are universal]
The rule on the hyperbolic mesh supports universal computation. A two-counter Minsky machine has been realized on
the substrate with charged regions as registers and create, annihilate, and count as its operations, and it
computes correctly at toy scale.
\end{proposition}

Universality is generic to hyperbolic cellular automata, so it carries across the candidate geometries rather than
depending on a particular one. The Minsky construction asks only for charge-region registers and the create,
annihilate, and count operations, and those are geometry-agnostic. The conclusion therefore holds on $\{3,4,3,4\}$
exactly as it holds on the alternatives, which matters because it means the choice of geometry is not what buys
computational power. That power is already present wherever the rule runs.

\subsection{The ends: the arrow}

Computation by itself is goal-neutral. A universal machine computes garbage as readily as it computes a solution,
because nothing in the bare notion of computation says which output is wanted. The direction has to come from
somewhere, and in this theory it comes from the arrow, a base ingredient that is a gap carrying a
better-or-worse ordering. The gap-closing dynamics of the rule then act as the selection. Put these together and
computation becomes intentional the moment a universal machine runs under the arrow's value. No reward signal is
attached and no planner is grafted on, because the reward and the planner are already present. The reward is the
arrow's ordering, and the planner is the rule that descends it.

\begin{principle}[Computation is goal-neutral and the arrow supplies the ends]
A universal machine has no preferred output on its own. Intention appears when the same machine runs under the
arrow's better-or-worse ordering, with the gap-closing rule acting as selection. The ends are not a separate
module. They are the arrow.
\end{principle}

The effect of aiming computation is large and measurable. A goal-directed search reaches a $K$-cell target in
about $K$ steps, where an un-goaled search wandering at random would need on the order of $2^K$ trials to stumble
onto the same target by chance \cite{pollard2026vibetest}. At $K = 30$ that is an intention gap of roughly ten
million. The arrow does not make the impossible possible, since the substrate was universal already. What it does
is convert an astronomically unlikely accident into a short directed walk, and that conversion is the whole of
what it means for computation to have a purpose.

\subsection{Maximal by integration, not criticality}

A solver that aims to be maximal must coordinate a global problem across all its parts, and it must do so quickly.
The hyperbolic geometry delivers exactly this through fast integration. The coordination distance across a region
of $N$ cells grows only as $O(\log N)$, a handful of hops, far below the $N^{1/3}$ scaling of a flat
three-dimensional lattice \cite{pollard2026vibetest}. A problem posed anywhere in the structure can be felt
everywhere within a few steps, which is the geometric precondition for a large mind to act as one thing rather
than as a loose federation of distant parts.

It is worth being explicit that this maximal behavior does not ride on a critical edge. The substrate shows no
self-organized criticality. Perturbations spread ballistically with a span near unity rather than fanning out into
branching avalanches, and activity self-tunes to a single homeostatic operating point rather than hovering at a
phase boundary \cite{pollard2026vibetest}. The same ballistic light cone that makes the model well behaved for
relativity is precisely what rules criticality out. Intelligence here is a property of the geometric hierarchy and
its fast mixing, not of a delicate tuning to the brink of instability.

\subsection{The level-relative principle}

A recurring trap in reasoning about this model is to take a limit observed at the base and treat it as absolute.
Most apparent limits are properties of the hyperbolic bulk and they lift at emergent levels, because the substrate
is universal and the levels are programmable. Only a few limits hold all the way up. There is no minting of net
charge, no signaling faster than the light cone, and no hidden state at the base. Everything else is reachable at
some level, and a claim that something is impossible has to name the level at which the impossibility is supposed
to hold.

\begin{principle}[Level-relativity of limits]
A limit observed in the hyperbolic bulk need not survive to emergent levels. Absolute limits are few, namely no
net-charge creation, no faster-than-light-cone signaling, and no hidden base state. All other constraints are
level-relative, and a forbidding claim must specify its level.
\end{principle}

The affordance ladder makes this concrete. A single ternary cell stores three symbols. A small block of cells
stores seven, then nineteen, then fifty-five, climbing as the block grows. A $K$-ary symbol that is plainly
impossible to hold in one base cell becomes trivial one level up, simply by reading a block as a unit. The same
move applies to most of the apparent ceilings one meets while studying the substrate, which is why the careful
phrasing of nearly every limit begins with the words at this level.


Intelligence is universal computation aimed by the arrow and made maximal by the geometry's fast integration. The
means come from universality, the ends come from the arrow's ordering, and the maximality comes from logarithmic
coordination across the hyperbolic hierarchy. None of these is an addition to the base. They are three readings of
the four ingredients already in place. The means and the ends are on solid ground, measured at toy scale. The
higher coarse-graining rungs, where one would hope to see open-ended problem solving rather than fixed targets,
remain open and are treated where the scope of the program is set out.
