\section{The Complete Regular Hyperbolic Tessellation Catalog}
\label{sec:tessellation-catalog}

This appendix gives two things. First the complete catalog of regular hyperbolic tessellations, every dimension
from 2D to 9D, one Schl\"afli symbol per row, with a one-line note on each. Then the measured sweep data for the
substrate-relevant ones, dimension by dimension. The catalog is the complete list of what exists, so the
selection of $\{3,4,3,4\}$ can be seen against the whole field. The sweeps give the degree, growth, and class
that the selection argument rests on.

A tessellation is crystallographic when its symbol uses only the marks $3$, $4$, and $6$. Any $5$, $7$, or higher
mark makes it non-crystallographic, with no root system. Physical-space dimension equals the bulk dimension minus
one, because the horosphere lives one dimension below the bulk. The classification and per-dimension symbols
follow \cite{coxeter1973} and the catalog computations are recorded in \cite{pollard2026vibetest}.

The regular hyperbolic substrate search is a finite, fully enumerated set living in dimensions 2--5. Within it,
$\{3,4,3,4\}$ is the unique regular that is simultaneously crystallographic, with marks $3$ and $4$ only,
spinor-carrying, with 24-cell facets giving $D_4 = 8v + 8s + 8c$, and equipped with a flat 3D cubic cusp,
$\{4,3,4\}$. Its only peers on the spinor axis are the 5D pentacombs $\{3,4,3,3,4\}$ and $\{3,4,3,3,3\}$, which
have the same $D_4$ hook one dimension up but yield 4D physical space. The 3D compacts, $\{5,3,4\}$ and kin, are
all non-crystallographic with no spinor. That triad of properties is the whole selection.
