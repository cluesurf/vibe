\section{Predictions You Could Test}

A picture that only explains what we already know is interesting but not yet science. Science needs predictions, statements that could turn out false. This chapter gathers what the crystal predicts that you could, at least in principle, go and check, and is honest about how sharp each one is.

\subsection{The sharp one: no grain in the speed of light}

The crystal's cleanest prediction comes from the Lorentz chapter. Because the curved web has no preferred direction, the crystal predicts there is no first-order grain in the speed of light: no direction-dependence, and no leading energy-dependence. High-energy and low-energy light travel at exactly the same speed, no matter how far they go.

This is a real bet. A flat-lattice universe would fail it, predicting a grain that distant gamma-ray bursts would have revealed. The crystal passes the current observations comfortably, because it predicts the effect simply is not there. And it is falsifiable: if astronomers ever did detect that high-energy light from a distant burst arrives even slightly out of step with low-energy light, in the first-order way, the curved-crystal picture would be wrong. So far, every measurement says no grain, which is exactly what the crystal expects and the flat lattice does not.

\subsection{The faint one: the swerve}

The crystal also predicts a tiny restlessness called the swerve. On a discrete substrate, a particle cannot travel a perfectly straight line forever, because there is no cell exactly straight ahead at every step. It must hop to the nearest available one, picking up a vanishingly small random nudge each time. Over cosmic distances these nudges would, in principle, slightly blur a particle's motion.

The crystal predicts this effect shrinks rapidly as the graininess gets finer, and at the genuine smallest scale it is so tiny as to be far below anything we could currently detect. Cosmic-ray measurements are consistent with it. So the swerve is more a consistency check than a live test: the crystal predicts an effect too small to see yet, and indeed we do not see it. If future instruments ever became sensitive enough and still saw nothing, or saw too much, that would constrain the theory.

\subsection{The cosmic one: dark energy's size}

From the cosmology chapter, the crystal predicts that dark energy is not a fixed fundamental constant but the shrinking jitter of spacetime, falling with the size of the universe. The headline success is that this gives the right rough size for the dark energy we observe, a number ordinary physics gets catastrophically wrong. The sharper, testable edge would be subtle variation, a dark energy that is not perfectly constant but tied to the growing universe. Pinning that down is future work, but the order-of-magnitude hit is already a real, nontrivial contact with data.

\subsection{The honest status}

Be clear about what kind of predictions these are. The no-grain-in-light prediction is sharp and falsifiable today, and the theory passes where a lattice fails, which is genuinely meaningful. The swerve and the dark-energy details are softer, consistency checks and order-of-magnitude hits rather than knife-edge tests. And much of the theory, especially the inner and the speculative parts coming up, makes no sharp numerical predictions at all yet.

That is the honest position of a young, ambitious framework. It has at least one clean falsifiable prediction that it currently passes, a striking order-of-magnitude success on the deepest cosmic number, and a great deal of structure that fits known physics. It is a serious hypothesis with skin in the game, not yet a confirmed theory. The next parts turn from the physical world to the mind, where the claims are richer and the testability is harder, and the walkthrough keeps flagging which is which.

\textbf{Takeaway:} the crystal's sharpest prediction is no first-order grain in the speed of light, falsifiable today and currently passed where a flat lattice fails. The swerve and the details of dark energy are softer consistency checks, with dark energy's rough size already a real hit. It is a hypothesis with genuine skin in the game.
