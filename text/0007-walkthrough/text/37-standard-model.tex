\section{The Standard Model Pieces}

The Standard Model is physics' catalogue of the actual particles and forces of our world: three families of matter particles, specific forces, specific charges and masses. It is the most precisely tested theory ever, and also the most arbitrary-looking, full of numbers that seem dialed in by hand. A theory like the crystal cannot expect to derive all of it. This is the hardest and most open frontier. But it makes real, honest progress on several pieces, and this chapter reports what is genuinely earned and what is still open.

\subsection{Charge quantization: why the electron is exactly minus one}

Electric charges in our world come in exact, tidy ratios. The electron is exactly minus one, the proton exactly plus one, so atoms are perfectly neutral. Quarks come in exact thirds. Why so exact? The Standard Model assumes it.

The crystal earns it from a consistency requirement. A gauge theory on the web cannot be internally inconsistent under its own symmetries, a condition physicists call anomaly cancellation, and it is the same kind of consistency the index theorem expressed earlier. When you demand only that, plus that the particles can have mass, and you leave every charge as an unknown to be solved for, the answer comes out unique, and it is exactly the real-world pattern of charges. The electron lands at exactly minus one, the quarks at their exact thirds, atoms exactly neutral, not because anyone set them, but because no other assignment is consistent. That exact electron-proton cancellation, a fact the Standard Model leaves unexplained, is forced. This one is solid, granted that the cast of particle types is assumed.

\subsection{The mass hierarchy: why masses span a huge range}

Fermion masses are wildly spread, the top quark is hundreds of thousands of times heavier than the electron, and the Standard Model just lists them. The crystal offers a reason from geometry. A particle's mass comes from how much its pattern overlaps with the background Higgs field, and on a curved substrate, overlaps fall off exponentially with separation. So patterns sitting at evenly spaced positions get masses spread exponentially, which naturally spans the enormous observed range from modest differences in position. A flat space could not do this. Exponential spreading is a gift of curvature. The mechanism is earned. The exact masses are not.

\subsection{Baryogenesis: why there is more matter than antimatter}

The universe is made of matter, with almost no antimatter, though they seem like mirror images. The physicist Andrei Sakharov listed three conditions any explanation needs: a way to change the matter count, a difference between matter and antimatter, and a departure from equilibrium. The crystal supplies all three for free, the growth changes counts, the directed notes distinguish matter from antimatter, and the eternal growth is never at equilibrium. A toy on the crystal then builds up a matter excess, and removing any one of the three conditions erases it. The mechanism is present. The exact amount of the excess is open.

\subsection{What is honestly open}

Two big pieces are not derived, and the theory says so plainly. Why there are exactly three families of matter particles, not two or four, is not forced. There are suggestive hints (the three dimensions, the three-valued tone) but nothing that pins it. And the specific menu of forces, the exact gauge group, is assumed rather than derived. These are marked open, not quietly papered over.

So the Standard Model chapter is a mix: charge quantization earned, the mass hierarchy and baryogenesis given working mechanisms, and the family count and the force menu honestly left open. That mix is the honest state of the hardest frontier.

\textbf{Takeaway:} the crystal earns charge quantization (the exact electron and quark charges forced by consistency), gives a geometric mechanism for the huge spread of masses, and supplies all three Sakharov conditions for the matter-antimatter imbalance. Why there are three families, and the exact menu of forces, remain honestly open.
