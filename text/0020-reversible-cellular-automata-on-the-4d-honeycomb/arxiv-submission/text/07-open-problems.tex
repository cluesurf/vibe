\section{Open Problems}
\label{sec:open-problems}

Each construction and formal proof in this paper isolates a clear boundary where current verification stops and open mathematical questions begin (Table~\ref{tab:verification-map} maps every theorem and obstruction to its exact verification domain and scope boundary). We conclude with five concrete problems on $\{3,4,3,4\}$ spanning reversible cellular automata, Coxeter spectral theory, and formal language complexity.

\paragraph{1. Homogeneous spatial compilation and chiral orientation.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:counter-machine} executes homogeneous $U_{\mathrm{rev}} = S \circ L_{\mathrm{rev}}$ on all non-origin cells while placing a bounded $17$-phase reversible controller at cell $c_0$, and Section~\ref{subsec:reversible-gates} verifies standalone non-isotropic oscillating catalysts $K_F$, $B$, and $R_{0,1}$. Compiling that origin controller into a strictly spatially homogeneous local rule (so every cell executes the exact same transition table, matching Margenstern's strictly homogeneous hyperbolic automata~\cite{margenstern2024heptagrid}) while preserving full $W(F_4)$ isotropy remains open. In particular, even restricting to the chiral proper-rotation subgroup $W(F_4)^+ \subset SO(4)$ admits proper $4\mathrm{D}$ rotations such as $(x_0, x_1, x_2, x_3) \mapsto (x_0, -x_1, x_3, x_2)$ that fix $\ell_{10}$ pointwise while reversing $2\mathrm{D}$ orientation in the orthogonal $(\ell_0, \ell_1)$ plane. Realizing an isotropic rotary element therefore requires multi-port composite motifs (an oriented $2$-line pair under chiral $W(F_4)^+$, or an oriented $3$-line handed motif under full $W(F_4)$).

\paragraph{2. Minimal bijective state count under $W(F_4)$ isotropy.}
Our line-decoupled bijection $L_{\mathrm{rev}}$ uses $12$ port states ($12^{24}$ composite cell states) on a $24$-facet cell, and adding the three standalone catalyst species raises the port alphabet to $15$. What is the minimum per-port alphabet size $|\Sigma|$ (or per-cell state count) needed for a spatially homogeneous, $W(F_4)$- or $W(F_4)^+$-isotropic, globally bijective strongly universal cellular automaton on $\{3,4,3,4\}$, and does an explicit halting-decidability barrier analogous to Margenstern's two-state theorem on $\{5,4\}$~\cite{margenstern2018halting} appear at $|\Sigma| = 2$ or $3$?

\paragraph{3. Trackless emergent universality.}
Every confirmed universal automaton in Table~\ref{tab:symmetry-frontier} depends on engineered spatial structures (tracks, markers, or catalysts), and Section~\ref{sec:obstructions} proves seven concrete obstructions for one canonical chiral unitary automaton $U$ over $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_9][1/6]$. Whether any small-alphabet, $W(F_4)^+$-isotropic, globally reversible cellular automaton on $\{3,4,3,4\}$ supports spontaneous self-assembling computational structures from generic finite seeds without pre-built tracks or catalysts remains open.

\paragraph{4. Exact spectral radius and horospherical contour complexity.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-bracket} traps the adjacency spectral radius of $\{3,4,3,4\}$ strictly inside $(11.049, 11.9254)$, proving $\lambda_{\mathrm{gap}} > 1/2$. Extending reciprocal-Schur weights and stratum Rayleigh--Ritz test vectors from depth-$1$ right-descent strata to depth-$2$ and depth-$3$ Cartier--Foata suffix automata should narrow the interval below $10^{-2}$ and test whether $\rho(\{3,4,3,4\})$ is algebraic. Separately, Margenstern proved that spherical ball contour words on $\{5,4\}$, $\{7,3\}$, $\{5,3,4\}$, and $\{5,3,3,4\}$ lie in $2$-iterated pushdown languages (languages accepted by a nested stack of stacks)~\cite{margenstern2009contour}. Determining the exact formal-language class of spherical contours and horospherical cusp slices on paracompact $\{3,4,3,4\}$ remains open.

\paragraph{5. Isotropic injectivity and surjectivity decidability.}
Because the cusp stabilizer $W_{L_{\mathrm{cusp}}} \subset W(\Gamma_{24})$ generates the intrinsic flat Euclidean cubic grid $\{4,3,4\}$ on every horosphere (exponentially distorted in the bulk hyperbolic metric) and therefore contains a subgroup isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}^2$, a three-line subgroup-coset lift immediately transfers Kari's planar undecidability of both global injectivity and global surjectivity~\cite{kari1994} to arbitrary $W(\Gamma_{24})$-shift-equivariant cellular automata on $\{3,4,3,4\}$. That stands in sharp contrast to Gromov-hyperbolic $\{7,3\}$, which contains no $\mathbb{Z}^2$ subgroup and where surjectivity decidability remains open~\cite{margenstern2008injectivity}. However, a $\mathbb{Z}^2$-coset lift breaks rotational symmetry completely. Deciding global injectivity and global surjectivity for strictly $W(F_4)$-isotropic (rotation-invariant) cellular automata on $\{3,4,3,4\}$ therefore remains a clean open problem.
