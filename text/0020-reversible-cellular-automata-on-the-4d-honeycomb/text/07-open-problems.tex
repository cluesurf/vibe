\section{Open Problems}
\label{sec:open-problems}

Table~\ref{tab:verification-map} maps every theorem and obstruction to its exact verification domain and scope boundary. Five concrete open problems on $\{3,4,3,4\}$ stand out across reversible cellular automata, Coxeter spectral theory, and formal language complexity.

\paragraph{1. Full reflection-including $W(F_4)$ homogeneous compilation.}
Theorems~\ref{thm:homog-weak-univ} and~\ref{thm:homog-strong-univ} establish strictly homogeneous, globally bijective universality under $W(F_4)^+$ ($576$ proper rotations) using ordered orthogonal $k = 2$ anchors $(A,B)$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:chirality-threshold}(3), invariance under full $1{,}152$-element $W(F_4)$ requires $k^* = 4$ handed anchors per oriented gate cell (every triple of facets is fixed pointwise by an improper reflection). Constructing an explicit minimal-alphabet bijective universal rule under full $W(F_4)$ remains open.

\paragraph{2. Minimal bijective state count and purely curved universality.}
Our homogeneous chiral constructions use $5$ port symbols ($U_{\mathrm{univ}}^{(5)}$) for weak universality and $15$ ($U_{\mathrm{strong}}^{(15)}$) for finite-seed strong universality. What minimum per-port alphabet $|\Sigma|$ suffices for homogeneous, $W(F_4)^+$-isotropic, globally bijective universality on $\{3,4,3,4\}$, and does a halting barrier analogous to Margenstern's two-state theorem on $\{5,4\}$~\cite{margenstern2018halting} appear at $|\Sigma| = 2$ or $3$? Equally, does a purely curved (non-horosphere-embedded) homogeneous reversible universal automaton exist in $\{3,4,3,4\}$ or compact $\{5,3,3,4\}$?

\paragraph{3. Trackless emergent universality.}
Every confirmed universal automaton in Table~\ref{tab:symmetry-frontier} depends on localized spatial structures (tracks, markers, or anchor motifs), and Section~\ref{sec:obstructions} proves seven obstructions for one canonical chiral unitary automaton $U$ over $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_9][1/6]$. Whether any small-alphabet, $W(F_4)^+$-isotropic, globally reversible automaton on $\{3,4,3,4\}$ supports self-assembling computation from generic finite seeds without pre-placed tracks or anchors remains open.

\paragraph{4. Exact spectral radius and horospherical contour complexity.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-bracket} traps $\rho(\{3,4,3,4\})$ inside the Lean~4 Depth-2 interval (width $< 0.086$ in~\eqref{eq:rho-bracket}) and big-integer Depth-5 interval (width $< 0.0047$ in~\eqref{eq:rho-depth5}). Determining whether $\rho(\{3,4,3,4\})$ is algebraic, tightening the Lean~4 certificate below $10^{-3}$, and identifying the formal-language class of spherical contours and horospherical slices on paracompact $\{3,4,3,4\}$ (extending Margenstern's $2$-iterated pushdown theorem, accepted by a nested stack of stacks, for compact tilings~\cite{margenstern2009contour}) remain open.

\paragraph{5. Isotropic injectivity and surjectivity decidability.}
Because $W_{L_{\mathrm{cusp}}} \subset W(\Gamma_{24})$ generates the flat grid $\{4,3,4\}$ on every horosphere (exponentially distorted in the bulk) and contains $\mathbb{Z}^2$, a coset lift transfers Kari's planar undecidability of injectivity and surjectivity~\cite{kari1994} to arbitrary $W(\Gamma_{24})$-shift-equivariant automata on $\{3,4,3,4\}$, unlike Gromov-hyperbolic $\{7,3\}$ where surjectivity decidability remains open~\cite{margenstern2008injectivity}. Since a $\mathbb{Z}^2$-coset lift breaks rotational symmetry, deciding injectivity and surjectivity for strictly $W(F_4)$- or $W(F_4)^+$-isotropic rules on $\{3,4,3,4\}$ remains open.
