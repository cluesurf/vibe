\section{Open Problems}
\label{sec:open-problems}

Each construction and formal proof in this paper isolates a clear boundary where current verification stops and open mathematical questions begin (Table~\ref{tab:verification-map} maps every theorem and obstruction to its exact verification domain and scope boundary). We conclude with five concrete open problems on $\{3,4,3,4\}$ spanning reversible cellular automata, Coxeter spectral theory, and formal language complexity.

\paragraph{1. Full reflection-including $W(F_4)$ homogeneous compilation.}
Theorems~\ref{thm:homog-weak-univ} and~\ref{thm:homog-strong-univ} resolve strictly homogeneous, globally bijective universality on $\{3,4,3,4\}$ under the $576$-element chiral rotation group $W(F_4)^+$ using ordered orthogonal $k = 2$ anchors $(A,B)$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:chirality-threshold}(3), extending invariance to the full $1{,}152$-element reflection-including Weyl group $W(F_4)$ requires $k^* = 4$ handed anchor ports per oriented gate cell (since every triple of facets is fixed pointwise by an improper reflection). Constructing an explicit, minimal-alphabet, globally bijective universal rule invariant under full $W(F_4)$ remains open.

\paragraph{2. Minimal bijective state count and purely curved universality.}
Our strictly homogeneous chiral constructions use $5$ port symbols ($5^{24}$ composite cell states) for weak universality ($U_{\mathrm{univ}}^{(5)}$) and $15$ port symbols ($15^{24}$ cell states) for finite-seed strong universality ($U_{\mathrm{strong}}^{(15)}$). What is the minimum per-port alphabet size $|\Sigma|$ (below $5$ for weak universality or below $15$ for finite-seed strong universality) needed for a spatially homogeneous, $W(F_4)^+$-isotropic, globally bijective universal cellular automaton on $\{3,4,3,4\}$, and does an explicit halting-decidability barrier analogous to Margenstern's two-state theorem on $\{5,4\}$~\cite{margenstern2018halting} appear at $|\Sigma| = 2$ or $3$? Equally, does a purely curved (non-horosphere-embedded) homogeneous reversible universal cellular automaton exist in $\{3,4,3,4\}$ or in compact $\{5,3,3,4\}$ (which has no flat horospheres)?

\paragraph{3. Trackless emergent universality.}
Every confirmed universal automaton in Table~\ref{tab:symmetry-frontier} depends on localized spatial structures (tracks, markers, or anchor motifs), and Section~\ref{sec:obstructions} proves seven concrete obstructions for one canonical chiral unitary automaton $U$ over $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_9][1/6]$. Whether any small-alphabet, $W(F_4)^+$-isotropic, globally reversible cellular automaton on $\{3,4,3,4\}$ supports spontaneous self-assembling computational structures from generic finite seeds without pre-placed tracks or anchor motifs remains open.

\paragraph{4. Exact spectral radius and horospherical contour complexity.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:spectral-bracket} traps $\rho(\{3,4,3,4\})$ strictly inside the Lean~4 Depth-2 interval of width below $0.086$ in~\eqref{eq:rho-bracket} and the exact big-integer Depth-5 interval of width below $0.0047$ in~\eqref{eq:rho-depth5}. Determining whether $\rho(\{3,4,3,4\})$ is algebraic, tightening the Lean~4 kernel certificate below $10^{-3}$, and establishing the exact formal-language class of spherical ball contours and horospherical cusp slices on paracompact $\{3,4,3,4\}$ (extending Margenstern's $2$-iterated pushdown theorem, where languages are accepted by a nested stack of stacks, for compact tilings~\cite{margenstern2009contour}) remain open.

\paragraph{5. Isotropic injectivity and surjectivity decidability.}
Because the cusp stabilizer $W_{L_{\mathrm{cusp}}} \subset W(\Gamma_{24})$ generates the intrinsic flat Euclidean cubic grid $\{4,3,4\}$ on every horosphere (exponentially distorted in the bulk hyperbolic metric) and therefore contains a subgroup isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}^2$, a three-line subgroup-coset lift immediately transfers Kari's planar undecidability of both global injectivity and global surjectivity~\cite{kari1994} to arbitrary $W(\Gamma_{24})$-shift-equivariant cellular automata on $\{3,4,3,4\}$. That stands in sharp contrast to Gromov-hyperbolic $\{7,3\}$, which contains no $\mathbb{Z}^2$ subgroup and where surjectivity decidability remains open~\cite{margenstern2008injectivity}. However, a $\mathbb{Z}^2$-coset lift breaks rotational symmetry completely. Deciding global injectivity and global surjectivity for strictly $W(F_4)$- or $W(F_4)^+$-isotropic cellular automata on $\{3,4,3,4\}$ therefore remains a clean open problem.
