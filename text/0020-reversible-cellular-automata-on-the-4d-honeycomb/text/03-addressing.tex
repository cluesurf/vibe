\section{Addressing}
\label{sec:addressing}

Without an exact, drift-free coordinate system whose neighbor queries run in time linear in word depth, hyperbolic cellular automata cannot be simulated or reasoned about at large radius. On the planar pentagrid $\{5,4\}$ and heptagrid $\{7,3\}$, Margenstern's geometric region-splitting method yields a two-state Fibonacci substitution whose regular spanning-tree language supports linear-time preferred-son neighbor arithmetic~\cite{margenstern2007vol1,margenstern2016coords,margenstern2019fib1}. Yet geometric region splitting degrades rapidly above two dimensions: in the three-dimensional dodecagrid $\{5,3,4\}$, the maximal-representation language of the four-region splitting tree is not context-free and neighbor computation requires cubic time and quadratic space~\cite{margenstern2003skordev,margenstern2007vol1}, and in the four-dimensional 120-cell honeycomb $\{5,3,3,4\}$, the 16-corner splitting numeration is not regular~\cite{margenstern2004fourd,margenstern2007vol1}.

Switching from geometric region-splitting numeration to algebraic Cartier--Foata normal forms~\cite{cartier1969} restores strict finite-state regularity for every right-angled hyperbolic honeycomb, including $\{5,4\}$, $\{5,3,4\}$, $\{5,3,3,4\}$, and $\{3,4,3,4\}$. Among the two right-angled regular four-dimensional honeycombs, $\{3,4,3,4\}$ reduces the Coxeter automaton from $120$ generators and $2{,}641$ cliques down to $24$ generators, $217$ cliques, and four $W(F_4)$ symmetry orbits, delivering exact linear-time bitmask neighbor updates and a third-order shell recurrence.

\subsection{Right-Angled Automaton}
\label{subsec:racg-automaton}

Picture walking outward from the base dock $c_0$ by reflecting across facets: whenever two successive reflections cross orthogonal facets, their order does not matter, so they can be bundled into one commuting slice. Stacking maximal commuting slices as far to the right as possible assigns every dock a unique normal form recognized by a finite automaton.

Let $\Gamma_{24}$ be the commutation graph on $\mathcal{F} = \{0,\dots,23\}$ with edge $a \sim b$ iff $r_a \cdot r_b = 1$. Its \emph{clique $f$-vector} $(f_0, f_1, f_2, f_3, f_4)$ counts subsets of pairwise adjacent vertices of each cardinality. Direct enumeration over the $24$ roots of $D_4$ yields Table~\ref{tab:clique-fvectors}: $\Gamma_{24}$ has clique number $3$ (zero $4$-cliques) and $1 + 24 + 96 + 96 = 217$ total cliques.

Because adjacent facets meet at exact angle $\pi/2$ while non-adjacent pairs are parallel or ultraparallel (Theorem~\ref{thm:right-angled}), the $24$ facet reflections generate the right-angled Coxeter group~\cite{davis2008racg}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:racg-pres}
W(\Gamma_{24}) = \bigl\langle s_0, \dots, s_{23} \;\big|\; s_a^2 = 1 \;\forall a,\;\; s_a s_b = s_b s_a \text{ whenever } a \sim b \bigr\rangle.
\end{equation}
By Poincar\'e's polyhedron theorem applied to the ideal right-angled 24-cell (with exact $O^+(4,1;\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}])$ matrix distinctness formally verified in Lean~4 through depth $r \le 2$, giving $1, 24, 456$ distinct docks~\cite{pollard2026repo}), $W(\Gamma_{24})$ embeds faithfully into $O^+(4,1;\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}])$ and acts simply transitively on docks. Its Cayley graph is therefore the dual graph of $\{3,4,3,4\}$, which is biautomatic~\cite{brink1993,davis2008racg} (in contrast to the specific Fibonacci-coded planar tessellation groups proved non-automatic by Gasperin and Margenstern~\cite{margenstern2014gasperin}).

Let $\ell(w)$ denote the shortest Coxeter word length of $w \in W(\Gamma_{24})$, and define its right-descent set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:right-descent}
D_R(w) = \bigl\{a \in \mathcal{F} : \ell(w s_a) < \ell(w)\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
\begin{definition}[Right-Greedy Cartier--Foata Normal Form]
\label{def:foata}
A word $w \in W(\Gamma_{24})$ is in right-greedy Cartier--Foata normal form~\cite{cartier1969} when written as a sequence of nonempty commuting cliques $w = C_1 C_2 \cdots C_m$ ($|C_i| \le 3$) such that for every $1 \le i < m$ and every $x \in C_i$, $x \notin C_{i+1}$ and $x$ fails to commute with at least one generator in $C_{i+1}$.
\end{definition}

By Tits's deletion condition for Coxeter groups (a generator cancels on the right only if its rightmost occurrence commutes with every letter after it), every $w \in W(\Gamma_{24})$ has a unique right-greedy normal form whose rightmost block $C_m$ equals $D_R(w)$ identically. Reading blocks right-to-left, a nonempty clique $C \neq \emptyset$ may be preceded on the left by any clique disjoint from the forbidden set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:forb-set}
\mathrm{Forb}(C) = C \cup \bigcap_{a \in C} \mathrm{Adj}(a), \qquad \mathrm{Forb}(\emptyset) = \emptyset.
\end{equation}
Because this transition depends only on a single adjacent clique state, the language of Cartier--Foata normal forms is recognized by a $217$-state deterministic finite automaton and is strictly regular in four dimensions.

Furthermore, the full $1{,}152$-element symmetry group $W(F_4)$ acts transitively on cliques of each fixed size $k \in \{0,1,2,3\}$ (while its signed-permutation subgroup $W(B_4)$ of order $384$ splits the $96$ $3$-cliques into two orbits of sizes $64$ and $32$, both verified in Lean~4 to share identical extension counts~\cite{pollard2026repo}). Consequently, the $217$-state automaton collapses to just four symmetry orbits.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform Four-Orbit Transfer Matrix]
\label{thm:foata-matrix}
For every nonempty clique $C$ of $\Gamma_{24}$ of size $i \in \{1,2,3\}$, the counts of cliques of sizes $(0,1,2,3)$ (including the empty clique) disjoint from $\mathrm{Forb}(C)$ depend only on $i$ and form the $3 \times 4$ matrix
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:m24-matrix}
M_{24} =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 15 & 44 & 36 \\
1 & 19 & 63 & 54 \\
1 & 21 & 75 & 68
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
For any fixed $k$-clique $C$ ($k \in \{0,1,2,3\}$), let $a_k(r)$ denote the per-clique multiplicity of words $w \in W(\Gamma_{24})$ of word length $r$ satisfying $D_R(w) = C$, with initial values $a_0(0) = 1$ and $a_k(r) = 0$ whenever $r < k$ (so $a_2(1) = a_3(1) = a_3(2) = 0$). Then $a_0(r+1) = 0$ and for all $r \ge 0$,
\begin{align}
a_1(r+1) &= a_0(r) + 15\,a_1(r) + 44\,a_2(r) + 36\,a_3(r), \label{eq:foata-a1}\\
a_2(r+2) &= a_0(r) + 19\,a_1(r) + 63\,a_2(r) + 54\,a_3(r), \label{eq:foata-a2}\\
a_3(r+3) &= a_0(r) + 21\,a_1(r) + 75\,a_2(r) + 68\,a_3(r). \label{eq:foata-a3}
\end{align}
The total number of length-$r$ words with $|D_R(w)| = k$ is $N(r,k) = f_k\,a_k(r)$. On the $8$-facet wall cube $W_{\mathrm{wall}} \cong Q_3$, rows $i \in \{1,2\}$ counting disjoint cliques of sizes $(0,1,2)$ form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mwall-matrix}
M_{\mathrm{wall}} =
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 4 & 3 \\
1 & 6 & 7
\end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
giving per-clique wall multiplicities $w_0(0)=1$, $w_0(h+1)=0$, $w_2(1)=0$, $w_1(h+1) = w_0(h) + 4 w_1(h) + 3 w_2(h)$, and $w_2(h+2) = w_0(h) + 6 w_1(h) + 7 w_2(h)$ for all $h \ge 0$.
\end{theorem}

When a word $w$ of length $r$ is multiplied on the right by an outward generator $s_b \notin D_R(w)$, the new right-descent set is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:right-descent-step}
D_R(w s_b) = \{b\} \cup \bigl(D_R(w) \cap \mathrm{Adj}(b)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Exhaustive verification across all $217$ cliques certifies that the single-hop upward neighbor counts depend only on $k = |D_R(w)|$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:m-up}
M_{\mathrm{up}} =
\begin{pmatrix}
0 & 24 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 15 & 8 & 0 \\
0 & 11 & 8 & 3 \\
0 & 9 & 6 & 6
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Because every dock in stratum $C_k$ ($k \in \{1,2,3\}$) has exactly $k$ inward parents at depth $r-1$, detailed balance links $M_{\mathrm{up}}$ to the exact stratum counts $N(r,k) = f_k\,a_k(r)$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:detailed-balance}
k\,N(r+1, k) = \sum_{j=0}^{3} N(r, j)\,(M_{\mathrm{up}})_{jk}, \qquad
T = M_{\mathrm{up}}^{(1..3)}\operatorname{diag}\!\Bigl(1,\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{3}\Bigr) =
\begin{pmatrix}
15 & 4 & 0 \\
11 & 4 & 1 \\
9 & 3 & 2
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Direct computation confirms $\det(\lambda I - T) = \lambda^3 - 21\lambda^2 + 51\lambda - 23$, bridging single-hop graph degrees directly to the global cubic shell recurrence. \subsection{Shell Recurrences}
\label{subsec:shell-recurrences}

Both spherical balls around a dock and parabolic cusp layers descending from a flat $\{4,3,4\}$ horosphere expand faster than $18$-fold per hop, while lateral wall links across a fixed hyperplane grow strictly more slowly as $4 \cdot 5^{h-1}$. Combining the clique $f$-vectors of Table~\ref{tab:clique-fvectors} with Theorem~\ref{thm:foata-matrix} gives exact closed-form counts at every depth.

For parabolic cusp depth $h$, a dock at distance $h$ below the cusp layer corresponds to a minimal-length right coset representative $w$ of the parabolic stabilizer $W_{L_{\mathrm{cusp}}}$ in $W(\Gamma_{24})$. Inversion $w \mapsto w^{-1}$ maps minimal-length right coset representatives bijectively onto length-$h$ words whose right-descent clique satisfies $D_R(u) \subseteq \mathcal{F} \setminus L_{\mathrm{cusp}}$, yielding
\begin{align}
|\partial B_r| &= a_0(r) + 24\,a_1(r) + 96\,a_2(r) + 96\,a_3(r), \label{eq:ball-shell}\\
N_h &= a_0(h) + 18\,a_1(h) + 60\,a_2(h) + 52\,a_3(h), \label{eq:cusp-shell}\\
l_h &= w_0(h) + 4\,w_1(h) + 4\,w_2(h). \label{eq:wall-cusp}
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Cubic Growth and Wall Links]
\label{thm:cubic-growth}
For every integer $n \ge 0$, both spherical ball shells $|\partial B_r|$ and parabolic cusp shells $N_h$ satisfy the exact third-order recurrence
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cubic-rec}
X_{n+4} = 21\,X_{n+3} - 51\,X_{n+2} + 23\,X_{n+1},
\end{equation}
whose characteristic polynomial
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:char-poly}
\chi_{3434}(\lambda) = \lambda^3 - 21\lambda^2 + 51\lambda - 23
\end{equation}
has three real roots separated by sign changes at $(0, 1, 3, 18.2787, 18.2788)$ and unique dominant root $18.2787 < \lambda_{3434} < 18.2788$. Moreover:
\begin{enumerate}
\item For all $h \ge 0$, parabolic cusp shells obey $N_0 = 1$, $N_{h+1} \ge 18\,N_h$, and $N_h \ge 18^h$.
\item For all $h \ge 1$ and $r \ge 1$, adjacent shell ratios lie strictly inside
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ratio-brackets}
18.27 \le \frac{N_{h+1}}{N_h} \le 18.34, \qquad 18.27 \le \frac{|\partial B_{r+1}|}{|\partial B_r|} \le 19.00.
\end{equation}
\item Single-face lateral wall links satisfy $l_0 = 1$ and $l_h = 4 \cdot 5^{h-1}$ for all $h \ge 1$, with strict domination $l_h < N_h$ for all $h \ge 1$ and cumulative sum
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wall-sum}
\sum_{d=0}^{h} l_d = 5^h \qquad (h \ge 0).
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Direct root evaluation gives $\lambda_{3434} \approx 18.27871$, producing an asymptotic surface-to-volume fraction
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:surface-vol}
\lim_{r \to \infty} \frac{|\partial B_r|}{|B_r|} = \frac{\lambda_{3434} - 1}{\lambda_{3434}} \approx 0.945292.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Neighbor Arithmetic}
\label{subsec:neighbor-arithmetic}

Given the canonical address of a dock $w$ and a facet index $a \in \mathcal{F}$, computing the canonical address of its neighbor $w s_a$ requires no geometry checks or floating-point arithmetic. Using a 24-bit integer mask per clique block and the precomputed 24-entry bitmask table $\mathrm{Adj}(a)$, right-greedy Cartier--Foata reduction resolves every neighbor query in linear worst-case time $O(m)$, directly mirroring Margenstern's preferred-son algorithm on $\{7,3\}$~\cite{margenstern2016coords}.

\needspace{6\baselineskip}
\begin{proposition}[Linear-Time Word Reduction]
\label{prop:neighbor-lookup}
Given a right-greedy Cartier--Foata normal form $w = C_1 \cdots C_m$ ($|C_i| \le 3$, $C_m = D_R(w)$ for $m \ge 1$) and a facet generator $s_a$, the normal form of $w s_a$ is computed in worst-case $O(m)$ time and $O(m)$ space.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Base case ($m = 0$).}
For the identity word $w = 1$, return the single block $C'_1 = \{a\}$.

\paragraph{Cancellation (inward step, $a \in C_m$).}
Remove $a$ from $C_m$ to form $C'_m = C_m \setminus \{a\}$. If $C'_m = \emptyset$, drop $C'_m$ and return $C_1 \cdots C_{m-1}$ immediately. Otherwise execute the right-to-left sweep below starting at $i = m - 1$.

\paragraph{Extension (outward step, $a \notin C_m$).}
If $C_m \subseteq \mathrm{Adj}(a)$, replace $C_m$ by $C_m \cup \{a\}$ in $O(1)$ time and stop. Otherwise form two nonempty rightmost blocks,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:foata-split}
C'_{m+1} = \{a\} \cup \bigl(C_m \cap \mathrm{Adj}(a)\bigr), \qquad C'_m = C_m \setminus \mathrm{Adj}(a),
\end{equation}
and execute the right-to-left sweep below starting at $i = m - 1$.

\paragraph{Unified right-to-left sweep ($i = m - 1$ down to $1$).}
Compute the subset $S_i = \{x \in C_i : C'_{i+1} \subseteq \mathrm{Adj}(x)\}$ of generators commuting with every remaining letter of $C'_{i+1}$. Move $S_i$ right into $C'_{i+1}$ and set $C'_i = C_i \setminus S_i$. If $S_i = \emptyset$, stop. If $C'_i = \emptyset$, drop $C'_i$ and stop.

\paragraph{Proof of correctness.}
Before step $i$, reducedness of $w$ guarantees $C_i \cap C_{i+1} = \emptyset$ and $C'_{i+1} \subseteq C_{i+1}$, so $C_i \cap C'_{i+1} = \emptyset$. Because $C_i$ is a clique, every pair in $S_i$ commutes, so $C'_{i+1} \cup S_i$ stays a clique. No letter ever slides more than one block right: every $z \in C_{i-1}$ fails to commute with some $u \in C_i = C'_i \sqcup S_i$, so even if $z$ slides into $C'_i$, the element $u \in S_i \subseteq C'_{i+1} \cup S_i$ blocks $z$ from advancing past $C'_i$. In particular, when $C'_i = \emptyset$, all of $C_i = S_i$ now sits inside the block immediately right of $C_{i-1}$, blocking every letter of $C_{i-1}$ so dropping $C'_i$ and halting yields the exact right-greedy normal form in at most $m$ constant-time bitmask operations.

Because $\Gamma_{24}$ has no $4$-cliques, every dock has at most three shortest-path parents toward the origin and at least $21$ outward neighbors. Unlike black-rooted planar Fibonacci trees where direct preferred-son rules break under root-type changes~\cite{margenstern2019fib1}, transitivity of $W(\Gamma_{24})$ keeps Proposition~\ref{prop:neighbor-lookup} uniform across every dock of the mesh. Yet this $94.53\%$ boundary dominance also destroys Euclidean amenability, reshaping both diffusion and global cellular-automaton reversibility.
