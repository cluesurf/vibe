\section{Universality}
\label{sec:universality}

Reversible cellular automata on non-amenable hyperbolic tilings avoid post-hoc injectivity undecidability~\cite{margenstern2008injectivity} by factoring every synchronous step into two explicit local bijections~\cite{margolus1984,morita2017book}. We establish to our knowledge the first strictly spatially homogeneous, rotation-invariant ($576$-element chiral $W(F_4)^+$-isotropic), globally bijective universal cellular automata in four-dimensional hyperbolic space with zero special origin cell: a $5$-symbol weakly universal Morita rotary automaton $U_{\mathrm{univ}}^{(5)}$ operating on flat $\{4,3,4\}$ horospheres and a $15$-symbol finite-seed strongly universal automaton $U_{\mathrm{strong}}^{(15)}$ coupling finite horosphere circuits to empty four-dimensional bulk geodesic counter rays, grounded in a Lean~4-certified sharp four-dimensional chirality threshold.

\subsection{Partitioned Factorization}
\label{subsec:partitioned-factorization}

Rather than updating an entire cell from all $24$ neighbors through an unstructured lookup table, a partitioned cellular automaton first applies a local bijection $L : \Sigma^{24} \to \Sigma^{24}$ identically inside every cell and then streams every port value one hop across its shared facet. Because each half-step inverts locally, global injectivity and global surjectivity hold simultaneously for all time without invoking Moore--Myhill. Two canonical bijective one-hop facet streams act on $\{3,4,3,4\}$.

\paragraph{Involutive Coxeter facet-exchange stream $S_{\mathrm{exch}}$ on $W(\Gamma_{24})$.}
For every cell word $w \in W(\Gamma_{24})$ and facet $s \in \mathcal{F}$, exchange port $s$ across the shared facet with neighbor $w F_s$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exch-stream}
(S_{\mathrm{exch}}\,x)(w F_s,\, s) = x(w, s), \qquad S_{\mathrm{exch}}^2 = I.
\end{equation}
Because $F_s^2 = I_5$ identically, $S_{\mathrm{exch}}$ is an exact global involution, and any fixed port token bounces across its shared facet at exact period $2$ with zero separate bounce step. Inside the $6$-facet octahedral cusp layer $L_{\mathrm{cusp}} = \{E^+, E^-, N^+, N^-, U^+, U^-\}$, opposite parallel faces satisfy $r_a \cdot r_b = 0$ while adjacent orthogonal faces satisfy $r_a \cdot r_b = +1$. The two mutually commuting pairs $\{F_{E^+}, F_{E^-}\}$ and $\{F_{N^+}, F_{N^-}\}$ ($F_{E^\pm} F_{N^\pm} = F_{N^\pm} F_{E^\pm}$) generate the planar reflection group $D_\infty \times D_\infty$ with index-$4$ parabolic translation lattice $\langle T_E, T_N \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$ ($T_E = F_{E^+} F_{E^-}$, $T_N = F_{N^+} F_{N^-}$). Under $S_{\mathrm{exch}}$, every horosphere cell $(X,Y) \in \mathbb{Z}^2$ preserves the same $4$-port horosphere subset $\{E^+, E^-, N^+, N^-\}$, while single steps alternate local compass polarity by chessboard sign $(-1)^{X+Y}$ (absorbed homogeneously on odd-parity chiral turners by swapping $(A,B) \leftrightarrow (B,A)$).

\paragraph{Frame-aligned geodesic stream $S$ on $K$.}
Using the frame transport operators $k_s = F_s Z$ of~\eqref{eq:transport-ids}, define the simply transitive frame group
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:frame-group}
K = \langle k_0,\dots,k_{23}\rangle \subset O^+(4,1;\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]),
\end{equation}
where each cell $w(c_0)$ has unique frame representative $g = w Z^{\ell(w) \bmod 2} \in K$. The frame-aligned stream
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stream-bijection}
(S x)(g k_s,\, s) = x(g, s), \qquad (S^{-1} x)(g k_{\bar{s}},\, s) = x(g, s)
\end{equation}
governs our standalone origin-controller benchmark $U_{\mathrm{sys}}$ and the chiral unitary automaton $U$ of Section~\ref{sec:obstructions}.

\begin{proposition}[Global Bijectivity Without Moore--Myhill]
\label{prop:global-bijection}
Let $\Sigma$ be any finite port alphabet containing a quiescent symbol $0$, and let $L : \Sigma^{24} \to \Sigma^{24}$ be any bijection fixing the all-$0$ cell. Then the partitioned cellular automaton $U = S_{\mathrm{exch}} \circ L$ on $\Sigma^{W(\Gamma_{24}) \times \mathcal{F}}$ is simultaneously globally injective and globally surjective on both full and finite-support configurations, with exact radius-$1$ local inverse $U^{-1} = L^{-1} \circ S_{\mathrm{exch}}$ for all $t \in \mathbb{Z}$ (proved polymorphically in Lean~4~\cite{pollard2026repo}). The exact same statement holds for $S \circ L$ on $\Sigma^{K \times \mathcal{F}}$ with inverse $L^{-1} \circ S^{-1}$.
\end{proposition}

Proposition~\ref{prop:global-bijection} bypasses Margenstern's injectivity undecidability theorem~\cite{margenstern2008injectivity} algebraically: global bijectivity reduces completely to certifying a finite local bijection on $\Sigma^{24}$.

\subsection{Chirality Threshold}
\label{subsec:chirality-threshold}

Compiling planar reversible logic such as Morita's clockwise rotary element~\cite{morita2001} into a single spatially homogeneous, rotation-invariant $4\mathrm{D}$ local rule requires knowing the minimum number of oriented anchor ports inside a 24-cell that canonically fixes a $90^\circ$ rotation on an orthogonal $2\mathrm{D}$ plane. In Lean~4, we resolve this threshold sharply for both chiral proper rotations $W(F_4)^+$ ($|W(F_4)^+| = 576$, $\det = +1$) and the full reflection-including Weyl group $W(F_4)$ ($|W(F_4)| = 1{,}152$).

For any ordered pair $(a, b) \in \mathcal{F}^2$ of $D_4$-orthogonal roots ($r_a \cdot r_b = 0$), partition the remaining facets via the inner products $(r_a \cdot r_x,\, r_b \cdot r_x)$:
\begin{align}
Q(a,b) &= \{\, q \in \mathcal{F} : r_a \cdot r_q = +1 \text{ and } r_b \cdot r_q = +1 \,\}, \label{eq:q-set}\\
P(a,b) &= \{\, p \in \mathcal{F} : r_a \cdot r_p = 0 \text{ and } r_b \cdot r_p = 0 \,\}. \label{eq:p-set}
\end{align}
For the canonical opposite cusp pair $(a,b) = (U^+, U^-) \subset L_{\mathrm{cusp}}$, $Q(a,b) = \{E^+, N^+, E^-, N^-\}$ is the exact $4$-port horosphere wire cross, while $P(a,b)$ is the $4$-port bulk-diagonal frame (a union of two antipodal lines) disjoint from both $\{a,b\}$ and $Q(a,b)$. Define canonical order-$4$ rotations $J^Q_{a,b} : Q(a,b) \to Q(a,b)$ and $J^P_{a,b} : P(a,b) \to P(a,b)$ uniquely by the positive $4 \times 4$ integer root determinant:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:det-rot}
\det[r_a,\, r_b,\, r_q,\, r_{J^Q_{a,b}(q)}] = +2, \qquad
\det[r_a,\, r_b,\, r_p,\, r_{J^P_{a,b}(p)}] = +4,
\end{equation}
and define the unique northern coupling port $q_N(p) \in Q(a,b)$ and perpendicular predicate $\mathrm{perp}(p, q)$ by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:qn-perp}
r_p \cdot r_{q_N(p)} = r_p \cdot r_{J^Q(q_N(p))} = +1, \qquad
\mathrm{perp}(p, q) \iff q \in \bigl\{q_N(p),\, (J^Q)^2(q_N(p))\bigr\}.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp 4D Chirality Threshold]
\label{thm:chirality-threshold}
Every claim below is machine-checked unconditionally in Lean~4 over exact $D_4$ integer root vectors and $W(F_4)$~\cite{pollard2026repo}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{$k = 1$ obstructed under $W(F_4)^+$:} For every facet $a \in \mathcal{F}$ and every $D_4$-orthogonal pair $(p,q)$ perpendicular to $a$ ($r_a \cdot r_p = r_a \cdot r_q = r_p \cdot r_q = 0$), there exists an explicit proper rotation $g \in W(F_4)^+$ ($\det g = +1$) fixing $a$ while swapping $p \leftrightarrow q$, negating $\det[r_a, r_b, r_p, r_q]$ (witnessed nonzero by an explicit $b \in \mathcal{F}$) and preventing any single-anchor rule from orienting a $2\mathrm{D}$ plane.
\item \textbf{$k = 2$ sharp and sufficient under $W(F_4)^+$:} The $144$ ordered $D_4$-orthogonal pairs $(a,b)$ form one transitive $W(F_4)^+$-orbit with cyclic order-$4$ pointwise stabilizer $C_4$, sizes $|Q(a,b)| = |P(a,b)| = 4$, and pairwise disjoint sets $\{a,b\}$, $Q(a,b)$, and $P(a,b)$. The maps $J^Q_{a,b}$ and $J^P_{a,b}$ in~\eqref{eq:det-rot} are unique order-$4$ cycles with swap-invariant square $(J^Q_{a,b})^2$, preserve $\mathrm{perp}$ under $(J^P, J^Q)$, and are unconditionally $W(F_4)^+$-equivariant ($g(J^Q_{a,b}(q)) = J^Q_{g(a),g(b)}(g(q))$ and likewise for $J^P$).
\item \textbf{$k^* = 4$ required under full $W(F_4)$:} Every facet triple $(a,b,c) \in \mathcal{F}^3$ is fixed pointwise by an improper element $g \in W(F_4) \setminus W(F_4)^+$ ($\det g = -1$), and all $576$ improper elements reverse~\eqref{eq:det-rot}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:chirality-threshold} sets the exact minimal symmetry budget of four-dimensional hyperbolic space: one ordered pair of orthogonal anchor symbols $(A, B)$ canonically orients every planar gate identically across all $576$ proper rotations of the 24-cell, with no global coordinate frame and no privileged origin cell.

\subsection{Homogeneous Chiral Universality}
\label{subsec:homog-chiral-univ}

Equipped with Theorem~\ref{thm:chirality-threshold}, we construct a strictly spatially homogeneous, $W(F_4)^+$-isotropic, globally bijective $5$-symbol cellular automaton on $\{3,4,3,4\}$ that natively embeds arbitrary planar Morita reversible circuits~\cite{morita2001,morita2017book} on any flat $\{4,3,4\}$ horosphere slice.

\begin{definition}[$5$-Symbol Automaton $U_{\mathrm{univ}}^{(5)}$]
\label{def:u-univ5}
Let $\Sigma_5 = \{0, P, A, B, R\}$ consist of quiescent $0$, signal pulse $P$, chiral anchors $A, B$, and rotary control token $R$. Define $L_{\mathrm{univ}}^{(5)} : \Sigma_5^{24} \to \Sigma_5^{24}$ purely from a cell's own $24$ ports. Whenever a cell holds exactly one $A$ on port $a$, one $B$ on a $D_4$-orthogonal port $b$, zero catalysts in $Q(a,b)$, and zero $P$ tokens in $P(a,b)$, let $m = |\{p \in P(a,b) : x_p = R\}|$, fix all anchors and outer ports outside $Q(a,b) \cup P(a,b)$, and dispatch by $m$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{$m = 0$ (Wire and crossover):} Apply $(J^Q_{a,b})^2$ on $Q(a,b)$, routing orthogonal pulses straight through simultaneously without interference.
\item \textbf{$m = 4$ ($90^\circ$ corner turner):} Apply $J^Q_{a,b}$ on $Q(a,b)$ (swapping $A \leftrightarrow B$ reverses the turn to $(J^Q_{a,b})^{-1}$).
\item \textbf{$m = 1$ (Morita Reversible Rotary Element):} With the unique $R$ on $p_{\mathrm{bar}} \in P(a,b)$ (a $C_4$-equivariant $2$-to-$1$ double cover of Morita's $\{H,V\}$ bar modulo antipodes) and a single pulse $P$ on $q \in Q(a,b)$, map $P$ straight to $(J^Q)^2(q)$ keeping $p_{\mathrm{bar}}$ fixed if $\neg\mathrm{perp}(p_{\mathrm{bar}}, q)$, or turn $P$ to $J^Q(q)$ while advancing $R$ to $J^P(p_{\mathrm{bar}})$ if $\mathrm{perp}(p_{\mathrm{bar}}, q)$. All $0$-pulse and $\ge 2$-pulse states on $Q(a,b)$ stay fixed.
\item \textbf{Fallback (All other cells, including bulk bounce):} Fix every antipodal line containing any catalyst $\{A,B,R\}$ pointwise, and swap antipodal ports on every catalyst-free line.
\end{enumerate}
Set $U_{\mathrm{univ}}^{(5)} = S_{\mathrm{exch}} \circ L_{\mathrm{univ}}^{(5)}$ on $\Sigma_5^{W(\Gamma_{24}) \times \mathcal{F}}$.
\end{definition}

Because $P(a,b)$ is an exact union of two antipodal lines, the fallback branch never transports non-catalyst tokens into or out of $P(a,b)$ and preserves all catalyst positions, guaranteeing partition disjointness across all $5^{24}$ states. Placing catalysts at $t = 0$ on even-parity horosphere cells ($X+Y$ even) and on their $1$-step bounce neighbors $w F_s$ for odd-parity cells ($X+Y$ odd, swapping $A \leftrightarrow B$ on odd turners) synchronizes all tiles to arrive in phase with signals. Because the $600$ bounce neighbors of a $10 \times 10$ horosphere patch are pairwise distinct and disjoint from the horosphere, neighboring tiles never interfere.

\begin{theorem}[Homogeneous Weak Universality in 4D]
\label{thm:homog-weak-univ}
The $5$-symbol automaton $U_{\mathrm{univ}}^{(5)} = S_{\mathrm{exch}} \circ L_{\mathrm{univ}}^{(5)}$ on $\{3,4,3,4\}$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Global bijectivity, isotropy, and conservation (Lean~4):} On all $5^{24}$ local states of $\Sigma_5^{24}$, $L_{\mathrm{univ}}^{(5)}$ is an exact bijection with explicit inverse, commutes with all $576$ proper rotations $W(F_4)^+$, conserves each species $(N_P, N_A, N_B, N_R)$ individually, and fixes the quiescent cell, so $U_{\mathrm{univ}}^{(5)}$ is globally bijective by Proposition~\ref{prop:global-bijection}.
\item \textbf{Flat horosphere embedding and weak universality:} In Lean~4, the cusp reflections in $Q(U^+, U^-)$ generate distinct commuting non-trivial cusp-fixing translations $T_E, T_N$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:horo-trans}
T_E T_N = T_N T_E, \qquad 2B(T_E c_0, c_0) = 2B(T_N c_0, c_0) = -18, \qquad T_E \neq T_N.
\end{equation}
Homogeneously embedding Morita's reversible rotary element, wires, crossovers, and turners on an infinite ultimately periodic horosphere wire grid makes $U_{\mathrm{univ}}^{(5)}$ weakly Turing universal~\cite{morita2001,morita2017book}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[$15$-Symbol Automaton $U_{\mathrm{strong}}^{(15)}$]
\label{def:u-strong15}
Let $\Sigma_{15} = \Sigma_{\mathrm{ray}} \cup \{A, B, R\}$ (identifying horosphere pulse $P$ with ray probe $P$). Extend $L_{\mathrm{univ}}^{(5)}$ to $L_{\mathrm{strong}}^{(15)} : \Sigma_{15}^{24} \to \Sigma_{15}^{24}$ by applying $L_{\mathrm{line}}^{\mathrm{exch}}$ on every catalyst-free antipodal line in the fallback branch, requiring $P(a,b) \subseteq \{0, R\}$ for $m \in \{0, 1, 4\}$ (and $Q(a,b) \subseteq \{0, P\}$ for $m = 1$), and activating the $m = 3$ sector on $P(a,b)$. When exactly three ports of $P(a,b)$ carry $R$, let $p_{\mathrm{empty}} \in P(a,b)$ be the unique non-$R$ bulk port (carrying any non-catalyst symbol) and let $(q_0, q_1, q_2, q_3)$ be the cyclic $J^Q$-order starting at $q_0 = q_N(p_{\mathrm{empty}})$. Whenever $(q_0, q_1, q_2, q_3, p_{\mathrm{empty}})$ lies among the $11$ single-particle configurations below (with the other four ports equal to $0$), permute them via three disjoint $3$-cycles and one transposition:
\begin{align}
(q_0{:}P \to p_{\mathrm{empty}}{:}F \to p_{\mathrm{empty}}{:}F_r \to q_0{:}P), &\qquad
(q_1{:}P \to p_{\mathrm{empty}}{:}G \to p_{\mathrm{empty}}{:}G_r \to q_1{:}P), \label{eq:launcher-cycles}\\
(q_2{:}P \to p_{\mathrm{empty}}{:}H \to p_{\mathrm{empty}}{:}H_r \to q_2{:}P), &\qquad
(q_3{:}P \leftrightarrow p_{\mathrm{empty}}{:}P), \label{eq:launcher-cycles-2}
\end{align}
fixing all other states on $(q_0, q_1, q_2, q_3, p_{\mathrm{empty}})$ identically. Set $U_{\mathrm{strong}}^{(15)} = S_{\mathrm{exch}} \circ L_{\mathrm{strong}}^{(15)}$.
\end{definition}

Equations~\eqref{eq:launcher-cycles}--\eqref{eq:launcher-cycles-2} couple a finite horosphere circuit directly into empty four-dimensional bulk space (Figure~\ref{fig:spacetime}). Sending plain horosphere pulses $P$ into $q_0$ at $t=0$ and $q_1$ at $t=6$ launches $F$ and $G$ along $p_{\mathrm{empty}}$, shifts remote bookmark $M$ outward by $+2$ cells, and returns plain pulses $(F_r, G_r) \mapsto (q_0, q_1)$ separated by constant $\Delta t_{\mathrm{inc}} = 10$. Analogously, sending $P$ into $q_2$ at $t=0$ and $q_1$ at $t=10$ launches $H$ and $G$ for $\mathrm{DEC}$, returning $(H_r, F_r) \mapsto (q_2, q_0)$ separated by constant $\Delta t_{\mathrm{dec}} = 6$, while sending $P$ into $q_3$ reflects off $M$ at depth $n = 8 + 2c$ and returns along the $2$-cell horosphere arm at $t_{\mathrm{ret}} = 20 + 4c$ ($2 \cdot 8 + 4 = 20$).

\begin{theorem}[Homogeneous Strong Universality in 4D]
\label{thm:homog-strong-univ}
The $15$-symbol rule $U_{\mathrm{strong}}^{(15)}$ of Definition~\ref{def:u-strong15} on $\{3,4,3,4\}$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Global bijectivity, isotropy, and conservation (Lean~4):} On all $15^{24}$ local states of $\Sigma_{15}^{24}$, $L_{\mathrm{strong}}^{(15)}$ is an exact bijection with explicit inverse, commutes with all $576$ proper rotations $W(F_4)^+$, conserves total occupied-port count $N_{\mathrm{occ}}$, and fixes the quiescent cell, so $U_{\mathrm{strong}}^{(15)}$ is globally bijective with zero special origin cell by Proposition~\ref{prop:global-bijection}.
\item \textbf{Finite-seed strong Turing universality:} Every bounded reversible Minsky/Bennett two-counter machine~\cite{minsky1961,bennett1973,morita2017book} compiles into a finite horosphere network of $0$-$R$/$1$-$R$/$4$-$R$ Morita RE tiles whose $3$-$R$ launchers drive unbounded, globally disjoint single-marker counter rays in empty $4\mathrm{D}$ bulk space.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Proof sketch of Theorem~\ref{thm:homog-strong-univ}(2).}
Two finite-seed obstructions close constructively inside $U_{\mathrm{strong}}^{(15)}$. First, because any waiting pulse held on a finite reversible horosphere circuit is periodic by pigeonhole, naive echo timing cannot separate $c = 0$ from $c = P$. Instead, circulate a $3$-pulse bundle (separating launch and return paths per arm via auto-resetting REs) leaving \emph{zero} moving horosphere pulses during bulk waits. Sending $(q_3, q_0, q_1)$ at $(t=0, 16, 22)$ implements $\mathrm{TEST\_AND\_INC}$ for depth $n = 8 + 2c$: at $c = 0$, the returning probe $P$ strikes $q_0$ inside the launcher at $t = 16$ via the $2$-particle identity branch, reflecting $q_0$ to flip an auto-resetting RE diverter at $t = 18$ that routes $q_1$ onto the $c = 0$ branch at $t = 20$ while $q_3$ rebounds from $M$ at $t = 32$, giving spacings $(0, +4, +16)$ with $M$ fixed. For $c \ge 1$, $q_3$ returns at $t = 16 + 4c$ (missing $16$ and $22$), launching $F, G$ cleanly and returning $(q_3, q_0, q_1)$ at $c$-independent spacings $(0, +16, +26)$ with $M$ shifted to $c + 1$. Analogously, $3$-pulse $\mathrm{INC}$ at $(q_3, q_0, q_1) = (4, 16, 22)$ and $\mathrm{DEC}$ at $(q_3, q_2, q_1) = (0, 16, 26)$ ($c \ge 1$) return at $c$-independent spacings $(0, +12, +22)$ and $(0, +16, +22)$ with zero waiting pulses. Second, by Coxeter parabolic intersection $W_A \cap W_B = W_{A \cap B}$~\cite{davis2008racg}, every bulk-ray word $g_k = F_p F_{\bar{p}} \cdots$ ($p \in P(U^+, U^-) \subset \mathcal{F} \setminus L_{\mathrm{cusp}}$) obeys $W_Q \cap W_{\{s, p, \bar{p}\}} = \{1\}$ for all $s \in \{U^+, U^-\} \cup P(U^+, U^-)$, forcing distinct bulk rays to stay pairwise disjoint and disjoint from the horosphere and all bounce cells for all $k \ge 1$.

\subsection{Geometric Latency}
\label{subsec:geometric-latency}

Any hyperbolic tiling provides a logarithmic ball-diameter advantage over polynomial Euclidean grids: Margenstern already implemented linear-depth geodesic message routing on planar $\{7,3\}$ ($\lambda = \varphi^2 \approx 2.618$)~\cite{margenstern2006comm}. On four-dimensional $\{3,4,3,4\}$, Theorem~\ref{thm:cubic-growth} lifts the base of the logarithm to $\lambda_{3434} \approx 18.27871$.

In flat $\mathbb{Z}^4$, the exact count of lattice points within $\ell_1$-radius $R$ is the four-dimensional cross-polytope polynomial
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:z4-vol}
V_{\mathbb{Z}^4}(R) = \frac{2R^4 + 4R^3 + 10R^2 + 8R + 3}{3},
\end{equation}
giving $V_{\mathbb{Z}^4}(0) = 1$, $V_{\mathbb{Z}^4}(1) = 9$, and round-trip diameter $2R = \Omega(M_{\mathrm{mem}}^{1/4})$ for storing $M_{\mathrm{mem}}$ memory sites. By contrast, a $\{3,4,3,4\}$ cusp ball of depth $h$ encloses cumulative capacity
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cusp-cap}
V_h = \sum_{d=0}^{h} N_d = \Theta(\lambda_{3434}^{h}), \qquad T_{\mathrm{hyp}}(M_{\mathrm{mem}}) = 2h = O\bigl(\log_{18.28} M_{\mathrm{mem}}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Table~\ref{tab:ram-speedup} compares these exact geometric lower bounds at matched capacity $M_{\mathrm{mem}} = V_h$, reaching an $8.40\times$ round-trip reduction at depth $h = 5$. This is strictly a geometric diameter-versus-capacity bound rather than an implemented tree-RAM circuit (the bulk counters of Section~\ref{subsec:counter-machine} use linear unary rays), and congestion-free reversible tree routing remains open.
