\begin{abstract}
Cellular automata on hyperbolic tilings face three structural barriers in higher dimensions: geometric region-splitting numerations lose formal-language regularity in three and four dimensions, non-amenable boundary growth breaks the Euclidean Moore--Myhill equivalence and renders global injectivity undecidable for arbitrary rules, and tight local rotational symmetry severely restricts trackless computation. We analyze these barriers on the unique paracompact four-dimensional regular honeycomb $\{3,4,3,4\}$ of 24-cells meeting four per triangular ridge, whose ideal vertices carry flat cubic grids $\{4,3,4\}$. Realized over the exact integer-ring Lorentz isometry group on $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]^5$, its 24 orthogonal facet reflections generate a right-angled Coxeter group whose commuting-clique (Cartier--Foata) normal forms stay strictly regular across four cell-symmetry orbits. This yields linear-time bitmask neighbor reduction, an exact cubic shell recurrence with growth rate above eighteen, exact exponential wall-crossing counts, and a Lean~4-certified random-walk spectral gap exceeding one half. Factoring each step into a cell-symmetric local line bijection and a bijective one-hop facet stream guarantees global injectivity and surjectivity by construction, supporting constant-particle reversible ray counters, a bounded-origin counter machine with exact reversal, and standalone oscillating reversible gate catalysts. A certified case study of a minimal unitary ternary automaton over an exact ninth-root cyclotomic ring isolates seven algebraic obstructions to trackless ballistic computation, leaving fully homogeneous spatial compilation, minimal bijective state counts, and isotropic surjectivity decidability open.
\end{abstract}
