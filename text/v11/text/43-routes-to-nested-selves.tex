\section{Routes to Nested Selves}

A \emph{self} is an emergent bound knot woven across many docks over many beats, and it holds itself together by the shadow it casts in the active vacuum. A \emph{nested self} is a self made of selves, an atom inside a molecule inside a cell inside an organism. This section asks what the mesh must supply for that nesting to happen, and it gives the only honest answer, which is a short list of routes that compose rather than compete.

Two background facts frame the whole discussion. First, the bulk law is the \emph{knit}, collide then stream, and it is reversible and tone-conserving. Second, a self is not an exact micro-object. It is a macro-pattern that many distinct micro-arrangements all stand for. Those two facts pull against each other, and the tension between them is the entire problem of this section.

\subsection{The core problem}

A perfectly reversible closed system has a hard limitation that is easy to state and impossible to argue around. It never forgets, never merges two histories into one, never truly settles, and never has a genuine attractor. Each of those is the same fact wearing a different coat. Reversibility means the present determines the past as exactly as it determines the future, so no information about where the system came from is ever lost.

But a self lives on forgetting. Millions of micro-arrangements of tones must all read as \emph{this self}, and they must keep reading as this self even as the micro-arrangement churns underneath. That many-to-one collapse is degeneracy, and degeneracy is exactly what a reversible bulk cannot produce from inside.

\begin{principle}[Selves need degeneracy]
Many different microscopic situations must behave like the same macroscopic thing. That degeneracy is what a self is made of, and a reversible bulk cannot supply it from inside.
\end{principle}

This is forced, not a shortcoming of one particular knit. A reversible permutation of states has zero degeneracy by definition, because it is a bijection, and a bijection maps distinct inputs to distinct outputs forever. So the effective loss a self needs must come from somewhere other than the bulk knit. The whole game is finding that somewhere.

\subsection{The five routes}

There are five routes to the degeneracy a self needs, and they layer on top of one another. Each handles a different piece, and a working nested self uses all five at once.

\subsubsection{Coarse-grained selves}

This is the thermodynamics route, and it is the most physically realistic. Microscopically everything stays reversible. Macroscopically many microstates look like the same thing. Many tone arrangements all correspond to one self.

The self exists as a stable macro-pattern, not as an exact micro-object. The degeneracy is real but observer-relative, it lives in the choice of which micro-differences to ignore. A structured coarse map keeps more effective information than a random one, but only when the underlying dynamics already carries a slow metastable mode to coarse-grain over.

\begin{result}[Coarse-graining, conditional]
A structured coarse map preserves more effective causal information than a random one on real self dynamics, but only where the dynamics already has a slow metastable mode. The route is proven where such a mode exists and null where none does.
\end{result}

\subsubsection{Mobile soliton selves}

Before anything can compose, the parts must be able to travel and meet. This route asks for localized patterns that move, survive collisions, and keep their identity across them. Gliders, solitons, vortices, topological defects. These are the primitive particles from which larger structures are built. Without mobile persistent patterns nothing composes and nothing nests.

\begin{result}[Mobility without binding]
The conservative reversible knit supports localized patterns that move and that scatter off one another while keeping their identity. The experiments found mobility and scattering, but not binding. There are particles, but not yet molecules.
\end{result}

\subsubsection{Bound composites}

This is the critical threshold, the true beginning of nesting. A bound composite is a structure that stays together, resists perturbation, keeps its internal organization, and survives interaction. This is where atoms, molecules, cells, and organisms come from. Without binding, everything eventually disperses. Binding is what makes reusable modules, hierarchy, and compositionality possible at all.

The route ran into a wall, and the wall turned out to be informative. No single local collision can bind two moving structures into one at a single speed. Every one of the $24$ sites out of a dock has the same length, one dock per beat, so the gas has exactly one speed. A local table working at one speed can only do one of two things. It can pin a pattern in place by reflection, which kills its mobility, or it can leave the vacuum active without confining anything, which gives no binding. There is no third option a local table can reach.

\begin{principle}[The single-speed obstruction]
Mobility and a local binding engine are mutually exclusive in a single collision at one speed. The only mobile local knit has an inert vacuum and no binding move. The local knits that carry a binding engine either pin the moving pattern or leave the vacuum globally active without confining it.
\end{principle}

The resolution is that the binding is not local. The body binds by the collective shadow it casts in the active vacuum, a radiation pressure that is transparent to the very waves the body sheds. The full mechanism, with its sign and its consistency with reversibility, is the self-binding section. The point here is that this step is resolved, and resolved precisely by giving up on the local collision as the source of capture.

\subsubsection{Open-system selves}

A closed reversible system has no preferred direction of time. An open one can. By injecting fresh empty low-tone docks at the growing edge, the mesh creates new room and sustained currents, and that is sustained non-equilibrium structure. This is how real life works. A living thing is not an equilibrium object. It exists because energy flows through it continuously.

The growing edge is the \emph{wake}, and the wake is where the arrow of time lives. New docks wake into being at peace, radiation falls behind the wake, and the past is carried away and never returns. The wake supplies the loss that the reversible bulk cannot.

\begin{result}[The arrow lives at the wake]
The closed bulk recovers exactly under time reversal. The open growing system does not, and it holds a sustained non-equilibrium current. The arrow can live at the boundary while the bulk stays reversible, which is very close to how thermodynamics actually works.
\end{result}

\subsubsection{Recursive coarse-grained selves}

This is the deepest route and the ultimate target. Stable microscopic patterns form stable mesoscopic patterns, which form stable macroscopic patterns. Particles, atoms, molecules, cells, organisms, societies. Each level treats the level below it statistically, coarse-graining it into its own effective parts.

That recursion is what produces nested causation, nested memory, and nested selves. It is not a new mechanism. It is the first route applied again and again, each application standing on the bound composites the third route makes available.

\subsection{Where the reversible knit stands}

It helps to be exact about what the bare conservative dynamics already delivers and what it does not. The reversible knit has persistence, mobility, and scattering. It lacks attraction, retention, stable binding, and compositional hierarchy.

So a local collision alone gives no pull, no holding, and no glue. Two approaching structures pass through each other instead of fusing into a larger whole. The behavior reads \textbf{gas-like, not organism-like}. The single gap that separates the two is one sentence.

\begin{principle}[The one remaining gap]
How to turn mobile reversible solitons into stable bound composites inside an open driven system.
\end{principle}

\subsection{The sharp answer}

This gap has been chased to its root, and the answer is sharp rather than hopeful. \textbf{Capture cannot come from the local bulk collision at all.}

Every local reversible knit on offer was tried. The momentum rotate, the pair table, the bind-and-move collision, the two-speed sites, and a purpose-built sticky reflection meant to bounce two colliding structures back together. Every one of them \textbf{scatters elastically}. Two approaching gliders leave as two separate components.

This is forced, not a tuning failure. A reversible collision cannot turn two incoming structures into one bound outgoing structure, because doing so would erase the distinction between the bound state and the many ways it could later come apart. Capture sheds the relative motion of the pair, and shedding is dissipation, which reversibility forbids inside the bulk.

\begin{theorem}[Capture is non-local]
A reversible local collision at a single speed cannot bind two incoming structures into one bound outgoing structure. Binding comes from the collective shadow pressure of the active vacuum and the open bath, never from a local collision.
\end{theorem}

The bulk stays reversible and elastic, exactly as it must. The body binds by the shadow of its own absence, and the wake is where the relative motion of a colliding pair is finally shed. The loss that capture demands is real, and it is sourced at the boundary, not in the table.

\subsection{The concrete moves}

Three moves carry the program from the gap to the bound composite, and the first two are already in hand.

\textbf{The bath is in hand.} An absorbing boundary is a genuine bath. A localized burst radiates outward and the center relaxes to empty, a tone count falling from eight to zero, while the very same burst on the closed torus recurs fully to the center, eight back to eight. The infinite mesh supplies the \emph{away} that capture needs, and the closed torus does not. The full account of why the bulk is reversible and what the bath is sits in the arrow-and-bath section.

\textbf{The attraction is in hand too.} A bath alone does not capture. A binary star inspirals only with both an attraction to fall into and radiation to a bath, and the same pairing is needed here. So the recipe is

\[
\text{capture} \;=\; \text{an attractive interaction} \;+\; \text{a bath}.
\]

The attraction is emergent, not a tweak to the base knit, and it is resolved. It is the shadow-pressure pull of the active vacuum, the same mechanism that binds the self to itself.

\textbf{Read off the self-level.} With capture in place, coarse-grain the captured composites into selves, and the recursive hierarchy is what that unlocks. The degeneracy trick reads the macro identity off the micro churn, and the recursive route applies it level by level up the tower.

\subsection{Why it matters}

Nested selves require selves made of selves, and that only works if the substrate supports stable semi-independent modules. A module needs partial decoupling so it has a private state, partial persistence so it lasts, and partial binding so it composes. Not too rigid, not too chaotic.

The pieces now divide cleanly. The reversible bulk gives the physics. The wake gives the loss. The shadow pressure of the active vacuum gives the binding. Coarse-graining reads off the self. What remains is the long settling movie and the recursive tower itself, the showcase of the mechanism rather than a missing part of it.

\begin{result}[The routes, assembled]
Each requirement for nesting is met by a named route. Reversible mobility supplies persistence and scattering, the open wake supplies the loss and the arrow, the shadow pressure of the active vacuum supplies the binding, and coarse-graining supplies the macro identity. The five routes compose into a single account of how nested selves arise while the bulk stays reversible.
\end{result}
