\section{The Arrow and the Bath}

The mesh has two laws. The \emph{knit} says how the state changes each beat, and the \emph{wake} says how the space changes each beat. The knit is reversible. It runs the same forward and backward, conserves charge, and never destroys information. This raises a sharp puzzle. A reversible law cannot lose information, yet a bound body must lose something to ever come to rest, and time must run one way for anything to settle at all. If the knit is reversible, where does the arrow of time come from, and where does a settling body dump what it sheds?

This section answers both. The arrow of time and the loss that lets bodies settle do not live in the knit. They live at the \emph{wake}, the growing edge of the mesh. The wake is what physicists call a \emph{bath}, a reservoir that swallows excess and never gives it back. We say first why the bulk knit must stay reversible, then what a bath is and why a self needs one, then how the wake assembles a genuine arrow out of three pieces that already sit among the eight atoms, and finally how a bound body rests at the still center while the disturbance it sheds streams away into the bath forever.

\subsection{Why the bulk is reversible}

Reversibility is a constraint on one thing only, the \emph{bulk update}. This is the local collision of the knit, the part of the law that runs inside every interior dock at every beat. It is not a constraint on the whole mesh. The wake adds fresh docks at the frontier, and adding docks enlarges the state space itself. Enlarging a space is not a permutation of a fixed space. So the mesh as a whole is already not a closed reversible map. Only its bulk knit is.

\begin{definition}[Reversible bulk knit]
The bulk knit is \emph{reversible} when its collision is a bijection on the fixed state of a closed patch of docks. Running it forward by one beat and then backward by one beat returns the patch exactly to where it began. The wake is excluded, because the wake changes the space rather than the state.
\end{definition}

\begin{principle}[The arrow must be earned]
The arrow of time must be earned, not smuggled in. An irreversible base is the same kind of cheat as a base attraction or a base gravity. It explains nothing, because it assumes the very thing it was meant to explain.
\end{principle}

A reversible permutation has a last practical virtue. It is exact integer arithmetic, bit-identical across machines, and testable by running forward then backward to the identity. An irreversible floating-point dynamics is none of these.

\subsubsection{The tradeoff, stated plainly}

Reversibility makes attractors, stable identities, dissipation, and robust nesting much harder, because nothing can truly merge or disappear. The contrast is simple.

\begin{itemize}
\item Irreversible systems create order by \textbf{throwing information away}.
\item Reversible systems create order by \textbf{reorganizing conserved information into stable patterns}.
\end{itemize}

The second is harder. It is also far more physically meaningful, and it is the work the rest of the theory carries out.

\subsubsection{The non-reversible routes are easier and worse}

If one wanted selves and did not care about physics, irreversible knits form attractors directly. Dissipative automata, stochastic dynamics, reaction-diffusion patterns, and energy-descent networks each grow selves more cheaply than a reversible knit ever could. Each pays the same bill. It loses quantum mechanics, relativity, and conservation, and it assumes the arrow at the start. The result is a generic self-organizing automaton, one of thousands, not a candidate fundamental theory. The reversible route is harder on purpose, because it is the only one that also delivers the physics.

\subsubsection{Why you never have to choose}

Two facts let the physics and the easy self-formation coexist, so the choice is false.

\begin{lemma}[The wake supplies the irreversibility]
Reversibility binds the bulk knit only. The wake adds fresh peace docks at the frontier, which is an irreversible enlargement of the state space. So the mesh as a whole is already non-reversible, with the irreversibility localized to the wake and the bulk physics left intact. Measured, the closed bulk recovers exactly under time reversal (Loschmidt echo zero), the growing mesh does not (echo $0.40$) and holds a sustained current (gradient $1.40$ against $0.01$).
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[Every irreversible knit is a reversible knit plus a bath]
Every irreversible dynamics equals a reversible dynamics on a larger space, with the extra degrees of freedom treated as environment. Classically, Landauer and Bennett show irreversible computation equals reversible computation plus erasure, and Toffoli and Morita show every cellular automaton, including every irreversible one, embeds in a reversible one on a larger space. The price is extra docks to hold the information the irreversible knit would have destroyed. The quantum analog is the Stinespring dilation, every non-unitary channel is a unitary on system-plus-environment with the environment traced out.
\end{theorem}

\begin{result}[The wake is the bigger space]
The wake is exactly the larger space the dilation theorem requires. The newly born docks are the blank tape, the garbage register, the bath. Coarse-graining, the act of forgetting the fine detail, is the tracing-out. A reversible bulk knit plus the wake plus coarse-graining is the dilation of an effective irreversible dynamics. It gives the attractor-forming behavior of the irreversible routes, realized as a reversible knit on a growing space, with the bulk physics never corrupted.
\end{result}

\subsection{What the bath is}

A \emph{bath} is everything you do not track that a structure can dump its excess into and never get back. It is the reservoir that makes dissipation possible while the whole mesh stays perfectly reversible. Other names for the same thing are the reservoir, the environment, and the rest of the world.

In every case the energy is never destroyed. It is spread into so many degrees of freedom, so far away, that it never comes back coherently. From the small thing's point of view that is loss. From the whole mesh's point of view nothing was lost.

\subsubsection{Why selves need a bath}

A self needs \emph{capture}, two structures binding into one. Capture means shedding the relative motion of the two structures so they settle together instead of bouncing off. Shedding means dumping that motion somewhere it will not come back. That somewhere is a bath.

This is exactly why reversible collisions could not capture. A reversible bulk knit has nowhere to dump the relative motion, so two structures can only scatter elastically and fly apart. No bath, no capture, no self.

\subsubsection{The three ways to get a bath}

The same idea wears three costumes. Each is a way to make somewhere for the excess to go and not return.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The infinite mesh itself, radiation to infinity.} On an infinite mesh a local structure radiates its excess outward as waves that travel forever and never return. The endless surrounding mesh is the bath. Nothing is added, no growth and no special boundary. A hyperbolic infinite mesh is an exceptionally good bath, because the number of docks grows exponentially with radius, so radiation disperses even faster and essentially never returns.
\item \textbf{The wake, new docks born at the frontier.} A finite growing mesh makes fresh empty low-entropy docks at its edge every beat. Those new docks are a reservoir, and the wake dilutes and absorbs. This is how to fake an away inside a finite mesh.
\item \textbf{Coarse-graining, the detail you stop watching.} The degrees of freedom you do not observe act as a bath. The relative motion hides in the unobserved fine detail and, as far as the coarse description is concerned, is gone.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Capture needs two things, attraction and a bath}

A bath alone does not capture. A binary star inspirals only because there is both an attractive force, a bound state to settle into, and radiation to infinity, a bath to shed the binding energy into. Remove the attraction and the bodies fly past each other, radiating a little but never binding.

\begin{principle}[The capture recipe]
Capture equals an attractive interaction, a bound state to fall into, plus a bath, somewhere to shed the binding energy into.
\end{principle}

The bath is in hand. The attraction is the remaining piece, and it must be an emergent force, not a tweak to the base knit.

\subsubsection{A filled mesh is not a bath}

A bath is not just infinite space. It is infinite \emph{empty}, low-entropy space.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Infinite and empty} equals a bath. Radiation flies out into quiet space and never returns.
\item \textbf{Infinite and filled} at maximum occupancy equals no bath. That is heat death. An outgoing wave just adds to the existing churn, carrying nothing away.
\end{itemize}

A bath works by having somewhere emptier and quieter for the excess to go. Heat flows from hot to cold, never the reverse. If the surroundings are already as hot and full as the structure, there is no gradient, no net flow, and nothing is absorbed. The requirement is therefore not infinity, it is infinite \emph{free capacity}. In cosmology this is the past hypothesis, the mesh can host an arrow of time only because it began near-empty and has stayed far from filled.

\subsubsection{The wake and the infinite mesh are partners}

The lean's create move tends to fill the mesh. Every patch of peace breeds a balanced pair, so left alone the mesh would fill toward heat death and the bath would saturate. The wake is what keeps it from filling. Fresh peace docks born at the frontier continuously refresh the empty capacity, so the bath never runs out. This is cosmic expansion, space stretching and cooling makes fresh low-entropy sink forever.

\begin{result}[A growing infinite mesh stays a bath forever]
A static infinite mesh can fill toward maximum entropy and die, ending the bath. A growing infinite mesh refreshes its empty capacity at the wake every beat, so it stays a bath forever. The infinite mesh provides the room, the away. The wake keeps that room empty, refreshing the bath's capacity so dissipation can continue without end.
\end{result}

\subsection{The arrow, assembled}

The arrow is emergent, not a base atom. It is produced by three tightly linked pieces, and each already lives among the eight atoms. The value ordering is the \emph{lean}, the create move is carried by the lean through the \emph{knit}, and the fresh empty room is the \emph{wake} on the open \emph{mesh}. Together they make the arrow.

\subsubsection{The lean, a value ordering}

The lean orders the tone, $-1 < 0 < +1$. This ordering does three things at once.

\begin{itemize}
\item It defines charge, the signed sum of the tones.
\item It makes peace, the value $0$, the middle, the empty state from which a balanced $+1, -1$ pair can be born.
\item It gives the create move a direction, peace can bring out a pair.
\end{itemize}

This is the smallest seed of an arrow, a built-in asymmetry in the value set.

\subsubsection{The create move, the active vacuum}

In the knit, peace $(0, 0)$ maps to $(+1, -1)$, which flips to $(-1, +1)$ and annihilates back to $(0, 0)$. Empty space is never truly still. It fluctuates, with balanced pairs appearing and vanishing. This is the \emph{active vacuum}. Its only force is a weak Casimir attraction. Mass excludes the vacuum, so nearby mass is pulled toward the depleted region. The force is sub-critical at long range, but enough at short range to repair a small displacement.

\subsubsection{The obstruction theorem}

\begin{theorem}[A closed mesh cannot host a self]
A closed toroidal mesh cannot host a self. By Poincare recurrence a reversible closed system returns arbitrarily close to its start, so any structure dissolves and reforms forever, never settling and never healing. The bare knit on a closed mesh produces only churn. The arrow, and therefore the self, require the open mesh with its wake.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Home, rest, and radiation}

A bound body can sit at \emph{home}, the still center, at rest. That is matter, a localized bound charge that can stop moving. Motion away from home is radiation, a net-zero ripple that is always moving and never at rest. The self heals by shedding a disturbance away from home into the bath, the ever-growing empty wake that swallows it and never returns it.

\begin{principle}[Rest and radiation]
The bound body rests at home. The shed disturbance leaves for the bath. The self returns to its ground state.
\end{principle}

\subsubsection{Binding by radiation}

The body holds itself together by the same bath it sheds into. This is \emph{binding by radiation}, and it is why the bath is non-negotiable.

\begin{itemize}
\item The bulk streaming stays an exact reversible bijection. Each tone shifts one dock along its site, and the shift is invertible. Nothing in the bulk is lost.
\item The only irreversibility is absorption at the body and radiation leaving to the bath. The body drinks the asymmetric vacuum flux that binds it, and the excess it sheds streams to the wake and never returns.
\item So a bound body can actually settle. The phase space it gives up does not vanish from the mesh, it leaves for the growing bath.
\end{itemize}

\begin{result}[Settling rides on what leaves for the bath]
Binding looks irreversible, but the bulk knit never breaks. The settling rides entirely on what leaves for the bath. A closed torus recurs by Poincare, the shed energy comes back, and the body never settles. The open mesh with its wake is the one-way street that lets a bound body come to rest while the bulk stays a perfect reversible bijection.
\end{result}

The full mechanism, the shadow of the depleted vacuum and why the restoring force carries the correct sign, is taken up where the self's self-binding is worked out in detail.

\subsection{The deep point}

Reversibility is never broken. The whole mesh, system plus bath, is always a reversible permutation. Dissipation, the arrow, capture, and selves all appear only when you stop following the bath. The bath is not a new ingredient in the laws. It is the part of the same reversible mesh you have chosen not to track. On an infinite mesh, that untracked part is simply everything far away.
