\section{Overview and Microscopic Automaton}
\label{sec:overview}

Can a single fixed, reversible discrete automaton in four hyperbolic dimensions host rigorous holographic error
correction, interacting bound states, quantum chaos, emergent gravity, and irreversible local decoherence while clearly
demarcating where additional gauge or continuum structure is forced? This question matters because discrete microscopic
models of physics routinely fail in one of two opposite ways: either they remain too simple to support bound matter,
field propagation, and classical records, or they smuggle in continuum structure through unstated approximations. We
investigate the question from first principles across spectral geometry, discrete holography, many-body scattering,
lattice gauge theory, quantum foundations, emergent gravity, cosmological asymmetry, and multiscale organization
\cite{schumacher2004, arrighi2019, farrelly2020}, pairing every positive theorem with its exact no-go boundary and
backing the suite with deterministic verification and a zero-dependency Lean~4 formalization
\cite{demoura2021lean4, cluesurf2026repo}.

We define each geometric object, operator, and convention at first use. Throughout the paper we distinguish cleanly
between three mathematical scopes: exact theorems of the bare automaton and its native geometry with nothing added,
dynamical extensions built on the same geometry by adding discrete subgroup gauge links or a local metric field, and
constructive lattice Yang--Mills bounds and null controls on the associated flat four-dimensional root lattice $D_4$.

\subsection{The 24-cell dock and the \texorpdfstring{$\{3,4,3,4\}$}{\{3,4,3,4\}} mesh}

The spatial substrate is the fixed, infinite regular tiling of four-dimensional hyperbolic space $\mathbb{H}^4$ by
regular 24-cells, denoted by its Schl\"afli symbol $\{3,4,3,4\}$ \cite{coxeter1956, coxeter1973}. In Schl\"afli
notation, triangles $\{3\}$ form octahedral facets $\{3,4\}$, octahedra bound self-dual 24-cells $\{3,4,3\}$, and
four 24-cells meet around every triangular ridge so that each vertex figure at infinity is a flat three-dimensional
cubic grid $\{4,3,4\}$. We refer to this fixed tessellation as the \emph{mesh} and to each individual 24-cell as a
\emph{dock}, reserving the word \emph{cell} for geometric polytopes and cellular automata. Each dock is self-dual and
bounded by 24 octahedral facets called \emph{slots}, indexed by the 24 roots of the four-dimensional root system $D_4$
\cite{conway1999}:
\begin{equation}
\Phi(D_4) = \bigl\{\,\text{all permutations of } (\pm 1, \pm 1, 0, 0) \in \mathbb{R}^4\,\bigr\},
\qquad
|\Phi(D_4)| = 24.
\label{eq:d4-roots}
\end{equation}
Unlike a four-dimensional hypercubic grid, the $D_4$ root vectors cancel all leading $O(a^2)$ rotational artifacts
identically through exact fourth-order $SO(4)$ isotropy:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \Phi(D_4)} r_a r_b r_c r_d
= 4\bigl(\delta_{ab}\delta_{cd} + \delta_{ac}\delta_{bd} + \delta_{ad}\delta_{bc}\bigr).
\label{eq:d4-isotropy}
\end{equation}
Pairing each slot $\mathbf{r}$ with its antipodal opposite $-\mathbf{r}$ forms 12 straight \emph{lines} passing through
the dock. Grouping these lines into three complementary orthogonal coordinate-plane pairs yields three disjoint
8-slot \emph{banks} (inscribed 16-cells). The full symmetry group of one dock is the Weyl group $W(F_4)$ of order
$1{,}152$, with proper rotation subgroup $W(F_4)^+$ of order $576$ and outer triality quotient
$W(F_4) / W(D_4) \cong S_3$.

Among all regular four-dimensional honeycombs, $\{3,4,3,4\}$ is uniquely selected by combining a finite self-dual
4-polytope, exact rank-4 isotropy, and a flat three-dimensional cubic vertex figure $\{4,3,4\}$ around each ideal point
at infinity (a \emph{cusp}). Fixing one ideal cusp foliates $\mathbb{H}^4$ into concentric \emph{horospheres} (level
surfaces orthogonal to all geodesics approaching the cusp, each carrying flat three-dimensional Euclidean geometry tiled
by unit cubes) indexed by integer depth $D = 0, 1, 2, \dots$ (Figure~\ref{fig:cusp-projection}). The outermost slice
$D = 0$ is the \emph{husk}, where three-dimensional observables are read. Behind every husk dock lies an exponentially
widening four-dimensional \emph{beam} of bulk docks whose counts grow roughly eighteenfold per depth floor. Crucially,
each husk dock has zero upward neighbors, 6 lateral neighbors inside $D = 0$, and 18 downward neighbors in $D = 1$, and
all 6 lateral slots pair antipodally across the dock with downward bulk slots.

Every dock center and facet transport is represented without floating-point rounding by an exact $5 \times 5$ Lorentz
matrix $g \in O^+(4,1; \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}])$ preserving the Coxeter bilinear form $2B(u, v) = u^T (2G) v$ and base
center $c_0$ with $2B(c_0, c_0) = -2$. Stepping across facet $a \in \{0, \dots, 23\}$ multiplies the dock frame on the
right by $k_a = F_{n_a} Z$, where $F_{n_a}$ reflects across facet normal $n_a$ and $Z$ is central inversion at $c_0$:
\begin{equation}
k_a^T (2G) k_a = 2G,
\qquad
\det(k_a) = -1,
\qquad
k_a k_{-a} = I_5.
\label{eq:transport}
\end{equation}
Because $\det(k_a) = -1$, every single hop flips orientation parity $\sigma(g) = \det(g) \in \{+1, -1\}$ and makes the
24-regular dock graph globally bipartite. Adjacent facets satisfying $\mathbf{r}_a \cdot \mathbf{r}_b = 1$ meet at right
angles, identifying the dock graph with the right-angled Coxeter group $W(\Gamma_{24})$ (generated by 24 involutions
$s_a^2 = 1$ that commute whenever facets $a$ and $b$ are orthogonal). Both spherical shell sizes
$|\partial B_r|$ ($1, 24, 456, 8{,}376, 153{,}192, \dots$) and cusp beam shell sizes $N_D$ ($1, 18, 330, 6{,}034, \dots$)
obey the exact Steinberg cubic recurrence:
\begin{equation}
x(n + 4) = 21\, x(n + 3) - 51\, x(n + 2) + 23\, x(n + 1),
\qquad
\lambda = 18.2787077744\ldots,
\label{eq:steinberg-cubic}
\end{equation}
yielding the 3D horosphere linear scale factor $a_1 = \lambda^{1/3} = 2.6341984907\ldots$ per depth floor and canonical
single-face lateral link counts $l_0 = 1$ and $l_D = 4 \cdot 5^{D-1}$ ($D \ge 1$).

\subsection{Cyclotomic amplitude ring and palindromic beat}

Each slot holds a signed ternary \emph{tone} $\tau \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$, the minimal signed alphabet admitting a neutral
center $0$ and an exact charge-conjugation involution $\mathcal{C} : \tau \mapsto -\tau$. Every occupied slot
($\tau = \pm 1$) also carries a discrete internal \emph{role} label $j \in \{0, \dots, 8\} \cong \mathbb{Z}_3^2$. The
all-zero configuration is the \emph{neutral quiescent mesh state} $|V_1\rangle$, an exact stationary fixed point with no
fluctuating vacuum. In the bare automaton, a \emph{link} denotes purely conceptual adjacency across a shared facet, with
no independent gauge register attached.

Quantum amplitudes live strictly inside the exact cyclotomic ring $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_9][1/6]$, where $\zeta_9$ satisfies
the sixth-degree cyclotomic polynomial $\zeta_9^6 + \zeta_9^3 + 1 = 0$ (geometrically $\zeta_9 = e^{2\pi i/9}$). Roots
of unity enter naturally rather than by choice: three-valued ternary phase turns force the cube root $\omega = \zeta_9^3$
($\omega^2 + \omega + 1 = 0$), deterministic splitting across antipodal slots forces denominator $2$, sixth-root phases
$\zeta_6 = 1 + \omega$ supply the determinant of the half-coin and chiral phase, and isotropic pair scattering across all
12 lines introduces denominator $12 = 4 \cdot 3$ together with the eighteenth root $\zeta_{18} = -\zeta_9^5$. No
transcendental constants or floating-point operations enter the discrete update.

One discrete time step, called a \emph{beat}, applies a single palindromic eight-layer unitary operator:
\begin{equation}
|\psi(t + 1)\rangle = U |\psi(t)\rangle,
\qquad
U = S \circ \Phi_{\mathrm{odd}} \circ D \circ M_{\mathrm{meet}} \circ M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\zeta_{18}) \circ K \circ D \circ \Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}.
\label{eq:bare-beat}
\end{equation}
Here every local \emph{coin} is a deterministic unitary splitting amplitude across directions with zero randomness:
\begin{equation}
D = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 2 + \omega & -\omega \\ -\omega & 2 + \omega \end{pmatrix},
\quad
C = D^2 = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 + \omega & 1 - \omega \\ 1 - \omega & 1 + \omega \end{pmatrix},
\quad
M_{\mathrm{meet}}^{\mathrm{diff}} = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 + \omega & \omega - 1 \\ \omega - 1 & 1 + \omega \end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:local-coins}
\end{equation}
The half-coin $D$ acts on lone-occupied lines and multiplies full lines by $\det D = \zeta_6$. At two-excitation
contact, $K$ swaps opposite-sign full lines with factor $-1$, while $M_{\mathrm{meet}}$ multiplies identical-role
same-sign full lines by $\omega$ and applies $M_{\mathrm{meet}}^{\mathrm{diff}}$ to distinct-role pairs. On any sole
identical-tone, identical-role antipodal pair, the 12-line pair mixer scatters isotropically across all 12 lines:
\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\zeta_{18}) = I_{12} + \frac{\zeta_{18} - 1}{12} J_{12},
\label{eq:mpair}
\end{equation}
where $J_{12}$ is the $12 \times 12$ all-ones matrix. Finally, $\Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ applies the chiral four-body phase
$\zeta_6^{\sigma(g)\chi_{\mathrm{odd}}(g)}$ built from the canonical $W(F_4) \times \mathbb{Z}_2^{\mathcal{C}}$
orientation sign $\chi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ (which is $\mathcal{C}$-odd, flipping sign when all tones negate, $\mathcal{P}$-odd
under spatial reflection, and $\mathcal{CP}$-even, turning on strictly at occupation $k^* = 4$). The final layer $S$
