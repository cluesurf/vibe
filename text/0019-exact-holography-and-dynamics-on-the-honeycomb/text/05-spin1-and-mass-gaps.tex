\section{Spin-1 Obstruction, Gauge Dynamics, and Mass Gaps}
\label{sec:gauge}

Testing whether massless transverse spin-1 gauge bosons can arise purely as multi-body bound states of the bare
automaton $U$, without link variables, separates what local matter rules generate on their own from where explicit link
degrees of freedom become structurally necessary. Here we prove an all-body parity superselection obstruction in bare
$U$, assemble discrete gauge--matter building blocks from native 24-cell and qutrit finite subgroups, and establish
strong-coupling, zero-truncation 4D $D_4$ lattice Yang--Mills mass gaps past $3\times$ Bakry--\'Emery.

\subsection{All-body spin-1 obstruction in the bare rule}

In the bare automaton, perturbative random-phase approximation (contact-RPA) analysis requires an unphysical coupling
$g_c = 8.7353$ to close the vector gap, and exact two-body $1^{--}$ dipoles (spin-1 vector states odd under parity and
charge conjugation) suffer complete $K$-layer cancellation that leaves a fixed $2\pi/3$ gap and zero transverse
velocity. Higher-body multi-axis scattering ($k \ge 3$) and finite-density neutral pair condensates cannot bypass this
obstruction: exact symmetry analysis rules out a massless transverse photon across all excitation counts $k \ge 1$ and
all particle densities $\bar{n} \ge 0$.

\begin{theorem}[All-Body Bare-$U$ Spin-1 Algebraic Obstruction]
\label{thm:spin1-obstruction}
Let $N_l^{(\tau)}$ be the total count of tone $\tau \in \{-1, +1\}$ on antipodal $D_4$ line $l \in \{0, \dots, 11\}$,
and let $P_l^{(\tau)} = (-1)^{N_l^{(\tau)}}$. Under the bare automaton $U$ over $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_9][1/6]$ with no
external link registers:

\textbf{(i) Exact $\mathbb{Z}_2^{24}$ per-line tone-parity superselection:} Because $M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\zeta_{18})$
transfers identical pairs in steps $\Delta N_l^{(\tau)} = \pm 2$ while all other layers preserve every $N_l^{(\tau)}$
individually, $[U, P_l^{(\tau)}] = 0$ identically. Against any parity-definite background $|\Omega\rangle$ at any
particle density $\bar{n} \ge 0$ (including $|V_1\rangle$ and every pair-coherent condensate), a single-dipole creation
operator $O_l^\dagger(x)$ on line $l$ decouples strictly from every distinct line $m \neq l$ for all time:
\begin{equation}
\langle\Omega | O_m(y)\, U^t\, O_l^\dagger(x) | \Omega\rangle = 0
\qquad (\forall\, m \neq l,\; \forall\, t \in \mathbb{Z}),
\label{eq:z24-superselection}
\end{equation}
forbidding single-dipole cross-line transfer and elementary $(+,-)$ four-cycle plaquette ring-exchange.

\textbf{(ii) Exact flat-transverse-band theorem for $\min(N_+, N_-) = 1$:} Whenever a sector holds a single negative
(or positive) excitation, $M_{\mathrm{pair}}$ never moves that lone tone, so its transverse coordinates commute strictly
with bare $U$ ($[U, X_{-,\perp}] = 0$, verified across all $10{,}710$ real-space states on $\mathbb{Z}^2 / 3\mathbb{Z}^2$
with $288$ active $M_{\mathrm{pair}}$ firings). Conjugating by $e^{i q_\perp \cdot X_{-,\perp}}$ enforces $v_T = 0$
identically for all $k \le 3$, making $k = 4$ ($Q = 0$) the algebraic minimum for nonzero transverse dispersion.

\textbf{(iii) Exhaustive $k = 4$ mutual exclusion and absence of local $U(1)$:} Every one of the $220$ triples of $D_4$
lines is fixed pointwise by an improper reflection $g \in W(F_4)^-$, forcing $\chi_{\mathrm{odd}} = 0$ on all
$43{,}296$ quads of at most three lines so $M_{\mathrm{pair}}$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ never coexist below $k = 5$.
Every local layer preserves charge $Q(g)$, whereas any spatially varying $U(1)$ gauge rotation fails to commute with the
stream $S$ ($\|[G_2(\pi/3), S_2]\|_2 = 1$).
\end{theorem}

Exact sparse multi-axis diagonalization on $\mathbb{Z}^2 / 3\mathbb{Z}^2$ at $k = 4$ ($\dim = 9{,}282$, unitarity
$\le 3.3 \times 10^{-16}$) confirms that $M_{\mathrm{pair}}$ opens only minor cross-axis leakage
($1.345 \times 10^{-3}$) while leaving the dipole peak gapped near $-2\pi/3$ ($\min|\Omega| = 0.3665$, $v_T = 0.0083$).

Finite-density neutral pair condensates ($\bar{n} \in [1/6, 1]$) likewise remain gapped: exact transverse Kubo response
(cross-checked on $4{,}096$-state evolution to $2.78 \times 10^{-15}$) and stationary Floquet Bogoliubov
diagonalization on two-line $D_4$ ($\dim = 210, \bar{n} = 2/3$) yield zero gapless weight ($W(\Delta E = 0) = 0$) with
transitions at $-2.47$ and $+2.96\text{ rad}$. Excluding an unmotivated spontaneous breaking of $\mathbb{Z}_2^{24}$ into
a pair-only spin liquid, \textbf{dynamical gauge link registers are structurally required}.

\subsection{Discrete gauge--matter building blocks from native subgroups}

Because Theorem~\ref{thm:spin1-obstruction} forces explicit gauge link registers, we construct and audit three separate
discrete gauge--matter building blocks anchored to finite groups native to the 24-cell and ternary qutrit
(Table~\ref{tab:unified-gauge}).

\textbf{Engineered single-speed 3D Maxwell--Dirac QCA:} Uncoupled bare pair transport ($c_{\mathrm{eff}} = 0.0213$) and
warped cusp-beam propagation ($0.9869$) differ by $46.4\times$. Coupling a palindromic split-step three-dimensional
$\{4,3,4\}$ cusp Weyl/Dirac automaton at the bare coin angle $\theta = \pi/3$ to a discrete-time Maxwell link beat whose
leapfrog weights match $\cos(\pi/3) = 1/2$ by construction (inside the cubic Courant--Friedrichs--Lewy stability limit
$\max\mathrm{CFL} = \sqrt{3}/2 < 1$) removes this mismatch. Photons and fermions share one light speed with two
degenerate transverse massless polarizations and zero propagating longitudinal mode, verified alongside exact quantum
$\mathbb{Z}_3$ plaquette Gauss invariance, linear Maxwell Coulomb radiation from a 1D flux string, an independent static
lattice Green's function, and massive Dirac opposite-attraction / like-repulsion scattering
(Table~\ref{tab:unified-gauge}).

\textbf{Static chiral $2T$ domain-wall kinematics:} The 24 unit Hurwitz quaternions at the vertices of the 24-cell form
the order-24 binary tetrahedral subgroup $2T = \mathrm{SL}(2,3) < SU(2)_L$, matching continuous Haar averages
identically across all $126$ monomials of degree $\le 5$ (an exact spherical 5-design and unitary 2-design, first
deviation $1/64$ at degree~6). Its 24-cell electric Laplacian has exact spectrum
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{spec}(\Delta_{2T})
= \bigl\{0\,(\times 1),\; 1/2\,(\times 4),\; 1\,(\times 9),\; 5/4\,(\times 8),\; 3/2\,(\times 2)\bigr\},
\label{eq:2t-spectrum}
\end{equation}
matching the low-lying representation multiplicities $(1, 4, 9)$ and ordering $j = 0 < 1/2 < 1$ (at finite-group ratio
$2$ rather than $SU(2)$'s $8/3$), while its $SU(2)$ embedding generates the $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ commutator algebra. On a
$6^3 \times 12$ four-dimensional domain wall removing the seven sheared Brillouin-zone Weyl fermion doublers at the
eight time-reversal-invariant momenta (TRIM), a static background $2T$ gauge flux produces left-handed spin-flip
transitions on $D = 0$ while decoupling right-handed modes to machine precision.

\textbf{Standalone finite-subgroup algebraic checks:} Combining $2T < SU(2)_L$ with the qutrit Clifford subgroup
$\Delta(27) \trianglelefteq \Sigma(648) < SU(3)_C$ (an exact unitary 2-design matching continuous $SU(3)$ Haar moments
through degree $t = 2$) and the ring's native $\mathbb{Z}_{18}$ phase group yields a finite group of order
$|G_{\mathrm{fin}}| = 46{,}656$ that (unlike $\mathbb{Z}_6$) represents all six input one-generation Standard Model
hypercharges and all four $B - L$ charges faithfully, with standard five-channel gauge-anomaly cancellation verified
algebraically on input chiral matter. These audits validate separate Abelian, static-chiral, and algebraic building
blocks rather than a single dynamically gauged Standard Model automaton. Because $2T$ and $\Sigma(648)$ are finite
subgroups, their low-degree Haar design properties govern strong coupling, while weak-coupling freezing transitions
obstruct a naive continuum limit (Section~\ref{sec:lean-and-open}).

\subsection{Strong-coupling 4D \texorpdfstring{$D_4$}{D4} lattice Yang--Mills mass gaps}

Independently of bare $U$ and of the finite-subgroup building blocks above, we analyze continuous-group Euclidean Wilson
lattice Yang--Mills theory \cite{wilson1974} with compact gauge links $U_e \in SU(N)$ on the 24-cell's flat
four-dimensional partner lattice $D_4$. On $D_4$, every site has 24 nearest neighbors, every link borders eight
equilateral triangles, $O(a^2)$ rotational artifacts vanish identically via Equation~\eqref{eq:d4-isotropy}, and
Osterwalder--Schrader reflection positivity across the $x_0 = 0$ site hyperplane holds exactly \cite{celmaster1982,
osterwalder1978, seiler1982}, reconstructing a positive-definite physical Hilbert space and self-adjoint transfer matrix
whose spectral gap $m(\beta) > 0$ certifies exponential correlation decay. Standard strong-coupling tools establish
$m(\beta) > 0$ at small $\beta$, including Koteck\'y--Preiss polymer cluster expansions, Bakry--\'Emery log-Sobolev
curvature bounds, and Dobrushin uniqueness criteria. Baseline 24-cell Bakry--\'Emery and Combes--Thomas bounds
\cite{bakry1985, combes1973} certify an infinite-volume mass gap up to $\beta_{\mathrm{BE}}^{24c} = N^2 / (16\sqrt{7})$
($0.094491$ for $SU(2)$, $0.212605$ for $SU(3)$). Four exact analytical theorems push this zero-truncation
strong-coupling window past the single-link Dobrushin ceiling without any Migdal--Kadanoff approximation
\cite{dobrushin1970, dobrushin1985, holley1987, chen1997, kotecky1986}.

\textbf{No-free-lunch ceiling for single-link sweeps:} Let $c$ denote the single-link Dobrushin influence coefficient of
one neighboring link across a shared triangle, and $\alpha_{13}$ the corresponding influence after exact two-link wedge
marginalization. Any block cover updated solely by iterated single-link conditionals yields influence matrix
$C_{\mathrm{pure}} = (I - A_{\mathrm{in}})^{-1} A_{\mathrm{out}}$, whose critical point $\rho(C_{\mathrm{pure}}) = 1$
coincides identically with the single-link ceiling $\rho(A) = 1$. Exceeding that ceiling requires genuine multi-link
marginalization satisfying $\gamma_{\mathrm{shared}} < c$, which holds precisely when $\alpha_{13} < c / (1 + c)$.

\textbf{Exact 1+2 block-alternating complete triangle cover:} Freezing one spatial axial direction preserves $x_0 = 0$
reflection positivity and drops the link Perron root from $16$ to $4 + 4\sqrt{7} = 14.5830$ (proved in Lean~4), while a
four-direction alternating-plane forest lowers the link Perron root further to $14.5684$. Updating one link
conditionally and the remaining two-link wedge by exact marginalization yields the symmetrized internal coupling and
$3 \times 3$ block factors:
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{\mathrm{shared}} = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(\alpha_{13} + c(1 + \alpha_{13})\bigr) < c,
\qquad
g_{\mathrm{self}} = \frac{c}{1 - 2c\gamma_{\mathrm{shared}}},
\qquad
g_{\mathrm{other}} = \frac{c\gamma_{\mathrm{shared}}}{1 - 2c\gamma_{\mathrm{shared}}}.
\label{eq:block-factors}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Sharp linear-split potential for $SU(2)$ and $SU(3)$ $\ln Z$ convexity:} For $SU(2)$, subtracting the optimal
midpoint linear staple term proportional to $(U_1^\dagger U_3^\dagger) \cdot W_{\mathrm{bg}}$ (where $W_{\mathrm{bg}}$
is the background staple sum) from the integrated two-link Bessel potential $V_2$ reduces the Holley--Stroock residual
oscillation from $0.56$ to below $0.0080$ at $\beta = 0.277274$, cutting $\alpha_{13}$ from $0.04578$ to $0.03224$. For
$SU(3)$, expanding $V_2(U_1, U_3)$ around $W_{\mathrm{bg}}$ keeps the linear staple inside $\operatorname{conv}(SU(3))$
and bounds the convex remainder uniformly by $\beta^2 / 6$ times its maximum eigenvalue variance. A deterministic
branch-and-bound sweep over $20{,}384$ boxes at $\beta_0 = 0.5635$ (cross-checked by analytic Rouch\'e tails and Weyl
quadrature) certifies $\alpha_{13}^{SU(3)}(\beta_0) \le 0.04055$.

Evaluating time-tilted block Dobrushin matrices $G(m)$ ($\rho(G(m)) < 1$) yields strong-coupling infinite-volume mass
gaps past $3\times$ Bakry--\'Emery for $SU(2)$ ($\beta_* = 0.285141$, $30.55\times$ Koteck\'y--Preiss) and past
$60\times$ Koteck\'y--Preiss for $SU(3)$ ($\beta_* = 0.563019$, $2.65\times$ Bakry--\'Emery), summarized in
Figure~\ref{fig:mass-gap-bars} and Table~\ref{tab:d4-mass-gaps}. These remain strong-coupling lattice theorems and do
not address the continuum Clay Yang--Mills limit $\beta \to \infty$ \cite{jaffe2000ym, balaban1989}.
