\section{Gauge Fields}
\label{sec:gauge}

Section~\ref{sec:composites} established that bare $U$ binds collinear multi-body composites with integrable scattering,
level repulsion, and light-mass relativistic clock scaling. The next dynamical test asks whether massless transverse
spin-1 gauge bosons can also arise purely as multi-body bound states of bare $U$ without link variables, separating
what local matter rules generate on their own from where explicit link degrees of freedom become structurally forced.
Here we prove an all-body parity superselection obstruction in bare $U$, assemble separate discrete gauge--matter
building blocks from native 24-cell and qutrit finite subgroups, and establish strong-coupling, zero-truncation 4D
$D_4$ lattice Yang--Mills mass gaps past $3\times$ Bakry--\'Emery.

\subsection{Spin-1 obstruction}

Many discrete emergent-gauge programs hope that massless transverse spin-1 bosons will condense spontaneously from local
matter rules once higher-body scattering or a dense pair background is included, often relying on perturbative
approximations or small finite clusters without checking exact conservation laws. In bare $U$, perturbative random-phase
approximation (contact-RPA) analysis already flags an obstruction by requiring an unphysical coupling $g_c = 8.7353$ to
close the vector gap, and exact two-body $1^{--}$ dipoles (spin-1 vector states odd under parity and charge conjugation)
suffer complete $K$-layer cancellation that leaves a fixed $2\pi/3$ gap and zero transverse velocity.

One might still expect multi-axis collisions ($k \ge 3$), four-body plaquette exchange, or a neutral pair condensate to
bend a dipole onto orthogonal axes and close that gap. Exact symmetry analysis of bare $U$ closes every one of those
escape routes before any approximation enters. Every local layer of $U$ preserves individual tone counts on each of the
$12$ antipodal $D_4$ lines except $M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\zeta_{18})$, which transfers identical pairs strictly in even
steps $\Delta N_l^{(\tau)} = \pm 2$. The parity of positive and negative tones on every line separately is therefore an
exact constant of motion, producing $24$ independent $\mathbb{Z}_2$ superselection charges. Against any parity-definite
vacuum or pair condensate, a lone dipole created on line~$l$ flips parities solely on line~$l$ and can never hop onto a
transverse line $m \neq l$, ruling out a massless composite photon across all excitation counts $k \ge 1$ and all
densities $\bar{n} \ge 0$.

\begin{theorem}[All-Body Bare-$U$ Spin-1 Algebraic Obstruction]
\label{thm:spin1-obstruction}
Let $N_l^{(\tau)}$ be the total count of tone $\tau \in \{-1, +1\}$ on antipodal $D_4$ line $l \in \{0, \dots, 11\}$,
and let $P_l^{(\tau)} = (-1)^{N_l^{(\tau)}}$. Under the bare automaton $U$ over $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_9][1/6]$ with no
external link registers:

\textbf{(i) Exact $\mathbb{Z}_2^{24}$ per-line tone-parity superselection:} Because $M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\zeta_{18})$
transfers identical pairs in steps $\Delta N_l^{(\tau)} = \pm 2$ while all other layers preserve every $N_l^{(\tau)}$
individually, $[U, P_l^{(\tau)}] = 0$ identically. Against any parity-definite background $|\Omega\rangle$ at any
particle density $\bar{n} \ge 0$ (including $|V_1\rangle$ and every pair-coherent condensate), a single-dipole creation
operator $O_l^\dagger(x)$ on line $l$ decouples strictly from every distinct line $m \neq l$ for all time:
\begin{equation}
\langle\Omega | O_m(y)\, U^t\, O_l^\dagger(x) | \Omega\rangle = 0
\qquad (\forall\, m \neq l,\; \forall\, t \in \mathbb{Z}),
\label{eq:z24-superselection}
\end{equation}
forbidding single-dipole cross-line transfer and elementary $(+,-)$ four-cycle plaquette ring-exchange.

\textbf{(ii) Exact flat-transverse-band theorem for $\min(N_+, N_-) = 1$:} Whenever a sector holds a single negative
(or positive) excitation, $M_{\mathrm{pair}}$ never moves that lone tone, so its transverse coordinates commute strictly
with bare $U$ ($[U, X_{-,\perp}] = 0$, verified across all $10{,}710$ real-space states on $\mathbb{Z}^2 / 3\mathbb{Z}^2$
with $288$ active $M_{\mathrm{pair}}$ firings). Conjugating by $e^{i q_\perp \cdot X_{-,\perp}}$ enforces $v_T = 0$
identically for all $k \le 3$, making $k = 4$ ($Q = 0$) the algebraic minimum for nonzero transverse dispersion.

\textbf{(iii) Exhaustive $k = 4$ mutual exclusion and absence of local $U(1)$:} Every one of the $220$ triples of $D_4$
lines is fixed pointwise by an improper reflection $g \in W(F_4)^-$, forcing $\chi_{\mathrm{odd}} = 0$ on all
$43{,}296$ quads of at most three lines so $M_{\mathrm{pair}}$ and $\Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ never coexist below $k = 5$.
Every local layer preserves charge $Q(g)$, whereas any spatially varying $U(1)$ gauge rotation fails to commute with the
stream $S$ ($\|[G_2(\pi/3), S_2]\|_2 = 1$).
\end{theorem}

Exact sparse multi-axis diagonalization on $\mathbb{Z}^2 / 3\mathbb{Z}^2$ at the algebraic minimum $k = 4$
($\dim = 9{,}282$, unitarity error $\le 3.3 \times 10^{-16}$) verifies how strongly this obstruction persists once
transverse scattering first becomes kinematically allowed. Active $M_{\mathrm{pair}}$ mixing leaves the vector dipole
peak pinned near $-2\pi/3$ with negligible cross-axis leakage and near-zero transverse speed:
\begin{equation}
\text{cross-axis leakage} = 1.345 \times 10^{-3},
\qquad
\min|\Omega| = 0.3665,
\qquad
v_T = 0.0083.
\label{eq:k4-dipole-gap}
\end{equation}

Finite-density neutral pair condensates across $\bar{n} \in [1/6, 1]$ do not soften this gap either. Exact transverse
Kubo linear response (cross-checked against $4{,}096$-state time evolution to $2.78 \times 10^{-15}$) confirms that a
background pair condensate keeps the transverse current strictly gapped.

Independently, stationary Floquet Bogoliubov diagonalization on two-line $D_4$ ($\dim = 210$, $\bar{n} = 2/3$) finds
exact zero gapless spectral weight, $W(\Delta E = 0) = 0$, with all vector transitions pushed to $-2.47$ and
$+2.96\text{ rad}$.

Rather than leaving an ambiguous failure, Theorem~\ref{thm:spin1-obstruction} turns a negative result into a precise
structural blueprint. It demonstrates that no parameter tuning or higher-body scattering inside bare $U$ can
manufacture a composite photon while preserving the local rule. Excluding an unmotivated spontaneous breaking of
$\mathbb{Z}_2^{24}$ into a pair-only spin liquid, breaking per-line parity locally requires parallel transport across
24-cell facets, so \textbf{dynamical gauge link registers are mathematically forced}.

\subsection{Discrete gauge blocks}

Once Theorem~\ref{thm:spin1-obstruction} compels explicit link registers, a natural structural question follows: which
discrete gauge registers belong on a 24-cell without importing unrelated external algebra. No arbitrary finite groups
need to be invented from outside. The 24-cell's own vertex polytope and the ternary qutrit state space already contain
finite subgroups that act as exact unitary 2-designs inside $SU(2)_L$ and $SU(3)_C$, matching continuous Haar moments
through degree $t = 2$, while the amplitude ring $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_9][1/6]$ supplies $\mathbb{Z}_{18}$. Anchored to
these native groups, we build and audit three separate discrete gauge--matter building blocks
(Table~\ref{tab:unified-gauge}).

\textbf{Engineered single-speed 3D Maxwell--Dirac QCA:} Uncoupled discrete automata typically separate matter and
radiation onto mismatched light cones. Here bare pair transport ($c_{\mathrm{eff}} = 0.0213$) and warped cusp-beam
propagation ($0.9869$) differ by $46.4\times$. To eliminate that mismatch, we couple a palindromic split-step
three-dimensional $\{4,3,4\}$ cusp Weyl/Dirac automaton at the bare coin angle $\theta = \pi/3$ to a discrete-time
Maxwell link beat whose leapfrog weights match $\cos(\pi/3) = 1/2$ by construction, staying safely inside the cubic
Courant--Friedrichs--Lewy stability limit ($\max\mathrm{CFL} = \sqrt{3}/2 < 1$).

Under this matched Abelian link beat, photons and fermions share one light speed with two degenerate transverse massless
polarizations and zero propagating longitudinal mode. Independent audits verify exact quantum $\mathbb{Z}_3$ plaquette
Gauss invariance, linear Maxwell Coulomb radiation from a 1D flux string, a static $64^3$ lattice Green's function, and
live massive Dirac scattering showing opposite-charge attraction and like-charge repulsion
(Table~\ref{tab:unified-gauge}). Because the leapfrog weights are matched to $\cos(\pi/3)$ by construction, this
subsystem is an engineered Abelian coupling rather than a consequence of bare $U$.

\textbf{Static chiral $2T$ domain-wall kinematics:} The 24 unit Hurwitz quaternions sitting at the vertices of the
24-cell form the order-24 binary tetrahedral subgroup $2T = \mathrm{SL}(2,3) < SU(2)_L$. Because $2T$ is an exact
spherical 5-design and unitary 2-design, it matches continuous $SU(2)$ Haar averages identically across all $126$
monomials of degree $\le 5$, with the first finite-subgroup deviation ($1/64$) appearing only at degree~6. Every
strong-coupling lattice observable determined by degree-2 Haar moments, including single-plaquette expectations and
leading link correlators, therefore agrees identically with continuous $SU(2)$. Its 24-cell electric Laplacian has exact
spectrum
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{spec}(\Delta_{2T})
= \bigl\{0\,(\times 1),\; 1/2\,(\times 4),\; 1\,(\times 9),\; 5/4\,(\times 8),\; 3/2\,(\times 2)\bigr\},
\label{eq:2t-spectrum}
\end{equation}
matching the low-lying representation multiplicities $(1, 4, 9)$ and ordering $j = 0 < 1/2 < 1$ (at finite-group ratio
$2$ rather than $SU(2)$'s $8/3$), while its $SU(2)$ embedding generates the full $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ commutator algebra.

Discretizing Weyl fermions directly on a three-dimensional cubic cusp runs into standard fermion doubling across the
eight time-reversal-invariant momenta (TRIM), where opposite chiralities cancel ($\chi = +4, -4$). Embedding the cusp as
the $D = 0$ boundary of a $6^3 \times 12$ four-dimensional domain wall strips away the seven sheared Brillouin-zone
doublers while preserving one isotropic $\Gamma$-point Weyl cone. Applying a static background $2T$ gauge flux produces
left-handed spin-flip transitions on $D = 0$ while decoupling right-handed modes to machine precision and suppressing
the $D = 11$ mirror wall tail (Table~\ref{tab:unified-gauge}).

\textbf{Standalone finite-subgroup algebraic checks:} Combining $2T < SU(2)_L$ with the qutrit Clifford subgroup
$\Delta(27) \trianglelefteq \Sigma(648) < SU(3)_C$ (the ternary Hessian group, an exact unitary 2-design matching
continuous $SU(3)$ Haar moments through degree $t = 2$) and the ring's native $\mathbb{Z}_{18}$ phase group yields a
finite group of order $|G_{\mathrm{fin}}| = 46{,}656$. Unlike $\mathbb{Z}_6$, the $\mathbb{Z}_{18}$ factor represents
all six input one-generation Standard Model hypercharges and all four $B - L$ charges faithfully, with standard
five-channel gauge-anomaly cancellation verified algebraically on input chiral matter.

These audits validate separate Abelian, static-chiral, and algebraic building blocks rather than a single dynamically
gauged Standard Model automaton. Moreover, finite subgroups carry a known dynamical frontier: because $2T$ and
$\Sigma(648)$ have a finite minimal angular separation between distinct elements, their unitary 2-design identities
control strong coupling accurately, whereas at weak coupling ($\beta$ large) Wilson plaquette dynamics undergoes a
freezing transition into a locked trivial phase that obstructs a naive continuum limit (Section~\ref{sec:lean-and-open}).

\subsection{Lattice mass gaps}

Because finite subgroups freeze at weak coupling, rigorous non-Abelian mass-gap analysis turns independently of bare $U$
and of the finite-subgroup building blocks above to continuous-group Euclidean Wilson lattice Yang--Mills theory
\cite{wilson1974} with compact gauge links $U_e \in SU(N)$ on the 24-cell's flat four-dimensional partner lattice $D_4$.
Most lattice Yang--Mills analyses work on the standard hypercubic lattice $\mathbb{Z}^4$, where square plaquettes break
rotational invariance at leading order $O(a^2)$. On $D_4$, every site meets 24 nearest neighbors and every link borders
eight equilateral triangles, so $O(a^2)$ rotational artifacts vanish identically via Equation~\eqref{eq:d4-isotropy}.
Simultaneously, Osterwalder--Schrader reflection positivity across the $x_0 = 0$ site hyperplane holds exactly
\cite{celmaster1982, osterwalder1978, seiler1982}, reconstructing a positive-definite physical Hilbert space and
self-adjoint transfer matrix whose spectral gap $m(\beta) > 0$ certifies exponential correlation decay.

Standard rigorous strong-coupling methods certify $m(\beta) > 0$ at small $\beta$, yet stall early on densely
triangulated lattices. Koteck\'y--Preiss polymer cluster expansions \cite{kotecky1986} lose convergence to the rapidly
growing combinatorial entropy of overlapping triangular polymers, stalling at $\beta \approx 0.0093$ for both $SU(2)$
and $SU(3)$. Single-link Bakry--\'Emery log-Sobolev curvature and Combes--Thomas bounds \cite{bakry1985, combes1973}
avoid polymer counting and lift the infinite-volume threshold to $\beta_{\mathrm{BE}}^{24c} = N^2 / (16\sqrt{7})$
($0.094491$ for $SU(2)$ and $0.212605$ for $SU(3)$), yet still bound each link's eight neighboring triangles as
independent worst-case perturbations, ignoring exact internal cancellations across shared plaquettes. Four exact
analytical theorems push this unconditional, zero-truncation strong-coupling window far past the single-link Dobrushin
ceiling without any Migdal--Kadanoff approximation \cite{dobrushin1970, dobrushin1985, holley1987, chen1997}.

\textbf{No-free-lunch ceiling for single-link sweeps:} Grouping links into multi-link blocks does not improve
contraction on its own. Let $c$ denote the single-link Dobrushin influence coefficient of one neighboring link across a
shared triangle, and $\alpha_{13}$ the corresponding influence after exact two-link wedge marginalization. Any block
cover updated solely by iterated single-link conditionals produces block influence matrix
$C_{\mathrm{pure}} = (I - A_{\mathrm{in}})^{-1} A_{\mathrm{out}}$, whose critical threshold $\rho(C_{\mathrm{pure}}) = 1$
coincides identically with the single-link ceiling $\rho(A) = 1$. Surpassing that ceiling requires genuine multi-link
marginalization inside each triangle so internal cancellations drive the shared coupling strictly below $c$
($\gamma_{\mathrm{shared}} < c$), which holds precisely when $\alpha_{13} < c / (1 + c)$.

\textbf{Exact 1+2 block-alternating complete triangle cover:} Fixing one spatial axial direction removes gauge
redundancy while preserving $x_0 = 0$ reflection positivity and dropping the link Perron root from $16$ to
$4 + 4\sqrt{7} = 14.5830$ (proved in Lean~4), while a four-direction alternating-plane forest lowers the link Perron
root further to $14.5684$. Covering the remaining active links with complete three-link triangles and running a $1 + 2$
block-alternating sweep (updating one link conditionally and integrating out the remaining two-link wedge exactly)
yields the symmetrized internal coupling and $3 \times 3$ block factors:
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{\mathrm{shared}} = \frac{1}{2}\bigl(\alpha_{13} + c(1 + \alpha_{13})\bigr) < c,
\qquad
g_{\mathrm{self}} = \frac{c}{1 - 2c\gamma_{\mathrm{shared}}},
\qquad
g_{\mathrm{other}} = \frac{c\gamma_{\mathrm{shared}}}{1 - 2c\gamma_{\mathrm{shared}}}.
\label{eq:block-factors}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Sharp linear-split potential for $SU(2)$:} Realizing the gain in Equation~\eqref{eq:block-factors} requires
controlling the effective two-link Bessel potential $V_2(U_1, U_3)$ without losing sharpness to coarse worst-case
bounds. For $SU(2)$, subtracting the optimal midpoint linear staple term proportional to $(U_1^\dagger U_3^\dagger)
\cdot W_{\mathrm{bg}}$ (where $W_{\mathrm{bg}}$ is the background staple sum) removes nearly all variation of $V_2$,
slashing the Holley--Stroock residual oscillation from $0.56$ to below $0.0080$ at $\beta = 0.277274$. This linear
split pulls the two-link influence $\alpha_{13}$ down from $0.04578$ to $0.03224$, opening exact multi-link contraction
cleanly past single-link limits.

\textbf{Uniform $\ln Z$ convexity for $SU(3)$:} For $SU(3)$, expanding $V_2(U_1, U_3)$ around $W_{\mathrm{bg}}$ keeps
the linear staple inside the convex hull $\operatorname{conv}(SU(3))$ and bounds the convex remainder uniformly by
$\beta^2 / 6$ times its maximum eigenvalue variance. A deterministic branch-and-bound sweep over $20{,}384$ boxes at
$\beta_0 = 0.5635$ (cross-checked independently by analytic Rouch\'e tails and Weyl quadrature) certifies
$\alpha_{13}^{SU(3)}(\beta_0) \le 0.04055$, bringing exact three-link triangle contraction well beyond single-link
$SU(3)$ thresholds.

Evaluating time-tilted block Dobrushin matrices $G(m)$ ($\rho(G(m)) < 1$) certifies unconditional, zero-truncation
infinite-volume mass gaps far beyond both classical barriers, summarized in Figure~\ref{fig:mass-gap-bars} and
Table~\ref{tab:d4-mass-gaps}:
\begin{equation}
\beta_*^{SU(2)} = 0.285141 \;\; (3.02\times\mathrm{BE},\; 30.55\times\mathrm{KP}),
\qquad
\beta_*^{SU(3)} = 0.563019 \;\; (2.65\times\mathrm{BE},\; 60.36\times\mathrm{KP}).
\label{eq:ym-thresholds}
\end{equation}
Structurally, these bounds confirm that $D_4$'s equilateral triangle plaquettes support exact multi-link contraction deep
past polymer and single-link convexity walls without uncontrolled decimation. At the same time, they remain
finite-$\beta$ strong-coupling lattice theorems and do not address the weak-coupling $\beta \to \infty$ Clay continuum
limit \cite{jaffe2000ym, balaban1989}.
