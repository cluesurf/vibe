\section{Automaton, Honeycomb Geometry, and Beat Rule}
\label{sec:one-rule}

This section defines the geometry and the rule. It starts inside one dock, with its 24 doorways and the values they
hold. It then glues docks into the infinite honeycomb and describes that honeycomb relative to a cusp, where it splits
into flat 3D floors backed by a widening 4D bulk. It constructs the exact number
ring in which all amplitudes live, assembles the eight layers of one beat, and traces a few configurations through
them. Everything here is Tier~A: it is the bare automaton, with nothing prescribed.

Two results at the end of the section are used throughout the paper. The 3D husk cannot be evolved on its own, because two
bulk states that look identical on the husk separate there within one beat (\code{E-HLG-0043}). And the rule keeps
exact $\mathcal{T}$, $\mathcal{CP}$, $\mathcal{CPT}$, and order-576 $W(F_4)^+$ covariance while 4-body interference
through $\Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ produces a local $\mathcal{C}$-asymmetry (\universecode, \code{E-FND-0184}).

\subsection{Docks, slots, tones, lines, and banks}

Table~\ref{tab:glossary} fixes the vocabulary and maps each term to its standard equivalent. Symbols are reserved
throughout: $C_{1/2}$ and $C$ are the half and full coins, $D \in \mathbb{N}$ is horosphere depth (distinct from the
root system $D_4$), $S$ is the stream (with $|\partial B_r|$ for ball shells, $\mathcal{S}$ for lattice gauge actions,
and $S_{\mathrm{vN}}$ for von Neumann entropy), and calligraphic $\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{P}, \mathcal{T}$ are the
discrete symmetries. One lattice site is a \emph{dock}, a four-dimensional junction where 24 slots attach. The word
avoids \emph{cell}, which collides with both the 24-cell and the cellular automaton.

Three terms carry no hidden content. The all-zero state $|V_1\rangle$ is a quiescent fixed point
($U|V_1\rangle = |V_1\rangle$), with no fluctuating vacuum. A \emph{coin} is a deterministic local unitary that splits
amplitude between directions, with no randomness. A \emph{link} in the bare rule is shorthand for adjacency across a
shared octahedral facet, and no independent gauge variable lives on it.

The value held in one slot is a signed ternary \emph{tone},
\begin{equation}
\tau \in \{-1, 0, +1\}.
\label{eq:trits}
\end{equation}
Three values form the smallest signed alphabet with a neutral center ($0$) and an exact charge conjugation
$\mathcal{C}: \tau \mapsto -\tau$ (\code{E-FND-0038}). For any finite region, \emph{charge} is the net signed count
$Q = N_+ - N_-$ and \emph{content} is the total occupation $N = N_+ + N_-$.

The beat conserves $Q$ exactly, so a globally neutral state has $N_+ = N_-$. Telling $+1$ from $-1$ locally inside such
a state therefore needs an observable that is $\mathcal{C}$-odd (it flips sign when every tone flips) and
$\mathcal{CP}$-even (it is unchanged when tones flip and space is mirrored together). Section~\ref{sec:cp-balance}
shows that the rule produces such an observable while its global dynamics stays $\mathcal{CP}$-balanced.

The dock itself is a regular four-dimensional 24-cell \cite{conway1999}. It is self-dual, with 24 vertices, 96 edges,
96 triangular faces, and 24 octahedral facets. The facets are the doorways. They are indexed by the 24 roots of $D_4$,
the vectors obtained by choosing 2 of 4 Cartesian axes with independent signs ($\binom{4}{2} \times 2^2 = 24$):
\begin{equation}
\Phi(D_4) = \bigl\{\,\text{all permutations of } (\pm 1, \pm 1, 0, 0) \in \mathbb{R}^4\,\bigr\},
\qquad |\Phi(D_4)| = 24.
\label{eq:d4-roots}
\end{equation}

Relative to any one doorway $\mathbf{r}$, the 24 doorways fall into five classes by angle (Table~\ref{tab:d4-nbrs}). The symmetry group of the dock is the Weyl group $W(F_4)$ of order $1{,}152$
(\code{E-GMT-0046}), with rotation subgroup $W(F_4)^+$ of order $576$. The 24 doorways are spread so evenly that their
fourth moment is already rotation-invariant in four dimensions:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \Phi(D_4)} r_a r_b r_c r_d
= 4\bigl(\delta_{ab}\delta_{cd} + \delta_{ac}\delta_{bd} + \delta_{ad}\delta_{bc}\bigr),
\label{eq:d4-isotropy}
\end{equation}
so $\sum r_0^4 = 12 = 3 \sum r_0^2 r_1^2$ with zero defect. A hypercubic $\mathbb{Z}^4$ grid breaks rotational
invariance at $O(a^2)$. The 24-cell cancels every $O(a^2)$ directional artifact identically, the property used in
face-centered hypercubic hydrodynamics \cite{frisch1987fchc} and lattice gauge theory \cite{celmaster1982}. The
isotropy found on the husk in Section~\ref{sec:relativity} rests on this property.

Two partitions of the doorways recur through the paper. Pairing each doorway $\mathbf{r}$ with its opposite
$-\mathbf{r}$ gives 12 straight \emph{lines} $\ell \in \{0, \ldots, 11\}$, corridors that pass straight through the
dock. Grouping the lines by complementary coordinate planes $\{12, 34\}$, $\{13, 24\}$, and $\{14, 23\}$ gives three
disjoint inscribed 16-cells, the \emph{banks} ($3 \times 8 = 24$). The 4 lines in one bank are mutually perpendicular
and span $\mathbb{R}^4$, while roots in different banks meet at $60^\circ$ or $120^\circ$. The three banks are cycled
by the inner coordinate 3-cycle
$T_3 \in S_4 < W(D_4)$ via $S_4/V_4 \cong S_3$, which is distinct from outer $D_4$ triality
$\operatorname{Out}(D_4) \cong W(F_4)/W(D_4) \cong S_3$ permuting the three dual 16-cells ($8_v, 8_s, 8_c$) of the
dual 24-cell (Figure~\ref{fig:dock-24cell}). Lines confine a lone excitation to one corridor until
Section~\ref{sec:relativity} lets pairs cross between them, and the three banks reappear in Section~\ref{sec:gauge} as
three copies of a chiral mode.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$\{3,4,3,4\}$}{\{3,4,3,4\}} honeycomb and its cusp}

Gluing docks face to face across their octahedral facets builds the regular hyperbolic honeycomb $\{3,4,3,4\}$
\cite{coxeter1956, coxeter1973, margenstern2007vol1}. Read the symbol from the inside out: triangles $\{3\}$ meet four
at a vertex to form octahedra $\{3,4\}$, octahedra meet three around an edge to form 24-cells $\{3,4,3\}$, and 24-cells
meet four around every triangular face. That last number forces the space to curve negatively, and it makes the
neighborhood of each ideal point at infinity (a \emph{cusp}) a flat cubic grid $\{4,3,4\}$.

To move through this infinite space without lookup tables or rounding, every dock center and every facet reflection is
an exact $5 \times 5$ matrix over $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$. Two objects fix the whole geometry: the doubled Coxeter Gram
matrix $2G$, whose leading principal minors $2, 3, 2, 1, -2$ confirm Lorentzian signature $(4,1)$, and the base center
$c_0$:
\begin{equation}
2G = \begin{pmatrix}
2 & -1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
-1 & 2 & -\sqrt{2} & 0 & 0 \\
0 & -\sqrt{2} & 2 & -1 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & -1 & 2 & -\sqrt{2} \\
0 & 0 & 0 & -\sqrt{2} & 2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
c_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \\ 3\sqrt{2} \\ 2\sqrt{2} \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:coxeter-gram}
\end{equation}
Three short rules turn Equation~\eqref{eq:coxeter-gram} into the exact neighbor map $\mathrm{step}(d, s)$, the
answer to the question ``which dock lies through doorway $s$ of dock $d$?'':
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Lorentzian distance:} The matrix $2G$ defines the bilinear form $2B(u, v) = u^T (2G) v$, normalized so
that $2B(c_0, c_0) = -2$. For any isometry $g$, hyperbolic distance from the origin is an exact integer invariant:
$-2B(g c_0, c_0) = 2\cosh d_{\mathbb{H}^4}(g c_0, c_0) \in \mathbb{Z}$.
\item \textbf{The 24 octahedral facets:} Reflecting across a spacelike facet normal $n$ ($2B(n, n) = 2$) acts by
$F_n(v) = v - (2B(n, v))\, n$ ($\det F_n = -1$). Acting on the fifth basis vector $e_4$ with the base stabilizer
$W(F_4) = \langle F_{e_0}, F_{e_1}, F_{e_2}, F_{e_3} \rangle$ produces all 24 facet normals $\{n_s\}_{s=0}^{23}$,
indexed bijectively by the $D_4$ roots via $(2B(n_a, n_b) + 2)/2 \in \{-2, -1, 0, 1, 2\}$.
\item \textbf{Single-hop cell transport:} Let $Z$ be the central inversion fixing $c_0$ while negating its 4D tangent
space ($Z(v) = -v - 2B(c_0, v)\, c_0$, $\det Z = +1$). Stepping across facet $s$ multiplies the cell frame on the right
by the orthochronous Lorentz isometry
\begin{equation}
k_s = F_{n_s} Z \in O^+(4,1; \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]),
\qquad
\det(k_s) = \det(F_{n_s})\,\det(Z) = -1 \quad (s = 0, \ldots, 23).
\label{eq:transport}
\end{equation}
\end{itemize}

Every single hop has determinant $-1$, so only even-length paths preserve orientation and the honeycomb is bipartite.
Starting from the origin dock with frame $I_5$, a dock with frame $g$ sits at center $g c_0$, carries orientation
parity $\sigma(d) = \det(g) \in \{+1, -1\}$, and reaches its neighbor through facet $s$ at frame $g k_s$ (verified on
all $481/481$ docks of $B_2$ with zero conflicts in \universecode). Because $k_s$ has flat holonomy, an excitation that
leaves through doorway $s$ arrives in the same slot index $s$ of the next dock. The stream
$S(d, s) = (\mathrm{step}(d, s), s)$ is therefore a bijection, with inverse $S^{-1}(d, s) = (\mathrm{step}(d, \bar{s}), s)$
and a strict speed limit of one hop per beat.

Fix one cusp. The space is foliated by concentric flat 3D \emph{horospheres}, the
level surfaces of the Busemann function toward that cusp, indexed by integer depth $D \in \{0, 1, 2, \ldots\}$
(Figure~\ref{fig:cusp-geometry}). The outermost floor $D = 0$ is the \emph{husk}. Behind each husk dock stands a
\emph{beam} of bulk docks at depths $D = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ that widens roughly eighteenfold per floor. Three-dimensional
observables are read on the husk. Section~\ref{sec:cp-balance} shows that the beam behind it cannot be dropped.

\subsection{The amplitude ring \texorpdfstring{$\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_9][1/6]$}{Z[zeta\_9][1/6]}}

A basis configuration assigns each occupied slot
$(d, s)$ a tone $\tau \in \{-1, +1\}$ and an internal \emph{role} label $j \in \{0, \ldots, 8\} \cong \mathbb{Z}_3^2$,
and leaves every other slot at $\tau = 0$. The set of such finite maps is $\Omega_{\mathrm{cfg}}$. A state is a finite
superposition $|\psi(t)\rangle = \sum_{c \in \Omega_{\mathrm{cfg}}} \alpha_c(t) |c\rangle$, and the empty map is the
neutral state $|V_1\rangle$.

The amplitudes $\alpha_c$ lie in an exact ring built from the integers. Start with $\mathbb{Z}$ and
adjoin one symbol $\zeta_9$ obeying
\begin{equation}
\zeta_9^6 + \zeta_9^3 + 1 = 0.
\label{eq:z9-poly}
\end{equation}
Because $\zeta_9^6 = -\zeta_9^3 - 1$, every element of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_9]$ is uniquely an exact 6-integer tuple
$(a_0,\allowbreak a_1,\allowbreak a_2,\allowbreak a_3,\allowbreak a_4,\allowbreak a_5)$ standing for
$a_0 + a_1 \zeta_9 + \cdots + a_5 \zeta_9^5$.

This integer ring already contains the cube-root generator $\omega = \zeta_9^3$ ($\omega^2 + \omega + 1 = 0$), the
sixth-root generator $\zeta_6 = 1 + \omega$ ($\zeta_6^6 = 1$), and the eighteenth-root generator
$\zeta_{18} = -\zeta_9^5$ ($\zeta_{18}^9 = -1$), together with exact integer conjugation
$\overline{\zeta_9^k} = \zeta_9^{-k}$. Adjoining $[1/6]$ allows exact denominators dividing powers of $2$ and $3$,
tracked as one positive \code{BigInt} denominator $d_{\mathrm{den}} = 2^a 3^b$. Neither $e$ nor $\pi$ enters the
discrete rule or the Lean~4 proofs at any step (geometrically, embedding $\zeta_9 \mapsto e^{2\pi i/9}$ visualizes
$\zeta_9$ as a $40^\circ$ unit-circle turn and $\zeta_{18} \mapsto e^{i\pi/9}$ as a $20^\circ$ turn,
Figure~\ref{fig:cyclotomic-circle}).

$\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_9][1/6]$ also closes the 648-element Tier~B single-qutrit Clifford group $\Sigma(648) \subset SU(3)$
(the 3-fold central extension of the order-216 projective Hessian group, \code{E-QTM-0117}).

Negative controls also decide how many excitations a dock must hold before each multi-body layer acts. Mixing a lone
excitation across lines drains bound line states (\code{E-SLF-0189}, \code{E-SLF-0198}), and parity-dependent neighbor
locks leak when bath excitations arrive (\code{E-SLF-0190}, \code{E-SLF-0195}). Restricting $M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\zeta_{18})$ to
Scale~2 solely on identical-tone, identical-role full-line pairs leaves $|V_1\rangle$ and all 24 single-excitation
slots invariant, scatters pairs isotropically across all 12 lines, and preserves exact $W(F_4)^+$ proper-rotation
covariance (verified across a 4-generator proper orthogonal set closing the full order-576 group $W(F_4)^+$ over 7
representative states with covariance defect $0$ in \universecode). Finally, exhaustive orbit enumeration under
$W(F_4) \times \mathbb{Z}_2^{\mathcal{C}}$ establishes $1, 8, 29$ orbits at occupations $k = 1, 2, 3$ with zero chiral
orbits, so $\Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ is strictly silent on 0-, 1-, 2-, and 3-excitation docks and turns on at Scale~4
($k^* = 4$, \universecode, \code{E-FND-0181}).

\subsection{The palindromic beat}

One beat updates the whole state by one palindromic eight-layer unitary built from six local operators. Seven layers act inside each dock, sorted by how many slots are occupied (the
\emph{scale} $k$, Figure~\ref{fig:dock-24cell}), and the eighth carries every excitation through its doorway. Writing
$\Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ for the chiral 4-body phase gate (named \code{ODD} in code):
\begin{equation}
|\psi(t+1)\rangle = U |\psi(t)\rangle,
\qquad
U = S \circ \Phi_{\mathrm{odd}} \circ C_{1/2} \circ M_{\mathrm{meet}} \circ M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\zeta_{18}) \circ K \circ C_{1/2} \circ \Phi_{\mathrm{odd}},
\label{eq:beat}
\end{equation}
with exact memoryless inverse obtained by reversing the factor order and conjugating each local gate:
\begin{equation}
U^\dagger = \Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}^\dagger \circ C_{1/2}^\dagger \circ K \circ M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\overline{\zeta}_{18})
\circ M_{\mathrm{meet}}^\dagger \circ C_{1/2}^\dagger \circ \Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}^\dagger \circ S^{-1}.
\label{eq:beat-dag}
\end{equation}

The palindromic order yields exact motion reversal. Two properties of the local
block $L = \Phi_{\mathrm{odd}} \circ C_{1/2} \circ (M_{\mathrm{meet}} \circ M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\zeta_{18}) \circ K)
\circ C_{1/2} \circ \Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ explain the ordering (Figure~\ref{fig:beat-circuit}):
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{The Scale-2 center commutes:} $M_{\mathrm{meet}}$ and $K$ act on disjoint sectors (like-sign
versus opposite-sign full lines), while $M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\zeta_{18})$ moves only a sole identical-tone, identical-role
pair between lines that each receive the identical scalar phase $\omega$ from $M_{\mathrm{meet}}$. Thus
$M_{\mathrm{meet}}$, $M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\zeta_{18})$, and $K$ commute pairwise on the full Fock space.
\item \textbf{Palindromic motion reversal:} Let $\mathcal{T} = \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{conj}} \circ S \circ R$, where
$R$ flips every slot $s \mapsto \bar{s}$ on each dock and $\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{conj}}$ conjugates amplitudes
($\mathcal{T}^2 = I$ and $S R S = R$). Every local layer individually obeys
$\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{conj}} R L_a R \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{conj}} = L_a^\dagger$. Because
$[\Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}, C_{1/2}] \neq 0$, mirroring both $\Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ and $C_{1/2}$ around the commuting
Scale-2 center forces $\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{conj}} R L R \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{conj}} = L^\dagger$ and guarantees exact
global symmetries:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{T} U \mathcal{T}^{-1} = U^\dagger,
\qquad
\mathcal{CP} U (\mathcal{CP})^{-1} = U,
\qquad
(\mathcal{CPT}) U (\mathcal{CPT})^{-1} = U^\dagger.
\label{eq:exact-symmetries}
\end{equation}
\end{itemize}

The half-coin $C_{1/2}$, the full coin $C = C_{1/2}^2$, the like-sign
meeting matrix for distinct roles $M_{\mathrm{meet}}^{\mathrm{diff}}$, and the Scale-2 pair-line mixer
$M_{\mathrm{pair}}(u)$ are
\begin{equation}
C_{1/2} = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 2 + \omega & -\omega \\ -\omega & 2 + \omega \end{pmatrix},
\quad
C = C_{1/2}^2 = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 + \omega & 1 - \omega \\ 1 - \omega & 1 + \omega \end{pmatrix},
\quad
M_{\mathrm{meet}}^{\mathrm{diff}} = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 + \omega & \omega - 1 \\ \omega - 1 & 1 + \omega \end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:coin}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{pair}}(u) = I_{12} + \frac{u - 1}{12} J_{12} \qquad (u = \zeta_{18} = -\zeta_9^5),
\label{eq:mpair}
\end{equation}
where $J_{12}$ is the $12 \times 12$ all-ones matrix. Three clauses complete the definitions:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Full-line action of $C_{1/2}$:} On any lone occupied slot of a line, $C_{1/2}$ applies the $2 \times 2$
matrix of Equation~\eqref{eq:coin} across $(+\mathbf{r}, -\mathbf{r})$ leaving its role $j$ unchanged. On any doubly
occupied (full) line, whether like-sign or opposite-sign, $C_{1/2}$ multiplies the amplitude by the determinant phase
$\det C_{1/2} = \zeta_6 = 1 + \omega$ and leaves slot occupations and roles untouched (a full-line rule
preserving strict single occupancy per slot).
\item \textbf{Multi-line and role action of $K$:} On every opposite-sign full line ($\tau_a \neq \tau_b$), $K$ swaps
the two opposite slots carrying each excitation's role label $j$ together with its tone $\tau$, and multiplies the
configuration amplitude by $(-1)^{m_{\mathrm{opp}}}$, where $m_{\mathrm{opp}}$ is the total count of opposite-sign
full lines across all docks ($K^\dagger = K$).
\item \textbf{Canonical $W(F_4) \times \mathbb{Z}_2^{\mathcal{C}}$ chiral invariant $\chi_{\mathrm{odd}}$:} At any
dock $d$ holding $k \ge 4$ excitations $\{(s_i, \tau_i)\}_{i=1}^k$, encode each excitation as
$c_i = 2 s_i + \mathbf{1}_{\tau_i < 0} \in \{0, \ldots, 47\}$. For each unordered 4-tuple
$q = \{c_{i_1}, c_{i_2}, c_{i_3}, c_{i_4}\}$, the order-$2{,}304$ symmetry group $G = W(F_4) \times \mathbb{Z}_2^{\mathcal{C}}$
acts by $(g, \varepsilon) \cdot (s, \tau) = (g(s), \varepsilon \tau)$ with chiral sign
$\eta(g, \varepsilon) = \varepsilon \det(g) \in \{+1, -1\}$. Let $q_{\min}$ be the lexicographically smallest sorted
4-tuple in the orbit $G \cdot q$. Define $\epsilon_{\mathrm{ch}}(q) \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$ to equal the unique value of
$\eta(g, \varepsilon)$ across all $(g, \varepsilon) \in G$ mapping $q \mapsto q_{\min}$ if all such elements share one
sign (chiral orbit), and $0$ if both signs occur (achiral orbit). Then
\begin{equation}
\chi_{\mathrm{odd}}(d) = \sum_{1 \le i_1 < i_2 < i_3 < i_4 \le k}
\epsilon_{\mathrm{ch}}\bigl(\{c_{i_1}, c_{i_2}, c_{i_3}, c_{i_4}\}\bigr),
\label{eq:chi-odd}
\end{equation}
and $\Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ multiplies the configuration amplitude by $\zeta_6^{\sum_d \sigma(d)\,\chi_{\mathrm{odd}}(d)}$
using the canonical orientation parity $\sigma(d) = \det(\mathrm{frame}(d))$. Because $\epsilon_{\mathrm{ch}}$ flips
sign under $\mathcal{C}$ ($\varepsilon = -1$) and under mirror reflection $\mathcal{P}$ ($\det g = -1$) while staying
invariant under $\mathcal{CP}$ ($\varepsilon \det g = +1$) and $W(F_4)^+$ ($\det g = +1$), $\Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ acts
nontrivially on both mixed-tone and all-like-tone chiral quads (including all-$+1$ slots $[0, 1, 4, 16]$ and
$[1, 3, 4, 10]$) while remaining silent on all $k \le 3$ docks.
\end{itemize}

Three configurations show how the occupation scales separate. A lone excitation $(d, s, +1, j)$ skips Step~1 ($\Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}$, since $k = 1 < 4$), splits across $(s, \bar{s})$ in
Step~2 ($C_{1/2}$), skips Steps~3--5 ($K, M_{\mathrm{pair}}, M_{\mathrm{meet}}$), receives $C_{1/2}$ again in Step~6
so that the net local gate is the full coin $C = C_{1/2}^2$ (keep weight $1/4$, flip weight $3/4$), skips Step~7, and
streams in Step~8 to the two antipodal neighbor docks along line $\ell$. A lone excitation therefore stays on its
line. Section~\ref{sec:quantum} studies this motion.

When two excitations occupy opposite slots $(s, \bar{s})$ of a full line with identical tone and identical role,
Steps~1, 3, and 7 are silent, Steps~2, 5, and 6 impart only the exact scalar phase
$\zeta_6 \cdot \omega \cdot \zeta_6 = \omega^2 = -1 - \omega$ (commuting with Step~4), Step~4 scatters the pair
isotropically across all 12 lines via $M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\zeta_{18})$, and Step~8 streams all 12 pairs to all 24
neighbor docks. If the two like-sign excitations carry distinct roles, Step~5 mixes their roles via
$M_{\mathrm{meet}}^{\mathrm{diff}}$ while Step~4 stays silent. If they carry opposite tones ($+1$ and $-1$), Steps~1,
4, 5, and 7 stay silent, Step~3 ($K$) bounces them across $(s, \bar{s})$ carrying their roles with phase $-1$,
Steps~2 and 6 impart $\zeta_6^2 = \omega$, and Step~8 streams them apart. Step~4 is the only layer that moves
amplitude to another line, and Section~\ref{sec:relativity} builds 3D motion from it.

Finally, when four excitations occupy a chiral 4-tuple of slots on dock $d$, Step~1 applies the chiral sixth-root phase
$\zeta_6^{\sigma(d)\chi_{\mathrm{odd}}(d)}$ via Equation~\eqref{eq:chi-odd}, Steps~2--6 act on any singletons or full
lines present, Step~7 applies $\Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ again on the post-coin branches (completing exact motion-reversal
symmetry even when $C_{1/2}$ changes $\chi_{\mathrm{odd}}$), and Step~8 streams all four excitations outward.

def beat(psi):
    """Apply one exact palindromic beat in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_9][1/6]$ on $\{3,4,3,4\}$.
    State psi = (amp_map: Config -> $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_9]$, den: $2^a 3^b$), finite support outside $|V_1\rangle$."""
    # Step 1: Scale-4 chiral phase $\Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ via $W(F_4)\times\mathbb{Z}_2^{\mathcal{C}}$ invariant $\chi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ (Eq. $\eqref{eq:chi-odd}$)
    for dock d holding occupation count(d) >= 4:
        multiply amplitude by $\zeta_6^{\sigma(d)\,\chi_{\mathrm{odd}}(d)}$

    # Step 2: Half-coin $C_{1/2}$ on lone lines (phase $\det C_{1/2} = \zeta_6$ on all full lines)
    psi = split_lone_lines(psi, matrix=[[2 + $\omega$, -$\omega$], [-$\omega$, 2 + $\omega$]], den=2, full_phase=$\zeta_6$)

    # Step 3: Opposite-sign collision bounce $K$ ($K^\dagger = K$, commutes with $M_{\mathrm{pair}}, M_{\mathrm{meet}}$)
    for every full line holding $\tau_a \neq \tau_b$:
        swap opposite slots $(a, b)$ carrying roles $(j_a, j_b)$ alongside tones, negate amplitude

    # Step 4: Scale-2 $W(F_4)$-isotropic pair-line mixer $M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\zeta_{18})$, $\zeta_{18} = -\zeta_9^5$
    for dock d holding solely one identical-tone, identical-role full-line pair on line $\ell$:
        keep amplitude $\times (11 + \zeta_{18}) / 12$ on line $\ell$
        hop  amplitude $\times (\zeta_{18} - 1) / 12$ to every other line $\ell' \neq \ell$

    # Step 5: Scale-2 like-sign role meeting $M_{\mathrm{meet}}$ on full lines
    psi = meet_like_sign_pairs(psi, same_role_phase=$\omega$,
                               diff_role_matrix=[[1 + $\omega$, $\omega$ - 1], [$\omega$ - 1, 1 + $\omega$]], den=2)

    # Step 6: Second half-coin $C_{1/2}$ (completes Strang splitting, $C_{1/2}^2 = C$ on lone lines)
    psi = split_lone_lines(psi, matrix=[[2 + $\omega$, -$\omega$], [-$\omega$, 2 + $\omega$]], den=2, full_phase=$\zeta_6$)

    # Step 7: Second chiral phase $\Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ (completes palindromic $\mathcal{T}$ and $\mathcal{CPT}$ symmetry)
    for dock d holding occupation count(d) >= 4:
        multiply amplitude by $\zeta_6^{\sigma(d)\,\chi_{\mathrm{odd}}(d)}$

    # Step 8: On-the-fly $O^+(4,1;\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}])$ antipodal stream $S$ on $\{3,4,3,4\}$
    psi = stream(psi, map_slot=lambda ds: (step(ds[0], ds[1]), ds[1]))
    return reduce_common_factors(psi, primes=(2, 3))
