\subsection{Signed ternary mesh, docks, lines, and banks}

Each elementary local degree of freedom on the mesh is a single signed ternary value (\emph{tone}),
\begin{equation}
\tau \in \{-1, 0, +1\}.
\label{eq:trits}
\end{equation}
Three values form the smallest signed alphabet carrying both a neutral center ($0$) and an exact charge-conjugation
involution $\mathcal{C}$ (sign inversion $\tau \mapsto -\tau$, exchanging $+1 \leftrightarrow -1$, \code{E-FND-0038}).
For any finite region of slots, \emph{charge} is the net signed count $Q = N_+ - N_-$ and \emph{content} is the total
occupation $N = N_+ + N_-$.

Because the beat operator conserves $Q$ identically, any globally neutral state has $N_+ = N_-$. Distinguishing $+1$
from $-1$ locally within a globally neutral state therefore requires a $\mathcal{C}$-odd (reversing sign under
$+1 \leftrightarrow -1$), $\mathcal{CP}$-even (invariant under simultaneous sign inversion $\mathcal{C}$ and spatial
mirror reflection $\mathcal{P}$) multi-excitation observable while the global dynamics remains $\mathcal{CP}$-balanced
and respects motion reversal $\mathcal{T}$ and combined $\mathcal{CPT}$ symmetry.

Each dock has the local geometry of a regular four-dimensional 24-cell \cite{conway1999}. A 24-cell is self-dual, with
24 vertices, 96 edges, 96 equilateral triangular faces, and 24 regular octahedral facets matching the vertices
one-to-one. Those 24 doorways are indexed by the 24 roots of the 4D root system $D_4$ (the integer reflection lattice
obtained by choosing any 2 of 4 Cartesian axes with independent signs, $\binom{4}{2} \times 2^2 = 24$ vectors):
\begin{equation}
\Phi(D_4) = \bigl\{\,\text{all permutations of } (\pm 1, \pm 1, 0, 0) \in \mathbb{R}^4\,\bigr\},
\qquad |\Phi(D_4)| = 24.
\label{eq:d4-roots}
\end{equation}

Relative to any fixed slot $\mathbf{r} \in \Phi(D_4)$, the 24 slots partition into five exact inner-product
neighborhoods listed in Table~\ref{tab:d4-nbrs}. The full symmetry group of the 24-cell is the Weyl group $W(F_4)$ of
order $|W(F_4)| = 1{,}152$ (\code{E-GMT-0046}), with proper rotational subgroup $W(F_4)^+$ of order $576$. Summing
fourth powers of the 24 root vectors in Equation~\eqref{eq:d4-roots} satisfies exact rank-4 $SO(4)$ rotational isotropy:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \Phi(D_4)} r_a r_b r_c r_d
= 4\bigl(\delta_{ab}\delta_{cd} + \delta_{ac}\delta_{bd} + \delta_{ad}\delta_{bc}\bigr),
\label{eq:d4-isotropy}
\end{equation}
so $\sum r_0^4 = 12 = 3 \sum r_0^2 r_1^2$ with zero defect. Unlike a hypercubic $\mathbb{Z}^4$ grid, whose fourth
moment breaks rotational invariance at $O(a^2)$, the 24-cell cancels all $O(a^2)$ directional artifacts identically
(Equation~\eqref{eq:d4-isotropy}), the same property exploited in face-centered hypercubic lattice hydrodynamics
\cite{frisch1987fchc} and lattice gauge theory \cite{celmaster1982}.

\subsection{The 4D \texorpdfstring{$\{3,4,3,4\}$}{\{3,4,3,4\}} honeycomb and flat 3D cusp}

Docks are glued face-to-face across their 24 regular octahedral facets to build the unique regular hyperbolic 4D
honeycomb of 24-cells, denoted $\{3,4,3,4\}$ \cite{coxeter1956, coxeter1973, margenstern2007vol1}: equilateral
triangles $\{3\}$ meet four per vertex to form octahedra $\{3,4\}$, three octahedra meet around each edge to form
24-cells $\{3,4,3\}$, and four 24-cells meet around every triangular face so that the vertex figure around each ideal
point at infinity (a \emph{cusp}) is the flat 3D Euclidean cubic grid $\{4,3,4\}$.

To navigate the infinite honeycomb on the fly without lookup tables or floating-point rounding, we represent every
24-cell center and facet reflection using exact $5 \times 5$ matrices over $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]$. The geometry is
fixed completely by the doubled Coxeter Gram matrix $2G$ of $\{3,4,3,4\}$ (whose leading principal minors
$2, 3, 2, 1, -2$ verify Lorentzian signature $(4,1)$) and the timelike base center $c_0$:
\begin{equation}
2G = \begin{pmatrix}
2 & -1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
-1 & 2 & -\sqrt{2} & 0 & 0 \\
0 & -\sqrt{2} & 2 & -1 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & -1 & 2 & -\sqrt{2} \\
0 & 0 & 0 & -\sqrt{2} & 2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
c_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 4 \\ 3\sqrt{2} \\ 2\sqrt{2} \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}.
\label{eq:coxeter-gram}
\end{equation}
Three short algebraic rules turn Equation~\eqref{eq:coxeter-gram} into the exact neighbor map $\mathrm{step}(d, s)$:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Lorentzian distance:} The matrix $2G$ defines the bilinear form $2B(u, v) = u^T (2G) v$, normalized so
that $2B(c_0, c_0) = -2$. For any isometry $g$, hyperbolic distance from the origin is an exact integer invariant:
$-2B(g c_0, c_0) = 2\cosh d_{\mathbb{H}^4}(g c_0, c_0) \in \mathbb{Z}$.
\item \textbf{The 24 octahedral facets:} Reflecting across a spacelike facet normal $n$ ($2B(n, n) = 2$) acts by
$F_n(v) = v - (2B(n, v))\, n$ ($\det F_n = -1$). Acting on the fifth basis vector $e_4$ with the base stabilizer
$W(F_4) = \langle F_{e_0}, F_{e_1}, F_{e_2}, F_{e_3} \rangle$ produces all 24 facet normals $\{n_s\}_{s=0}^{23}$,
indexed bijectively by the $D_4$ roots via $(2B(n_a, n_b) + 2)/2 \in \{-2, -1, 0, 1, 2\}$.
\item \textbf{Single-hop cell transport:} Let $Z$ be the central inversion fixing $c_0$ while negating its 4D tangent
space ($Z(v) = -v - 2B(c_0, v)\, c_0$, $\det Z = +1$). Stepping across facet $s$ multiplies the cell frame on the right
by the orthochronous Lorentz isometry
\begin{equation}
k_s = F_{n_s} Z \in O^+(4,1; \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]),
\qquad
\det(k_s) = \det(F_{n_s})\,\det(Z) = -1 \quad (s = 0, \ldots, 23).
\label{eq:transport}
\end{equation}
\end{itemize}

Because every single-hop transport $k_s$ has determinant $-1$ in $O^+(4,1; \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}])$, only even-length
paths lie in $SO^+(4,1; \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}])$, and the honeycomb is canonically bipartite. Starting at the origin
dock $d_0$ with $\mathrm{frame}(d_0) = I_5$, any dock $d$ with frame $g$ sits at center $g c_0$, carries orientation
parity $\sigma(d) = \det(g) \in \{+1, -1\}$, and steps across facet $s$ to neighbor frame $g k_s$ (verified across all
$481/481$ docks in $B_2$ with zero conflicts in \universecode). Because $k_s$ carries flat holonomy, crossing facet $s$
enters $\mathrm{step}(d, s)$ in the same slot index $s$. Thus the stream $S(d, s) = (\mathrm{step}(d, s), s)$ is
strictly bijective with inverse $S^{-1}(d, s) = (\mathrm{step}(d, \bar{s}), s)$ and 1-hop/beat Lieb--Robinson speed.

Fixing one ideal cusp foliates the 4D hyperbolic space by concentric flat 3D Euclidean \emph{Busemann horospheres}
(level surfaces perpendicular to all parallel geodesics converging at that ideal point), indexed by integer depth
$D \in \{0, 1, 2, \ldots\}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:cusp-geometry}). The outermost horosphere $D = 0$ is the \emph{husk}.
Behind each 3D cubic husk dock stands an exponentially branching 4D \emph{beam} of bulk docks at depths
$D = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$.

\subsection{Palindromic eight-layer reversible beat rule}

One discrete beat updates the global amplitude vector $|\psi(t)\rangle$ via one palindromic eight-layer unitary
operator composed from six distinct local operators (writing $\Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ for the chiral 4-body phase gate
named \code{ODD} in code):
\begin{equation}
|\psi(t+1)\rangle = U |\psi(t)\rangle,
\qquad
U = S \circ \Phi_{\mathrm{odd}} \circ C_{1/2} \circ M_{\mathrm{meet}} \circ M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\zeta_{18}) \circ K \circ C_{1/2} \circ \Phi_{\mathrm{odd}},
\label{eq:beat}
\end{equation}
with exact memoryless inverse obtained by reversing the factor order and conjugating each local gate:
\begin{equation}
U^\dagger = \Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}^\dagger \circ C_{1/2}^\dagger \circ K \circ M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\overline{\zeta}_{18})
\circ M_{\mathrm{meet}}^\dagger \circ C_{1/2}^\dagger \circ \Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}^\dagger \circ S^{-1}.
\label{eq:beat-dag}
\end{equation}

Two algebraic properties explain why the local block $L = \Phi_{\mathrm{odd}} \circ C_{1/2} \circ (M_{\mathrm{meet}}
\circ M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\zeta_{18}) \circ K) \circ C_{1/2} \circ \Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ is ordered palindromically
(Figure~\ref{fig:beat-circuit}):
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Pairwise commutation of the Scale-2 core:} $M_{\mathrm{meet}}$ and $K$ act on disjoint sectors (like-sign
versus opposite-sign full lines), while $M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\zeta_{18})$ moves only a sole identical-tone, identical-role
pair between lines that each receive the identical scalar phase $\omega$ from $M_{\mathrm{meet}}$. Thus
$M_{\mathrm{meet}}$, $M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\zeta_{18})$, and $K$ commute pairwise on the full Fock space.
\item \textbf{Palindromic motion reversal:} Let $\mathcal{T} = \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{conj}} \circ S \circ R$, where
$R$ flips every slot $s \mapsto \bar{s}$ on each dock and $\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{conj}}$ conjugates amplitudes
($\mathcal{T}^2 = I$ and $S R S = R$). Every local layer individually obeys
$\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{conj}} R L_a R \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{conj}} = L_a^\dagger$. Because
$[\Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}, C_{1/2}] \neq 0$, mirroring both $\Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ and $C_{1/2}$ around the commuting
Scale-2 core forces $\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{conj}} R L R \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{conj}} = L^\dagger$ and guarantees exact
global symmetries:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{T} U \mathcal{T}^{-1} = U^\dagger,
\qquad
\mathcal{CP} U (\mathcal{CP})^{-1} = U,
\qquad
(\mathcal{CPT}) U (\mathcal{CPT})^{-1} = U^\dagger.
\label{eq:exact-symmetries}
\end{equation}
\end{itemize}

Explicit matrices for the half-coin $C_{1/2}$, full coin $C = C_{1/2}^2$, distinct-role like-sign meeting matrix
$M_{\mathrm{meet}}^{\mathrm{diff}}$, and Scale-2 pair-line mixer $M_{\mathrm{pair}}(u)$ are
\begin{equation}
C_{1/2} = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 2 + \omega & -\omega \\ -\omega & 2 + \omega \end{pmatrix},
\quad
C = C_{1/2}^2 = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 + \omega & 1 - \omega \\ 1 - \omega & 1 + \omega \end{pmatrix},
\quad
M_{\mathrm{meet}}^{\mathrm{diff}} = \frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix} 1 + \omega & \omega - 1 \\ \omega - 1 & 1 + \omega \end{pmatrix},
\label{eq:coin}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{pair}}(u) = I_{12} + \frac{u - 1}{12} J_{12} \qquad (u = \zeta_{18} = -\zeta_9^5),
\label{eq:mpair}
\end{equation}
where $J_{12}$ denotes the $12 \times 12$ all-ones matrix. To keep the specification completely self-contained, three
exact clauses complete the operator definitions:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Full-line action of $C_{1/2}$:} On any lone occupied slot of a line, $C_{1/2}$ applies the $2 \times 2$
matrix of Equation~\eqref{eq:coin} across $(+\mathbf{r}, -\mathbf{r})$ leaving its role $j$ unchanged. On any doubly
occupied (full) line, whether like-sign or opposite-sign, $C_{1/2}$ multiplies the amplitude by the determinant phase
$\det C_{1/2} = \zeta_6 = 1 + \omega$ and leaves slot occupations and roles untouched (a hard-core full-line rule
preserving strict single occupancy per slot).
\item \textbf{Multi-line and role action of $K$:} On every opposite-sign full line ($\tau_a \neq \tau_b$), $K$ swaps
the two opposite slots carrying each excitation's role label $j$ together with its tone $\tau$, and multiplies the
configuration amplitude by $(-1)^{m_{\mathrm{opp}}}$, where $m_{\mathrm{opp}}$ is the total count of opposite-sign
full lines across all docks ($K^\dagger = K$).
\item \textbf{Canonical $W(F_4) \times \mathbb{Z}_2^{\mathcal{C}}$ chiral invariant $\chi_{\mathrm{odd}}$:} At any
dock $d$ holding $k \ge 4$ excitations $\{(s_i, \tau_i)\}_{i=1}^k$, encode each excitation as
$c_i = 2 s_i + \mathbf{1}_{\tau_i < 0} \in \{0, \ldots, 47\}$. For each unordered 4-tuple
$q = \{c_{i_1}, c_{i_2}, c_{i_3}, c_{i_4}\}$, the order-$2{,}304$ symmetry group $G = W(F_4) \times \mathbb{Z}_2^{\mathcal{C}}$
acts by $(g, \varepsilon) \cdot (s, \tau) = (g(s), \varepsilon \tau)$ with chiral sign
$\eta(g, \varepsilon) = \varepsilon \det(g) \in \{+1, -1\}$. Let $q_{\min}$ be the lexicographically smallest sorted
4-tuple in the orbit $G \cdot q$. Define $\epsilon_{\mathrm{ch}}(q) \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$ to equal the unique value of
$\eta(g, \varepsilon)$ across all $(g, \varepsilon) \in G$ mapping $q \mapsto q_{\min}$ if all such elements share one
sign (chiral orbit), and $0$ if both signs occur (achiral orbit). Then
\begin{equation}
\chi_{\mathrm{odd}}(d) = \sum_{1 \le i_1 < i_2 < i_3 < i_4 \le k}
\epsilon_{\mathrm{ch}}\bigl(\{c_{i_1}, c_{i_2}, c_{i_3}, c_{i_4}\}\bigr),
\label{eq:chi-odd}
\end{equation}
and $\Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ multiplies the configuration amplitude by $\zeta_6^{\sum_d \sigma(d)\,\chi_{\mathrm{odd}}(d)}$
using the canonical orientation parity $\sigma(d) = \det(\mathrm{frame}(d))$. Because $\epsilon_{\mathrm{ch}}$ flips
sign under $\mathcal{C}$ ($\varepsilon = -1$) and under mirror reflection $\mathcal{P}$ ($\det g = -1$) while staying
invariant under $\mathcal{CP}$ ($\varepsilon \det g = +1$) and $W(F_4)^+$ ($\det g = +1$), $\Phi_{\mathrm{odd}}$ acts
nontrivially on both mixed-tone and all-like-tone chiral quads (including all-$+1$ slots $[0, 1, 4, 16]$ and
$[1, 3, 4, 10]$) while remaining silent on all $k \le 3$ docks.
\end{itemize}
