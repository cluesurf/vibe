\section{Relativistic Kinematics and Speed Lock}
\label{sec:relativity}

Without cross-line coupling, every dock operator acts solely within one of the 12 antipodal $D_4$ lines, trapping
excitations on one-dimensional rails (the bare \emph{line law}) and breaking 3D rotational isotropy completely. How
can the automaton break rail confinement without destroying localized bound structures, recover isotropic relativistic
kinematics in three dimensions, and keep gravity, light, and chiral matter locked to one universal limiting speed
within the binary neutron-star merger observation GW170817 \cite{abbott2017gw170817}, which constrained the speed
difference between gravitational waves and light to $|\Delta c / c| < 6 \times 10^{-15}$?

\paragraph{Key findings.}
Restricting cross-line scattering to Scale~2 via $M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\zeta_{18})$ eliminates single-excitation drain
while breaking 1D rail confinement and recovering $O(k^{2.00})$ 3D isotropy plus boosted Dirac time dilation and length
contraction at bare speed $c_{\mathrm{eff}} = 0.021267$ (\code{E-RLT-0123}, \code{E-RLT-0124}). At Tier~B, shared-column
minimal coupling locks all massless species to a single speed $c_u = 0.977925$ to $3.37 \times 10^{-16}$
(\code{E-RLT-0125}), leaving a $46.0\times$ bare-versus-column speed gap explicitly open.

\subsection{Shared-column speed lock and the bare speed gap}

Relativistic kinematics in one sector is observationally ruled out if gravity, light, and chiral matter propagate at
different limiting speeds. Experiment \code{E-RLT-0125} audits species universality as a strict \textbf{Tier~B
shared-column kinematic minimal-coupling identity} rather than a bare Tier~A derivation.

On the $\{3,4,3,4\}$ cusp column equipped with the Tier~B pair-density warp $w(D) = 1/N_D^2$ (Section~\ref{sec:gravity}),
each floor $D = 0..F$ carries vertical inertia $M_D = N_D w(D) = 1/N_D$ and lateral stiffness $K_D = l_D w(D)$. Using
the canonical unaliased single-face lateral links $l_D = 1, 4, 20, 100$ of Table~\ref{tab:shells} gives
$K_{\mathrm{eff}} = 1.012532$ and $M_{\mathrm{eff}} = 1.058761$. In the decoupled negative control, unwarped husk light
and Weyl fermions travel at $c_{\mathrm{raw}} = 1.0$ while warped gravity travels at $c_{\mathrm{grav}} = 0.977925$,
creating a $2.21\%$ mismatch that exceeds GW170817 by over 12 orders of magnitude.

When spin-2 transverse-traceless (TT) gravitons (the two physical spin-2 gravitational-wave polarizations, $6(F+1)$
Voigt operator), spin-1 curl-curl Maxwell photons ($3(F+1)$), and spin-$1/2$ chiral Weyl fermions ($2(F+1)$) are
minimally coupled to the same warped column, the unique vertical zero mode $u_0$ obeys $L_{\mathrm{vert}} u_0 = 0$ and
cancels every polarization norm and momentum factor identically across all three species:
\begin{equation}
c_{\mathrm{grav}} = c_{\mathrm{light}} = c_{\mathrm{matter}}
= \sqrt{\frac{K_{\mathrm{eff}}}{M_{\mathrm{eff}}}}
= \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{D=0}^{F} l_D w(D)}{\sum_{D=0}^{F} N_D w(D)}}
= 0.977925 < 1.
\label{eq:speed-lock}
\end{equation}

Two quantitative disclosures qualify Equation~\eqref{eq:speed-lock}. First, canonical single-face links
$l_D = 1, 4, 20, 100$ (cumulative $5^D$) give $c_u = 0.977925$, whereas the aliased \code{period = 2} torus incremental
sequence $1, 10, 40, 200$ (cumulative $2 \cdot 5^D$) gives comparison control $c_u^{(p=2)} = 0.986916$. Both obey the
identical Rayleigh-quotient cancellation (matching stiffness-to-inertia ratios fixing wave speed), remain strictly
subluminal ($c_u < 1$, proven in Lean~4 for all $F \ge 2$ in Section~\ref{sec:unified}), and separate the unique
vertical zero mode from a gapped vertical Kaluza--Klein (KK) tower (massive bulk excitations gapped above the 3D mode,
noting that linearized KK gapping does not by itself exclude nonlinear Ho\v{r}ava--Lifshitz scalar modes
\cite{horava2009}).

Second, the bare Tier~A floor-0 Dirac speed $c_{\mathrm{eff}} = 0.021267$ (\code{E-RLT-0124}) sits $46.0\times$ below
the canonical Tier~B warped-column speed $c_u = 0.977925$ ($46.4\times$ below the \code{period = 2} control). Bridging
