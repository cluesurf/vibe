\section{Chirality, Confinement, and Gauge EFT Checks}
\label{sec:gauge}

Sections~\ref{sec:relativity} and~\ref{sec:gravity} gave the husk motion, one speed, and gravity. What it lacks is
matter with the structure of the Standard Model. A lattice model of particle physics meets three obstacles. Lattice fermions come in pairs of
opposite handedness, as the Nielsen--Ninomiya theorem requires \cite{nielsen1981}. Quarks must be confined at zero
temperature, with exact color triality and Gauss's law. And matter comes in three chiral generations with hierarchical
masses and $\mathcal{CP}$-violating mixing. This section tests one structure of the honeycomb against each obstacle:
the one-sided husk against the first, the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ center of the amplitude ring against the second, and the
three banks of every dock against the third.

All three tests use simplified Tier~B models: 1D depth chains and a lattice ladder (\code{E-SPN-0221},
\code{E-SPN-0222}, \code{E-FRC-0322}, \code{E-FRC-0323}). A unitary domain wall with coins in $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_9][1/6]$
separates left and right modes by 29 orders of magnitude while keeping net chirality zero, as Nielsen--Ninomiya
demands. A cold $\mathbb{Z}_3$ ladder confines linearly with exact Gauss's law. The section then steps out to Tier~C
grand-unified EFT checks (\coderange{E-FRC-0324}{0332}), which are not derived from the bare automaton. Dynamical gauge
bosons from the bare rule stay open.

\subsection{Domain-wall chirality}

A periodic cubic lattice has no preferred side. The cusp does: every husk dock connects to 18 bulk neighbors below it
and to none above. Following Kaplan and Shamir \cite{kaplan1992, shamir1993}, a boundary of this kind can trap a
massless mode of one handedness. \code{E-SPN-0221} (Tier~B, L1) first tests a Hermitian domain wall on a flat 1D depth-24 chain ($m_0 = 0.5$), trapping a
Left-handed mode at $D \le 2$ (weight $63/64 = 0.9844$ versus Right $2.09 \times 10^{-13}$), verifying the 1D dispersion
at $\mathbf{k} = (0.1, 0, 0)$ ($5.61 \times 10^{-15}$) and the $W = 2$ doubler class (gap $0.5200$). The full 3D cone
and all seven Brillouin-zone doublers are audited in \code{E-SPN-0222}.

The Hermitian wall is then replaced by a unitary one. To place a discrete-time split-step quantum walk
\cite{asboth2013} in the ring of the automaton ($C$, $C_{1/2}$ with $C_{1/2}^2 = C$, and $\zeta_{18} = -\zeta_9^5$), \code{E-SPN-0222} (Tier~B, L2) builds
a flat 1D depth-24 momentum-parameterized walk (a Tier~B domain-wall analog, not the bare $\{3,4,3,4\}$ QCA) with
Wilson-projector angles $\alpha_1(\mathbf{k}) = \pi/3 - (2\pi/9) P(\mathbf{k})$ and
$\alpha_2(\mathbf{k}) = (2\pi/3) P(\mathbf{k})$, where $P(\mathbf{k}) = \prod_{j=1}^3 (1 + \cos k_j)/2$:
\begin{equation}
U(\mathbf{k}) = \bigl(U_1(\mathbf{k})\, U_2(\mathbf{k})\, U_1(\mathbf{k}) \otimes I_2\bigr)\, S_{\mathrm{husk}}(\mathbf{k}).
\label{eq:unitary-wall}
\end{equation}
Here $U_1(\mathbf{k})$ applies the intra-layer rotation $\widetilde{C}_1(\mathbf{k}) = R_x(2\alpha_1(\mathbf{k}))$ on
$(|D, L\rangle, |D, R\rangle)$ for each $D \in \{0, \dots, 23\}$, $U_2(\mathbf{k})$ applies the inter-layer rotation
$\widetilde{C}_2(\mathbf{k}) = R_x(2\alpha_2(\mathbf{k}))$ on $(|D, R\rangle, |D+1, L\rangle)$ for $D \in \{0, \dots, 22\}$ (leaving
$|0, L\rangle$ and $|23, R\rangle$ unpaired), and $S_{\mathrm{husk}}(\mathbf{k}) = \operatorname{diag}(V_W(\mathbf{k}),
V_W(\mathbf{k})^\dagger)$ is a hand-inserted 3D Strang-split Pauli shift
$V_W(\mathbf{k}) = e^{-i k_x \sigma_x/2} e^{-i k_y \sigma_y/2} e^{-i k_z \sigma_z} e^{-i k_y \sigma_y/2} e^{-i k_x \sigma_x/2}$
acting on the two-component Weyl spinor. At the 8 high-symmetry TRIM points $\mathbf{k} \in \{0, \pi\}^3$, $V_W(\mathbf{k})
= \pm I_2$ and the depth coins reduce strictly into $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_9][1/6]$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{k} = \mathbf{0}:&\quad \widetilde{C}_1 = R_x(2\pi/9), \quad \widetilde{C}_2 = R_x(4\pi/3) = \omega^2 C^\dagger,\\
\mathbf{k} \in \{0,\pi\}^3 \setminus \{\mathbf{0}\}:&\quad \widetilde{C}_1 = R_x(2\pi/3) = (-\omega) C, \quad \widetilde{C}_2 = I.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:wall-coins}
\end{equation}
At generic $\mathbf{k}$ away from the 8 TRIM points, $\cos\alpha_1(\mathbf{k})$ is transcendental and the real-space 3D
hopping is exponentially localized rather than strictly finite-range. Bulk topological winding $(\nu_0, \nu_\pi) = (1, 0)$
traps a Left-handed zero mode near $D = 0$ (weight $0.999914$ on $D \le 2$), while all seven 3D doublers remain gapped
at $\pi/3$. In strict compliance with Nielsen--Ninomiya \cite{nielsen1981}, the finite $N = 24$ slab carries an exact
Right-handed mirror localized at weight $0.999914$ on the opposite wall window $D \ge 21$ (weight $3.56 \times 10^{-29}$ on
$D \le 2$), so net slab chirality is identically $0$. Decoupling the mirror requires the non-compact $N \to \infty$
limit, and dynamical chiral gauging without mirror recoupling remains open (Figure~\ref{fig:domain-wall}).

\subsection{Three-bank multiplicity}

Each dock has three banks (Section~\ref{sec:one-rule}), and one wall per bank gives three copies of the chiral mode.
Experiment \code{E-FRC-0323} (Tier~B) forms the block-diagonal product $I_3 \otimes U(\mathbf{k})$ across the three
mutually orthogonal 16-cell banks, cycled by the inner coordinate 3-cycle $T_3 \in S_4 < W(D_4)$ via
$S_4 / V_4 \cong S_3$ (distinct from outer $D_4$ triality $\mathrm{Out}(D_4) \cong W(F_4)/W(D_4)$) and commuting with
the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ center $\omega = \zeta_9^3$ of $\Sigma(648)$. Total winding is $\nu_{\mathrm{total}} = 3$ with no
fourth mode. The count of three comes from the three banks put in as input. Nothing in the dynamics selects against a
fourth generation. Part~2 is a qualitative toy hierarchy using hand-assigned
shell anchors $[3, 2, 0]$, UV coin steps $m_{\mathrm{UV}} = 2$ (for $Q_L, d_R$) and $1$ (for $u_R$), off-diagonal coupling
$1/\sqrt{2}$, $w(D) = 1/N_D^2$, and discrete $\zeta_9$ phase matrices. It yields a unitary Cabibbo--Kobayashi--Maskawa
(CKM) matrix and nonzero Jarlskog $\mathcal{CP}$ invariant \cite{jarlskog1985}, while missing Particle Data Group (PDG)
$m_b/m_d \approx 895$ by $31\times$ ($27{,}598$) and $J_{\mathrm{CP}} = 3.08 \times 10^{-5}$ by $3.1\times$
($+9.48 \times 10^{-5}$). Its $1.000$ degenerate control sets off-diagonal mixing $K_{\mathrm{mix}} = I_3$.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\mathbb{Z}_3$}{Z3} ladder confinement}

The ring's cube root of unity $\omega$ is the center of $\Sigma(648)$, and it supplies a $\mathbb{Z}_3$ gauge group,
the smallest with color triality. Classical link updates freeze at $T = 0$, because zero kinetic energy blocks
tunneling out of $U_p = 1$, so the test must be quantum. Experiment \code{E-FRC-0322} (Tier~B) evaluates the exact zero-temperature quantum Kogut--Susskind
Hamiltonian \cite{kogut1975} and discrete-time Floquet unitary ($\Delta t = 4\pi/9$ at $g^2 = 1$) for the $\mathbb{Z}_3$
center $\{1, \omega, \omega^2\}$ of $\Sigma(648)$ ($EU = \omega UE$) on a quasi-(1+1)D open 6-plaquette ladder (14
vertices, 19 links, gauge-invariant $\dim = 729$):
\begin{equation}
H = H_E + H_B = g^2 \sum_{\ell} \bigl(1 - \operatorname{Re} E_\ell\bigr) - \frac{1}{g^2} \sum_p \operatorname{Re} U_p,
\qquad
U_{\Delta t} = e^{-i\Delta t H_E} e^{-i\Delta t H_B}.
\label{eq:ks-z3}
\end{equation}
Because a 1-plaquette-wide ladder confines linearly for all $g^2 > 0$ by 1D flux conservation (unlike 3+1D $\mathbb{Z}_3$
gauge theory, which has a finite-coupling deconfinement transition), \code{E-FRC-0322} is a quasi-(1+1)D
exact-diagonalization benchmark. Across quark separations $r_{q\bar{q}} = 1, \dots, 5$, it verifies linear confinement,
exact $\mathbb{Z}_3$ baryon triality (three like charges bind while two like charges have empty physical Hilbert space
and triality-neutral $q \equiv 0 \bmod 3$ pairs have zero string tension), and exact $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_9][1/6]$ Floquet
tunneling (survival $1/729 = 0.00137$), with exact Gauss-law invariance holding by construction of the gauge-invariant
loop basis. Table~\ref{tab:gauge-b} collects all Tier~B chirality, generation, and confinement metrics.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{Spin}(10)$}{Spin(10)} EFT checks}

The Tier~C checks ask a narrower question: if a standard grand-unified theory were placed on this geometry, would its
parameters be compatible with the geometry's invariants? Nine
\textbf{Tier~C} continuum EFT scripts (\coderange{E-FRC-0324}{0332}) test whether standard
$\mathrm{Spin}(10)$ \cite{georgi1975, fritzsch1975} (often written $SO(10)$ in particle literature, denoted
$\mathrm{Spin}(10)$ throughout this paper) and Pati--Salam \cite{pati1974} grand unified theory (GUT), electroweak (EW),
Yukawa, and quantum chromodynamics (QCD) axion EFTs are compatible with geometric and algebraic invariants read off from
$\{3,4,3,4\}$ (the three 8-slot banks, the $F_4$ root ratio $\sqrt{2}$, shell ratio $b = 6{,}034/330$, and
$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_9)$ phases). They are \textbf{not} claimed as derivations of the Standard Model gauge group or
couplings from the bare automaton.

Algebraically, \code{E-FRC-0324} verifies textbook $\mathrm{Cl}(10)$ representation theory replicated across 3 copies
($3 \times 16 = 48$ Weyl states, not derived from 24-cell operators) in exact \code{BigInt}, checking $6/6$ perturbative
gauge and Witten anomaly cancellations, $\sin^2\theta_W|_{\mathrm{GUT}} = 3/8$, and an $S_3$ toy free energy whose
minimum selects staggered shells $(0, 2, 3)$ for tuned crowding parameters $g_{\mathrm{Pauli}} = 2.5$ and
$g_{\mathrm{cusp}} = 40$. Under the quadratic mass ansatz $M = M_0^2$ with $3 \times 3$ Hermitian circulant
$M_0(r, \delta) = v_0\bigl(I_3 + \frac{r}{2}(e^{i\delta} T_3 + e^{-i\delta} T_3^\dagger)\bigr)$ and any positive diagonal
warp $D_w = \operatorname{diag}(w_0, w_1, w_2)$, exact algebra (\code{E-FRC-0325}, \code{E-FRC-0327}) verifies Koide's
relation \cite{koide1983}, the warped-shell lower bound, and the cyclotomic phase obstruction:
\begin{equation}
Q_{\mathrm{Koide}}(M_0^2) = \frac{2 + r^2}{6} = \frac{2}{3} \iff r^2 = 2,
\qquad
Q_{\mathrm{Koide}}\bigl((D_w M_0 D_w)^2\bigr) \ge \frac{2}{3},
\qquad
e^{i 2/9} \notin \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_9][1/6].
\label{eq:koide-identities}
\end{equation}
Direct $\arg(\zeta_9) = 2\pi/9$ fails ($93\%$ error), $e^{i 2/9} \notin \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_9][1/6]$ follows by
Lindemann--Weierstrass, and \code{E-FRC-0327}'s standalone texture uses $M_{\mathrm{PS}} = 1.44 \times 10^{16}\,\mathrm{GeV}$,
in five-decade tension with \code{E-FRC-0328} (Tension~5 in Section~\ref{sec:open}).

Experiment \code{E-FRC-0326} audits standard electroweak symmetry breaking ($m_\gamma^2 = 0$, custodial
$\rho_{\mathrm{EW}} = 1$) and a 2-loop SM RG null test in which $0/10$ zero-parameter lattice candidates pass the
pre-registered CODATA null judge. In \code{E-FRC-0328}, two-loop Machacek--Vaughn renormalization-group equations (RGEs,
\cite{machacek1983}, at 2-loop gauge order without Yukawa feedback) perform an exact 3-for-3 parameter inversion (zero
degrees of overdetermination) of the three measured SM couplings into an intermediate Pati--Salam scale:
\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{PS}} = 1.434 \times 10^{11}\,\mathrm{GeV},
\qquad
M_{\mathrm{GUT}} = 7.946 \times 10^{15}\,\mathrm{GeV},
\qquad
\alpha_{\mathrm{GUT}}^{-1} = 45.1271.
\label{eq:ps-gut}
\end{equation}
Under canonical single-face $K_{\mathrm{eff}} = 1.012532$ (Section~\ref{sec:gravity}) and ball growth rate
$\lambda_{\mathrm{ball}} = 18.2787$ (Table~\ref{tab:shells}), running the bare lattice coupling
$\alpha_{\mathrm{lat}}^{-1} = 4\pi K_{\mathrm{eff}} \ln\lambda_{\mathrm{ball}} = 36.9722$ downward without threshold matching hits a
2-loop QCD Landau pole slightly above $M_Z$ in minimal Pati--Salam ($\alpha_3^{-1}(M_Z) = -1.38$), while remaining
perturbative in Georgi--Jarlskog PS ($\alpha^{-1}(0) = 129.52$, $-5.48\%$) and D-parity PS ($127.01$, $-7.32\%$).

To audit lattice-to-continuum matching and electroweak shell stabilization, \code{E-FRC-0329} combines two continuum
ans\"atze. First, with shell ratio $b = 6{,}034/330$, the Planck-to-electroweak hierarchy spans
$\log_b(M_{\mathrm{Pl}}/v_{\mathrm{EW}}) = 13.23$ shells ($M_{\mathrm{Pl}}/N_{13} = 484.3\,\mathrm{GeV} \approx
2 v_{\mathrm{EW}}$), which a continuum Goldberger--Wise scalar \cite{goldberger1999} can be tuned to stabilize near
$D_* = 13.15$ ($m_\phi^2 = 0.18$, $v_{\mathrm{UV}}/v_{\mathrm{IR}} = 9.7$). Second, a non-compact $\mu = \pi/a$
Gaussian-stiffness Brodsky--Lepage--Mackenzie (BLM) matching ansatz \cite{brodsky1983} (explicitly omitting the
opposite-sign $-17.91$ compact Wilson cosine tadpole) lifts bare $\alpha^{-1}(0) = 114.86$ ($-16.19\%$) to zero-fit
$128.73$ ($-6.06\%$ below CODATA $137.036$). Closing to CODATA requires an underdetermined $3 \times 4$ heavy
$\mathrm{Spin}(10)$ threshold fit (ratios in $[0.291, 1.346]$, with a fixed $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$-to-on-shell offset).

Minimal non-supersymmetric $10_H + \overline{126}_H$ Yukawa unification at $M_{\mathrm{GUT}} = 7.946 \times

$2.92\%$), matching known $10_H + \overline{126}_H + 120_H$ EFT compatibility \cite{dueck2013}. This is explicitly an
over-parameterized consistency fit using 14 continuous real parameters and 11 discrete $\mathbb{Z}_9$ phase exponents
($9^{11} \approx 3.1 \times 10^{10}$ basins) with synthetic 2\%--12\% weights over 18 SM observables, and $120_H$ is
omitted from Part~2's Higgs Hessian and 2-loop gauge RGEs.
