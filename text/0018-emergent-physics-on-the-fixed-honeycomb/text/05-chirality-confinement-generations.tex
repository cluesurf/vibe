\label{sec:gauge}

$\{3,4,3,4\}$ cusp while documenting their remaining open boundaries.

\paragraph{Why this matters in practice.}
Three of the strangest structural features of particle physics are that the weak force acts only on left-handed
particles, that quarks never appear alone even in a cold vacuum, and that nature repeats the same 16-particle family
exactly three times with widely separated masses. In this model, all three features trace back to one finite geometric
object, the 24-cell: its one-sided cusp boundary traps left-handed modes while burying their mirror twins deep in the
bulk, its $\mathbb{Z}_3$ phase center binds quarks into color-neutral baryons at zero temperature, and its three
orthogonal 8-slot banks pair with their antipodes to produce 48 anomaly-free Weyl fermions whose spontaneous crowding
separates their mass scales.
