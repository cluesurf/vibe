\section*{Introduction}

This paper studies one object: a deterministic, reversible quantum cellular automaton (QCA) that lives on a fixed,
infinite tiling of four-dimensional hyperbolic space by regular 24-cells, the honeycomb $\{3,4,3,4\}$
\cite{coxeter1956, coxeter1973, margenstern2007vol1}. It asks how much of quantum mechanics, relativistic kinematics,
gravity, gauge structure, and cosmology can be reached from that object, and it marks where each answer depends on the
bare rule alone and where it depends on structure added by hand.

The paper is a mathematical specification, a Lean~4.35.0 proof audit, and a deterministic computational baseline in
one \cite{cluesurf2026repo}. Every geometric object, ring, operator, symmetry, and observable is defined on first use,
so the text can be read and the rule re-implemented with no outside prerequisites.

\subsection*{Construction overview}

The construction has four stages. The first is a single regular 24-cell, which we call a \emph{dock}. It has 24 octahedral facets, one doorway for each of the 24 roots of the $D_4$ root system
\cite{conway1999}. Each doorway is a \emph{slot}, and each slot carries one signed ternary value
$\tau \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$: an excitation of either sign, or nothing. Opposite doorways pair into 12 straight
\emph{lines}, and the lines group into 3 \emph{banks} of 4 mutually perpendicular lines each.

Second, docks are glued face to face to fill the space. The result is the honeycomb $\{3,4,3,4\}$, a curved
four-dimensional space in which the number of docks within distance $r$ grows roughly eighteenfold with each step.
Third, one ideal point at infinity, a \emph{cusp}, is fixed. Relative to it, the honeycomb splits into a stack of flat
three-dimensional floors, each an
ordinary cubic grid $\{4,3,4\}$. The outermost floor is the \emph{husk}, where three-dimensional observables are read.
Behind each husk dock lies an exponentially widening \emph{beam} of docks at depths $D = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$, the
four-dimensional \emph{bulk}.

Fourth, time advances in discrete \emph{beats}. Each beat applies one palindromic eight-layer unitary operator built from six
local operators,
\begin{equation}
U = S \circ \Phi_{\mathrm{odd}} \circ C_{1/2} \circ M_{\mathrm{meet}} \circ M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\zeta_{18}) \circ K \circ C_{1/2} \circ \Phi_{\mathrm{odd}},
\label{eq:intro-beat}
\end{equation}
read right to left. The local layers act inside each dock according to how many of its slots are occupied, and the
final layer $S$ streams every excitation through its doorway into the neighboring dock. All matrix entries lie in the
cyclotomic ring $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_9][1/6]$. The rule has no continuous angle, no floating-point operation, no added
depth register, and no reflecting wall in the bulk. A self-contained reference script (\universecode) in the companion
repository \cite{cluesurf2026repo} implements the infinite honeycomb in exact integer arithmetic over
$O^+(4,1; \mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}])$ together with all eight layers of Equation~\eqref{eq:intro-beat}.

\subsection*{Related work}

The construction draws on four lines of research in mathematical physics and computer science. In quantum
information theory, Meyer \cite{meyer1996} formulated Dirac quantum lattice gases and proved the scalar 1D QCA no-go
theorem, Schumacher and Werner \cite{schumacher2004} axiomatized reversible quantum cellular automata and strict causal
propagation, Arrighi \cite{arrighi2019} and Farrelly \cite{farrelly2020} surveyed QCA dynamics, and
Bia\l ynicki-Birula \cite{bialynicki1994}, Strauch \cite{strauch2006}, and Asb\'oth and Obuse \cite{asboth2013} showed
how unitary discrete-time lattice walks reproduce Weyl, Dirac, Maxwell, and topological boundary-mode kinematics.
't~Hooft's cellular automaton interpretation \cite{thooft2016} restricts microscopic evolution to deterministic
permutations of a classical basis. The automaton here instead evolves complex superpositions with exact cyclotomic
amplitudes, and it does so on a curved 4D honeycomb in place of a flat lattice.

On the geometric side, Coxeter \cite{coxeter1956, coxeter1973} classified regular hyperbolic honeycombs including
$\{3,4,3,4\}$ in $\mathbb{H}^4$, Conway and Sloane \cite{conway1999} systematized the $D_4$ root lattice and 24-cell,
and Margenstern \cite{margenstern2007vol1} analyzed computation and cellular automata on curved tessellations. Modern
discrete holography uses negatively curved tilings to model bulk-to-boundary maps, notably in the holographic
error-correcting codes of Pastawski, Yoshida, Harlow, and Preskill \cite{pastawski2015happy}, while Randall and Sundrum
\cite{randall1999} showed how warped higher-dimensional geometry localizes gravity onto a lower-dimensional slice. Here
the flat $\{4,3,4\}$ ideal cusp horosphere of $\{3,4,3,4\}$ furnishes a canonical 3D Euclidean slice backed by an
exponentially branching 4D bulk beam.

In lattice field theory and statistical mechanics, the 24-vector 4D face-centered hypercubic ($D_4$) lattice (similar in
4D to the dual body-centered hypercubic lattice $D_4^*$ up to a $45^\circ$ rotation and $\sqrt{2}$ scaling) is known
to eliminate leading $O(a^2)$ rotational anisotropy, both in the lattice-gas hydrodynamics of Frisch et al.
\cite{frisch1987fchc} and in Celmaster's lattice gauge formulation \cite{celmaster1982}. Our non-perturbative mass-gap
audits draw directly on Wilson's lattice gauge action \cite{wilson1974}, the Kogut--Susskind Hamiltonian
\cite{kogut1975}, Osterwalder--Seiler reflection positivity \cite{osterwalder1978, seiler1982}, Koteck\'y--Preiss
cluster expansions \cite{kotecky1986}, Callan--Wilczek negative-curvature infrared gapping \cite{callan1990}, Tomboulis
character-expansion decimation \cite{tomboulis1983}, and Bakry--\'Emery Ricci curvature bounds \cite{bakry1985}, while
maintaining a sharp boundary between discrete lattice theorems and the continuum Clay Millennium Yang--Mills problem
\cite{jaffe2000ym}.

Finally, machine-checked proof engineering in Lean~4 \cite{demoura2021lean4} makes it practical to verify infinite-time
and infinite-depth algebraic laws directly inside a small trusted kernel rather than relying solely on finite-grid
simulation.

\subsection*{Verification tiers}

The later sections, on gravity, matter, and cosmology, depend increasingly on structure supplied by hand. Every claim
and every registered run therefore carries one of three tiers (Table~\ref{tab:tiers}, Figure~\ref{fig:tiers-map}). \textbf{Tier~A} is
what the bare automaton and the exact honeycomb do on their own. \textbf{Tier~B} places the same geometry under a
prescribed background, such as an imposed depth warp or a fixed drift, and asks what follows. \textbf{Tier~C} brings
in standard lattice gauge theory or continuum effective field theory (EFT) and tests whether it is compatible with the
invariants of $\{3,4,3,4\}$. Tier~C results are \textbf{not derived from the bare automaton}. The six open gaps between
the tiers are the subject of Section~\ref{sec:open}.

Every quantitative claim cites a deterministic experiment identifier in \code{test/registry.csv}
\cite{cluesurf2026repo} (1,790 registered runs). No script uses pseudo-randomness, and each pairs pre-registered
pass/fail gates with negative controls (line-confined, unwarped bulk, dephased classical, frozen $g^2 = 0$, and
sector-off ablations). The bare reference kernel (\universecode), exact Coxeter frame builders,
Lean-mirrored certificates (\code{E-FND-0185}), exact small-horizon QCA runs (\code{E-QTM-0203}, \code{E-QTM-0205},
\code{E-FND-0181}, \code{E-FND-0183}, \code{E-FND-0184}), and the infinite-honeycomb radial engine (\code{E-GRV-0163})
execute in arbitrary-precision \code{BigInt} cyclotomic arithmetic, while larger long-time, spectral, multi-qubit, and
continuum benchmark runs (\code{E-QTM-0202}, \code{E-QTM-0206}, \code{E-QTM-0207}, \code{E-HLG-0043},
\code{E-FND-0182}, \code{E-RLT-0125}, and downstream EFT runs) execute in IEEE-754 \code{Float64} with explicit
unitarity and norm-drift gates.

A second grade records how much of the honeycomb a run touches. \textbf{L1} is pure algebra or a finite-qubit
benchmark, \textbf{L2} is an exact sector or prescribed-background run on true $\{3,4,3,4\}$ balls, infinite Coxeter
traversals, or cusp beams, and \textbf{L3} is unfactored many-body dynamics across large 4D regions. Every run here is
L1 or L2. No L3 claim is made. The runs are paired with 94 headline Lean~4.35.0 theorems (113 public theorems including
19 exported helper lemmas) across six proof modules and the root aggregator \code{Vibe.lean}, with 0~\code{sorry},
0~\code{axiom}, and 0~\code{native\_decide}. Reproducing the Lean audit requires building the \code{.olean} artifacts
with \code{pnpm run check:lean}. Failed gates stay in the catalog and are reported beside the passing ones.

\subsection*{Outline}

Sections~\ref{sec:one-rule}--\ref{sec:cosmology} proceed from a single dock to the full honeycomb and then to the
physics tested on it.

\textbf{Section~\ref{sec:one-rule}} defines the geometry and the rule (Tier~A). It describes one dock, glues docks into
$\{3,4,3,4\}$, stacks the flat husk floors around a cusp (shells $N_D = 1, 18, 330, 6{,}034$, single-face lateral
links $l_D = 1, 4, 20, 100$), constructs the ring $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_9][1/6]$, and assembles the beat $U$. It closes
with two facts used throughout: the 3D husk cannot be evolved on its own because the 4D bulk behind it
matters within one beat, and the rule keeps exact $\mathcal{CP}$, $\mathcal{T}$, and $\mathcal{CPT}$ while breaking
$\mathcal{C}$ locally through 4-body interference.

\textbf{Section~\ref{sec:quantum}} follows a single excitation through the rule's two-slot coin and asks whether its
finite set of gates is enough for quantum mechanics (Tier~A and~B). It proves the algebraic hypotheses for density in
$SU(2)$, verifies the symmetry prerequisites of Zurek envariance under an operationally posited Born measure, runs
finite-register benchmarks of pointer decoherence and Bell violation, and reports an open-line Bell-correlation stall.

\textbf{Section~\ref{sec:relativity}} considers two excitations in one dock. Pair scattering frees motion from the 12 straight
lines, recovers 3D isotropy and relativistic dispersion ratios on the husk at bare speed $c_{\mathrm{eff}} = 0.021267$
(Tier~A), and, on a prescribed warped beam, locks gravity, light, and matter to one speed $c_u = 0.977925$ (Tier~B). The
$46\times$ gap between the two speeds is recorded.

\textbf{Section~\ref{sec:gravity}} treats the beam behind the husk. A prescribed depth warp $w(D) = 1/N_D^2$ stops the
bulk from swallowing a husk potential, restores $1/r$ gravity with $G_{\mathrm{eff}} = 0.078593$, and supports live
Newtonian orbits (Tier~B). An exact walk on the infinite honeycomb under a prescribed inward drift tips both light rays
inward past a horizon (Tier~B), and continuum general-relativity benchmarks are run beside it (Tier~C).

\textbf{Section~\ref{sec:gauge}} treats the husk as a one-sided boundary. A unitary domain wall with coins in the
automaton's ring separates left and right modes by 29 orders of magnitude, the three banks of a dock supply three
copies, and a $\mathbb{Z}_3$ ladder confines (Tier~B). Grand-unified EFT checks follow (Tier~C).

\textbf{Section~\ref{sec:cosmology}} follows an excitation moving deeper into the cusp. Because each deeper floor is
larger, steady inward drift reads on the husk as de Sitter expansion with no sites created (Tier~B). The bare rate sits
about 61 decades above the observed Hubble rate, and continuum cosmology checks are run beside it (Tier~C).

\textbf{Section~\ref{sec:unified}} collects results that hold for all times and depths: the Lean~4
proof suite, constructive mass-gap bounds on $\{3,4,3,4\}$ and flat $D_4$, and coupled runs in which several sectors
share one reversible state.

\textbf{Section~\ref{sec:open}} gathers the six open gaps, the five active cross-sector EFT tensions, and the relation
between discrete ultraviolet (UV) finiteness and the Clay Yang--Mills problem. \textbf{Appendix~\ref{sec:index}}
indexes all 64 cited experiments.
