\subsection{Fixed-mesh horosphere drift and reversible braking}

As a localized quantum disturbance moves radially into the fixed $\{3,4,3,4\}$ cusp bulk at mean Busemann depth
$\langle D(t) \rangle$, the 3D horosphere volume behind the wavefront grows exponentially as
$V(D) \sim r_{\mathrm{vol}}^D$ ($r_{\mathrm{vol}} \approx 18.285$). Defining the effective 3D scale factor $a(t)$,
ballistic radial drift at speed $v_D = d\langle D\rangle / dt$ yields a constant effective Hubble rate with zero
creation of new lattice sites:
\begin{equation}
a(t) = V\bigl(\langle D(t)\rangle\bigr)^{1/3} \propto r_{\mathrm{vol}}^{\langle D(t)\rangle / 3},
\qquad
H_\infty = \frac{\dot{a}}{a} = \frac{\ln r_{\mathrm{vol}}}{3}\, v_D.
\label{eq:horosphere-hubble}
\end{equation}

exact reversibility under $U^\dagger$, and convergence onto a de Sitter attractor (exponential expansion at constant
Hubble rate with $w = -1$), whereas a flat 3D cubic control ($b = 1$) gives zero expansion identically. Crucially, the
bare drift rate in lattice Planck units sits at \textbf{$H_\infty = 0.51713\, M_{\mathrm{Pl}}$}, which is \textbf{122
orders of magnitude above the observed late-time Hubble constant $H_0$}.
