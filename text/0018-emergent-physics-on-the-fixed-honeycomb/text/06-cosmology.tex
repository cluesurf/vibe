\section{Horosphere Cosmology and Three Eras}
\label{sec:cosmology}

Because the $\{3,4,3,4\}$ honeycomb is completely fixed and never adds new sites, cosmic expansion cannot arise from
lattice growth. Experiments \code{E-CSM-0064} and \code{E-CSM-0065} demonstrate an exact fixed-mesh mechanism: a finite
reversible quantum disturbance spreading radially into the exponentially widening horospheres of $\{3,4,3,4\}$
generates de Sitter expansion in the dilute limit and a complete radiation, matter, turnaround, and dark-energy cosmic
history when coupled to reversible multi-vibe density backreaction.

\subsection{Fixed-mesh horosphere de Sitter expansion}

As a finite quantum disturbance propagates radially into the fixed $\{3,4,3,4\}$ cusp bulk at mean depth
$\langle D(t) \rangle$, the 3D horosphere volume enclosed behind the wavefront grows exponentially with depth,
$V(D) \sim r_{\text{vol}}^{D}$ ($r_{\text{vol}} \approx 18.285$). Identifying the emergent 3D cosmological scale factor
$a(t) = V(\langle D(t)\rangle)^{1/3} \propto r_{\text{vol}}^{\langle D(t)\rangle / 3}$, ballistic radial propagation at
asymptotic speed $v_D = d\langle D\rangle / dt$ yields an emergent constant Hubble rate
\begin{equation}
H_\infty = \frac{\dot{a}}{a} = \frac{\ln r_{\text{vol}}}{3}\, v_D,
\label{eq:horosphere-hubble}
\end{equation}
with zero creation of new lattice sites and exact global unitarity.

\paragraph{Why this matters in practice.}
Most cosmological descriptions picture cosmic expansion as space itself stretching or creating new cells out of
nothing, which conflicts with exact time reversibility. On a fixed, infinite hyperbolic honeycomb, no cells are ever
created or destroyed: expansion is simply a finite localized disturbance spreading into exponentially wider shells.
Early crowding brakes that outward spread through radiation and matter deceleration eras, and once expansion dilutes
the density, the fixed geometry smoothly takes over as late-time de Sitter acceleration, all while remaining strictly
reversible backward to the initial seed.
