\section{Horosphere Cosmology and Downstream EFT Checks}
\label{sec:cosmology}

On a fixed honeycomb where no docks are created or destroyed, cosmic expansion cannot arise from adding lattice sites.
Instead, because each deeper horosphere floor in the $\{3,4,3,4\}$ cusp contains roughly $18.285$ times as many docks
as the floor above it, an excitation drifting steadily inward occupies an exponentially expanding 3D horosphere volume,
producing an effective de Sitter scale factor $a(t) \propto r_{\mathrm{vol}}^{\langle D(t)\rangle/3}$ on the husk
(Tier~B, \code{E-CSM-0064}). Adding a prescribed mean-field braking density drives a reversible transition from early
deceleration to late acceleration (Tier~B, \code{E-CSM-0065} Part~A). In the Planck units fixed by
Section~\ref{sec:gravity}, the bare lattice Hubble rate sits about 61 decades above $H_0$ (122 decades in vacuum energy
$\Lambda \propto H^2$), leaving Weinberg's no-go theorem \cite{weinberg1989} intact alongside continuum cosmology
compatibility checks at Tier~C (\code{E-CSM-0065} Part~B, \coderange{E-CSM-0066}{0068}).

\subsection{Fixed-mesh horosphere drift and reversible braking}

As a localized disturbance moves into the cusp at mean depth $\langle D(t) \rangle$, the 3D horosphere volume behind
the wavefront grows exponentially as
$V(D) \sim r_{\mathrm{vol}}^D$ ($r_{\mathrm{vol}} \approx 18.285$). Defining the effective 3D scale factor $a(t)$,
ballistic radial drift at speed $v_D = d\langle D\rangle / dt$ yields a constant effective de Sitter Hubble rate
$H_{\mathrm{dS}}$ (in $\text{beat}^{-1}$) with zero creation of new lattice sites:
\begin{equation}
a(t) = V\bigl(\langle D(t)\rangle\bigr)^{1/3} \propto r_{\mathrm{vol}}^{\langle D(t)\rangle / 3},
\qquad
H_{\mathrm{dS}} = \frac{\dot{a}}{a} = \frac{\ln r_{\mathrm{vol}}}{3}\, v_D.
\label{eq:horosphere-hubble}
\end{equation}
Here $q_{\log} = -a\ddot{a}/\dot{a}^2$ denotes the logarithmic deceleration parameter and
$w_{\mathrm{eff}} = (2 q_{\log} - 1)/3$ the effective equation-of-state parameter.

Direct enumeration of 51,064 exact cusp docks (\code{E-CSM-0064}) verifies uniform bank connectivity across depths
$D = 0, \dots, 3$, exact reversibility under $U^\dagger$, and convergence onto a de Sitter attractor ($w_{\mathrm{eff}} = -1$),
whereas a flat 3D cubic control ($b = 1$) gives zero expansion. The three radial walks tested in \code{E-CSM-0064} give:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{$(18\text{ down}, 1\text{ up}, 5\text{ lateral})$ Grover tree:} $H_{\mathrm{dS}} = 0.29808\text{ beat}^{-1}$
at leading branching base $b = 18$ (or $0.29983\text{ beat}^{-1}$ at $r_{\mathrm{vol}} = 6{,}034/330$, $v_D = 0.30952$,
$q_{\log} = -1.0000$, $w_{\mathrm{eff}} = -1.0000$, reversal $5.33 \times 10^{-15}$).
\item \textbf{Bare walk without $\mathrm{F\text{-}G}$:} $H_{\mathrm{dS}} = 0.32047\text{ beat}^{-1}$ at $b = 18$ (or
$0.32236\text{ beat}^{-1}$ at $r_{\mathrm{vol}} = 6{,}034/330$, $v_D = 0.33278$).
\item \textbf{$\mathrm{F\text{-}G}(\pi)$ single-bank control:} $H_{\mathrm{dS}} = 0.51713\text{ beat}^{-1}$ at
$r_{\mathrm{vol}} = 6{,}034/330$ (or $0.51455\text{ beat}^{-1}$ at $b = 18$, $v_D = 0.53384$, $q_{\log} = -0.9994$,
$w_{\mathrm{eff}} = -0.9996$, superseded at Scale~1 by $M_{\mathrm{pair}}(\zeta_{18})$ in Section~\ref{sec:relativity}).
\end{itemize}

The rates are in inverse beats. Converting to Planck units with Section~\ref{sec:gravity}'s $t_{\mathrm{Pl}} = \sqrt{G_{\mathrm{eff}}/c_u^5} =
0.2964\text{ beats}$ ($M_{\mathrm{Pl}} = 3.373\text{ beat}^{-1}$) gives {\bfseries\boldmath $H_{\mathrm{dS}} =
0.153\, M_{\mathrm{Pl}}$} for $\mathrm{F\text{-}G}(\pi)$ and {\bfseries\boldmath $0.088\, M_{\mathrm{Pl}}$} for the
Grover tree. Both sit {\bfseries\boldmath $\sim 60\text{--}61$ orders of magnitude above the observed
late-time Hubble constant $H_0$}, which corresponds to {\bfseries\boldmath $\sim 121\text{--}122$ orders of magnitude
in vacuum energy density} ($\Lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}/\Lambda_{\mathrm{obs}} \propto (H_{\mathrm{dS}}/H_0)^2$,
$\rho_{\mathrm{Pl}}/\rho_\Lambda = 8.83 \times 10^{122}$).

A dilute walk stays at $q_{\log} = w_{\mathrm{eff}} = -1$ forever, while the observed universe decelerated before it
accelerated. To see whether matter can brake the drift, Part~A of \code{E-CSM-0065} tests a
160-beat reversible unitary braking walk on a 1-particle 1D radial chain where a prescribed global mean-field density
$\rho(\langle D\rangle) = n_r a^{-4} + n_m a^{-3}$ ($n_r = 1{,}200$, $n_m = 400$, $\kappa = 1$, a mean-field ansatz rather
than local multi-excitation scattering) slows the radial coin angle via
\begin{equation}
\theta(\rho) = \frac{\pi}{1 + \kappa G_{\mathrm{eff}} \rho}.
\label{eq:braking-angle}
\end{equation}
Early drift brakes strongly ($q_{\log} > 0$) before relaxing onto the late-time de Sitter attractor as expansion dilutes
$\rho$, while preserving memoryless $U^\dagger$ reversal because the depth-preserving coin leaves $\langle D\rangle$
invariant. Table~\ref{tab:cosmo-b} collects all Tier~B horosphere measurements.

\subsection{Continuum cosmological EFT compatibility checks}

The Tier~C checks place standard continuum cosmology on the honeycomb's scales, using the shell ratio
$b = 6{,}034/330$ and the grand-unified scales of Section~\ref{sec:gauge}. Four downstream \textbf{Tier~C} checks (\code{E-CSM-0065} Part~B and \coderange{E-CSM-0066}{0068},
none derived from the bare QCA) audit standard three-era Friedmann dynamics, early-universe particle cosmology, and
cosmological-constant bounds expressed in $\{3,4,3,4\}$ shell units ($b = 6{,}034/330$). In \code{E-CSM-0065} Part~B, a
reversible 6th-order Yoshida symplectic integrator \cite{yoshida1990} benchmarks accuracy on the standard flat $\Lambda$CDM
Friedmann ODE (with prescribed $a_{\mathrm{eq}} = 10^{-5}$ and $H_{\mathrm{dS}}$ as the de Sitter asymptote), traversing
radiation domination, matter-radiation equality, matter domination, turnaround $q = 0$, and late-time de Sitter with
machine-precision Hamiltonian conservation.

Three further Tier~C scripts (\coderange{E-CSM-0066}{0068}) audit early-universe particle cosmology and holographic
$\Lambda$ bounds in $\{3,4,3,4\}$ shell units (Table~\ref{tab:cosmology}):
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Baryogenesis and WIMP relic density (\code{E-CSM-0066}):} Pairs a standalone 5-state $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_9]$
Sakharov toy walk \cite{sakharov1967} ($\Delta B = +0.2749$ at $t = 1$, $+0.1913$ at $T = 40$) with standard
$\mathrm{Spin}(10)$ thermal leptogenesis at $M_{\mathrm{PS}} = 1.434 \times 10^{11}\,\mathrm{GeV}$ (bisecting
$\tilde{m}_1 \in [m_2, m_3]$ to match $\eta_B = 6.12 \times 10^{-10}$) and a $\mathbb{Z}_2$-protected WIMP benchmark at
$m_{\mathrm{DM}} = M_{\mathrm{Pl}}/N_{13} = 484.35\,\mathrm{GeV}$ ($\Omega h^2 = 0.1147$ at canonical
$\langle\sigma v\rangle = 2.2 \times 10^{-26}\,\mathrm{cm}^3/\mathrm{s}$, sharing the dark-matter budget with
Section~\ref{sec:gauge}'s axion).
\item \textbf{Inflation, BBN, and nuclear shells (\code{E-CSM-0067}):} Benchmarks textbook Starobinsky $\alpha = 1$
plateau inflation \cite{starobinsky1980} ($H_{\mathrm{infl}} = 1.50 \times 10^{13}\,\mathrm{GeV}$, flagging the
$1.51 \times 10^6\times$ axion isocurvature tension), a stiff SDIRK4 Big Bang Nucleosynthesis solver with PDG inputs,
and a 1D Woods--Saxon nuclear magic-number control.
\item \textbf{Holographic and flux-landscape bounds (\code{E-CSM-0068}):} Expresses the Cohen--Kaplan--Nelson bound
\cite{cohen1999} in shell units ($D_\Lambda - D_{\mathrm{CKN}} = \log_b(1/\Omega_\Lambda) = 0.13$, a base-independent
Friedmann identity), verifies Weinberg's $500/729$ collapse window \cite{weinberg1989} ($1.54$ shells above $D_{\mathrm{Weinberg}} = 95.87$),
and runs a $D = 95$-anchored toy $N_{\mathrm{flux}} = 8$ Bousso--Polchinski scan \cite{bousso2000} (assuming 120 decades
of prior warp suppression, versus $N_{\mathrm{flux}} = 146$ from $D = 0$) alongside an explicit Weinberg (1989) No-Go
ledger.
\end{itemize}

Inward horosphere drift on the fixed mesh thus produces exact unitary de Sitter expansion without site creation, while
prescribed density braking recovers an early decelerating era. Both the 61-decade bare Hubble discrepancy (122 decades
in $\Lambda$) and Weinberg's no-go theorem remain open. Because the simulations in
Sections~\ref{sec:one-rule}--\ref{sec:cosmology} run for finite times on finite regions, Section~\ref{sec:unified}
establishes which structural laws hold unconditionally for all times and depths through Lean~4 proofs, infinite-volume
lattice mass-gap bounds, and coupled multi-sector runs.
