\section{Related Works}

The theory stands in a long line of attempts to build the world from something discrete and simple, and it owes each of them.

The idea that the universe is at bottom a computation runs from Zuse and Fredkin through Wolfram, and Wheeler's \emph{it from bit} put the slogan on it. This work keeps the discreteness and the rule but reverses the slogan to \emph{it from feeling}, the base layer experience rather than information. The machinery for running a rule on a curved discrete space is Margenstern's, whose cellular automata in hyperbolic spaces supply the addressing and the tilings the computation part leans on \cite{margenstern2008, margenstern2013}, and the universality is built in the lineage of von Neumann, Conway, and the reversible lattice gases of Margolus and Toffoli, with the associative memory in the sense Potter set down \cite{potter1992}.

The geometry draws on Coxeter's regular polytopes and the lattices of Conway and Sloane, and the appearance of the Standard Model from the octonions follows the maps of Furey, Baez, and Dixon, with Lisi's exceptional geometry a nearby cousin. The holography is the bulk-boundary correspondence of Maldacena \cite{maldacena1998} and the area law of Ryu and Takayanagi \cite{ryu2006}, realized here on a discrete crystal, with the perfect-tensor codes and tensor networks of the Swingle and Pastawski school giving the error-correcting reading, and ER equals EPR in the background. That correspondence is cleanest in anti-de Sitter space, a negatively curved world that is not ours, so it serves as a theoretical laboratory rather than a map of the sky. The lesson that carries over is the mechanism, that geometry and gravity can be woven from the entanglement of a quantum system, and the substrate takes it literally, holding the hyperbolic bulk that does the weaving while expanding in the de Sitter manner our own universe shows. Gravity emerges as Jacobson's equation of state \cite{jacobson1995} and in the entropic spirit of Verlinde.

A word on where this sits among the discrete approaches. The tensor-network and hyperbolic-tiling models capture the structure of spacetime impressively, the holographic scaling, the entanglement geometry, the minimal-surface entropy, the renormalization hierarchy read off a coarse-graining network that already looks like a curved tiling. But a fixed network is a still photograph, with no law that runs and no geometry that bends in answer to energy. What this substrate adds is the missing verb, the knit, a reversible local rule whose running yields the conservation, the finite light cone, the emergent Lorentz invariance, the Dirac and Maxwell sectors, the masses, and the gauge group that a frozen graph cannot. It clears the same structural bar the famous models clear, supplies much of the dynamical content they lack, and shares with them exactly one open frontier, the fully nonlinear curved-bulk gravity, which it marks rather than hides.

On the felt side the account of consciousness as integrated experience is Tononi's, the workspace picture Baars and Dehaene's, and the boundary and self-maintenance read through Friston's Markov blankets and the autopoiesis of Maturana and Varela. The metaphysics has deep roots, the one substance of Spinoza, the monads of Leibniz, the process philosophy of Whitehead in which experience is fundamental, the neutral monism of Russell, and the will of Schopenhauer, with the modern panpsychism of Chalmers, Goff, and Strawson as the living form of the view. The determinism that still yields quantum statistics is in the spirit of 't Hooft's cellular-automaton interpretation, and the place of all this in a space of possible mathematical worlds is Tegmark's question. The debt is broad, and the contribution is to draw these threads onto a single discrete substrate, taken as felt, and to compute what it does.
