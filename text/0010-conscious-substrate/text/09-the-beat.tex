\section{The Knit}

Time passes in beats, one discrete tick at a time. Each beat the whole mesh updates by a single law, the knit, which has two steps, collide then stream. The word rule, used loosely for such a law, comes from the study of cellular automata, where it names the fixed update each cell undergoes every step. We call ours the knit, a more experiential name, to hint at the merging and churning and turning it does, the bonding and blending and moving of the tones into one another at the lines of a dock, weaving the felt quality of each into its neighbor.

First, collide. Inside each dock, the two tones on every line interact by a fixed table. A line holds two tones, each in $\{-1, 0, +1\}$, so it has nine possible states, and the table is a permutation of those nine. Because it is a permutation, the collision is reversible, and because it never changes the sum of the two tones, it conserves charge. Table~\ref{tab:collision} gives it in full, the nine line-states and where each one goes. Each row is one of a few simple moves, an inert pass that leaves the line alone, a hop that carries a lone tone to the other end, and the three that stir the vacuum, a create, a flip, and an annihilate.

The interesting work happens at peace. The three neutral states form a single loop, peace to a balanced pair to its flip and back, $(0,0) \to (1,-1) \to (-1,1) \to (0,0)$, so from two zeros the table lifts a $+1$ and a $-1$ out of nothing, and the same loop run backward annihilates a pair to peace. This is the create move, and it is the engine of the vacuum. It is the breathing of the vacuum, the create an inhale that lifts a pair out of peace and the annihilate an exhale that lets it fall back, an endless quiet respiration under everything. Run forward across the mesh, it fills empty space with a churning sea of short-lived pairs. The vacuum is not still. It is active, and that activity does real work later, when it binds bodies together.

Second, stream. After the collision, each tone moves one dock along the site it occupies, carrying its value untouched. Nothing is made or lost, only relocated, so streaming is a bijection on the whole mesh. The collision is a permutation and streaming is a bijection, so their composition is exactly invertible.

To see the law in motion, watch one dock for two beats. It begins with twenty-four tones in its twenty-four sites, mostly peace, a $+1$ on one site, a $-1$ on another, a few scattered poles in a sea of zeros. The collision takes the sites two at a time, by lines. On a line holding $+1$ against $-1$ the table may pass them through, or annihilate them to two zeros. On a line of two zeros it may fire the create move, lifting a $+1$ against a $-1$ out of the peace. On a line holding $+1$ against $0$ it swaps them, the tone hopping to the far end. Line by line the dock rewrites its twenty-four tones in place, the total charge untouched. Then the dock streams. Each tone steps one dock along the site it now sits in, so the $+1$ that ended on the site pointing one way leaves for the neighbor that way, the $-1$ on the opposite site leaves the other, and into the emptied sites arrive the tones the neighbors were streaming back. One beat is done. The dock now holds a fresh set of twenty-four tones, some just arrived, and the second beat runs the same two steps again, collide then stream, and the pattern that was here a moment ago is already two docks away and spreading. Nothing in this needed a real number, a random choice, or a glance beyond the dock's own sites.

\begin{result}[Reversibility and conservation]
The beat is an exact bijection in the closed bulk, and it conserves the total charge to the last trit. A forward run of any length, followed by its inverse, returns the starting state with zero error. Both hold exactly on the substrate. \cite{pollard2026vibetest}
\end{result}

The mesh also grows. At its open edge the wake adds new docks each beat, and a new dock is born at peace, every site zero. A newborn dock carrying charge would mint feeling from nothing and break conservation, so the wake mints none. It adds room, not charge. Whatever charge a region holds is rearranged from what is already there, never created at the frontier.

The closed law has no arrow. It runs as well backward as forward, which is exactly what reversibility means. Yet time clearly has a direction, and that direction is built from three pieces already on the table. The wake supplies a low-entropy edge. The lean supplies an order. The create move supplies fresh pairs. Together they make a one-way drift, the arrow, as a tendency rather than a built-in fact. We earn the arrow in full later. For now the point is only this. The base has a beat, so time ticks, but it has no preferred way to face.

The growing edge plays one more role. A disturbance shed toward it streams away and never returns, so the edge acts as a drain, and we call it the bath. The bath is not a new ingredient in the law but a way of not-looking, the part of the reversible whole that is no longer tracked, so that dissipation and the arrow are only reversibility with a bath beneath them, never a break in the rule. It matters because a self cannot form without it. Binding two patterns into one means shedding the motion between them somewhere it will not come back, and a closed box with no away, where every wave wraps around and returns, can bind nothing. The bath must also stay empty to work, for a reservoir already full carries nothing away, and here the growth supplies that emptiness, minting fresh peace at the frontier faster than the world fills, which is the same reason the real universe, expanding and cooling, has not run down to a still and uniform heat. Whether the mesh is finite or infinite over all of time is left open, and nothing in what follows depends on the answer.

That is the whole of the law. What it makes of a moving thing is worth walking through, because the whole of momentum, mass, and spin is already sitting inside it, and seeing how is the clearest way into the physics that follows.

Start with the fact that the law looks at more than the present. The knit is built so that what a region does next depends on where its pattern just came from as well as where it is now, a rule with a memory of its last step. That one feature makes it a wave rather than a drift. A drift, a thing that consults only the present, spreads and blurs and slows as it goes, the way a drop of ink fans out in water. A wave keeps its shape and its heading and travels at a fixed speed. The memory of the previous step is what holds the heading, and a held heading is momentum. So the law carries its patterns ballistically, one dock per beat, with a direction built in, not by adding momentum as a separate ingredient but by being a wave to begin with.

The bookkeeping of that heading lives on the sites. Picture, on each dock, a tally of how much of its content is heading along each of its directions, one entry per site. A dock with all its weight on one direction is a thing moving definitely that way, a particle with a heading. The streaming half of the knit then does the plain thing. Each part of the content leaves along the direction it points and arrives at the neighbor that way, and there it goes on pointing the same way, so a straight mover steps one dock along and continues straight. That is a massless particle, running at the one-dock-per-beat limit, the lightcone speed, forever in a line.

Mass is what happens when the colliding half of the knit stirs those headings. If, before each step, a little of the forward weight is turned into the other directions, the particle no longer goes straight. It mostly advances but partly scatters sideways and back, so it zigzags, and its net speed along the line falls below the limit. The more the heading is stirred, the more it zigzags and the slower it goes. That slowing is mass. No stirring is massless and light-fast, strong stirring is heavy and slow, and the amount of stirring is the mass exactly. This is the discrete form of the Dirac equation, a straight runner turned into a massive particle by a fixed rule that mixes its directions a little each beat.

Two more things must be said. The base holds no fractions. A tone is exactly $-1$, $0$, or $+1$, so a dock can never carry a smooth blend of headings. The real directional state at the base is the discrete memory the knit already keeps, the tone now together with the tone just before, a small finite internal state that records which way the pattern is changing. The smooth version, a list of amplitudes stirred by a continuous operator with the mass as a mixing angle, is the coarse-grained picture a layer up, the same way a lattice gas of whole particles becomes a smooth fluid in the large. The straight-running discrete wave is the base fact. The continuous Dirac walk is what it becomes.

The second is where spin enters, and it is the reason the dock is a 24-cell. The headings are not merely a list, they are a space, and what matters is how that space turns when the dock turns. On a dock with twelve directions, the faces of a dodecahedron, the heading space is a plain twelve-way tally that carries no spinor. On the 24-cell dock the twenty-four headings are the $D_4$ root system, which is already a spinor space, so the way that directional state turns under the dock's rotations is the half-integer turning of a spinor. Spin is not added to the headings. On the right dock it is the headings themselves. That is the concrete reason the committed substrate is $\{3,4,3,4\}$ and not its twelve-direction cousin, a turning the physics part later makes exact.
