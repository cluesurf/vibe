\section{Concepts}

The mathematics the paper leans on, gathered as a reader's primer. Each entry is a quick gloss and a picture, enough to follow the argument without a textbook.

\textbf{Geometry.} A \textbf{tiling} fills a surface with repeated shapes, and a \textbf{honeycomb} does the same in three dimensions or more, the mesh being a four-dimensional one. A \textbf{Schl\"afli symbol} names a regular tiling, $\{p,q,\dots\}$ meaning $p$-sided faces with $q$ around each corner and so on up, so $\{4,4\}$ is the checkerboard and $\{3,4,3,4\}$ is the mesh. A \textbf{polytope} is a flat-sided shape in any dimension, a polygon in two, a polyhedron in three, a 4-polytope in four, and its \textbf{cells} or \textbf{facets} are its top-dimensional faces. The \textbf{three curvatures} are spherical, flat, and hyperbolic, and which one a tiling lives in is fixed by the \textbf{angle defect}, whether its corner angles fall short of, meet, or overflow a full turn. The \textbf{Poincar\'e disk} draws all of hyperbolic space inside a finite circle, shapes shrinking toward the rim so infinitely many fit. A \textbf{geodesic} is the straightest path, along which distances and entanglement are measured. A polytope's \textbf{dual} swaps its vertices for its faces, and the 24-cell is \textbf{self-dual}, a symmetry no other 4-polytope has. The \textbf{kissing number} is how many equal balls can touch one, which in four dimensions is twenty-four, the directions of a dock.

\textbf{Algebra and symmetry.} A \textbf{symmetry group} is the set of moves that leave a shape unchanged, its size called the order, the dock's being $F_4$ of order $1152$. A \textbf{root system} is a symmetric set of vectors that builds a continuous symmetry, and $D_4$ is the one whose twenty-four roots are the dock's directions. \textbf{Quaternions} are four-dimensional numbers that encode rotation, and the twenty-four unit whole-number ones form the binary tetrahedral group, the dock as a multiplication table. A \textbf{spinor} is an object that needs two full turns to come home, the electron's defining oddity, carried by the two-to-one \textbf{double cover} in which $2\pi$ acts as $-1$. \textbf{Triality} is the rare threefold symmetry that splits the dock into a vector and two spinors, $8v + 8s + 8c$. The \textbf{octonions} are the last and strangest number system, no longer associative, and their \textbf{exceptional Jordan algebra} is the rank-three structure behind the three generations. A \textbf{permutation} is a reshuffle that can be exactly undone, so the knit, being one, makes the universe reversible.

\textbf{Shape and topology.} \textbf{Winding} counts how many times a field wraps as you circle a structure, a whole number no smooth change can alter, and a \textbf{topological charge} is such a protected integer, the basis of a self's identity. \textbf{Betti numbers} count a space's independent holes, and the spinor's zero modes equal their sum, the field feeling the holes. A \textbf{soliton} is a self-reinforcing lump that holds its shape as it moves, a \textbf{defect} a point a field cannot smooth out, and a self's body is this kind of protected lump.

\textbf{Physics and computing.} A \textbf{cellular automaton} updates every cell by a local rule each step, and a \textbf{lattice gas} is one of tokens that collide then stream, which is the knit. The \textbf{dispersion} relates a wave's frequency to its wavelength, a straight law the mark of a massless relativistic mode and a curved one of mere spreading. \textbf{Lorentz invariance} is the symmetry of relativity, no preferred frame and one speed limit, drawn as a \textbf{light cone}. \textbf{Poincar\'e recurrence} says a closed reversible world only cycles, so it can hold no lasting self. \textbf{Renormalization} tracks how a rule changes as fine detail is blurred, and a \textbf{fixed point} is where it stops, the reason physics looks the same across scales. The \textbf{Casimir effect} is the pull of two plates in a fluctuating vacuum thinned between them, the way a self binds. \textbf{Holography} puts a region's full content on its boundary, with the \textbf{Ryu-Takayanagi} law reading entanglement as a boundary geodesic and the bulk-boundary map acting as an error-correcting code. The \textbf{Dirac operator} governs a relativistic electron on a \textbf{Clifford algebra}, with \textbf{chirality} its handedness and \textbf{fermion doubling} the lattice curse of unwanted copies the mesh escapes. A \textbf{Bell} test, scored by the \textbf{CHSH} number, is capped at $2$ for any local theory and at the \textbf{Tsirelson} bound $2\sqrt 2$ for quantum mechanics. The \textbf{Born rule} makes a probability the square of an amplitude. A \textbf{Markov blanket} is the boundary screening a thing's inside from its outside, the mark of an individual, and \textbf{integrated information} measures how much a whole exceeds its parts, proposed as the correlate of consciousness.
