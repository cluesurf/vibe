\section{Data Structures}

The addressed tree of the mesh is not only a place to compute, it is a place to store, and the storing is unreasonably good. Because the bulk branches exponentially and every dock's neighbors are fixed by its address, the classical data structures of computer science sit on the geometry for free, with no stored pointers and no extra bookkeeping. We built a catalog of them on the substrate and measured each. Table~\ref{tab:data} lists the headline cases. \cite{pollard2026vibetest}

The lesson behind the table is one fact, structural universality. The geometry does not merely permit these structures, it is them, so a database, an index, a routing fabric, or a memory is a region of the mesh rather than a thing laid over it. The associative memory makes the point sharpest, and it earns its own treatment next.

What hangs at an address comes in two kinds. A still ornament is a stored value, read when you arrive, like a fruit examined and put back. A living ornament is a small running pattern, a program that acts on whoever reaches it, like a fruit that, once eaten, changes the eater. The first is memory, the second a process waiting to run, and both are found only by navigating to them.

Seen whole, an entire storage engine sorts itself into the crystal, each region holding the part its geometry is built for. The branching tree of the bulk is the index, kept balanced for free with no stored pointers, since the curvature leaves no long thin paths for it to degenerate along. The exponential boundary is the hash space, a vast cheap field of addresses where keys scatter without collision. The radial shells are the sorted levels of scale, a pyramid from coarse to fine. The flat cusp is where dense arrays belong, the one place with the even grid they need. And the append-only log of every write runs along the arrow of growth itself. The crystal is not a place to bolt a database onto. It already is one, each kind of structure living exactly where the curvature fits it.

None of this is special to the committed substrate. The catalog was built and checked across the buildable tessellations, so the structures are properties of hyperbolic space itself, not of one honeycomb. The geometry does set limits, and they are reported plainly. A range scan over a region visits exponentially many docks, and a dense flat array belongs on the boundary horosphere, the flat sheet at the edge, not in the sparse interior. The bulk is a magnificent tree and a poor array, and the catalog respects the difference.
