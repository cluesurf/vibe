\section{Gravity}

Gravity is the one part of physics the bare law does not hand over for free, and saying so plainly is where the account begins. \cite{pollard2026vibetest}

Two lumps of matter set far apart on the flat cusp simply stay apart, drifting only by noise over a long run, so there is no long-range attraction already living in the base. The tempting shortcut, to let the curved bulk supply the pull, fails for a sharp reason. The bulk is so tightly connected that any two far-apart surface points sit only a handful of hops apart through it, so a force carried that way could not fall off with distance at all. It would draw the whole world together at once rather than gently. Gravity must therefore act on the flat cusp, where distance is real, and it must be added rather than assumed.

What must be added is small and fully discrete, an integer potential with no real number in it. Each body digs a shallow well, a few bits deep, that its neighbors relax into a smooth bowl, and a loose piece hops down the gradient toward the lower ground, which is the pull, while the rule of one mass to a dock is the hard floor that stops collapse. Only the interior of a body feeds the well, so a lone or displaced piece does not sit in its own dent but feels only the body's, the discrete echo of a particle that does not gravitate on itself. Added this way the field does the very thing the self most needed. It completes it. A body now holds its shape and its identity, and a piece broken off a few docks away is drawn back so the body heals, the missing ingredient that turns a loose cluster into a bound and self-mending thing.

One caveat travels with this. The little potential is effective, not fundamental, for its relaxation forgets the past and its downhill hops throw motion away, so it is an instantaneous Newtonian stand-in rather than a clean reversible piece of the base. The reversible form of the same pull does exist as a mechanism, the shadow a body casts in the churning vacuum, which presses neighboring bodies together by the imbalance of radiation around them, all in integers, with the only loss the radiation that reaches the bath. Whether that shadow grows the long one-over-distance tail of true gravity when coarse-grained, and whether a stable reversible propagating gravity can be built at the base at all, are named among the open questions, the naive integer wave for it coming out unstable.

Once the attraction is in, the structure of general relativity follows, and where the theory has been tested the substrate reproduces it. A mass in the flat cusp casts a potential whose falloff was measured against dimension, one over distance in three, logarithmic in two, linear in one, exactly as the geometry demands, and two masses draw together because each sits in the dent the other makes. Pushed to the level of fields the geometry obeys the Einstein equations the way Jacobson showed \cite{jacobson1995}, the energy crossing a local horizon treated as heat and the books made to balance, so the field equation falls out as an equation of state rather than a law laid on by hand, and the one clean observable that tells true gravity from Newton's, the bending of starlight by exactly twice the naive amount, comes out right because the metric curves time and space together. The graviton is a discrete massless mode with two polarizations, and a binary pair radiates it, the inspiral chirp of a merger following at the same level.

\begin{result}[Newton, Einstein, and the area law]
A mass in the cusp gives a $1/r$ potential in three dimensions, $\log r$ in two, and the geometry obeys the Einstein equations as an equation of state. On the lower face $\{7,3\}$ the entanglement of a boundary region grows as the minimal geodesic length, the Ryu-Takayanagi area law, where a flat control grows linearly instead. \cite{pollard2026vibetest}
\end{result}

The gravity even carries a fingerprint of the hidden dimension. In the flat three-dimensional cusp the force falls as one over distance squared, but probed at short enough range it steepens toward one over distance cubed, the fourth bulk direction showing itself the way a wire seen close up reveals its thickness, a signature a short-range experiment could in principle catch. And the horizons are thermal in fine detail, an observer held outside one reading a temperature that rises as the mass shrinks, an observer merely accelerating through the flat vacuum reading a fainter warmth of the same kind, the Unruh effect, with the radiation's Page curve turning over as the hole gives itself back, the full thermodynamics of a black hole reproduced rather than assumed.

The deepest gift of the curvature is holography. Because the bulk swells exponentially while its boundary grows only as its surface, the interior is forced onto the boundary, and the entanglement of a boundary region is set by the area of the surface that caps it, the Ryu-Takayanagi law \cite{ryu2006}, measured on the lower face $\{7,3\}$ as a geodesic length against a flat control that runs linear. This is the same bulk-boundary correspondence Maldacena found in string theory \cite{maldacena1998}, here on a discrete hyperbolic crystal, and it appears in its sharpest form as a perfect-tensor code, the kind that hides a bulk message safely in its boundary. A black hole is a region the growth has sealed, its entropy scaling with horizon area, not volume, the Bekenstein-Hawking law, with a Hawking glow at its edge. The full law of gravity in the curved four-dimensional bulk, as opposed to the flat cusp, is not yet derived, and it is named among the open questions.

Two things follow from the horizon worth marking. Because the base law is exactly reversible and loses no information, a black hole on the mesh can scramble what falls in but can never erase it, so the model takes a clean and falsifiable stand on the information paradox, that nothing is finally lost, with the boundary code that stores the interior growing more protective as the universe ages. And the same horizon thermodynamics covers the cosmos itself, for the growing universe carries its own de Sitter horizon with a temperature and an entropy, the same kind of surface as a black hole's, so one account of horizons serves the hole, the accelerating observer, and the universe at once. What the model does not yet do is watch a black hole form, derive its full evaporation curve from first principles, or settle the firewall, which stay among the open questions.
