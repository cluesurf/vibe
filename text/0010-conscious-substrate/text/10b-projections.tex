\section{Projections}

The pictures in this paper are not the space. Hyperbolic space cannot be drawn flat without distortion, the way a globe cannot be peeled onto a page without stretching, so every figure is a projection, a faithful shadow that trades one true thing away to show another. Knowing which is which keeps the pictures from misleading.

The most faithful model is the hyperboloid. Hyperbolic space sits as a curved bowl inside a flat space with one extra dimension, a Minkowski space with one minus sign in its measure, and on that bowl every distance is exact and undistorted. The reward is more than tidiness. The motions that slide the bowl onto itself are exactly the Lorentz transformations, the boosts and rotations of relativity, so the symmetry of hyperbolic space and the symmetry of spacetime are one group, met here in its geometric home before it returns later as physics.

For drawing, the bowl is flattened to a disk by stereographic projection, a sightline cast from a single pole that lands every point of the curved model on a flat page. This is the Poincar\'e disk of the figures, and it keeps angles true while shrinking sizes toward the rim, so the whole infinite space fits inside a finite circle and the cells look smaller and smaller as they crowd the edge, the Escher picture. Nothing out there is actually shrinking. The shrinkage is the cost of flattening, the way Greenland looks continent-sized on a flat map of the round Earth.

The deeper gap is dimension. The base is four-dimensional, and no figure can show four, so each picture is a slice or a face of the real thing. A tiling drawn in a flat disk is a two-dimensional cousin, the heptagrid $\{7,3\}$ standing in for the pattern. A rendering of cells packed in a ball is a three-dimensional cross-section of the four-dimensional honeycomb, the 24-cells caught one dimension short. And the flat three-dimensional space we live in is itself a projection, the boundary face of the curved four-dimensional bulk. So the images should be held lightly. What is drawn is always a step or two below what is meant, a flat shadow of a curved space, or a three-dimensional slice of a four-dimensional crystal, chosen to show one true feature clearly at the cost of the rest.
