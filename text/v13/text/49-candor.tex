\section{Perspective}

Every computational result is graded by what it actually establishes, not by whether it prints a success. A result is
circular if the answer was put in by hand, and it proves nothing. It is known math if it confirms an established
mathematical fact, like the cell being the binary tetrahedral group. It is known physics if it reproduces a known
construction on the substrate, like a lattice gauge theory. And it is emergent and novel if one base rule produces the
result as a measured consequence, with a control where the answer should come out no, ideally a number that could have
been wrong. The grade is stated with each result, and a deep claim that lacks a control is downgraded, so a result cannot
promote itself by assertion. Most results in a young program are reproductions and confirmations, which is acknowledged
rather than hidden, and the genuinely novel ones are a smaller, marked set.

The base is deterministic and discrete, and this is enforced. No randomness enters the law or the initial conditions of
the foundational results, the Bell violation and the Born rule and the genesis use no random numbers, and robustness
comes from varying the size of the system rather than averaging over seeds, because a result that holds only on average
over random draws is a statistical claim about an ensemble, not a property of the deterministic rule, and is labeled as
such. Where a phenomenon does not emerge from the small base, the negative is reported, not imposed, because
adding a new ingredient and calling the result emergent would be the worst kind of self-deception.

The failures are part of the integrity. A handful of results are negatives or open frontiers, and they are stated
as openly as the wins: the bare causal-set action does not make smooth spacetime dominate, the known entropy problem.
Willpower does not emerge as a pumped reserve. A fully reversible gravity at the base is unsolved. And the identification
of the forced threefold structure with the three observed generations is the deepest open conjecture. A wall of green
checkmarks would be worth less than these negatives, because each one narrows the search and earns the right to the
next claim. The program is built to find out where the base gives the physics we
see, where it only reproduces what is already known, and where it fails or stays open.
