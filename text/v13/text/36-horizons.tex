\section{Horizons}

A black hole is a region the dynamics cannot leave, and on the mesh its thermodynamics comes out of the same
holography that gave the area law. A horizon has a temperature and an entropy, and no information is finally lost.

The entropy first. A region's entropy, the count of the ways its inside can be arranged given its boundary, scales with
the boundary AREA, not the enclosed volume, because the exponential growth crowds the interior's information onto the
boundary. This is the Bekenstein-Hawking law, that a horizon's entropy is its area in fundamental units, and an
experiment measures it directly: it computes the entropy of a region on the mesh and finds it tracks the horizon
area, not the volume, the holographic fingerprint that a flat control does not show.

The temperature next. Near a horizon the modes of the field are stretched, and the steady stretching gives the outgoing
radiation a thermal spectrum at a temperature set by the surface gravity, the Hawking temperature, the surface gravity
divided by two pi. This is reproduced on the substrate, and combined with the area law it lets the Einstein equations
themselves be derived as an equation of state: treat the energy crossing a local horizon as heat, set the heat equal to
the temperature times the change in horizon entropy, and the field equation falls out, the way Jacobson showed, rather
than being assumed.

The information last. Because the local rule is exactly reversible, nothing is ever destroyed, so the model takes a
sharp and falsifiable stand on the black-hole information question: information is not finally lost, it is stored on the
boundary code, which grows more protective as the universe ages. The growing holographic code raises its erasure
threshold with age, which an experiment measures, and a spread-encoded bit survives a bounded erasure where a localized
bit is destroyed, the error-correcting structure of the boundary made concrete. So a horizon on the mesh has an
area-entropy, a Hawking temperature, and a reversible interior whose information is held on a growing protective
boundary, each a consequence of holography and reversibility, and each demonstrated by an experiment with a control.
