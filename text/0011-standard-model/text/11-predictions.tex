\section{Predictions and Tests} \label{sec:predictions}

A derivation earns its keep by being falsifiable. The forced structure makes several commitments that could fail, and
we list the sharpest ones, separating clean consequences from softer expectations.

\subsection{Sharp commitments}

\paragraph{Exactly three generations, no fourth.} The generations are the three eight-dimensional representations of
$\mathrm{Spin}(8)$, and there are exactly three. There is no room for a fourth chiral generation. A discovered fourth
chiral family, with its own light neutrino, would break the derivation at the triality step. The current data, the
invisible width of the $Z$ and the Higgs production rate, already favor three, consistent with this.

\paragraph{No proton decay from a unifying gauge symmetry.} The gauge group is the product
$SU(3) \times SU(2) \times U(1)$, read off rung by rung from the algebra tower. It is not the restriction of a larger
simple group in the usual grand-unified sense, since the factors come from three different division algebras rather
than one embedding. So the dimension-six proton decay mediated by grand-unified gauge bosons is not predicted. A clean
observation of proton decay in the classic $SU(5)$ channels would sit awkwardly with this picture.

\paragraph{One Higgs doublet, custodial $\rho = 1$ at tree level.} The Higgs is the single quaternionic doublet, and the
custodial relation $\rho = 1$ is structural, not tuned. Robust tree-level deviation from $\rho = 1$, or a second
fundamental scalar doublet that is not a composite, would be a problem.

\paragraph{Four spacetime dimensions, no large extra ones.} Spacetime is exactly the rank of $D_4$, four. The seven
imaginary octonion units are internal, not spatial. The framework does not predict large or warped \emph{physical}
extra dimensions of the kind sought at colliders. The warping here is in the internal hyperbolic bulk, not in an extra
space dimension one could produce Kaluza-Klein gravitons from.

\subsection{Softer expectations}

\paragraph{Geometric mass ladder.} The fermion masses should organize as powers of a single ratio near
$\lambda \approx 18.3$, with integer depth steps, rather than as unrelated numbers. This is a pattern claim, testable in
principle by whether the measured masses fit a one-ratio ladder with small integer depths better than chance, but it is
softer because the depth assignments are free.

\paragraph{Discreteness at the bottom.} The substrate is discrete, and the continuum is emergent. Any genuine discovery
of continuous structure below the emergent scale, or conversely a clean signal of spacetime discreteness at an
accessible scale, would bear on the picture, though both lie far from current reach.

\subsection{What would settle it}

The cleanest single test is the generation count, because it is a hard three with no adjustable part. The cleanest
structural test is the absence of a unifying gauge symmetry of the grand-unified type, since the construction builds the
group from three separate algebras rather than one. We state these plainly so the framework can be wrong in a definite
way. It is exploratory work, and it is meant to be checkable.
