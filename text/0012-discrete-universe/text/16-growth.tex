\section{Growth}

The mesh grows explosively, and the rate is an exact number fixed by the shape. Count the docks at each distance from a starting dock, layer by layer: one, then twenty-four, then four hundred fifty-six, then eight thousand three hundred seventy-six, and a few layers out the count runs past a billion. In flat space these counts grow like a power of the distance. In hyperbolic space they grow exponentially, with a fixed growth ratio.

For this mesh the ratio is about eighteen and a quarter per layer, the warp factor. The early counts are exact and verified, and they pin the warp factor to several figures. Its exact closed form is a higher-degree algebraic number with no simple recurrence, an open computation, but the numerical value is firm and is all the physics uses.

This single number governs two things the later sections lean on. The exponential growth crowds almost all of any region near its edge, which is the geometric content of holography. And depth into a warped throat of the mesh scales lengths by the warp factor per shell, which is the geometric content of the mass hierarchy, where a few steps of depth produce the huge spread of particle masses without any one absurdly tuned number.

At any beat the mesh is finite, a definite patch of docks, and it is never finished. New docks appear at its open edge each beat, so the space is unbounded over time though bounded at every instant. That open edge is the present, the growing now, and the steady birth of new docks there is the source of cosmic expansion and the seed of time's direction, which the law makes precise.
