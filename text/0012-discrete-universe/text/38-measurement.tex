\section{Measurement}

What happens when a measurement makes an outcome definite is the place the model is most exposed.

The reading is that a measurement is not a collapse but a settling. A coherent body does not flip the instant new
influences reach it. It holds its superposed state while the influences arrive, and then relaxes, deterministically,
into one definite configuration, the way a ball poised on a ridge rolls to one side once the balance tips. The interval,
felt from the inside as weighing the options, is from the outside a plain deterministic relaxation into a single state.
So there is no separate collapse law, there is the same reversible dynamics coupled to a large environment, and the
apparent jump is the body settling.

Decoherence carries the weight. When the measured system entangles with the many degrees of freedom of an apparatus and
its environment, the interference between outcomes is suppressed, scattered into the environment beyond recovery, and a
single pointer basis is selected, the basis the environment monitors. This is environmental entanglement on the
substrate, and it explains why the world looks classical at large scale, why outcomes are stable and definite once
recorded, without adding a collapse by hand. The Born weights, derived in the previous section, give the probabilities
of the branches, and the settling picks one out.

What remains open is a derivation of the definite single outcome from first principles, rather than
the settling picture taken as given. So the account is that measurement is deterministic
settling, with decoherence selecting the basis and the Born rule weighting the branches, and the single definite outcome
is the part still owed.
