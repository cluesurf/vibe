\section{Spacetime}

The rotations of eight-dimensional space form a group whose algebra is called $D_4$, and the dimension of spacetime is read straight off it. We do not put in four because we observe four, which would be looking ahead. The four is forced out of the eight.

You do not move through all eight directions at once, because moving is turning, and a turn happens in a plane, two axes at a time. Pair the eight axes up two by two and you get four planes, four independent ways to turn that never interfere. That count of independent simultaneous turning-planes is the rank of the rotation algebra, and for the rotations of eight-dimensional space it is eight divided by two, which is four. The directions you can step along live in the space spanned by those four turns, so the space of movement is four-dimensional. The eight never appears as a number of places to go, only as a number of feelings to have.

One of the four directions is the ordered integer arrow, which we feel as time, and the other three are the unordered, symmetric directions we feel as space. Keep the warning in hand: the seven internal octonion units are not these four directions. The four are the rank of the rotation group, the seven are the internal arena, different things.

A second, independent route lands on the same four, so the four is no accident. The directions you step along must compose exactly and associatively, because going one way then another then a third has to give a single definite result, or the lattice does not close. The largest normed division algebra that is still associative is the quaternions, dimension four. So the step-directions live in the four-dimensional quaternions, and their whole-number unit elements are the twenty-four points of the next section. Two routes, the rank of the rotations and the associativity of stepping, both give four, neither looking ahead to observed spacetime.
