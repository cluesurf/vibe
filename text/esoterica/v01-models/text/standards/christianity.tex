\section{Christianity}

Esoteric Christianity is the contemplative and mystical stream within the
Christian tradition. It runs through the negative theology of Pseudo-Dionysius,
the German mysticism of Meister Eckhart and Jacob Boehme, the Eastern practice
of Hesychasm, and the Gnostic systems of late antiquity. These currents share
the mainstream framework of a triune God and a created world, but they push
toward a source above being and a return of the soul into God.

\subsection{The Creation Model}

In the beginning there is a source that is pure goodness and beyond all
telling. It does not create out of need. It creates out of overflow, the way
the sun gives light without losing any of itself. The source speaks, and the
speaking is itself a power. By that word the worlds are made. Goodness passes
downward through ordered ranks of being, each rank receiving the light and
passing it to the rank below. The same drawing that sends the light out also
draws everything back. All things go out from the source and all things are
drawn home to it. The pattern is a circle, out and then back.

There is a second telling. In it the world we live in is not the work of the
true source at all. A lower maker, born from a flaw within the fullness above,
is ignorant of the source over him. He mistakes himself for the highest and
fashions the material world as a kind of prison, ruled by his powers and by
fate. Yet a spark of the true light is trapped inside the human being. Most
people never notice it and live unaware of it. A messenger comes from above to
wake the spark, to remind it where it came from, and to show it the way back up
past the rulers to the source. To know is to be freed. The return of the
scattered light to the fullness ends the account.

\subsection{The Source Beyond Being}

The mystics agree that the ultimate exceeds every name. Mainstream doctrine
calls God a unity of three persons. The contemplatives go further. They hold
that the source is above being itself.

\textbf{Pseudo-Dionysius} teaches two ways of speaking. The
affirmative way (\textbf{kataphatic}) names God by what creation reveals. God
is good, God is light, God is being. The negative way (\textbf{apophatic})
unsays every name, denying each attribute until thought falls silent in the
\textbf{divine darkness}. For Dionysius the negative way is truer, because the
source is beyond every assertion and every denial. The ultimate is the
\textbf{superessential} Godhead, above name and knowing.

\textbf{Meister Eckhart} sharpens this into a distinction the church resisted.
\textbf{God} (Gott) is the source as related to creatures, the persons in act,
the God who works and gives. The \textbf{Godhead} (Gottheit) is the silent
ground behind the persons, beyond even the name God, a still desert where no
distinction ever entered. Eckhart prays for God to make him free of God. In
the depth of the soul there is an uncreated \textbf{spark} (Fünklein), one with
the ground of God. There the Father eternally begets the Word, and Eckhart
teaches that this same begetting happens in the empty, still ground of the
soul. Beyond it lies the \textbf{breakthrough} (Durchbruch), where the soul
passes past God into the bare Godhead.

\subsection{The Groundless Abyss of Boehme}

\textbf{Jacob Boehme} places a principle below God. Below God lies the
groundless abyss (the \textbf{Ungrund}). It is not yet God. It is bare will
without object, an eternal nothing that wills to know itself. From that
self-seeking, God and the whole cosmos unfold. The will turns inward, desire
arises, and a mirror forms. That mirror is divine \textbf{Wisdom}
(\textbf{Sophia}), the virgin in which God beholds himself.

From the turning will unfold the \textbf{seven qualities}, also called the
source-spirits (Quellgeister), the forms by which God becomes manifest nature.
The first three are dark and wrathful. The fourth is the turning point of fire.
The last three are light and gracious.

\begin{longtable}{L{0.06\linewidth} L{0.30\linewidth} L{0.50\linewidth}}
\caption{Boehme's seven qualities, the source-spirits of manifest nature.}\\
\toprule
\# & Quality & Character \\
\midrule
1 & Astringency & The first drawing-in, dark and cold \\
2 & Attraction & Motion against contraction, the sting \\
3 & Anguish & The turning torment between the first two \\
4 & Fire & The flash where dark turns to light, the crisis \\
5 & Light & The gentle light born from the fire, love \\

\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

For Boehme the cosmos is an interplay of wrath and love, dark fire and light
fire, both from the one source. Evil is not a separate principle. It is the
dark root taken alone, the first three qualities without the turn to light.

\subsection{The Nine Angelic Orders}

In \textit{The Celestial Hierarchy} Pseudo-Dionysius ranks the angels in
\textbf{nine orders} grouped in three triads. Each triad receives the divine
light and passes it down to the next. Light flows only downward. No order skips
its place.

\subsection{The Return of the Soul}

The goal of the Eastern path is deification (\textbf{theosis}). The human becomes
god by grace, not by nature. The fathers state it directly. God became man so
that man might become god. This is union, not absorption. The creature stays a
creature and yet is filled with God.

The classical map of the ascent has \textbf{three ways}. The
\textbf{purgative} way roots out sin and attachment. The \textbf{illuminative}
way grows in virtue and knowledge of God. The \textbf{unitive} way joins the
soul to God in love. These match the angelic functions of purifying,
illuminating, and perfecting.

The Eastern monks practice inner stillness (\textbf{Hesychasm}). They gather the
intellect into the heart and repeat the Jesus Prayer until prayer becomes
ceaseless and they behold the \textbf{uncreated light}, the same light that
shone from Christ on Mount Tabor. \textbf{Gregory Palamas} defended this with a
distinction. God's \textbf{essence} (ousia) stays forever hidden. God's
\textbf{energies} (energeiai) are God truly present and active, really shared
and seen. So union is real through the energies, while the essence remains
beyond all approach.

\subsection{The Gnostic Variant}

The Gnostic stream keeps the same structure but darkens the middle. At the
summit is the unknowable source (the \textbf{Monad}), also called the Depth
(\textbf{Bythos}), beyond name and being. From it unfolds the divine fullness
(the \textbf{Pleroma}), emanating in male-female pairs of \textbf{aeons}.

The youngest aeon, \textbf{Sophia}, oversteps. She desires to grasp the source
directly, or she acts alone outside the law of pairs. Her disordered act
produces a flaw, a formless offspring cast outside the fullness. That offspring
is an ignorant maker (the \textbf{Demiurge}, called \textbf{Yaldabaoth}) who does
not know the source above him. He fashions the material world and its ruling
\textbf{Archons} and declares there is no God beside him. A \textbf{divine
spark} of the true light is trapped in the human form. Salvation comes through
the knowledge (\textbf{gnosis}) that wakes the spark and guides its ascent past
the Archons back to the fullness.

\subsection{Comparison to the Base Models}

The structure is the same as the base models. A source beyond being overflows or
speaks the worlds into being, the worlds descend through ranked degrees, and
the soul climbs back to union. Boehme's Ungrund parallels the groundless first
principle, and his seven qualities parallel the staged unfolding seen elsewhere.
The Gnostic version is the sharpest statement of the go-out-and-return theme,
since here the going-out is a fall and the return is the central point. In every
telling the light goes out and the light returns.
