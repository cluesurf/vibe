\chapter{The Six Living Esoteric Traditions}

The two base models in this collection received the deepest treatment because they anchor the comparison. The six traditions gathered here are treated in solid depth but more briefly. Each is a living faith, still practiced by millions, and each carries a layered model of the cosmos and the self. This chapter and those that follow cover \textbf{Hinduism}, \textbf{Christianity}, \textbf{Sufism}, \textbf{Sikhism}, \textbf{Taoism}, and \textbf{Hermeticism}. Each holds an account of how the one becomes the many, how the self is structured, and how it returns to the source. Hinduism receives the most space of the six, because it holds several rival models of the ultimate side by side and so offers the widest field for comparison.

Each tradition is presented the same way. First comes a brief statement of what the tradition is. Then its \textbf{creation story in plain modern English}, with the proper names and technical vocabulary set aside, to give a clear picture before the terms arrive. The later parts add the native words, the structured systems, and the tables. The aim across all six is to show the shared pattern underneath. A source beyond being and non-being. A stirring that breaks the one into the many. A layered cosmos and a layered self. And a return of the many back to the one.
