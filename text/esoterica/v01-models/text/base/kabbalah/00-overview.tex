\chapter{The Kabbalah Model}

Kabbalah is one of the two systems treated here in the most detail, because it
sets out an unusually complete and explicit account of reality. Where many
traditions describe the ultimate only in broad terms, Kabbalah specifies each
stage. It names the source, the initial act that makes room for a finite world,
the structured order that emanates from the source, the failure that occurs early
in that order, and the process by which that failure is corrected. The human being
is assigned a defined role in that process. The tradition is carried by three
works, the \textbf{Sefer Yetzirah} (the Book of Formation), the \textbf{Zohar}
(the Book of Splendor), and the \textbf{Etz Chaim} (the Tree of Life), which
records the later Lurianic account of contraction, shattering, and repair.
