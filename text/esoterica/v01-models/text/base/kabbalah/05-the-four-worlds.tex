\section{The Four Worlds and the Chain of Descent}

The Tree of Life is not a single diagram. It \textbf{repeats across descending worlds}, each one a
denser garment of the one above. The standard set is four, the \textbf{ABYA} worlds. Above them stands
a fifth, \textbf{Adam Kadmon}, the primordial will, often counted separately and sometimes treated as a
transition beyond the four.

\subsection{The Four (or Five) Worlds}

\textbf{Emanation} (Atzilut) is pure divinity. The sefirot here are still one with Ein Sof. It is mapped
to the Yod of YHVH and to the soul level Chayah.

\textbf{Creation} (Beriah) is the throne world, the seat of mind, the home of the highest angels. It is
mapped to the first He and to the soul level Neshamah.

\textbf{Formation} (Yetzirah) is the world of angels and feeling. It is mapped to the Vav and to the
soul level Ruach.

\textbf{Action} (Assiah) is the lowest world, including the physical cosmos. It is mapped to the final
He and to the soul level Nefesh.

\textbf{Primordial Man} (Adam Kadmon) stands above all four. It is pure will and light before any
vessels. It is mapped to the tip of the Yod and to the root soul level Yechidah.

\subsection{Hishtalshelut, the Chain of Descent}

The \textbf{chain of descent} (Hishtalshelut) is the graded unfolding of the worlds, link by link,
from the highest to the lowest.

\begin{itemize}
 \item Each world holds its own \textbf{complete set of ten sefirot}. So the basic grid is four worlds
 times ten sefirot, forty sefirot across ABYA, or fifty if Adam Kadmon is counted.
 \item The \textbf{Malkhut of one world becomes the Keter of the next}. This interlock is how the
 worlds chain into one another. The Malkhut of Atzilut becomes the Keter of Beriah, and so on down.
 \item Each sefirah itself contains ten sub-sefirot, and the recursion continues without end. This is
 the \textbf{holographic} principle. Ten inside each one, palaces inside palaces.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hitlabshut, the Worlds as Garments}

\textbf{Enclothement} (Hitlabshut) is how the worlds become \textbf{nested garments}. Each lower world is a
denser covering over the light of the one above. The same divine light shines all the way down. It is
simply wrapped in thicker and thicker veils as it descends, growing more concealed and more particular
toward the bottom.

This is why the light is undimmed at the top and almost wholly hidden at the base. The descent does not
weaken the light. It only adds layers of garment over it.

\subsection{The Seven Heikhalot, the Palaces}

The world of \textbf{Beriah} is structured as \textbf{seven palaces} (heikhalot), nested one inside the
next, leading to the Throne. The mystic of the Heikhalot literature ascends through all seven to reach
the divine presence. The texts call this ascent a descent to the chariot.

The Zohar names seven palaces of holiness in Beriah, each tied to a sefirah. The lowest is the
\textbf{sapphire pavement} (Livnat ha-Sappir). The highest is the \textbf{Holy of Holies} (Kodesh
Kodashim), the Throne itself. The Holy of Holies as the summit is consistent across versions. The exact
names and sefirah links of the middle palaces vary between passages.
