\section{Cosmic Time and the World to Come}

Kabbalah gives time a shape. History is not an open-ended drift. It is a
structured span with a beginning, a labor, and a rest, and behind it some schools
see vast repeating cycles of worlds.

\subsection{The Shape of Cosmic Time}

The basic frame is a \textbf{week writ large}. The seven days of creation are
mapped onto seven millennia of history.

\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Six millennia of history}. The active span of the world, matching
 the six days of labor.
 \item \textbf{The seventh millennium of Shabbat}. A cosmic Sabbath, an age of
 rest and the messianic world.
\end{itemize}

This 6,000-plus-1,000 frame is widely held. The larger cycle-doctrine built on top
of it is far more contested.

\subsection{The Shemittot and the Great Yovel}

One Kabbalistic school extends the agricultural sabbatical laws into cosmology.
The doctrine comes from the \textbf{Book of the Image} (Sefer HaTemunah), a
work of the thirteenth to fourteenth century.

A \textbf{shemittah} is, in law, the seventh year when the land rests. In the
cosmic version each shemittah is a world-cycle of \textbf{7,000 years}, six
millennia of activity plus one of rest. A \textbf{yovel} (jubilee) is, in law, the
fiftieth year after seven cycles of seven. In the cosmic version the great yovel
is the span of seven shemittot.

\begin{longtable}{L{0.30\linewidth} L{0.20\linewidth} L{0.32\linewidth}}
\caption{The cosmic cycles of the Sefer HaTemunah.}\\
\toprule
\textbf{Cycle} & \textbf{Length} & \textbf{Made of} \\
\midrule
One shemittah & 7,000 years & six millennia of activity plus one of rest \\
Seven shemittot & 49,000 years & seven world-cycles in a row \\
The great yovel & 50,000 years & the seven cycles plus a final all-encompassing return \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

Each shemittah is governed by a different sefirah and has its own character, even
its own laws of nature. Some versions hold that we are now in the shemittah of
\textbf{severity} (Gevurah), which is why our world holds so much harshness. A
future cycle under a gentler sefirah would be a different kind of world entirely.
The picture is one of \textbf{many worlds}, one per cycle, each real, each running
its course and giving way to the next.

This doctrine is a \textbf{contested minority view}. It must be flagged honestly.
The Sefer HaTemunah and its followers affirm the cycles. The \textbf{Lurianic}
school and many later authorities reject or sharply downplay them, and some treated
the doctrine as mistaken or near to heresy. So the numbers 7,000, 49,000, and
50,000 belong to that specific tradition, not to the universal Jewish view.

\subsection{Eternal or Created in Time}

Beneath all of this sits a long-running debate. \textbf{Is the world eternal, or
created at a point in time?} The dominant Jewish position, against Aristotle, is
\textbf{creation in time}. The world had a beginning. It is not eternal.

The cycle-doctrines complicate this. If there are repeating shemittot, then this
world had a beginning, yet the cycling of worlds may stretch across immense spans.
The beginning becomes the start of a cycle, not of all reality. Even thinkers
committed to creation in time could hold that God made and unmade many worlds
before this one without conceding that the world is eternal. The cycle is created
and re-created, not beginningless.

\subsection{The Worlds God Built and Destroyed}

A famous teaching anchors the many-worlds intuition. \textbf{He was building
worlds and destroying them}, says the midrash, until He made this one and said,
\enquote{these please Me.} Before settling on this creation, God made others and
destroyed them.

Kabbalah ties this to the \textbf{kings of Edom} who reigned and died, read as the
shattered worlds of chaos that collapsed before our world was set in order, and to
the shemittot cycles, the worlds before and after our own. Evil itself, in this
frame, is not a rival power. It is broken structure, the leftover of a world that
could not hold.

\subsection{The Messianic Age, Resurrection, and the World to Come}

The end of the sixth millennium gives way to the final return.

\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{The messianic age}. The culmination of history, an era of peace,
 the knowledge of God, and the ingathering of the exiles.
 \item \textbf{The resurrection of the dead}. The doctrine that the dead will be
 restored to bodily life in the messianic future. It is woven into most
 accounts of the ultimate world.
 \item \textbf{The world to come} (olam haba). The final destination, the realm of
 ultimate reward and divine closeness. The term is used in more than one
 sense, the realm of souls after death for some, the resurrected world for
 others, and the schools do not fully agree.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The Shekhinah in Exile and Her Redemption}

The cosmic account has a relational core. The \textbf{Shekhinah} is the indwelling
divine presence, identified with the lowest sefirah and with the receiving,
feminine aspect of the divine. After the breaking of the vessels, the Shekhinah is
described as being \textbf{in exile} (galut), separated from the higher source,
fallen into the lower worlds, sharing in the suffering of the world.

The reversal is \textbf{redemption} (geulah). The redemption reunites the Shekhinah
with the higher source and ends the cosmic separation. The engine of this reunion
is human action. Every commandment done with intention raises a spark and draws
the exiled presence back toward union.

\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Exile}. The Shekhinah is fallen, separated, sharing the world's
 suffering.
 \item \textbf{The work}. Human deeds raise the scattered sparks and reunite
 above and below.
 \item \textbf{Redemption}. The presence is restored, the separation healed.
\end{itemize}

When the last spark is gathered and the last vessel mended, the exile ends. Evil
collapses for lack of borrowed light. The Shekhinah returns to union, and the
light of the Infinite shines with no broken shell to hide it. This is the point
where the cosmic cycles, the messianic age, the world to come, and the redemption
all converge. History was the long labor of the repair. Its completion is the
cosmic Shabbat.
