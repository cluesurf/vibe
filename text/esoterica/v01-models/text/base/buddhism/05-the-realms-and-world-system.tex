\section{The Realms and the World-System}

Samsara has a geography. Beings are reborn into stations that range from the deepest hell to the subtlest formless heaven. The map has two overlapping layers. One sorts existence into \textbf{three realms} and \textbf{thirty-one planes}. The other compresses it into the \textbf{six realms} of the wheel of life. Around all of this stands the physical cosmos, built on \textbf{Mount Meru}.

\subsection{The Three Realms and the Thirty-One Planes}

The whole field of rebirth divides into \textbf{three realms} (trailokya), ordered from coarse to subtle. Higher realms require deeper meditative attainment to reach.

\begin{longtable}{@{}
rL{2.8cm}L{8.4cm}@{}}
\toprule
\# & Realm & Gloss \\
\midrule
1 & Kamadhatu & Desire realm. Beings driven by sense-craving. Holds hells, animals, ghosts, humans, and the lower gods. \\
2 & Rupadhatu & Form realm. Subtle material gods of the four dhyanas. No sense-desire, but still embodied. \\
3 & Arupadhatu & Formless realm. Pure mind without form. The four formless attainments. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

Within these three realms sit \textbf{thirty-one planes} of existence. The desire realm holds eleven planes, the form realm holds sixteen, and the formless realm holds four.

\begin{longtable}{@{}

\toprule
Realm & Composition & Count \\
\midrule

Formless (Arupadhatu) & the four attainments & 4 \\

\end{longtable}

\subsection{The Six Realms}

The Tibetan and Mahayana tradition compresses the thirty-one planes into \textbf{six realms} (sadgati), the six classes of rebirth painted on the wheel of life. Each realm is driven by a root affliction and yields a characteristic suffering.

\begin{longtable}{@{}
rL{2.6cm}L{2.6cm}L{4.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
\# & Realm & Root affliction & Chief suffering \\
\midrule
1 & Gods (deva) & pride & the fall when merit runs out \\
2 & Demigods (asura) & jealousy & endless war over the wish-tree \\
3 & Humans (manusya) & desire & birth, aging, sickness, death \\
4 & Animals (tiryak) & delusion & predation, servitude, dullness \\
5 & Hungry ghosts (preta) & greed & unquenchable hunger and thirst \\
6 & Hell-beings (naraka) & hatred & extreme heat and cold \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

The first three realms are the \textbf{higher} or fortunate destinies. The last three are the \textbf{lower} or woeful destinies. The single god realm of the wheel expands into twenty-six separate deva planes in the full thirty-one-plane list.

\subsection{The Heavens}

The deva realm spreads across \textbf{twenty-six heavens}. Six lie in the desire realm, sixteen in the form realm, and four in the formless realm. The six \textbf{desire-realm heavens} run from Caturmaharajika on Meru's slopes up to Paranirmita-vasavartin, the home of Mara. Lifespans double up the tiers, and the day-length lengthens too, so the real spans grow steeply.

The sixteen \textbf{form-realm heavens} are set by the four dhyanas, three heavens for each of the first three dhyanas and seven for the fourth. The top five of these are the \textbf{Pure Abodes} (Suddhavasa), reachable only by non-returners. \textbf{Akanistha} is the highest form heaven of all.

The four \textbf{formless attainments} are planes of pure mind with no body and no location. They rise from the sphere of infinite space to the sphere of neither-perception-nor-non-perception. That fourth is the highest possible rebirth in all of samsara. Yet even there the being eventually falls. Only nirvana ends the cycle.

\subsection{The Hells}

The lowest of the six realms is \textbf{naraka}, hell. It is not eternal. A being stays only until the karma that sent it there runs out, then is reborn elsewhere.

There are \textbf{eight hot hells} stacked beneath Jambudvipa, ordered from least to most severe. They run from Sanjiva, the reviving hell, down to \textbf{Avici}, the unrelenting hell, the worst destiny in all of samsara, reserved for the gravest karmas. There are also \textbf{eight cold hells}, frozen plains swept by blizzards, named for what the cold does to the body, from Arbuda the blister hell down to Mahapadma, the great lotus, where the whole body cracks apart. Each hot hell is ringed by sixteen neighboring sub-hells, giving one hundred twenty-eight in all.

\subsection{The World-System of Mount Meru}

The physical cosmos is built around \textbf{Mount Meru} (Sumeru), the central axis-mountain. Around it lie \textbf{seven golden mountain ranges} and \textbf{seven seas}, then a great outer ocean. In that ocean float \textbf{four continents}, one at each face of Meru, each flanked by two subcontinents.

\begin{longtable}{@{}
L{1.8cm}L{2.8cm}L{2.2cm}L{4.0cm}@{}}
\toprule
Direction & Continent & Shape & Subcontinents \\
\midrule
South & Jambudvipa & triangular & Camara, Avaracamara \\
East & Purvavideha & crescent & Deha, Videha \\
West & Aparagodaniya & round & Satha, Uttaramantrina \\
North & Uttarakuru & square & Kurava, Kaurava \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\textbf{Jambudvipa}, the rose-apple continent in the south, is our world. It is the only continent where Buddhas appear. The whole world-system is enclosed by a circular wall of iron mountains, the \textbf{Cakravala}, which gives the world-system its name. A single sun and moon circle Meru's waist, producing day, night, and the seasons.

World-systems nest in powers of one thousand. A thousand of them make a small chiliocosm. A thousand of those make a medium dichiliocosm. A thousand of those make a \textbf{great trichiliocosm}, a system of \textbf{one billion worlds}. That billion-world system is the domain of a single Buddha. It forms and is destroyed together as one unit. Beyond it, the cosmos holds countless more such systems, endless in number.

\subsection{The Pure Lands}

Among the countless worlds are the \textbf{pure lands} or buddha-fields (Sanskrit buddhakshetra, Tibetan zhing khams). A pure land is a world-system shaped by a buddha's aspiration and merit, an ideal environment for practice. The most famous is \textbf{Sukhavati}, the Land of Bliss in the west, the pure land of \textbf{Amitabha}. Beings can aspire to be reborn there, where awakening is swift and sure. Others include \textbf{Abhirati}, the eastern pure land of Akshobhya, and the \textbf{Copper-Colored Mountain} of Padmasambhava in the Nyingma tradition. Pure lands are not heavens within one universe. They are entire world-systems among the many, each shaped by an awakened mind.
