\section{The Vajrayana and the Subtle Body}

Tibetan Buddhism adds a second, swifter vehicle on top of the bodhisattva path. This is the \textbf{diamond vehicle} (\textbf{Vajrayana}), the tantric path. Its signature move is to \textbf{take the fruit as the path}.

\subsection{Taking the fruit as the path}

The ordinary path treats buddhahood as a distant result, built up gradually over the grounds. The Vajrayana reverses the logic. Since all beings already have buddha-nature, the practitioner trains in the \textbf{form of a buddha now}. One visualizes oneself as a fully awakened deity, in a pure world, with awakened speech and mind, and so brings the result into the practice rather than waiting for it.

This is faster and more dangerous. It requires a qualified teacher, empowerment, and vows. The view, that one is already pure, is the whole engine.

\subsection{The five buddha families}

The core map of the Vajrayana is the \textbf{five buddha families} (pancakula). Reality, the self, and the awakened state are all sorted into five. The central doctrine is that the \textbf{five poisons are not destroyed but transmuted} into the five wisdoms. Anger becomes mirror-like wisdom, and so on.

\begin{longtable}{L{2.6cm} L{2.2cm} L{3.0cm} L{2.4cm} l l}
\caption{The five buddha families and their correspondences (Nyingma arrangement).}\\
\toprule
\textbf{Buddha} & \textbf{Wisdom} & \textbf{Poison} & \textbf{Aggregate} & \textbf{Element} & \textbf{Direction} \\
\midrule
Vairochana & dharmadhatu & ignorance & form & space & center \\
Akshobhya & mirror-like & anger & consciousness & water & east \\
Ratnasambhava & equality & pride & feeling & earth & south \\
Amitabha & discriminating & attachment & perception & fire & west \\
Amoghasiddhi & all-accomplishing & jealousy & formations & wind & north \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

Each family also carries a \textbf{color} (white, blue, yellow, red, green), a female buddha as co-equal consort, a seed syllable, and a throne. School traditions differ on some pairings (the form and consciousness aggregates, and the ignorance and anger poisons, are sometimes swapped).

\subsection{Deity, mantra, mandala}

Three tools structure tantric practice.

\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Deity} (yidam). A buddha-form, visualized in detail and identified with, until the practitioner experiences self and world as that pure form.
 \item \textbf{Mantra}. Sacred sound, the awakened speech of the deity, recited to transform ordinary speech and stabilize the visualization.
 \item \textbf{Mandala}. The sacred world, the pure palace and field of the deity, entered in imagination. Center plus four directions are the five families. The practitioner enters from a gate and travels to the center, which is realization.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The four classes of tantra}

The Sarma schools (Gelug, Kagyu, Sakya) grade the tantras into \textbf{four classes} by how much inner yoga they use.

\begin{longtable}{l L{8.0cm} }
\caption{The four classes of tantra in the Sarma system.}\\
\toprule
\textbf{Class} & \textbf{Emphasis} \\
\midrule
kriya & outer ritual, the deity as lord and you as servant \\
charya & balance of outer ritual and inner yoga, the deity as friend \\
yoga & inner yoga dominant, self-generation as the deity \\
anuttarayoga & highest, subtle-body yoga, full identity with the deity \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\textbf{Anuttarayoga} has two stages. The \textbf{generation stage}, building the deity and mandala in imagination, and the \textbf{completion stage}, working the actual subtle body. The Nyingma uses a six-class system whose summit is \textbf{Ati yoga}, that is \textbf{Dzogchen}.

\subsection{The subtle body}

The completion stage works the \textbf{subtle body}, the inner map of energy. Its principle is that \textbf{mind rides on wind}. Move the winds into the central channel and the mind shifts to subtle, blissful, clear states.

It has four parts. \textbf{Channels} (nadi), \textbf{winds} (vayu), \textbf{drops} (bindu), and the \textbf{wheels} (chakra) where the channels knot.

The \textbf{three main channels} run up the body. The \textbf{central channel} (avadhuti) is the goal. The \textbf{left} and \textbf{right} channels carry the ordinary, dualistic winds and wrap around the central channel at each wheel, forming knots that block it. Practice loosens the knots.

The \textbf{winds} split into five root winds (vital, downward-clearing, upward-moving, fire-accompanying, and pervading) and five branch winds governing the five senses. The \textbf{drops} are essences. The \textbf{white drop} from the father at the crown, the \textbf{red drop} from the mother at the navel, and the \textbf{indestructible drop} at the heart, which holds the very subtle wind and mind and is where the clear light dawns at death.

\subsection{The Six Yogas of Naropa}

The flagship completion-stage practices, central to the Kagyu school, are the \textbf{Six Yogas of Naropa}.

\begin{longtable}{l L{8.0cm} }
\caption{The Six Yogas of Naropa.}\\
\toprule
\textbf{Yoga} & \textbf{What it does} \\
\midrule
inner heat (tummo) & ignites navel heat, brings winds into the central channel, the foundation \\
illusory body & sees self and world as dream-stuff, builds the pure illusory body \\
dream yoga & lucid dreaming, recognized and trained \\
clear light & rest in the luminous nature of mind, even in deep sleep \\
bardo yoga & practice for the intermediate state after death \\
transference (phowa) & ejects consciousness at death through the crown \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\textbf{Tummo} is always the root. The other five depend on it. Clear light and bardo yoga are rehearsals for dying.

\subsection{Dzogchen and Mahamudra}

Above the effortful yogas, two traditions point directly at the nature of mind. \textbf{Dzogchen}, the Great Perfection, is the summit of the Nyingma. It treats the Ground as already pure and needs no construction. The practitioner rests in \textbf{pristine awareness} (\textbf{rigpa}), through trekcho (cutting through) and thogal (direct crossing). The \textbf{great seal} (\textbf{Mahamudra}) is the Kagyu summit, reached through the completion stage, pointing at ordinary mind as already awake. Both end the path not by building anything but by recognizing what was always present.
