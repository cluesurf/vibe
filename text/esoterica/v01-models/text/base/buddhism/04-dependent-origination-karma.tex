\section{Dependent Origination and Karma}

The Buddhist account of the world is a story of \textbf{causation}, not creation. Nothing arises on its own. Each thing comes to be because something else conditions it. This is \textbf{dependent origination} (pratityasamutpada), the causal base of the whole teaching. It explains how suffering arises and, read in reverse, how it ceases.

\subsection{Ignorance as the Root}

The chain begins with \textbf{ignorance} (avidya). Ignorance here is not a lack of facts. It is the failure to see the four truths and the failure to see that nothing has a fixed, independent self. From this misperception everything else follows. As long as ignorance holds, the wheel turns. When ignorance is cut, the wheel stops. Ignorance is the first link, and it is also the target of the path.

\subsection{The Twelve Links}

The chain has \textbf{twelve links} (nidanas). Each link conditions the next. The list runs from the root in ignorance down to aging and death, where the cycle restarts.

\begin{longtable}{@{}
rL{2.4cm}L{3.0cm}L{5.6cm}@{}}
\toprule
\# & Sanskrit & English & Gloss \\
\midrule
1 & avidya & ignorance & not seeing the four truths, the root \\
2 & samskara & formations & volitional, karmic constructions \\
3 & vijnana & consciousness & the resultant consciousness, links to rebirth \\
4 & nama-rupa & name-and-form & the mental and physical organism \\
5 & sadayatana & six sense-bases & the six faculties forming \\
6 & sparsa & contact & meeting of faculty, object, consciousness \\
7 & vedana & feeling & pleasant, unpleasant, or neutral tone \\
8 & trsna & craving & thirst for or against the feeling \\
9 & upadana & clinging & intensified craving, taking hold \\
10 & bhava & becoming & the karmic process of existence \\
11 & jati & birth & rebirth \\
12 & jara-marana & aging and death & with sorrow, lamentation, and pain \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

The chain is read two ways. Read \textbf{forward}, it shows how suffering arises. Ignorance conditions formations, formations condition consciousness, and so on down to aging and death. Read \textbf{reverse}, it shows how suffering ceases. When ignorance ends, formations end, and the whole chain unwinds.

The Tibetan tradition spreads the twelve links across \textbf{three lifetimes}. Links one and two are the causes laid down in a past life. Links three through seven are the results that ripen in this life. Links eight through ten are the causes built in this life. Links eleven and twelve are the results that will ripen in a future life. This is the standard reading of two sets of cause and effect across three lives. The twelve links are painted on the outer rim of the wheel of life.

\subsection{Karma and the Mindstream}

\textbf{Action} (\textbf{karma}, Sanskrit karman, Tibetan las) is the next idea. Intentional action plants imprints that ripen later as experience. Wholesome action ripens as happiness. Unwholesome action ripens as suffering. The defining factor is \textbf{intention} (cetana). The Buddha said it is intention that he calls karma.

Karma is not fate. It is not a reward handed down by a judge. There is no cosmic accountant. It is an impersonal causal law, like a seed and its fruit, operating on the stream of mind.

\subsection{Rebirth Without a Self}

This raises a hard puzzle. If there is no permanent self (anatman), what is reborn? The Tibetan answer is the \textbf{mindstream} (Sanskrit citta-santana, Tibetan sems rgyud). It is a continuum of moments of consciousness. Each moment conditions the next. No fixed self runs through it.

The traditional images make the point.

\begin{itemize}
 \item A \textbf{flame} lighting the next candle. The second flame is neither the same as the first nor wholly different.
 \item A \textbf{river}, continuous yet never the same water twice.
 \item \textbf{Waves} passing along, where the form continues but no fixed substance travels.
\end{itemize}

So rebirth is continuity, not the transmigration of a soul. Nothing solid passes from life to life. The karmic imprints carried by the mindstream condition the next moment, and at death they condition the next life. The continuity is real. The self inside it is not.

\subsection{The Four Noble Truths as the Frame}

The whole analysis sits inside the \textbf{four noble truths} (catvari arya satyani), the Buddha's first teaching at Sarnath. They are the frame that gives dependent origination its purpose.

\begin{enumerate}
 \item The truth of \textbf{suffering} (\textbf{dukkha}). Birth, aging, sickness, death, and the five aggregates of clinging are unsatisfactory.
 \item The truth of the \textbf{origin} (\textbf{samudaya}). Suffering arises from craving (trsna).
 \item The truth of \textbf{cessation} (\textbf{nirodha}). The ending of craving is nirvana.
 \item The truth of the \textbf{path} (\textbf{marga}). The noble eightfold path leads to cessation.
\end{enumerate}

The four truths and the twelve links describe the same machinery. The second truth points to craving, which is link eight. The first truth points to aging and death, the last links. The third and fourth truths are the chain run in reverse, the unwinding of the whole sequence. To see dependent origination clearly is to see the four truths. The Tibetan tradition expands each truth into four aspects, giving sixteen aspects in all, and stresses that the truths are realized in this fixed order.
