\section{The Path of Return}

The path is the engine run in reverse. Mind built the worlds by not recognizing itself. The path is the work of recognizing it. Everything in this section is a way of turning the twelve links of dependent origination backward, dismantling the ignorance at their head.

There is no outside savior. The work is done by the practitioner on the practitioner's own mind. What follows is the staged map of that work, from the rare chance to begin it, through the trainings, to the great vehicle's raising of the project to buddhahood for all beings.

\subsection{The precious human rebirth}

The path begins with a sober recognition. A human birth with the freedom and equipment to practice is rare. The Tibetan teaching counts \textbf{eighteen conditions} that make a life precious. Eight are \textbf{freedoms} (being free of states with no leisure to practice). Ten are \textbf{endowments} (richnesses one actually possesses).

The \textbf{eight freedoms} are freedom from eight unfree births. Four are non-human. Birth in a \textbf{hell realm} (too much suffering), as a \textbf{hungry ghost} (consumed by craving), as an \textbf{animal} (too dull), or as a \textbf{long-lived god} (too much pleasure, no urgency). Four are human. Birth as a \textbf{barbarian} where no teaching reaches, holding \textbf{wrong views}, in a \textbf{dark age} with no buddha, or with \textbf{impaired faculties}.

The \textbf{ten endowments} split into five personal and five circumstantial. The \textbf{five personal} are being born human, in a central land where the teaching exists, with sound faculties, not having committed the worst misdeeds, and having faith. The \textbf{five circumstantial} are that a buddha has appeared, has taught, the teaching still endures, has followers, and that there are kind supporters of practice.

The point is practical. One is meant to recognize that one has all eighteen \textbf{now}, see how rare this is, and not waste it.

\subsection{The path as reversing the twelve links}

The whole of cyclic existence runs on \textbf{ignorance}, the failure of mind to recognize its own empty and luminous nature. From ignorance the \textbf{twelve links} turn, from formations to consciousness to name-and-form to the senses to contact to feeling to craving to grasping to becoming to birth to aging-and-death, and around again.

The path attacks the head of this chain. Undo the ignorance and the rest of the chain has nothing to stand on. Every practice below is, in the end, a method for replacing ignorance with direct insight into how things actually are.

\subsection{The thirty-seven wings of awakening}

The classical list of the factors that lead to awakening is the \textbf{thirty-seven wings} (bodhipaksika dharma). The Buddha named these as the heart of his teaching. They fall into \textbf{seven groups} that sum to thirty-seven.

The \textbf{four foundations of mindfulness} are body, feelings, mind, and mental objects. The \textbf{four right efforts} are to prevent, abandon, arouse, and maintain (unwholesome states stopped, wholesome states grown). The \textbf{five faculties} are faith, energy, mindfulness, concentration, and wisdom. The \textbf{five powers} are the same five matured to the point where they cannot be overcome. The \textbf{seven factors of awakening} are mindfulness, investigation, energy, joy, tranquility, concentration, and equanimity. The eighth group is the \textbf{noble eightfold path}: right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, and concentration.

Many factors recur across the groups. The thirty-seven count list-positions, not unique items.

\subsection{Calm abiding joined to insight}

The meditative method has two wings. \textbf{Calm abiding} (shamatha) stabilizes the mind. \textbf{Insight} (vipashyana) sees into emptiness. Calm abiding without insight cannot liberate. Insight without calm abiding cannot hold.

Calm abiding develops through \textbf{nine stages} (the nine mental abidings), illustrated in the elephant thangka where the dark elephant of the mind whitens stage by stage.

\subsection{The Mahayana raising of the aim}

The great vehicle raises the goal. The aim is no longer one's own liberation but \textbf{buddhahood for the benefit of all beings}. The motive that carries this is the \textbf{awakening mind} (\textbf{bodhicitta}).

Bodhicitta has two levels. \textbf{Relative bodhicitta} is the compassionate wish to become a buddha for all beings. \textbf{Ultimate bodhicitta} is the direct realization of emptiness, wisdom inseparable from compassion. Relative bodhicitta itself divides into \textbf{aspiring} (the wish) and \textbf{engaging} (actually entering the practices).

\subsection{The five paths and ten grounds}

The whole journey is mapped as \textbf{five paths} and \textbf{ten grounds} (bhumis). The five paths are the macro-stages. The path of \textbf{accumulation} (gathering merit, generating bodhicitta), the path of \textbf{preparation} (growing insight into emptiness), the path of \textbf{seeing} (the first direct realization of emptiness), the path of \textbf{meditation} (deepening), and the path of \textbf{no-more-learning} (buddhahood).

The ten grounds ride on the paths. The \textbf{first ground} begins at the path of seeing, with the first direct cognition of emptiness. The \textbf{eighth ground} is the point of irreversibility where effort is no longer needed. Grounds two through ten span the path of meditation. Each ground perfects one of the ten perfections. The whole engine runs on bodhicitta and the two accumulations of merit and wisdom.
