\section{Aztec}

The Aztec, or Mexica, were the dominant people of central Mexico before the Spanish conquest. Their cosmology is known from codices and from accounts gathered after contact. It centers on a primordial duality, a series of world-ages each ending in catastrophe, and a present age sustained by sacrifice.

\subsection{The Creation Model}

From a single source that is both male and female together came four shaping powers, who set about ordering the world. Their early worlds were imperfect and each ended in ruin. In the first age a fierce predatory power ruled and devoured the giants who lived then. In the second a flowing wind power ruled and the people were swept away. In the third a rain power ruled and fire fell from the sky. In the fourth a water power ruled and a great flood washed everything away.

In the present, fifth age the powers gathered in darkness, and to bring light one of them had to throw himself into a fire and become the sun. A second followed and became the moon. To set the sun in motion the rest gave up their own lives, and their blood gave it the power to move across the sky. To make people for this age, one of the shaping powers went down into the place of death, gathered the bones of the earlier kind, and sprinkled them with his own blood, so that the present humanity arose.

\subsection{Key Motifs}

The dual primordial source is \textbf{Ometeotl}, sometimes split as the pair \textbf{Ometecuhtli} and \textbf{Omecihuatl}. The four shaping powers include the night-sky lord \textbf{Tezcatlipoca} and the feathered serpent \textbf{Quetzalcoatl}. The present sun is \textbf{Tonatiuh}. The lord of the dead is \textbf{Mictlantecuhtli}. The five world-ages are the five suns.

This collection notes that the Aztec account is the one source going out into the many through five successive ages, with order and humanity restored each time by an act of self-sacrifice and a returning to the place of death to recover the bones of the kind.
