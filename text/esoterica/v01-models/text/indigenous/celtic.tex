\section{Celtic}

The \textbf{Celtic} traditions of Ireland and Wales preserve no single canonical cosmogony. The pre-Christian Celts left no written scripture, and what survives was recorded centuries later by Christian monks, mainly in medieval Irish manuscripts. The material below is reconstructed from fragments, above all the \textbf{Lebor Gabala Erenn}, the Book of the Taking of Ireland, and the Welsh \textbf{Mabinogi}.

\subsection{The Creation Model}

There is no told beginning of the cosmos, only a sequence of arrivals into an already given world. The story is framed as successive waves of inhabitation, each a fresh claiming of the ground, each layer of being giving way to the next. From a generative mother source, the spirit of waters and land, springs a bright divine race, the ordered powers. They arrive veiled in mist, bringing their treasures and their skills, among them the principle of abundance and life, the principle of universal skill and light, and the principle of war and fate.

Against the bright powers stands a chaotic counter-race of the deep, the powers of blight and the formless sea. The two contend in decisive battle for command of the world. When a later, more mortal wave takes the surface, the bright powers do not die. They withdraw beneath the visible, into the hollow places, and become the inhabitants of a parallel timeless realm that touches our own through mounds, mist, and water. The final act is a separation of realms, the divine retreating into a permanent adjacent world rather than departing.

\subsection{Key Motifs}

The divine race is the \textbf{Tuatha De Danann}, the People of the Goddess \textbf{Danu}, who is known almost only by name. Their adversaries are the \textbf{Fomorians}, a monstrous race of sea and blight, met in the battles of \textbf{Mag Tuired}. After the mortal \textbf{Milesians} take the land, the divine powers withdraw into the \textbf{sidhe}, the hollow hills, and become the people of the \textbf{Otherworld}, called \textbf{Annwn} in Welsh tradition. The framing list of settling waves is the \textbf{Lebor Gabala Erenn}.

The Celtic material fits the shared pattern of the one going out into the many and returning only in a shifted form, since the bright powers come forth, divide into their many functions, and then return not to a source but into a hidden adjacent realm beneath the seen world.
