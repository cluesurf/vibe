\section{Tamil}

The Tamil tradition is the religious and poetic culture of the Tamil-speaking south of the Indian subcontinent. It is one of the oldest continuous literary cultures in the world. Its esoteric heart is \textbf{Tamil Shaivism}, the worship of the great god under the names \textbf{Shiva} and his son \textbf{Murugan}, together with the female power \textbf{Shakti}. Its deepest current is carried by the \textbf{Siddhars}, wandering adepts who were at once mystics, alchemists, and physicians. The foundational esoteric text is the \textbf{Tirumantiram} of the sage \textbf{Tirumular}, counted among both the sixty-three poet-saints and the eighteen Siddhars.

\subsection{The Creation Model}

The one reality is at once the still ground and the moving power. The ground is the unmoving witness. The power is the dance that brings the worlds out of him. These are not two beings. They are one being seen as rest and as motion.

The motion plays out as five acts performed forever. The power brings the worlds into being. It holds them. It draws them back. It veils itself so the worlds can seem separate. Then it shows itself again and frees the soul that was bound. The same divine presence that fills the high heaven also fills the land, the body, and the breath. To find it the soul does not travel outward. It turns inward and downward into its own body, which is held to be a temple of the whole.

\subsection{Key Motifs}

The Tirumantiram is arranged in over three thousand verses across nine books called \textbf{tantiram}. Its teaching binds yoga, the inner fire of \textbf{kundalini}, and the worship of Shiva into one path. The Siddhars sought eight powers, the \textbf{ashta siddhi}, and a deathless body through \textbf{kaya kalpa}. The older \textbf{Sangam} poetry mapped the divine onto the land itself through the five \textbf{tinai}, the inner landscapes, each a region, a mood, and a presiding god.

This tradition is a southern branch of the wider \textbf{Shaiva} and \textbf{Tantra} material treated elsewhere in this collection. It shares their picture of one source that goes out into the many worlds through its own power and draws them back, with the return won by turning inward to find that source already present in the land and the body.
