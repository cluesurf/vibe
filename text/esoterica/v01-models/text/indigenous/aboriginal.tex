\section{Aboriginal Australian}

The Aboriginal peoples of Australia hold the oldest continuous living cultures on earth. There is no single Aboriginal cosmology. Hundreds of distinct peoples, with distinct languages and distinct lands, each carry their own sacred traditions. These traditions are owned, restricted, and passed on with care, and much of their substance is not open to outsiders. What follows is a careful and general account of widely shared features only, and it should not be read as one unified system.

\subsection{The Creation Model}

In the formative time the land lay unshaped and quiet. Great ancestral beings rose from beneath the ground or came across the empty country. As they moved they made the world. Where they walked, hunted, fought, and rested, the hills, rivers, waterholes, and rocks took their present forms. They made the plants and animals, set down the law for how people must live, and gave each group its land and its language. When their work was done some of these beings sank back into the ground or turned into features of the country or rose into the sky.

The making is not finished and set behind us. The formative time is also present and ongoing, held in the land, in ritual, and in the law. The ancestral beings remain in the places they made, and people stay bound to them through ceremony and through care of the country.

\subsection{Key Motifs}

A widely found ancestral being is the \textbf{Rainbow Serpent}, a creator linked to water, rain, rivers, and waterholes, who shaped deep channels and ridges as it moved and who both gives life and brings danger. It is known by many names across peoples, such as \textbf{Ngalyod}, \textbf{Wagyl}, \textbf{Yurlunggur}, and \textbf{Wanampi}. The paths the ancestral beings traveled are remembered as \textbf{songlines}, sung tracks that record the journeys and let people read and cross the land. The whole formative reality is often rendered in English as the \textbf{Dreaming}, a term that covers the formative time, the law, and a person's own inherited share of it.

This shared pattern appears here as the going-out of formless ancestral powers across an unshaped land, dividing it into named places and ordered life, with the return held open in that the formative time never closed and the ancestors remain present in the country they made.
