\section{Shinto}

The \textbf{Shinto} tradition is the indigenous tradition of Japan. Its cosmogony comes mainly from the opening books of the \textbf{Kojiki} (712 CE) and the \textbf{Nihon Shoki} (720 CE). The world is filled with \textbf{kami}, the spirits or powers of things, and the account runs as an unbroken genealogy from the first heavenly powers down to the ruling house.

\subsection{The Creation Model}

When heaven and earth begin, the first powers come into being alone in the high plain of heaven, then conceal themselves. Two of the earliest carry the generative force of growth in their names. After them follow seven generations, ending in a primal pair, the inviting male and the inviting female. Standing on the floating bridge of heaven, the pair lower a jeweled spear into the brine below and stir it, and the drops that fall from the spear pile up into the first island.

Descending, the pair wed and bring forth the islands and many further powers. In bearing the power of fire the female is burned and dies, passing down into the land of the dead, so death enters the world. The male follows but flees from her corrupted form and cleanses himself in a river. From this purification the three great powers are born, a radiant source of light from one eye, a serene power of the night from the other, and a tempestuous power of storm from the nose. The light is given the high heavens, the night its cycles, and the storm the sea. When the storm power runs wild, the light withdraws into a rock cave and the world goes dark, until the assembled powers draw her out with a mirror and a dance. At last the light sends her descendant down to rule the land, carrying the sacred mirror, sword, and jewel, and the ruling line is traced unbroken to her.

\subsection{Key Motifs}

The world is animate with \textbf{kami}. The generative force of growth and becoming is \textbf{musubi}, carried in the names of the early powers. The primal pair are \textbf{Izanagi} and \textbf{Izanami}. The three great children are the sun (\textbf{Amaterasu}), the moon (\textbf{Tsukuyomi}), and the storm (\textbf{Susanoo}). The hiding of the sun in the rock cave is the \textbf{Ama-no-Iwato}. The descent of the heavenly grandchild \textbf{Ninigi} with the three regalia is the \textbf{tenson korin}, the founding of the imperial line.

The Shinto account fits the shared pattern of the one going out into the many, since the generative force of growth and the primal pair unfold into the islands, the great luminous powers, and the unbroken line that governs the world.
