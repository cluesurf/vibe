\section{Zoroastrian}

The Zoroastrian tradition arose in ancient Iran from the teaching attributed to the prophet Zarathustra. Its fullest cosmological account is the Middle Persian \textbf{Bundahishn}, the book of primal creation, which expands the older hymns of the \textbf{Gathas}. The system is a dualism. Good and evil begin apart, mix in the world, and are sorted again at the end, with good triumphant.

\subsection{The Creation Model}

In the beginning there were two, not one. On high in endless light dwelt the wise and wholly good lord. In the depth below in endless darkness dwelt the destructive and wholly malign spirit. Between them lay a void. The good lord knew of his adversary. The adversary at first did not know of him. The whole order runs on a fixed span of twelve thousand years, divided into four equal ages.

For the first age all things were held in a \textbf{spiritual, invisible state}, pure thought before substance. For the second age the good lord brought his creation down into a \textbf{material, bodily state}, knowing the assault was coming, so that the adversary could be drawn into matter and beaten there. In the third age the adversary broke through and mixed evil into every living thing, slaying the first animal and the first man, from whose seed the kinds and the first human pair arose. This present age is the \textbf{mixture}, where nothing material is purely good or purely evil. In the fourth and final age comes a savior, the dead are raised, a flood of molten metal purifies the world, and the adversary is rendered powerless. The world is then made perfect, deathless, and wholly good forever.

\subsection{Key Motifs}

The supreme good lord is \textbf{Ahura Mazda} (Ohrmazd), faced by the destructive \textbf{Angra Mainyu} (Ahriman). Creation passes from the \textbf{menog} state (spiritual, ideal) into the \textbf{getig} state (material, visible). The present age is \textbf{gumezishn}, the mixture, ending in \textbf{Frashokereti}, the making-wonderful. The first emanations are the \textbf{Amesha Spentas}, the bounteous immortals, each both a quality of the lord and the guardian of one creation.

This collection notes that the Zoroastrian order is the one going out into the many through a deliberate descent of ideal patterns into matter, and a returning at the end when the mixture is separated and creation is restored to its first perfection.
