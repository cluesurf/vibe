\section{Maya}

The Maya are an Indigenous people of southern Mexico and Central America with a long literate tradition. Their fullest surviving creation account is the \textbf{Popol Vuh}, the council book of the K'iche' Maya, recorded in the colonial era from older material. It tells of the shaping of the world, several failed attempts at humanity, and the deeds of a pair of hero twins in the underworld.

\subsection{The Creation Model}

Before anything existed there was only the still water and the empty sky, and within them the makers and shapers. By their word they raised the earth from the water and set the mountains in place. Then they tried to make beings who would speak and honor them.

Their first attempt formed creatures from mud, but these had no mind and dissolved. Their second attempt formed beings from wood, but these had no heart and no memory, so they were destroyed by flood and by the rage of the storm. At last the makers ground white and yellow grain and from this paste formed the first true people, who had mind and heart and could give thanks. Beneath the world lay the place of fear and death, ruled by its lords. A pair of twin brothers descended into it, outwitting its lords through cunning and overcoming death itself, and rose to set the sun and moon in the sky and bring order to the world above.

\subsection{Key Motifs}

The unknowable source is sometimes named \textbf{Hunab Ku}. The principal shaping powers are the wise creator \textbf{Itzamn\'a}, the feathered serpent \textbf{Kukulkan} (Gucumatz), and the storm power \textbf{Huracan}. In the Popol Vuh the framing pair are \textbf{Tepeu} and \textbf{Gucumatz}. The successful people are made of maize. The underworld is \textbf{Xibalba}, ruled by \textbf{Ah Puch}. The hero twins are \textbf{Hunahpu} and \textbf{Xbalanque}. Four \textbf{Bacabs} stand at the world's corners holding up the sky.

This collection notes that the Maya account is the one going out into the many through repeated shaping attempts, and a returning of order when the twins descend into death and rise again to fix the lights of the sky.
