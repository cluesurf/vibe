\section{The Ancient Cosmologies in Comparison}

The six ancient cosmologies in this part each use their own gods and images. Set side by side, they share a small set of recurring elements. This section lays them out together and then relates this anthropomorphic ancient layer to the more abstract esoteric systems treated elsewhere in this collection.

\subsection{The Shared Moves}

Read across the table and the same handful of steps appear in each tradition.

\textbf{The primordial water or void.} Every one of the six begins from an undivided state that is not nothing. It is formless potential. It is the \textbf{water} of Mesopotamia and Egypt, the \textbf{void or gap} of Greece and the Norse, the \textbf{shapeless mass} of Rome, and the \textbf{egg-chaos} of China. The world does not start from emptiness. It starts from a full but undifferentiated source.

\textbf{The separation of sky and earth.} The first real act of creation is a splitting. The joined whole is pulled into two and the two are held apart. Enlil parts An and Ki. Marduk halves Tiamat into the heavens above and the earth below. The air-god Shu lifts Nut off Geb. Pangu pushes the sky and earth apart and grows to keep them parted. The Greek and Norse worlds likewise come from a primal body or pair being divided. The making of \textbf{room} between two poles is the move that lets a world exist.

\textbf{The succession or council of gods.} Once the world has room, the gods come into order. Sometimes by \textbf{succession}, one ruling generation overthrowing the last, as in the Greek and Norse. Sometimes by \textbf{council and decree}, an assembly that fixes fates and ranks, as in Mesopotamia. The order is rarely given all at once. It is won, or assigned, or fought for.

\textbf{The making of humans to serve.} Humanity is shaped late and from humble material, usually \textbf{clay} or the body of a slain being. In Mesopotamia humans are made of clay and divine blood expressly to carry the gods' labor. In China they are shaped from clay to fill the empty earth. The ancient human is not the crown of creation. He is a worker, a worshipper, a latecomer made to keep the order fed and tended.

\textbf{The underworld.} The dead go down. There is a realm below, ruled by its own powers. Its character ranges from the joyless land of no return in Mesopotamia, through the dim shade-worlds of Greece and Rome, to the moral judgment of Egypt where conduct sorts the dead. The going-down is universal. The hope attached to it is what varies.

\subsection{From the Anthropomorphic to the Abstract}

The ancient cosmologies state the pattern in the language of \textbf{persons}. The source is a mother or a sleeping giant. The separation is a god prying two bodies apart. The order is a quarrel and a kingship. The abstract esoteric systems state the same pattern in the language of \textbf{principle}. The source is the Infinite, or Emptiness, or the unnameable Way. The separation is a contraction or a first distinction. The order is an emanation through graded levels.

The two are not rivals. They are the same structure at different levels of abstraction. Behind the chaos-mother slain into sky and earth stands the bare idea of one undivided source dividing into a polar two. Behind the council of gods stands the idea of a graded order unfolding from the source. The ancient layer dresses the structure in faces and names so it can be told and remembered. The esoteric layer removes the faces to show the structure plainly. Each ancient figure is a personified principle, and each abstract principle could be told again as a god.

\subsection{The Go-Out-and-Return}

Under all six runs the deep theme this collection traces everywhere. The \textbf{going-out} and the \textbf{return}. The one source goes out into the many. The undivided water or void differentiates into sky, earth, gods, and people. That is the going-out, present in every account.

The return is where the six diverge. The Norse world goes out into its full order and then \textbf{returns} through the world-ending into a renewed world, a complete cycle of out and back. The Egyptian sun goes out across the sky each day and \textbf{returns} through the dark below to be reborn each dawn, the cycle run daily. The Mesopotamian descent gives a half-return in the seasonal death and rising of the vegetation god, but for the human dead there is no return at all, only the land of no return. The Chinese pattern holds the going-out as its whole picture and leaves the return to the philosophical layer, where the ten thousand things settle back toward the Way.

So the ancient world already held the shape. One source, going out into the many, with the longing or the promise of a return. The traditions differ mainly in how far the return is granted, to the cosmos, to the seasons, or to the human soul.
