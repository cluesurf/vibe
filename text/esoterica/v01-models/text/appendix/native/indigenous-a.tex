\subsection{Zoroastrian}

In the beginning there were two. On high in endless light dwelt \textbf{Ahura Mazda} (Ohrmazd), wholly good. In the depth in endless darkness dwelt \textbf{Angra Mainyu} (Ahriman), wholly malign. The order runs twelve thousand years in four ages. For the first age all creation was held in the \textbf{menog} state, spiritual and invisible. For the second age Ohrmazd brought it into the \textbf{getig} state, material and bodily, so the assault could be met in matter. The first emanations were the \textbf{Amesha Spentas}, the bounteous immortals, each a quality of the lord and the guardian of one creation. In the third age Ahriman broke through and mixed evil into all life. This present age is \textbf{gumezishn}, the mixture, where nothing material is purely good. The fourth age brings \textbf{Frashokereti}, the making-wonderful, when a savior comes, the dead are raised, molten metal purifies the world, and Ahriman is rendered powerless. Creation is then made perfect and deathless forever.

\subsection{Maya}

Before anything there was only still water and empty sky, and within them the makers \textbf{Tepeu} and \textbf{Gucumatz}, with the wise \textbf{Itzamn\'a}, the feathered serpent \textbf{Kukulkan}, and the storm power \textbf{Huracan}. The unknowable source is named \textbf{Hunab Ku}. By their word they raised the earth from the water and set the mountains. Their first people, formed from mud, had no mind and dissolved. Their second people, formed from wood, had no heart and were destroyed by flood and storm. At last they ground white and yellow maize and from the paste formed the first true people, who had mind and heart and gave thanks. Beneath the world lay \textbf{Xibalba}, the place of fear and death, ruled by \textbf{Ah Puch}. The twin brothers \textbf{Hunahpu} and \textbf{Xbalanque} descended into it, outwitting its lords and overcoming death, then rose to set the sun and moon in the sky. Four \textbf{Bacabs} stand at the world's corners holding up the sky.

\subsection{Aztec}

From \textbf{Ometeotl}, the dual source that is male and female together, split as \textbf{Ometecuhtli} and \textbf{Omecihuatl}, came four shaping powers. Their early worlds were imperfect and each ended in ruin, the five world-ages or five suns. In the first sun \textbf{Tezcatlipoca} ruled and the giants were devoured. In the second \textbf{Quetzalcoatl} ruled and the people were swept away by wind. In the third \textbf{Tlaloc} ruled and fire fell from the sky. In the fourth \textbf{Chalchiuhtlicue} ruled and a great flood washed everything away. In the present fifth sun the powers gathered in darkness, and one threw himself into a fire to become the sun, \textbf{Tonatiuh}, while a second became the moon. To set the sun in motion the rest gave up their lives, and their blood gave it power to move. To make people, \textbf{Quetzalcoatl} went down to \textbf{Mictlantecuhtli}, lord of the dead, gathered the bones of the earlier kind, and sprinkled them with his own blood, so the present humanity arose.

\subsection{Navajo}

In the primordial darkness the first beings, \textbf{First Man} and \textbf{First Woman}, lived in the lowest underworld. As they climbed from world to world they changed and learned the proper ways, each world marking a stage and color. The trickster \textbf{Coyote} went with them, causing trouble but also shaping each world. When they reached the present world it was barren. The Holy People ordered it. \textbf{Talking God}, \textbf{Calling God}, and \textbf{Black God}, who set the stars, established the rituals. Then \textbf{Changing Woman} (Asdz\'a\'a N\'adleeh\'e) formed the first clans from her own skin and breathed life into them. The world was troubled by monsters. The twin sons of Changing Woman and \textbf{Sun Bearer}, \textbf{Monster Slayer} and \textbf{Born For Water}, set out with guidance from \textbf{Spider Woman} and defeated the monsters one by one. The animating wind \textbf{Nilch'i} was then breathed into all living things, giving life, thought, and speech.

\subsection{Zuni}

In the beginning there was only the all-containing maker \textbf{Awonawilona}, who shaped himself into the sun father \textbf{Yatoka}. From the primordial mist came the earth mother \textbf{Awitelin Tsita} and the sky father \textbf{Apoyan Ta'chu}, who together conceived all life in the fourfold womb of the world. The first living things were unfinished, webbed and scaled, dwelling in the dark deep. To bring them up, the sun father produced twin sons, \textbf{Ko'wituma} and \textbf{Wats'usi}, together the \textbf{Ahayuta}, who led the people up out of the lowest cave-womb through the layered worlds, \textbf{Anosin Tehuli}, \textbf{K'olin Tehuli}, \textbf{Awisho Tehuli}, and \textbf{Tepahaian Tehuli}, each lighter than the last, into the daylight. There the people were made whole, losing their scales and webbing. They set out to find \textbf{Itiwana}, the middle place, the destined center. During a river crossing some children were transformed into water creatures. After long wandering the people came at last to rest at the center.

\subsection{Hopi}

In the boundless void the creator \textbf{Taiowa} (Tawa), the sun spirit, first conceived the universe. He sent the executive sky power \textbf{Sotuknang} to give the worlds form, and that power, aided by the wise \textbf{Spider Grandmother} (Kokyangwuti), molded the first beings from the earth and brought them to life in the first world. The people of the first world failed to live in harmony, so it was destroyed and the worthy were led up to a second world, and again to a third when its people too fell out of order. Each time Spider Grandmother guided the way. At last they emerged into the fourth world, the present one. There they found \textbf{Masauwu} (Maasaw), guardian and keeper of death, earth, and fire, who taught them to live on the land and plant corn. The \textbf{Katsinam}, spirit beings of the ancestors and natural powers, came bringing rain and teaching through ceremony. The people divided and set out on long migrations, with one of the \textbf{Two Brothers}, Pahana, departing eastward under a promise to return. The trickster \textbf{Coyote} also went with them.

\subsection{Andean (Inca)}

In the beginning the world lies in darkness, with no sun and no day. The maker \textbf{Viracocha}, titled \textbf{Pachayachachic}, rises out of the great primordial lake, \textbf{Lake Titicaca}. This is not the first attempt. An earlier world and a first race had already been shaped and destroyed, by a flood that ended that age, the water-turning \textbf{unu pachakuti}. Beginning anew, the maker brings forth the sun, the moon, and the stars and sets their courses at \textbf{Tiwanaku}. He fashions a new humanity from stone and clay, shaping each people in its own form and sending them underground to emerge at appointed places, each its own \textbf{pacarina}. Having ordered the world, the maker walks among the peoples as a teacher, then departs westward across the sea, leaving the world running and promising return. The ages move through their turnings, each ended by a \textbf{pachakuti}.

\subsection{Celtic}

There is no told beginning, only a sequence of arrivals into an already given world, the settling waves listed in the \textbf{Lebor Gabala Erenn}. From a generative mother source, the goddess \textbf{Danu}, springs a bright divine race, the \textbf{Tuatha De Danann}, the People of the Goddess Danu. They arrive veiled in mist, bringing their treasures and their skills, among them the principle of abundance, the principle of universal skill and light, and the principle of war and fate. Against them stand the \textbf{Fomorians}, a monstrous counter-race of sea and blight. The two contend in the decisive battles of \textbf{Mag Tuired} for command of the world. When a later, more mortal wave, the \textbf{Milesians}, takes the surface, the bright powers do not die. They withdraw into the \textbf{sidhe}, the hollow hills, and become the people of the \textbf{Otherworld}, called \textbf{Annwn} in Welsh tradition, a parallel timeless realm that touches our own through mounds, mist, and water.

\subsection{Finnish}

In the beginning there is only air and the boundless primordial waters. \textbf{Ilmatar}, daughter of the air, also called \textbf{Luonnotar} and the \textbf{Water-Mother}, descends and floats upon the deep. Stirred by wind and wave, she becomes the carrier of the unborn world, suspended for ages on the formless sea. A waterfowl, a \textbf{scaup}, seeks a place to nest. Ilmatar raises a single point above the water, the first locus of form, and on it the bird lays its eggs. Heat builds, the point recoils, and the eggs shatter. From the broken shell the world differentiates by parts. The lower shell becomes the earth, the upper shell becomes the vault of the sky, the yolk becomes the sun, the white becomes the moon, and the scattered specks become the stars and clouds. Ilmatar then shapes the raw earth, drawing out coast and depth, and at last brings forth the first being, \textbf{Vainamoinen}, the aged singer whose power works through song. The sowing spirit \textbf{Sampsa Pellervoinen} clothes the bare ground in greenery. Creation is completed by the word.
