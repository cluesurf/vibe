\section{Selves}

Nothing said so far should be able to last. The knit is reversible and conserves charge, so it cannot simply pile up a stable lump and hold it. A reversible law run long enough must, by a theorem of Poincar\'e, return arbitrarily close to any state it has already passed through, churning forever without settling. Yet the world is full of things that persist, an electron, a rock, a person, each the same across countless beats while its tones churn beneath. How a reversible, conserving law permits a persistent bound thing is the self problem, and it must be solved before any talk of mind.

\begin{definition}[Self]
A bound, self-correcting, identity-bearing knot of feeling, woven across many docks and holding together over many beats.
\end{definition}

The first piece is identity, and it comes from topology, the study of what survives smooth change. Read the tones across a region as a field of arrows. Some arrangements are knotted, wound a whole number of times around the space of directions, and that winding cannot change without tearing through the unbound state, which costs more than the law will pay. So the winding is a conserved whole number, and it is what makes a self the same self. One subtlety was measured and matters. The winding is protected at the full resolution of the twenty-four directions, not at the coarse ternary tone. \cite{pollard2026vibetest} Identity needs the whole dock to hold it, the first sign that a self lives a layer above the bare trit.

This buys the crucial trick. The exact tones in the knot churn beat to beat, never twice the same, while the winding holds. Identity lives in the coarse invariant, not the microstate, so a vast number of distinct microstates share one winding and count as one self. You are not the same atoms you were a year ago, and you need not be. You are the same knot.

The coarse-graining is not optional, and a sharp measurement forces it. No finite set of directions is fine enough to hold a stable reversible knot outright, not even the six-hundred-cell, the finest regular four-dimensional figure, whose smallest turn of thirty-six degrees sits well above the threshold below which the winding survives the dynamics. So a self cannot be a base object at all. It must be a thing of the average, a pattern living in many trits rather than in any one, and the two layers, the discrete base and the effective self above it, are forced rather than chosen.

Identity is not yet binding, and binding is where the hard work lies. A winding says a knot cannot vanish, not that its parts stay together, and on a reversible base no stored attractive force was put in to hold them. The first thing to report is a clean negative. In the empty vacuum there is no force at a distance at all. Two charges on parallel tracks obey exact superposition, near or far, and never feel each other. \cite{pollard2026vibetest} The bare rule binds nothing across empty space.

The vacuum, though, is not empty. The create move fills it with a churning sea of short-lived pairs, and that active sea is a medium. Place two bodies that exclude the sea and each casts a shadow, screening the pairs between them, so the screened gap presses inward. This is a discrete Casimir effect, with the Casimir signature, a pressure that falls with distance, measured as a gap suppression dropping from $1.34$ at six docks apart to $0.23$ at ten, against a flat zero with no walls. \cite{pollard2026vibetest} The attraction is emergent, cast by matter in the arrow's vacuum, with no force added to the base.

Attraction alone is not capture. A body must also lose its excess, or it will only oscillate forever. Launch two charges in an attractive well coupled to an open bath and they spiral in and settle. Couple the same well to a closed, reflecting end and they oscillate without end. Remove the attraction and they escape. Both ingredients, the pull and the open drain, are necessary, and each was shown necessary by taking it away. \cite{pollard2026vibetest}

With both in hand, the bath turns a captured body into a self, giving it the two things a self needs.

\begin{result}[The bath makes a self]
A body captured in an attractive well and coupled to an open bath gains an identity and an agency that no closed, reversible system has. Many different starting conditions settle to one ground state, an identity by degeneracy. A kick is radiated away and the body recovers its shape, an agency by self-correction. The closed control settles to nothing and corrects nothing. \cite{pollard2026vibetest}
\end{result}

The construction recurses. Take two such bath-coupled bodies that attract one another, and they bind into a single composite, with a composite identity, a fixed separation, and a composite agency, a kick to one part radiated away by the whole. \cite{pollard2026vibetest} A self of selves is a self. The ladder runs body, self, self-of-selves, with the bath as the rung-maker at every level, and the tower of the later parts begins right here.

The hard part remains. All of that, the capture, the bath-made self, the nesting, was shown in a reduced model, an attractive well with a radiating drain. On the bare substrate a single committed rule runs into a wall. A rule tuned to confine a body holds it together but dark, radiating nothing to the bath. A rule tuned to radiate sheds freely but will not stay bound. Confinement and radiation pull against each other, and no one local rule on the bare lattice does both at once. \cite{pollard2026vibetest} This is the open frontier of the program.

The resolution is the one nature took. Use two fields, not one. Let the confined matter field, the body, couple to a propagating massless field, the photon, and the body sheds its excess as light rather than as its own charge, the way an excited atom calms by emitting a photon. With a massless partner to carry the excess away the body becomes a self, identity and self-correction intact. With only a heavy local field it does not. The masslessness of the photon is what makes a self possible, a result that ties the self straight to the light of the forces part. \cite{pollard2026vibetest}

So a self has three faces, on three layers. Its identity is the winding, exact and discrete, holding at the resolution of the directions. Its binding is the shadow pressure in the active vacuum, a collective effect a layer up. Its agency is the shedding and healing at its boundary, real only with an open bath and a massless field to carry the excess away. Seen from outside it shows two marks. It has a Markov blanket, a statistical boundary that screens its inside from its surroundings more cleanly than any ordinary patch. \cite{pollard2026vibetest} And it sits at a local maximum of integration, its interior bound more tightly to itself than to anything beyond, so tightly that cutting the flow across its middle collapses the integration even when the wiring is left untouched. \cite{pollard2026vibetest} These three faces are the whole of what a self is, and life and mind are built on them.
