\section{Life}

A self persists. A living self works to persist, holding its boundary against the wear of the bath, and the difference is upkeep. A bare knot drifts where the mesh takes it. A living knot spends effort to keep its shape, and that effort needs a source.

The source is the arrow. The growing edge keeps the mesh far from equilibrium, and a self that taps the flow can pump order into itself faster than it leaks, which is metabolism in the thinnest true sense. The pumping was measured. A self that balances its creation, lifting a tone into itself for each one it sheds to the ground, holds near-full fidelity over many beats, while an unmaintained self decays toward noise, its lifetime longer than bare diffusion but finite. This is the exact line between a rock that merely endures and a thing that pays to stay itself, and the cost is real, the books still balancing. A self does more than hold its shape, it tunes itself, settling its own activity toward a working set-point and holding there, the homeostasis that keeps a living thing poised rather than slack or running away.

A self can also heal. Punch a hole in a self surrounded by its own tissue and the rule alone draws the right tone back from the redundant surround, recovering most of the wound, while the same self with no surround does not recover at all, so first aid is emergent from the dynamics rather than added to it. And a self can reproduce, though not by splitting. On the hyperbolic bulk a body cannot pinch in two, since the geometry has no thin necks and a small ball is nearly all surface, so fission is suppressed and new selves are born fresh from peace at the frontier, inheriting a parent's pattern with a tunable rate of copy-error. The geometry itself decides that life here multiplies by seeding rather than dividing, and hands it heredity and variation for nothing.

Put heredity, variation, and the arrow's selection together and evolution runs on the base. A population that copies with error and is culled by the arrow climbs in mean fitness, beats neutral drift, re-adapts when its world changes, and even tunes how far ahead it looks to the hardness of its task. The Darwinian loop is not imported. What emerges is the shape of life, a bounded self-correcting pattern that feeds on a gradient, heals, copies, and evolves, while the full machinery of biology, and some of the self-organization, which leans on a cohesive fill that is flagged rather than hidden, stay past what is shown.
