\section{The Knit}

The beat was given as a rule on tones. The word rule is borrowed from the study of cellular automata, where it names the fixed update each cell undergoes every step, the transformation applied across the blocks of the lattice. Our name for it is the knit, chosen to feel more experiential, to hint at the merging and churning and turning it does, the bonding and blending and moving of the tones into one another at the lines of a dock, weaving the felt quality of each into its neighbor. It is worth walking through what that knit does to a moving thing, because the whole of momentum, mass, and spin is already sitting inside it, and seeing how is the clearest way into the physics that follows.

Start with the fact that the law looks at more than the present. The knit is built so that what a region does next depends on where its pattern just came from as well as where it is now, a rule with a memory of its last step. That one feature makes it a wave rather than a drift. A drift, a thing that consults only the present, spreads and blurs and slows as it goes, the way a drop of ink fans out in water. A wave keeps its shape and its heading and travels at a fixed speed. The memory of the previous step is what holds the heading, and a held heading is momentum. So the law carries its patterns ballistically, one dock per beat, with a direction built in, not by adding momentum as a separate ingredient but by being a wave to begin with.

The bookkeeping of that heading lives on the sites. Picture, on each dock, a tally of how much of its content is heading along each of its directions, one entry per site. A dock with all its weight on one direction is a thing moving definitely that way, a particle with a heading. The streaming half of the knit then does the plain thing. Each part of the content leaves along the direction it points and arrives at the neighbor that way, and there it goes on pointing the same way, so a straight mover steps one dock along and continues straight. That is a massless particle, running at the one-dock-per-beat limit, the lightcone speed, forever in a line.

Mass is what happens when the colliding half of the knit stirs those headings. If, before each step, a little of the forward weight is turned into the other directions, the particle no longer goes straight. It mostly advances but partly scatters sideways and back, so it zigzags, and its net speed along the line falls below the limit. The more the heading is stirred, the more it zigzags and the slower it goes. That slowing is mass. No stirring is massless and light-fast, strong stirring is heavy and slow, and the amount of stirring is the mass exactly. This is the discrete form of the Dirac equation, a straight runner turned into a massive particle by a fixed rule that mixes its directions a little each beat.

Two more things must be said. The base holds no fractions. A tone is exactly $-1$, $0$, or $+1$, so a dock can never carry a smooth blend of headings. The real directional state at the base is the discrete memory the knit already keeps, the tone now together with the tone just before, a small finite internal state that records which way the pattern is changing. The smooth version, a list of amplitudes stirred by a continuous operator with the mass as a mixing angle, is the coarse-grained picture a layer up, the same way a lattice gas of whole particles becomes a smooth fluid in the large. The straight-running discrete wave is the base fact. The continuous Dirac walk is what it becomes.

The second is where spin enters, and it is the reason the dock is a 24-cell. The headings are not merely a list, they are a space, and what matters is how that space turns when the dock turns. On a dock with twelve directions, the faces of a dodecahedron, the heading space is a plain twelve-way tally that carries no spinor. On the 24-cell dock the twenty-four headings are the $D_4$ root system, which is already a spinor space, so the way that directional state turns under the dock's rotations is the half-integer turning of a spinor. Spin is not added to the headings. On the right dock it is the headings themselves. That is the concrete reason the committed substrate is $\{3,4,3,4\}$ and not its twelve-direction cousin, and the next part makes the turning exact.
