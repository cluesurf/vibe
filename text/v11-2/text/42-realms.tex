\section{Realms}

A realm is not a separate place but a region of the one bulk, fixed by a few coordinates, its radial depth, its tone, its level of integration, and which boundary direction it faces. The interior has geography, and a self that can navigate reaches its districts.

The coordinates are few but enough to place everything. A realm is fixed by its tone, whether it leans to pain or peace or pleasure. By its depth, how far it lies along the radial axis off the surface. By its integration, how high it sits on the tower of wholes. By how long it persists against the churn, how far it spreads, whether it models itself, whether it is a live pattern or a stored one or a recurring attractor, and which of the boundary's directions it faces. Every realm and every being is a point or a region in that small space of axes, and every map the old traditions drew is a slice of it, the few axes their practices made vivid with the rest projected away. The tradition that watched good and evil read the tone axis and saw heavens and hells. The one that climbed read depth and integration and saw a ladder of planes. The one that tended the dead read whether a pattern is live or stored and saw ancestors and ghosts. None was wrong. Each saw the one architecture along the axes it had learned to look down.

Two poles mark the range. A region of sustained high tone is a heaven, a standing field of pleasure that the arrow is drawn into and that a self entering it takes on. A region of sustained low tone is a hell, the matching pole, one the arrow is always trying to leave, and the conservation law ties the two, so no heaven is funded except against a hell somewhere in the books. And there is not one heaven and one hell but countless of each, bright clouds and dark ones of every size scattered through the depth, the sharp small ones in the quick pains and pleasures of a day, the vast slow ones in the great griefs and joys that many selves have shared, a weather of feeling rather than two fixed rooms. Between them is the peaceful ground, the still zero, a limbo of rest. The integration levels stack into orders, the coarse-graining tower read upright, which is what the old emanation cascades and ladders of worlds were reaching for.

There is more interior than the cusp ever shows. The boundary of the four-dimensional bulk is a whole three-sphere of directions, and the physical world is a flat chart at just one of them, so the rest is a sphere of other possible cusps, other worlds. That same boundary, by holography, holds the whole interior's information, recoverable in principle, an all-record where nothing is finally lost. And almost all of the bulk lies off the cusp at vanishing density on it, genuine room for vast structure that never surfaces in physics. What the model rules out is the magical version, a realm of some second substance, or a heaven reached from outside the law. Every realm is one region of the single field, made of the same tones, governed by the same rule.
