\section{Relativity}

This is the hardest objection. A fixed deterministic lattice has a rest frame, the
frame in which the lattice sits still, and special relativity says there is no
such preferred frame. So a fixed-lattice theory looks ruled out from the start. The
objection is real, and we do not dismiss it. What the substrate shows so far is a
deformed, infrared-restored relativity that is measured at low order and
conjectured to be exact, with full Lorentz invariance left open and the
obstruction named.

\begin{finding}
The base carries a finite causal speed and a conserved momentum current with a
sharp speed of one (\texttt{E-RLT-0039}), and a doubly-special dispersion that bends with a
second invariant and recovers Einstein at low energy (\texttt{E-RLT-0010}), with the
Lorentz violation suppressed to high order (\texttt{E-RLT-0018}, \texttt{E-RLT-0023}). These are
Tier P, \textbf{Parallel}, reached by a discrete route rather than the
continuum's argument. Full exact Lorentz invariance on the fixed lattice is Tier
O, \textbf{Open}.
\end{finding}

The reply rests on two invariants. Ordinary relativity has one invariant everyone
agrees on, the speed of light. A doubly-special relativity has two, the speed of
light and a fixed length scale, here the lattice spacing. The claim is that all
observers agree on both, so the lattice spacing does not single out a frame any
more than the speed of light does. A second invariant is a deformation of
relativity, not a contradiction of it. This is what \texttt{E-RLT-0010} measures, a
dispersion with a second invariant that returns to the smooth relativistic one at
low energy.

The lattice hides at low energy because everything we measure is enormously
larger than the lattice spacing. At those scales the discreteness is averaged
over so completely that the dispersion, how energy depends on momentum, is the
smooth relativistic one to extraordinary precision. The grain shows only in how
the dispersion bends, and that bending is pushed to impossibly high energy. So in
the infrared the theory looks exactly Lorentz invariant. The load-bearing
conjecture behind this is symmetry restoration. The substrate's exact symmetry is
the finite group $F_4$, not the continuous rotation group, and the claim is that
under coarse-graining the discrete $F_4$ flows to a continuous symmetry, the way
a fine enough polygon is indistinguishable from a circle. That is a flow, not an
identity, and it is the open part of the rung.

What is shown and what is owed are kept separate. Shown, and firm where measured:
a ballistic light cone with a sharp speed of one, a doubly-special dispersion,
and a Lorentz violation suppressed to high order. Owed: exact Lorentz invariance
derived from the fixed lattice. The obstruction is well known. A fixed
deterministic lattice is standardly expected to pick a preferred frame, and the
causal-set program achieves exact Lorentz invariance only by random sprinkling,
the move the base forbids \cite{dowker2004}. A deterministic discrete rule that
can fail to be Lorentz invariant and can fail to look local once coarse-grained
is exactly the frontier the cellular-automaton interpretation names
\cite{thooft2016}.

The status is a deformed, infrared-restored relativity measured at low
order and conjectured to be exact, with a stated falsifier. There is precedent
for relativistic dynamics emerging from a local discrete rule. The Dirac equation
has been derived from a quantum cellular automaton along a doubly-special route,
the closest analogue to what the base would need
\cite{dariano2014}, and the iterant construction builds relativistic structure
from a discrete sign-alternation kin to the ternary tone and its reversible coin
\cite{kauffman1996}. The caveat is that those automata are quantum and unitary
while the base is classical, deterministic, and ternary, so the precedent points
the way without transferring directly. The falsifier is straightforward. If the lattice
leaves a permanent preferred frame at low order, precision astrophysics would
already have seen the Lorentz violation, and it has not \cite{cluesurf2026repo}.
