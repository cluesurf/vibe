\section{The Coin}

The substrate is not an arbitrary choice. The cell is forced, and
the same structure that forces it is the structure a four-dimensional lattice gas
needs to be isotropic. The $\{3,4,3,4\}$ honeycomb is one reflection group, a
five-node Coxeter path labeled $3,4,3,4$, and its subdiagram signatures force
three geometries from one object \cite{coxeter1973}. The finite end gives the
spherical 24-cell as the cell. The affine cubic end gives ordinary
three-dimensional space on the cusp horosphere. The bulk is the expanding
hyperbolic curvature between them. The cell's 24 directions are the $D_4$ root
system, and they arrive in four independent ways: as the two-step tone-words in
four slots, as the $D_4$ roots, as the 24 unit quaternions that form the binary
tetrahedral group, and as the four-dimensional kissing number \cite{conway2003}.
The binary tetrahedral group is the double cover, so spin is built into the
directions from the start.

\begin{finding}
The 24-cell is selected from two unrelated directions at once. The spinor test
requires 24 directions so that matter can carry spin without bolting it on, and
the isotropy requirement of a four-dimensional lattice gas independently requires
24 directions so that emergent flow has no preferred axis. \texttt{E-GMT-0026} confirms
the 24-direction coin carries the order-1152 isotropy while the cubic-coin
controls at 384 and 48 fail. Tier F, \textbf{Derived} for the base. Neither
requirement was tuned to the other.
\end{finding}

The spinor test is one question, walked through with as little machinery as
possible. A spinor is a thing that does not come home after one full turn. Rotate
it 360 degrees and it returns flipped, carrying a minus sign, and only a second
full turn brings it truly back. Everything ordinary, a scalar or a vector,
returns after one turn with a plain plus. Spin one-half is the odd case, and all
matter, every electron and quark, is spin one-half. So the test is just this.
Rotate the world once. Does the internal state come back as itself or as its own
negative. A cell's tone and its memory are scalars, and scalars do not move under
rotation, so they always come back plus. The only place a spinor could hide is
the set of directions, because directions do shuffle when you rotate.

For the 24 directions of $\{3,4,3,4\}$ the shuffles sort into three irreducible
families. The split is $24 = 8v + 8s + 8c$. The $8v$ is a vector, ordinary, plus
after one turn. The $8s$ and $8c$ are the two spinors of $\mathrm{Spin}(8)$, the
minus-after-one-turn families. This three-way split is triality, the unique
threefold symmetry only $D_4$ carries, and the 24 directions are exactly the
$D_4$ roots, so they carry it automatically \cite{baez2002octonions}. The
spinors are already sitting in the direction set.

The contrast with twelve directions is decisive. The $\{5,3,4\}$ honeycomb has 12
directions, and under the icosahedral group they split as $1 + 3 + 3' + 5$. Every
piece is whole-number spin and every piece comes back plus. There is no minus
anywhere. A plain reshuffle of twelve things cannot hold a spinor, because
spinors live only in the double cover, which a permutation can never reach. So
$\{5,3,4\}$ is a scalar substrate, and spin would have to be added by
hand. The 24 directions of $\{3,4,3,4\}$ are the $D_4$ roots whose cell-group is
the binary tetrahedral double cover, where a full turn already acts as minus one.
This was computed, and it is the single decisive difference.
Everything else tested came out equal for both honeycombs, so spin is the one
place they differ.

Now drop spin and ask a separate question. A coarse-grained lattice gas is only
physical if its emergent flow keeps no leftover preferred direction, and in four
dimensions that demands a specific symmetry. The classical lattice-gas literature
already found which one. The face-centered hypercubic lattice, with exactly 24
directions and symmetry of order 1152, is what four-dimensional hydrodynamics
needs to come out isotropic, and below that count the emergent flow keeps a grain
of the lattice \cite{frisch1986, dhumieres1986}. The 24-cell has exactly 24
directions and that same order-1152 symmetry. \texttt{E-GMT-0026} confirms the
24-direction coin is isotropic while the cubic controls at 384 and 48 fail.

Two independent requirements meet at one cell. The spinor test knows nothing about fluids. The
isotropy requirement knows nothing about spin. One demands 24 directions so that
matter can exist without bolting spin on, the other demands 24 directions so that
emergent flow has no preferred axis, and they arrive at the same cell from
opposite concerns. The cellular-automaton program independently predicts that a
deterministic discrete rule should underlie quantum behavior \cite{thooft2016},
the reversible-rule tradition grounds the rule on the same directions, and the
discrete route to relativistic dynamics has a clear precedent \cite{dariano2014}.
The substrate is the most over-determined choice in the theory
\cite{cluesurf2026repo}.
