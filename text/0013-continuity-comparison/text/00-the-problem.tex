\section{The Problem}

Programs that begin in very different places keep arriving at the same small set of objects. Gravity comes out as the thermodynamics of horizons and the entanglement of the vacuum \cite{jacobson1995, padmanabhan2010}. Spacetime geometry comes out as the pattern of entanglement in a quantum state \cite{vanraamsdonk2010}. Quantum theory comes out as the only information-processing structure that meets a short list of operational axioms \cite{chiribella2011, hardy2001}. Spacetime itself comes out as a discrete causal order \cite{bombelli1987}, and the dynamics of matter come out of a reversible local rule \cite{thooft2016, dariano2014}.

The conserved-current program of Chronoflux pushes this furthest \cite{herbert2025chronoflux}. There the whole of physics is forced from one quantity whose only law is that it is neither created nor destroyed. Inertia, relativity, gravity, the quantum, and cosmic expansion are read as faces of a single conserved flow obeying a continuity equation. The discipline is that conservation comes first and geometry comes second.

Each of these programs starts in the continuum and posits the principle it needs, a thermodynamic identity, a Hilbert space, a conserved current, a causal set, then shows that the rest of physics is the only consistent completion. The principle is the input. What is missing in each case is a base underneath the principle. Why a conserved current. Why this much entanglement. Why a finite count of states in a bounded region. Each program takes its principle as given rather than explaining where it comes from.

This paper works in the opposite direction, from the base upward. It starts from a single discrete substance with no continuum, no randomness, and no fitted constant, and asks whether the structures the top-down programs reach are forced from below. The base is one growing mesh of a ternary value moved by one reversible, charge-conserving rule. The conjecture is that its coarse-grained face is a conserved-current field theory of the kind those programs describe.

We do not claim to merge the programs or to finish the derivation. We position the discrete base against the continuum programs in one frame and show, one result at a time, where the base derives a continuum result, where it runs parallel to a program by a different route, and where the bridge is an open conjecture with a stated falsifying test. The spine is the continuity of one conserved value, which holds exactly on the lattice. Everything else is graded against it.
