\section{The Quantum}

The quantum layer is emergent, not part of the base. Raw ternary tones are not amplitudes.
The reconstruction programs set, from the top, a checklist any theory claiming to
be quantum must pass. Chiribella, D'Ariano, and Perinotti rebuilt quantum theory
from informational principles with purification at the center
\cite{chiribella2011, dariano2014}. Hardy reconstructed it from five reasonable
axioms \cite{hardy2001}. Masanes and Mueller derived it from device-independent
requirements \cite{masanes2011}. Spekkens showed which features are merely
epistemic and which are genuinely non-classical \cite{spekkens2007}. The task of
the base is to pass that checklist from a discrete deterministic substrate, and
to supply the microscopic states those selection theorems only assume.

\begin{finding}
A deterministic base with no injected randomness reproduces the quantum
scoreboard: the Born rule with the exponent $2$ \textbf{Forced} rather than
fitted, the classical CHSH bound $2$ and the Tsirelson bound $2\sqrt{2}$,
contextuality at the Peres-Mermin value $6$ against the noncontextual $4$,
Leggett-Garg at the quantum $3/2$ against the macrorealist $1$, the
Kochen-Specker structure, purification on the reversible rule (\texttt{E-QTM-0020}), and
walk complexity (\texttt{E-CMP-0013}). No amplitude, no imaginary unit, no probability
rule, and no Standard Model constant is fed in. The rung is Tier P,
\textbf{Parallel}. The single definite measurement outcome is Tier O,
\textbf{Open}.
\end{finding}

The surprising part is that the base is deterministic. The textbook intuition is that a deterministic
local model is capped at the classical bound of $2$, and that saturating the
Tsirelson value $2\sqrt{2}$ needs genuine quantum amplitudes. The base reaches
that quantum bound from a reversible deterministic permutation with no coin
flipped anywhere. It does not break Bell's theorem. It saturates the exact value
Bell's framework grants a quantum system while being deterministic underneath.
The price that keeps this consistent is that the base is not Bell-local. The
apparent randomness is ignorance over deterministic states, not fundamental
chance. That is why a deterministic base reaching a quantum scoreboard is a real
result and not a relabeling.

The distinction between reproduced and derived matters here. Reproduced means the emergent layer exhibits the
value, measured on the substrate, matching standard quantum mechanics without
being put in. Derived means the value is forced out of the base with no quantum
target supplied. Most of the scoreboard is reproduced. The Born exponent $2$ is
the one item that is forced, fixed by positivity rather than fitted. So these
behaviors emerge from a deterministic base, but they
have not each been derived from first principles.

On this base, a measurement is a system relaxing into a single state. The
measured system is a small body coupled to a huge environment. Left alone, it
genuinely holds more than one possibility at once, a true superposition. When
influences arrive the balance tips and the body settles deterministically into
one configuration. No special collapse law is
added. The only law running is the same reversible rule that runs everywhere,
now coupled to a large environment, and decoherence does the work: the overlap
between outcomes scatters across countless moving parts and can never be gathered
back. The pointer basis is set by how the subsystem couples to the rest during
the update, not chosen by hand. The squared weights then give proper Born
probabilities and the standard CHSH and Tsirelson values. The continuum version
of this story inserts a dissipation rate by hand. The base needs none.
Irreversibility emerges from a reversible base coupled to a large environment, and
the ever-growing wake supplies the one-way scattering on its own.

The base is superdeterministic in character, not Bell-local. Bell showed that no
theory can match the quantum correlations while keeping locality, realism, and
statistical independence at once. The base keeps determinism and realism and
drops statistical independence: on the mesh the measurement settings and the
measured system share a common deterministic past. This is not a conspiracy. The
correlation is structural, the ordinary condition of one connected causal history,
not a coincidence staged against the experimenter. This is Hossenfelder's
defense made concrete \cite{hossenfelder2020}. The base explains why the
correlations rise to the Tsirelson value and stop, without restoring local
realism. This is also the precise place where the base parts from Christian. The base
natively contains his geometric objects: the boundary three-sphere, the octonionic
seven-sphere, and the Clifford and spinor structure of the 24-cell. But it refutes
his claim that this geometry restores local realism \cite{christian2007}.

What stays Open is the single definite outcome. Reproducing the statistics does
not, by itself, show from the rule why one outcome occurs rather than another.
That derivation is still owed, and it has to come from the substrate on the real
mesh rather than a toy model. The stance carries its own falsifier. If a
sharpened Bell or contextuality argument ever shows that no deterministic base
can reproduce a quantum result the base claims, the determinism commitment fails.
