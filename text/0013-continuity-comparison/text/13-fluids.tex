\section{Fluids}

The base rule is literally a lattice gas. Collide rotates tone among a dock's head-on direction pairs and conserves the dock total, then stream slides each tone to the neighbor it points at. That is the exact shape of the discrete machine the classical literature built to recover fluid behavior at coarse grain \cite{frisch1986}. So hydrodynamics is expected from the substrate rather than an added assumption. The grade for this rung is \textbf{parallel}: the engine is the right one and one cosmological scalar is measured, while a full continuum fluid with a proven regularity bound is not claimed.

The lattice-gas lineage imposes a sharp geometric requirement. A coarse-grained lattice gas is only physical if its emergent flow keeps no leftover preferred direction, and in four dimensions that isotropy needs a specific symmetry. The face-centered hypercubic lattice gas of the classical work needs exactly the 24-direction coin whose symmetry group has order 1152 \cite{dhumieres1986}. That is vibe's 24-cell, selected on independent grounds. The structure a four-dimensional fluid needs to come out isotropic is the same structure the base already carries.

\begin{finding}
The coarse-grained tone-current carries a hydrodynamic gradient energy, and the wake is a measured expansion scalar. The Navier-Stokes rung is partial: the fine bound is exact and the coarse gradient steepens only transiently, with no sustained blow-up.
\end{finding}

The conserved tone-current is a density and a current at every dock, the same pair that obeys continuity. At coarse grain it carries a velocity-gradient energy, the quantity whose possible runaway growth is the open question for the continuum equations. Two experiments read this quantity at the two grains. \texttt{E-FLD-0009} shows an enstrophy-like quadratic conserved exactly by the reversible knit, the fine lattice bound that a continuum truncation forgets. The lossy control collapses it, so the bound is caused by reversible conservation and not by generic smoothing.

The coarse-grained result is reported as measured. \texttt{E-FLD-0010} measures the coarse velocity-gradient energy and finds it rises above its start by a transient steepening of about 11 percent, then recurs rather than running away. There is no sustained blow-up at either scale. The fine quadratic is held exactly and the coarse gradient only flexes and returns. This is a partial result: a Millennium-grade regularity proof is neither needed nor claimed, and the absence of a blow-up on the reversible lattice is itself the finding.

The cosmological scalar is firmer. \texttt{E-CSM-0043} measures the divergence of the current on the growing mesh and finds it equals the count of newly born cells at the edge, positive and growing with the shell counts, while it is integer-exact zero in the settled bulk. The static-mesh control shows no divergence, so the divergence is sourced only by the growth of the mesh. This moves the wake's role as an expansion term from an assertion to a measured continuity identity, a hydrodynamic expansion scalar at the boundary where the mesh grows.

What remains is the link to lab physics. The target for the coarse-graining is relativistic hydrodynamics built from one conserved current, the kind of emergent fluid the lattice-gas program was designed to reach. That explicit map, for a single named continuum field with its flow direction and stress, is not built here. Fluids stays parallel: the discrete engine matches the canonical one, the fine bound and the wake scalar are measured, and the continuum regularity is left open \cite{cluesurf2026repo}.
