\begin{abstract}
Many programs in fundamental physics reach the same structures from above. Gravity appears as the thermodynamics
of entanglement, quantum theory as an information-processing reconstruction, spacetime as a discrete causal order,
and in the conserved-current approach all of physics follows from one quantity whose only law is that
it is neither created nor destroyed. This paper works from the bottom instead. The base is a single growing mesh of one substance
carrying a ternary value in $\{-1,0,+1\}$, moved by one local reversible charge-conserving rule, on the
four-dimensional hyperbolic honeycomb $\{3,4,3,4\}$ whose cells are 24-cells. A conserved current is built on it
directly, and the central conjecture is that its coarse-grained form is a conserved-current field theory of the
kind the continuum programs describe. We test this one result at a time and grade each as derived on the substrate,
parallel by a different route, or open with a stated obstruction. The firm-tier results are exact and integer-checked.
Charge conservation is the continuity law $\nabla\!\cdot J = 0$ on both a flat torus and the curved $\{3,4,3,4\}$
mesh, the 24-cell direction set is fixed by a spinor test and supplies the isotropy a four-dimensional lattice gas
requires, and one recoverability functional ties horizons, decoherence, and conservation together. The middle
rungs run parallel, reaching a finite causal speed, entanglement that builds geometry, the Born rule and the
Tsirelson bound from a deterministic base, and the wake as a measured expansion scalar. The open frontiers are
stated with their tests. Exact Lorentz invariance on a fixed lattice, three chiral generations, the single
measurement outcome, and a full field theory whose stress tensor sources curvature all remain unsolved. The whole
comparison rests on a reproducible suite of deterministic, controlled experiments written as fully open source software
\cite{cluesurf2026repo}. This is exploratory, feasibility-stage work.
\end{abstract}
