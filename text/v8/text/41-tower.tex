\section{The Tower of Selves}

Higher vibes are crowds read as units. But the process does not stop at one level. A self can be made of selves, which are made of selves, in a tower that runs from a single quivering vibe up to, in principle, the whole. This chapter climbs the tower, and answers the questions it immediately raises: does this go on forever, is everything a self, and do the levels fight each other.

\subsection{The body as the clear case}

Start with the cleanest example, your body. A cell is a self, an integrated crowd of molecules. Many cells make a tissue, which is a self of cells. Tissues make organs, organs make a body, and the body is a single self at the top of that stack. The simulation builds exactly this: a structure where small integrated wholes group into larger integrated wholes, level after level, thinning out by a roughly constant factor each time, until a single top self remains, with the same higher-level rule holding at every rung. Cells in tissues in organs in a body, one self made of selves made of selves.

So the tower is real and the body is its plain illustration. You are not a single vibe and not a mere heap of cells. You are the top self of a tower, a pattern of patterns of patterns.

\subsection{Does my cell have its own experience, separate from mine?}

If a cell is a self, does it have its own little inner life, and if so, is there a crowd of tiny experiencers trapped inside you, separate from the you that is reading this?

The theory's answer is that the levels are not separate experiencers stacked in a box. They are the same substance viewed at different grains. Your cell, viewed at its own scale, is an integrated self. You, viewed at your scale, are an integrated self made of those. There are not two competing experiences, the cell's and yours, sitting side by side. There is one field of feeling that is organized at several scales at once, the way a sentence is organized into words and letters at once without the letters being separate little sentences. Whether each level is a separate locus of felt experience, or whether feeling is concentrated at the most integrated level, is exactly the deep open question we keep flagging, and the walkthrough will not pretend to settle it. What the theory does say is that the structure is nested, not that the inner lives are boxed inside each other like prisoners.

\subsection{Do the levels fight?}

The second natural worry: if my cells are selves with their own tendencies, do they pull against the body-self? Sometimes, and the theory makes this precise. A small disturbance inside a cell gets pulled back into line by the body's overall pattern, the wound heals, the cell stays a team player. But if a whole cohesive part flips strongly enough, its own internal integration can make the new state stick, and it stops obeying the larger self, going its own way. That is the picture behind a cell that keeps its rogue identity, like a cancer. The crossover, healed versus runaway, is set by the same integration dial from the last chapter. So the levels usually cooperate, and the conditions under which a part breaks away are exactly specified, not hand-waved.

\subsection{How high does the tower go?}

If cells make bodies, do bodies make higher selves, a colony, a society, a planet, the whole universe? Structurally, the tower can keep going wherever integration is high enough. A beehive or an ant colony is genuinely more integrated than a random pile of insects, and the theory would treat it as a partial higher self. A nation, a market, an ecosystem are looser, less integrated, so they are weaker selves, real as structures but not unified in the way a body is. The honest position is that height is a matter of degree, set by how integrated each level actually is, and most large human groupings are only weakly integrated. The theory does not declare that your country is conscious. It gives you a dial, integration, and says: measure it, and that tells you how much of a self anything is. The top of the tower, the whole field at its fullest integration, is a limit we will treat carefully in the chapter on God.

\textbf{Takeaway:} selves stack into a tower, cells in tissues in organs in a body, the same nested rule at every rung. The levels are one substance at different grains, not experiencers boxed inside each other. They usually cooperate, with a precise integration threshold for when a part breaks away. And how high the tower goes is a matter of degree, set by how integrated each grouping actually is.
