\section{Motion in 3d}
\label{sec:motion}

\subsection{Every vibe is a lineon}

The line law makes every vibe mobile only along its line, and composites inherit it exactly. Composites on parallel
lines are independent (\code{E-SPN-0117}). A pair bound across crossing lines is held at the crossing whatever its
string does (\code{E-SPN-0116}).

\begin{theorem}[The star]
A composite whose lines all pass through one dock is pinned there at every order, because the only line-changing piece
acts at that dock alone (\code{E-SPN-0118}, \code{E-SPN-0119}).
\end{theorem}

Two such hubs set off a cascade through the vacuum, and binding them with a string does not stop it, since a string
that is a phase moves nothing (\code{E-SPN-0120}). No fused mover exists (\code{E-SPN-0125}), and the bulk's shadow
adds no motion (\code{E-SPN-0127}). The six other routes tried, from borrowing background matter to a vacuum of
mobile line pairs, each fail with a stated cause (\code{E-SPN-0121} to \code{E-SPN-0137}).

\subsection{Line-only motion is excluded by observation}

Averaged over line classes, a lineon's band is isotropic through $P^4$ by the 5-design, but its first
direction-dependent term is a series in $P/Mc$, not in $Pa$, so the anisotropy stays at every dock size. Its inertia is
4.02 times its rest energy, so its effective 3d speed is $c/2$, and a massless excitation's speed depends on direction
by 20\%: $0.354c$ along an axis, $0.417c$ along a face diagonal, with velocity up to $36^\circ$ off momentum. The
laboratory isotropy of $c$ holds to about $10^{-18}$, so line-only motion is excluded by some 17 orders of magnitude,
and a beam would spread sideways as fast as it moves forward (\code{E-SPN-0126}). The rule must change.

\subsection{A candidate change}

The change has four parts, each forced by a failure of the one before.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A filled love sea as the vacuum.} Any vacuum of line pairs that matter can unpair keeps the cascade's
source (\code{E-SPN-0137}). A sea full at every dock has nothing to unpair, and a hole in it is the particle.
\item \textbf{A fermionic mixer over all 24 slots of a dock.} On a full dock it is exactly blocked by Pauli exclusion,
so the sea stays one branch for 128 beats and the collision never fires (\code{E-SPN-0140}). A mixer over one frame's
8 slots confines velocity to that frame, reaching $0.5c$ elsewhere (\code{E-SPN-0136}). The 24-slot mixer's hop is
$(e^{i\theta} - 1)/24$, so it adds one number, $1/3$.
\item \textbf{The swap coin} ($\zeta = -1$: keep 0, cross 1, integer entries). Every other coin lets a lineon or the
massless pair outrun the rest: a lineon tops out at $\cos(\varphi/2)c$ and the massless pair at $c/2 + 2\gamma K$,
$\gamma = \cot(\varphi/2)/8$ (\code{E-SPN-0143}).
\item \textbf{The prime 7.} A norm-one unit of $\mathbb{Z}[\omega][1/6]$ is a sixth root of unity (Hilbert 90), which
pins the mixer's angle and leaves the lightest particle at $R = 1.1027$. Inverting $7 = N(3 + \omega)$ admits
$u = (8 + 5\omega)/7$, whose angle is an irrational multiple of $\pi$, so the mass is tunable. The ring becomes
$\mathbb{Z}[\omega][1/42]$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What it gives}

\begin{theorem}[The 24-cell bound]
Under any dock-local mixer a branch's velocity is the average of the hops it takes (Hellmann--Feynman), so it lies
inside the 24-cell of the roots. The best isotropic speed is $c/\sqrt2$, and a singlet spread over all 24 roots moves
at exactly $c/2$, the rms of $u \cdot r$ over a 4d 2-design (\code{E-SPN-0140}, \code{E-SPN-0142}).
\end{theorem}

The hole's uniform (singlet) level sits alone at the band edge for every angle past the working one. Its inverse mass
is isotropic to $3.7 \times 10^{-10}$ with its first warp at $K^6$, as the 5-design forces, and its dispersion is
relativistic with $c_\text{eff}^2 = m \cot(m)/2$ and inertia over rest energy $R = \tan(m)/m$, 1.0007 at $m = 0.047$
(\code{E-SPN-0142}, \code{E-SPN-0143}). Under the swap coin 22 of 24 bands are exactly flat and only the singlet
pair moves, with $\sin w = \sin(\theta/2)\, g(K)$, $g$ the mean of $\cos(K \cdot r)$ over the roots. So $\gamma = 0$
exactly (measured $7.6 \times 10^{-12}$), and $|v| \le \sin(\theta/2)\, c/2$ at every $K$ by Cauchy--Schwarz on the
2-design. Every excitation shares one limiting speed $c^* = c/2$, and nothing exceeds it at any of 4,096 momenta.

Built into the exact many-body rule, the one-hole matrix equals the float model to $4.9 \times 10^{-15}$. The sea stays
one branch for 128 beats. Interaction frees nothing faster than $c^*$: two holes meeting release frozen weight only as
free holes, and a fear, unmade at once into a store and two holes, walks one root every two beats. A hole's Born weight
equals an empty-mesh love's at every slot, so gravity's sign holds (\code{E-SPN-0145}). The graviton's slide
(\code{E-GRV-0141}) enters $c$ only as a number mod $p$, and at the residue for $1/2$ its speed equals the slide's, so
matter and gravity can share $c^*$.

What $c^*$ costs: the stream's one root a beat becomes a microscopic bound that only 8\% of a hole's weight reaches,
not a physical speed. The husk light has not been rerun on this rule.
