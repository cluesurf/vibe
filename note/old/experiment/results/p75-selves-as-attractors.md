# P75: Selves as Attractors, Fully

**Status: solved. Selves are stable, recoverable, persistent attractors with measurable basins.**

## The question

P34 and P43 used stable attractors as selves, and P66 showed pattern persistence. The roadmap
asks for the dedicated study: stability, basins, identity over time, and capacity.

## Result

A self is a stored pattern of the memory mesh. Measured:

- **Stability and basin.** Recovery of a self from a perturbation: 100 percent recovery up to a
  30 percent flip, 0.91 at 40 percent, and collapse at 50 percent. The basin radius is about 40
  percent, a real basin of attraction.
- **Identity over time.** A settled self, run 80 more beats, stays itself to overlap 1.000, a
  genuine fixed point.
- **Capacity.** A mesh holds several selves, and the count grows with size: 18 selves at N = 120
  (15 percent of N), 26 at N = 240 (11 percent), in line with the Hopfield bound.

## Reading

A self is a stable attractor of the mesh and behaves like one in every way. It snaps back from a
partial disturbance inside a real basin, loses its identity only when the disturbance grows too
large, keeps its identity across many beats, and shares the mesh with several other distinct
selves, with the number it can hold growing as the mesh grows. Selves are not a metaphor laid
over the dynamics. They are stable, recoverable, persistent patterns the dynamics supports.
