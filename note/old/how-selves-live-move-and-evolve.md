# How Selves Live, Move, Grow, Die, and Evolve

How does a higher self, an organism or a mind, move through space, grow, live, die, and evolve,
when the substrate is a fixed relational mesh of stationary vibes? This note maps it out. The
whole thing turns on one reframe, and the rest follows cleanly from results already in the
testbed. It is a brainstorm and a working specification together.

## The one reframe: a self is a pattern, not its vibes

A higher self (a cell, a body, a mind) is not a fixed lump of particular vibes. It is a
**pattern**, a self-maintaining shape of tones that reconstitutes itself beat after beat
(P34, P57 to P60). The vibes it is made of can change while the pattern stays the same, exactly
as a whirlpool keeps its form while the water flows through, or a wave keeps its shape while the
water only bobs in place. Once you see a self as a pattern rather than as its material, motion,
life, growth, death, and evolution all become things that happen to patterns, and each has a
clean answer.

## Motion: selves glide, the substrate stays still

The vibes do not move. They have no location to move in (space is the shape of their relations,
not a container, see the growing-mesh note). So how does an organism cross a room?

**A self moves by re-instantiating its pattern on neighboring vibes, beat after beat.** The
pattern translates across the stationary mesh while the vibes themselves stay put. This is
exactly a glider in a cellular automaton, or a soliton, or a wave: the form travels, the medium
does not. Position is therefore emergent and dynamic, it is simply where the pattern currently
sits, and it changes as the pattern propagates.

Three consequences follow for free:

- **A speed limit.** A pattern can only re-form as fast as the rule carries influence to the
  next vibes, which is the light-cone (P1, P37). So nothing can move faster than the propagation
  speed of the rule. The speed of light is just the top speed a pattern can glide.
- **Inertia and momentum.** A pattern that is propagating in a direction tends to keep
  propagating that way (the re-formation is biased forward), which is inertia. Its momentum is
  the conserved tendency of that motion, and a moving massive pattern obeys the relativistic
  energy-momentum relation already found for matter (P14).
- **Minds ride their bodies.** A mind is the high-level pattern of a body-region (P60), so it
  moves where the body-pattern moves. The mind does not travel on its own through space. (Direct
  mind-to-mind influence without motion is a different thing, a field coupling, see the
  contact-through-the-field note.)

## Life: a pattern that holds itself open and repairs

A rock is a stable pattern too, but it is not alive. What is the difference?

A **living self is a pattern that actively maintains itself**, with three marks:

- **It keeps a boundary.** It screens itself off from the rest, a clean Markov blanket, which
  the integration measure detects (P63). There is an inside and an outside.
- **It repairs perturbations.** Disturb it a little and it heals back to itself (homeostasis,
  P59). A rock, eroded, does not repair, it just degrades. A living self pushes back.
- **It stays far from rest, open to the world.** It must keep updating, taking in and putting
  out, to hold its form against decay. It is not a frozen equilibrium but a dynamic one,
  maintained by ongoing exchange (a flame, not an ash).

So life is a regime of self: a boundary-keeping, self-repairing, far-from-equilibrium integrated
pattern (high integration, P63, with active homeostasis, P59). Inert matter is the passive end
of the same spectrum, a stable pattern that does not maintain itself.

## Growth: recruiting vibes and adding levels

A self grows two ways, and both are already in the model:

- **Outward**, by recruiting neighboring vibes into its pattern, enlarging the region it spans
  and extending its boundary. The integrated whole gets bigger.
- **Upward**, by adding internal structure, more sub-selves nested inside it (a body growing by
  adding cells, then tissues), climbing the tower (P59, P60).

The universe itself grows at its frontier (P56), and a self grows by claiming and organizing
more of the substrate into its self-maintaining pattern. Growth is the pattern enlarging its
domain and deepening its hierarchy.

## Death: when self-maintenance fails

A self dies when its **integration falls below the threshold** that lets it reconstitute (P59,
P60). The repair can no longer keep up with the disturbance, the boundary fails, the pattern
can no longer hold itself, and it disperses. Crucially, the vibes are not destroyed (the
substance is conserved, monism), only the pattern ends. The material returns to the field, free
to be taken up into other patterns. Death is the dissolution of a pattern, not of its substance,
which is also why a pattern could in principle reconstitute later (pattern persistence, the
reincarnation question, a separate result).

## Evolution: selection for patterns that persist and copy

Why does the world fill with ever more complex, more capable selves? Because the substrate
naturally selects for them, by ordinary variation and selection at the level of patterns:

- **Variation.** Selves differ, in their patterns, their urges, their choices (P43). New
  variants keep arising as patterns form and recombine.
- **Selection.** A pattern that is better at maintaining itself (more persistent) and at
  copying itself onto fresh vibes (more reproductive) lasts longer and spreads, while fragile or
  sterile patterns vanish. The mesh is finite at any moment and growing, so patterns effectively
  compete for substrate, and the persistent self-replicators win.
- **Heredity.** Reproduction is a self copying its pattern (or its generative recipe) onto a new
  region of the mesh, so offspring inherit the parent's form.

The result is a Darwinian ratchet running on patterns: differential persistence and replication
drive the substrate to fill with patterns that are increasingly good at holding themselves
together and copying themselves, which means increasingly integrated, self-maintaining, higher
selves, climbing the tower (P60) over cosmic time. Evolution is the model's answer to why
complexity rises: in an ever-growing substrate, the most persistent self-replicating patterns
are selected, and life and mind are simply the most persistent patterns there are.

## The whole thing in one frame

> A higher self is a pattern on a still relational substrate. It MOVES by gliding (re-forming on
> the next vibes, capped at the speed of light). It LIVES by keeping a boundary and repairing
> itself far from rest. It GROWS by recruiting vibes and nesting sub-selves. It DIES when its
> integration falls too low to reconstitute, its material returning to the field. It EVOLVES
> because persistent, self-copying patterns are selected, climbing the tower over time.

Every verb is a thing that happens to a pattern, and each reuses a result the testbed already
has: propagation (P37) for motion, homeostasis (P59) and integration (P63) for life,
recruitment and nesting (P59, P60) for growth, dissolution (P59, P60) for death, and persistence
plus replication for evolution.

## Honest edges

- The glider account of motion is a clean qualitative picture and matches the propagation and
  dispersion results, but a fully worked traveling-self soliton on the hyperbolic mesh has not
  been built end to end yet. It is a strong next experiment.
- Life as far-from-equilibrium self-maintenance is the right shape, but a quantitative model of
  metabolism (the order-from-environment exchange) is a brainstorm here, not a finished result.
- Evolution as pattern selection is sound in principle and follows from persistence plus
  replication, but a running experiment showing selves actually compete and complexify on the
  mesh is open, and would be a beautiful thing to build.
- As always, the structure is what the model gives. The felt life of a living self, the
  what-it-is-like of being one of these patterns, is the territory the structure maps.
