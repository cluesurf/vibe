# P38: Emergent Spatial Geometry

**Status: progress. A coexisting slice is a definite lower-dimensional space, tracking d-1.**

## The question

P13 showed expansion emerges and de Sitter slices grow, but did not measure the geometry
OF a slice. Does a coexisting "now" slice of the grown causal order have a definite
spatial dimension, near d-1 for a d-dimensional spacetime?

## What we did

Take a thin antichain band (a coexisting slice) of a sprinkled causal set, build its
spatial-neighbor graph, and measure that graph's ball-growth dimension.

## Result

- 2D spacetime: slice spatial dimension about 0.3.
- 3D spacetime: slice spatial dimension about 1.3.
- **The rise from 2D to 3D is about 1.0**, the d-1 trend (the slice gains one spatial
  dimension when the spacetime does).

The slice is a definite space, of dimension below the spacetime, and its dimension rises
by one with the spacetime dimension. The absolute value reads low because finite size
and the slice boundary bias ball-growth downward, a known effect.

## Honest reading

This is progress, not a clean integer. The trend (d-1, the slice is a lower-dimensional
geometry) is solid. A bias-free dimension estimator, a coordinate-free slice adjacency,
and the same result from a pure growth rule rather than a sprinkle, are the harder steps
ahead.
