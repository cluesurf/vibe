# P59: Nested Selves (Cells in a Body)

**Status: solved. A self made of selves, with homeostasis and autonomy from one mechanism.**

## The question

Stage D of the recursion: a self made of selves, like cells in a body. The defining test is
what happens when you perturb a PART. Does the whole absorb it (homeostasis) or does the part
keep its own identity (autonomy)?

## The model

Nested selves need MODULAR structure, exactly like biology: a cell is strongly bound inside
and weakly coupled to other cells. So the substrate is a modular mesh: dense, cohesive cells
(fill +1 inside), sparse weak links between them. The body's self is all cells aligned.

## Result

Flip a fraction of one cell and re-settle:

| fraction of cell flipped | cell returns to body | rest of body intact |
| ------------------------ | -------------------- | ------------------- |
| 10% | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| 25% | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| 50% | 0.66 | 1.00 |
| 75% | 0.00 | 1.00 |
| 100% | 0.00 | 1.00 |

A small wound heals completely (homeostasis). A whole-cell flip never returns (autonomy). The
crossover sits at the integration threshold (about half the cell). The rest of the body is
perfectly undisturbed at every fraction.

## Reading

A small wound inside a cell heals: the cell's internal cohesion pulls the flipped parts back
to the body's pattern (homeostasis). Flipping a whole cell persists: its cohesion makes the
flipped state stable too, and the weak outside coupling cannot undo it, so the cell keeps a
new identity (autonomy), like a cell holding its type, or a growth that no longer obeys the
body. The crossover from healed to autonomous is a threshold in how much of the cell is hit,
the same integration logic that makes a cluster a genuine higher vibe (P57, P58). And the body
away from the wound is undisturbed (modularity gives locality), so the higher self keeps its
identity through the wound.

This is a self made of selves: cells in a body, the same shape repeating for organs in a being
and beings in a greater whole.
