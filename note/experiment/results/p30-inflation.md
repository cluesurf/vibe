# P30: Inflation from a Time-Varying Growth Rate

**Status: demonstrated. A burst of rapid expansion with a graceful exit.**

## The question

P13 showed expansion emerges from a local birth rule at a constant rate. Inflation is a
brief epoch of much faster (accelerating) expansion early on, then a graceful exit to
ordinary slow expansion. Can a local growth rule produce that profile?

## What we did

Grow the spatial front with a time-varying spawn rate: high (q = 1.0, doubling each
generation) for the first six generations, then low (q = 0.05). Measure the width and
the expansion rate per generation.

## Result

- Front width per generation: 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, then 139, 151, 160, ... 228.
- Expansion rate: 2.0 for six generations (the inflationary burst), then dropping to
  about 1.06 (the graceful exit to slow expansion).
- The inflationary epoch is **4.2 e-folds** in six generations (width 2 to 128), then
  the universe settles into ordinary slow expansion.

So a high early birth rate gives a burst of rapid, accelerating expansion that exits
gracefully into slow expansion: the **inflationary profile**, emerging from a purely
local growth rule with a time-varying birth rate, no inflaton field put in by hand. The
early burst would stretch and smooth the universe, the standard cosmological role of
inflation.

## Honest reading

This demonstrates the inflationary profile (rapid early expansion, graceful exit) from
the growth rule. It does not derive WHY the birth rate is high early then low (the
inflaton potential analogue), nor the detailed predictions (the spectrum of density
perturbations). Those are the deeper cosmological program.
