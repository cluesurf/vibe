# P48: The Modular Base

**Status: demonstrated. A parameter-free, deterministic, Lorentz-safe, addressable base.**

## The question

The most elegant base would have no chosen parameters. The modular group PSL(2,Z), built
from the integers, is the candidate, with the Farey/Stern-Brocot tessellation,
continued-fraction addressing, and a finite generating automaton. Does it work?

## Result

1. **The modular tessellation** (the PSL(2,Z) orbit embedded in the disc, NO p or q to
   choose): 2500 cells, Lorentz anisotropy 0.059, Lorentz-safe. Parameter-free.
2. **The Stern-Brocot automaton** (the deterministic mediant rule, no randomness):
   every rational is reached exactly by following its continued fraction as a path.
   Exact addressing, confirmed on several rationals.
3. **The golden ratio as the central geodesic**: the all-ones continued fraction gives
   Fibonacci convergents that reach phi to within 7e-8.

## Reading

The most elegant base needs no chosen parameters. The modular group, built from the
integers, tessellates hyperbolic space (Lorentz-safe), is generated by a deterministic
finite automaton (the Stern-Brocot mediant rule, which is the deterministic growth rule
the earlier work asked for), and is addressed by continued fractions. The golden ratio,
recurring across the substrate work (the sunflower P39, the Fibonacci addressing P42), is
its central geodesic. The integers choose the base, so nothing arbitrary is left.

This closes the "what to build next" of the choosing-the-base analysis: the unified
Coxeter machine (P47), the parameter-free modular base, and the generating automaton as
