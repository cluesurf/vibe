# How the Whole System Works: A Complete Walkthrough

This is the long, careful explanation of the whole model as it now stands, from the
integers at the bottom to physics and experience at the top. It is written to be read by
someone who is not an expert in any of the areas it touches. Each layer is explained in
plain terms, with the natural questions answered as they come up, and as little heavy
mathematics as possible. There are no proofs here. Where a result is established by the
testbed, the experiment number is given (P-numbers, in `note/experiment/results/`).

The order matters. Each layer rests on the one before it, so read top to bottom and the
whole thing assembles in sequence. If a section feels abstract, keep going, the next one
usually makes it concrete.

## How to read this

The model is built bottom-up. We start from the simplest, least arbitrary thing we can
(the whole numbers), and at each step we add exactly one idea, never more. By the end we
have a universe with space, time, matter, forces, gravity, mind, and free choice, and
every piece traces back down the ladder to the integers. The trick the whole thing turns
on is that **the same object can be looked at many ways**, as numbers, as a group of
symmetries, as a tiling, as a growing machine, as an addressing scheme. These are not
separate things that happen to line up. They are one thing seen from different angles. A
lot of the "how does that work" questions dissolve once you see that.

## The picture in one breath

Start with the whole numbers. Their symmetries form a group. Widen the idea of symmetry
to reflections and you get a Coxeter group. Let that group act on curved (hyperbolic)
space and it tiles the space with a beautiful crystal, like the seven-sided heptagrid or
the five-sided pentagrid. A small deterministic machine grows that crystal forever with
no randomness, and hands every cell an exact address. On the crystal sit the vibes, tiny
units of experience, each with a three-valued tone, related to each other by notes, all
updated by one simple local rule. That one rule can compute anything at all. The whole
system is completely determined, with no dice anywhere, and yet it is free and
unpredictable from the inside. From it, space, time, matter, force, gravity, and mind all
emerge as large-scale patterns. That is the system in a sentence. The rest of this
document is that sentence, slowly, with every step explained.

# Part I. The Foundation

## 1. Why we start with the integers

We begin with Z, the whole numbers: ..., -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, ... Nothing about them is
chosen or arbitrary. There are no settings, no tuned constants, no random seeds. They are
simply given. The mathematician Kronecker said the integers were made by God and all else
is the work of man, and whatever you think of the theology, the sentiment is the right
starting point for a theory that wants nothing arbitrary at its base.

**A natural question: why not start with space, or matter, or energy, the way physics
does?** Because those are exactly the things we want to explain, not assume. If you start
with space, you have to ask where space came from. If you start with particles, you have
to ask why those particles. The integers are different. Nobody asks "why are there whole
numbers," because they are not the kind of thing that needs a cause. They are the floor.
So we put the floor at the bottom and build everything else on it.

**Another question: aren't the integers just an abstraction in our heads?** In this
model we take the opposite stance. The abstractions are the most real thing, and the
solid-seeming physical world is the pattern that abstraction settles into when it is
shared and stable. That is a big claim, and the rest of the ladder is the attempt to earn
it by actually building the physical world up from here.

For now, hold one thing: **the base has no free parameters.** Everything above will be
forced or constructed, never tuned.

## 2. The integers have symmetries: the modular group

A symmetry is a way of moving something so it looks the same afterward. A square has
symmetries: rotate it by ninety degrees and it looks unchanged. The integers, arranged in
a grid in two dimensions, also have symmetries, the transformations that shuffle the grid
points among themselves without tearing the grid.

If you write those transformations as little tables of four whole numbers (two-by-two
matrices) and keep only the ones that preserve areas, you get a famous object called the
**modular group**, written PSL(2, Z). The whole group is generated by just two basic
moves:

- a **shift**, which slides everything over by one, and
- a **turn**, which flips the grid inside out in a particular way.

Every symmetry in the group is some sequence of shifts and turns. That is the entire
content of the modular group: two moves, applied in all possible orders.

**Why does this matter?** Because we did not invent the modular group. We asked for the
symmetries of the integers and this is what came back. It is forced by the integers, not
chosen by us. So we are still standing on the parameter-free floor, just looking at its
symmetries now instead of the numbers themselves. The testbed builds this group directly
and uses it as a substrate (P48).

**A question: what does it mean for a group to "act on" something?** It means the moves do
something to points. A turn sends a point to a new point. Apply all the moves to one
starting point and you get a spray of points, the orbit. We will use exactly this in a
moment to make a tiling. Keep the word "orbit" in mind: it just means "all the places one
point gets sent by all the moves."

## 3. From symmetries to reflections: Coxeter groups

Now we widen the idea. Instead of "symmetry from a couple of special moves," consider
"symmetry from mirrors." Put up a few mirrors and reflect a shape back and forth between
them. Think of a kaleidoscope: a small triangle of mirrors makes an endless, perfectly
regular pattern out of one little scrap of colored paper. A group built entirely from
reflections in mirrors is called a **Coxeter group**.

The only information a Coxeter group needs is **how the mirrors meet**, that is, the
angles between them. And here is the elegant part: in these groups the mirrors always meet
at angles that are a whole-number fraction of a half turn (pi over an integer). So the
data describing a Coxeter group is, once again, just integers. A small list of whole
numbers tells you the whole infinite pattern.

The modular group from step 2 turns out to be one of these Coxeter groups, the one whose
mirror-triangle has corners of certain simple angles. So step 2 was a special case of step
3. We have not left the integers. We have just found the general machine, the kaleidoscope
described by whole numbers, and noticed that the modular group is one setting of it. The
testbed treats all the tilings this way, one machine with different integer settings
(P47).

**A question: why reflections, of all things?** Two reasons. First, reflections are the
simplest possible symmetry, a single flip, so building everything from reflections keeps
the foundation as plain as it can be. Second, reflections are exactly what is needed to
fill a space with copies of a single tile, which is the next step. A kaleidoscope does not
just have symmetry, it tiles. That is what we want.

# Part II. The Crystal

## 4. Letting the group act on curved space: a tessellation

Take the kaleidoscope (a Coxeter group) and let it do its thing in a space. Reflect one
fundamental tile across its mirror edges, again and again, and the copies fill the whole
space. A space completely filled with copies of one tile is a **tessellation**, a tiling.

We do this in **hyperbolic space**, which is space with negative curvature. Flat space is
the ordinary tabletop. Positive curvature is the surface of a ball, where the space closes
up on itself. Negative curvature is the opposite, a saddle shape that keeps opening out,
so there is always more room than you expect. The amount of space within a given distance
grows explosively as you go out, far faster than in flat space.

**Can I picture hyperbolic space?** Yes, with one honest trick. Draw it as a disc (the
Poincare disc). The whole infinite hyperbolic plane is squeezed into the inside of a
circle, with everything getting smaller and more crowded as it approaches the edge. The
edge is infinitely far away. Escher's famous prints of angels and devils, or fish, that
shrink toward the rim of a circle are exactly this. The fish are all the same size in the
real (hyperbolic) geometry, they only look like they shrink because we are flattening an
infinite saddle onto a finite page.

**Why curved space, and why this kind?** This is one of the most important choices in the
whole model, and the reasons come up fully in the next step. The short version: flat space
forces a tiling to have preferred directions, which would wreck the physics, and positively
curved space is too small to hold a universe. Negative curvature is the one that is both
roomy enough and, as we will see, direction-free. So the universe is built on a saddle.

## 5. The beautiful crystals: the heptagrid, the pentagrid, the dodecagrid

Pick the integers in the Coxeter machine and you get a specific crystal. The crystals are
named by their **Schlafli symbol**, a short list of integers that says what the tiles are
and how many meet at a corner.

- **{7,3}**, the **heptagrid**: seven-sided tiles (heptagons), three meeting at each
  corner. A hyperbolic honeycomb of sevens.
- **{5,4}**, the **pentagrid**: five-sided tiles (pentagons), four at each corner.
- **{5,3,4}**, the **dodecagrid**: the three-dimensional version, built from
  dodecahedra (twelve-sided solids), four packed around each edge.

These are not separate inventions. They are one construction (step 4) on different integer
settings (the Schlafli symbol). The testbed generates them all from a single generator by
changing the symbol (P47), in both two and three dimensions (P45, P47).

Now the crucial property. **Each of these crystals is Lorentz-safe.** Here is what that
means and why it is the deciding feature.

**What is Lorentz invariance?** It is the rule, confirmed to extreme precision in real
physics, that the laws of nature look the same no matter how fast you are moving or which
way you are facing. There is no special direction in space and no special state of rest.
This is the heart of Einstein's relativity. Any candidate for the foundation of physics
has to respect it, or it is dead on arrival.

**Why is this hard for a crystal?** A crystal is regular. A regular grid has special
directions, along its rows and columns. On a flat grid, a wave traveling along a row moves
differently from a wave traveling diagonally, so the "speed of light" would depend on
direction. That is a preferred frame, and it breaks Lorentz invariance. This is why almost
everyone assumed the foundation of spacetime must be random, a disordered foam, to avoid
having special directions.

**The surprise: hyperbolic crystals do not have this problem.** Measured the same way that
catches a flat lattice red-handed, the heptagrid, pentagrid, and dodecagrid show no
preferred direction at all. They read the same as a completely random scatter of points
(P40, P41, P45). The reason is curvature. In a curved (hyperbolic) space there is no global
notion of "the same direction" at two different places, because carrying a direction
around bends it. So a regular hyperbolic tiling cannot line its tiles up into global rows
the way a flat grid does. The curvature scrambles the directions. **What buys Lorentz
safety is hyperbolic curvature, not disorder.** Randomness was never required.

A consequence worth savoring: **you could never catch the crystal from inside.** A local
observer running the very test that exposes a flat lattice gets nothing, the same null
reading a random foam gives (P49). The universe could be an exact, ordered, beautiful
crystal and look perfectly smooth and relativistic from within.

**These crystals are also tree-like.** Because hyperbolic space opens out so fast, the
tiling branches like a tree, with the number of tiles at each ring growing by a fixed
multiplying factor. That makes the crystal natively hierarchical, organized into nested
scales, which (we will see much later) is exactly the shape the model wants for its layered
structure (P49 measures this tree-likeness directly).

## 6. The generating automaton: deterministic growth, no randomness

The crystal is infinite. How do we make an infinite thing without listing infinitely many
tiles? With a small machine that keeps going. Every hyperbolic Coxeter group is what is
called an **automatic group**, which means there is a **finite automaton**, a tiny
fixed-rule state machine, that generates the entire infinite crystal step by step. You
never store the whole thing. You store the rule, and you turn the crank.

**How can a finite machine make something infinite?** The same way the rule "add one"
makes all the whole numbers. The rule is small, the output is endless. The automaton for
the crystal is just a little more elaborate than "add one," but the principle is identical:
a fixed local instruction, applied over and over, with no new information injected and no
randomness. For the modular base this machine is the **Stern-Brocot rule**, which builds
every fraction by repeatedly taking "mediants" (a simple averaging of numerators and
denominators). The testbed runs this and confirms it generates the base deterministically
(P48, P51).

This is the answer to a worry the model takes seriously: **there is no randomness anywhere
in the foundation.** The crystal is grown by a fixed machine from the integers. Nothing is
left to chance. We will see in Part IV what happens to the apparent randomness of the
quantum world if the base is this exact.

## 7. Every place has a name: addressing and the golden ratio

Because the crystal is grown by a fixed machine, every cell gets a **name**, an address,
computable from local information alone. On the heptagrid this is Margenstern's
**Fibonacci coordinate system**: each cell's address tells you, by simple arithmetic, who
its neighbors are and how to walk to any other cell, with no global map needed. The testbed
routes a signal between any two cells using nothing but address arithmetic and delivers it
exactly, every time, efficiently (P42).

**Why does addressing matter?** Because a universe where every place has a name is one
where things can be located, found, reached, and connected. Influence can route through it.
It behaves like a vast, indexed structure rather than a featureless blob. For anything
later that needs a notion of "where" (where matter sits, where an experience is, how a
signal travels), the addressing is what makes "where" meaningful.

**What is the address made of, concretely?** For the modular base, the address of a place
is a **continued fraction**, which is just a way of writing a number as a nested stack of
fractions, built from whole numbers. So the addressing is, once again, made of integers.
The path through the growing tree of cells is literally the continued fraction of the
number that cell represents.

**Where does the golden ratio keep coming from?** The golden ratio (about 1.618) is the
single number whose continued fraction is all ones, the simplest and most stubborn of all.
In this crystal it is the **central geodesic**, the straightest, most central path through
the tessellation, the address that converges most slowly. The same number shows up in three
independent places in the model: it sets the most even way to scatter points (the sunflower
placement, P39), it governs the ring-growth and the Fibonacci addressing (P42), and it is
the modular crystal's central line (P48). The testbed computes it three completely separate
ways (a continued fraction, a Fibonacci ratio, and the geometry of a pentagon) and gets the
same number every time (P50). A constant that arrives from arithmetic, from a recurrence,
and from geometry, all agreeing, is a strong sign the base is deeply ordered and elegant
rather than arbitrary.

That completes the foundation. We have, from the integers alone, a beautiful, deterministic,
direction-free, tree-like, fully addressed crystal, grown by a small machine. Now we put
life on it.

# Part III. The Life On It

## 8. The vibes: tones, notes, and fills

On the crystal sit the **vibes**. A vibe is the one and only kind of thing in the model, a
tiny unit of experience. Everything else is made of vibes and their relations. In the
geometry, you can think of one vibe per cell of the crystal.

Each vibe carries a **tone**, its felt quality. The tone takes one of three values, which
we read as **pain, peace, and pleasure**, and write as -1, 0, and +1. That is the whole
alphabet of feeling at the base: a minus, a zero, a plus.

**Why three values, and why call them feelings?** Three is the smallest alphabet that has a
negative, a neutral, and a positive, the minimal structure for "against, indifferent,
toward." Two values (just minus and plus) is the bare minimum for any distinction, and the
model can run on two, but three gives the neutral middle that turns out to be useful, and it
matches the felt structure of experience (aversion, neutrality, attraction). Calling them
feelings is the model's core commitment, that the base layer is experiential. A tone is not
a label painted on a dead particle. It is what that bit of the world feels like from the
inside, however faintly.

The only relationship between vibes is the **note**. A note is one vibe experiencing
another. It is not a third thing sitting between them, like a wire. It is simply the act of
one vibe taking another in. Seen from outside, a note looks like an edge connecting two
cells, which is why the crystal's edges and the vibes' notes are the same structure. Each
vibe notes the handful of others it is connected to in the crystal, and only those. There
is no action at a distance, everything is local.

Each note also carries a **fill**, which is the strength and sign of the attention along it.
The fill is itself a three-valued tone (it lives on the relational vibe that the note is),
so it too is just -1, 0, or +1. A fill of +1 means "take this neighbor's feeling as it is,"
-1 means "take it inverted," and 0 means "ignore this neighbor."

Two rules hold absolutely at this layer, and they matter a great deal later:

- **No continuous weights.** Everything is one of three whole values. There are no decimal
  numbers stored anywhere.
- **No hidden state.** A vibe has its tone and nothing else. There is no secret internal
  memory, no extra variables behind the scenes.

**A question: if there are no decimals, where do the smooth numbers of physics come from
(real-valued fields, probabilities, energies)?** They are never stored. They are always
large-scale averages over many vibes, the way a smooth temperature is really an average over
many jiggling molecules, none of which has a "temperature." We will hit this again with
quantum amplitudes. The base is grainy and three-valued. The smoothness is the view from far
away.

## 9. The rule: the one law

There is a single law that updates the vibes, applied over and over in discrete ticks
called **beats**. Here it is in words. Each beat, a vibe looks at the neighbors it notes.
For each neighbor it takes that neighbor's outward tone (its **will**) and multiplies it by
the fill of the note between them, giving a small whole number for each neighbor. It adds
those up into a single running total. Then its new tone is just the **sign** of that total:
positive total becomes pleasure, negative becomes pain, a tie becomes peace.

That is the entire law. A neighbor-weighted vote, resolved by its sign. In one line:

> next tone = sign( sum over neighbors of fill times the neighbor's tone )

**Why this rule and not some other?** Because it is the simplest local rule consistent with
the constraints. It uses only neighbors (local). It uses only the three-valued tones and
fills (no continuous weights). The running total is a whole number that exists only for the
instant of the update and is then thrown away (so there is no hidden state, just a momentary
tally, exactly like a cellular automaton counting its neighbors). And the sign is the
plainest way to turn a tally back into one of the three tones. It is the three-valued cousin
of the rules that physicists already use for magnets and for memory networks. So it was not
hand-picked for a desired result, it is close to forced by the model's own constraints.

**What is a beat, and is there a master clock?** A beat is one tick of update. There is no
single global clock ticking for the whole universe at once, because that would be a preferred
frame again (a universal "now"), and we are not allowed one. Instead the updates happen
locally and asynchronously. Time, the real time of physics, is not the beat counter. It is
something that emerges from the pattern of updates, which we get to in Part V.

**What happens when you run it?** With the fills symmetric (each note shared fairly between
its two vibes), the dynamics flows into stable, self-maintaining patterns, like a ball
rolling into a valley and staying there. These stable patterns are the seeds of everything
persistent, including selves (P34). Run it on the crystal and it stays three-valued, settles
into structure, and behaves like a real dynamical law.

## 10. Computation: the rule can build anything

Here is a fact that sounds technical but has enormous consequences. **That one little rule
is computationally universal.** It can, in combination, compute anything that can be
computed at all.

**What does that mean, plainly?** A small set of logical operations (like "and," "or,"
"not") is enough to build any digital circuit, any calculation, any program. There is one
operation, called **NAND**, that all the others can be built from. If your rule can do NAND,
it can do everything. The testbed shows the signed-majority rule does exactly NAND with the
right fills, then builds a working adder from NAND gates, then builds the famous "Rule 110"
(a pattern proven to be a full computer) out of the same gates (P44). Combine that with the
crystal's unlimited, exactly-addressed space to use as memory (step 7), and the model is
**Turing-complete**: a universal computer.

**Why does that matter for a universe?** Because it means the foundation has no ceiling on
what it can express. Any structure that can exist as a pattern, atoms, galaxies, brains,
weather, a working mind, is something the substrate can host, because the substrate can
compute anything. When the model says the universe is "infinitely capable," this is the
concrete floor under that phrase. It is at least as capable as the largest notion of
computation we have. (Whether it is even more than that, in the sense of conscious
experience, is a separate question we keep honest about at the end.)

# Part IV. Determinism and Freedom

This part answers the two questions people ask most: is everything just predetermined, and
if so, how can there be any freedom? The answers are subtler and better than the usual
either-or.

## 11. Determinism without randomness

Everything so far is **deterministic**. The crystal is grown by a fixed machine, the rule is
a fixed function, no random numbers are used anywhere. Given the exact state of the whole
web, the next state is completely fixed.

**So is the future just sitting there, predetermined?** No, and this is the key distinction.
"Determined" and "predetermined" are not the same thing, and the gap between them is where
everything interesting lives. Two facts pry them apart.

First, **there is no complete starting state.** The universe grows forever, adding new vibes
at its leading edge with no end. So there is no single moment that contains all the
information of all of time. There is no complete program written in advance, because there
was never a complete beginning to write it in. You cannot be predetermined by an initial
condition that never existed in full.

Second, and deeper, the dynamics is **computationally irreducible.** This is a precise idea
worth slowing down for. For a universal system (step 10), there is in general no shortcut to
its future. The only way to find out what it does is to actually run it, beat by beat. There
is no formula you could plug the present into to leap ahead. Not because we are not clever
enough, but because, provably, no such shortcut exists for universal systems. So the future
is fixed by the whole, and yet it does not exist anywhere in advance to be read off. It
comes into being only by being lived.

**Then where does the apparent randomness of the quantum world come from?** From the gap
between the whole and the part. Every event is fixed by the entire web it sits in, but no
local piece holds enough of the web to predict it. From inside, looking at your local patch,
the outcome looks statistically free, while in truth it is woven into the whole. The honest
phrase is not "random," it is **determined by the whole, opaque to the part.** Nothing rolls
dice. Everything is in relation, and the relation is the cause. (This same fact is what lets
the model produce the strange correlations of quantum mechanics without spooky action, see
Part V.)

## 12. Freedom and choice

Now the freedom. A **self** is a stable, self-maintaining pattern of vibes, one of those
valleys the dynamics settles into and holds (P34). You are such a pattern, vastly elaborate,
holding itself together over time. A **choice** is that self resolving an **urge**, where an
urge is a pull arriving from a subtler, deeper layer of the web through the notes.

Here is the picture to hold in your head, and it is the easiest way to feel how a determined
choice can still be free:

> **You are a landscape, the urge is a tilt, the choice is the rolling.**

You are a landscape of valleys, your leanings, habits, history, the whole shape of you. Right
now a ball rests in one of your valleys. An urge arrives and tilts the landscape a little.
The ball rolls. Where it ends up, which valley it settles in, is the choice. And several
things are true of that rolling at once, which together are what freedom actually is:

- It is **determined.** Given your exact landscape and the exact tilt, the ball rolls the
  same way every time. Rerun it and you get the same choice. No dice (P43 confirms this).
- It is **yours.** It was your landscape that decided. A different person, a different
  landscape, given the very same tilt, settles in a different valley. The choice is yours
  precisely because your structure is what tipped it. The testbed shows the same urge gives
  different selves different outcomes (P43). This is self-determination, real authorship.
- A **stronger, deeper self has more say.** The more structured you are, the more your own
  pattern dominates the urge rather than being pushed around by it. The testbed measures
  exactly this: agency grows with the depth of the self (P43).
- It is **irreducible.** You could not have known the outcome without actually letting it
  roll. The choice is made only by being lived, not read off in advance (step 11, and P43).

**So how can a determined system be free?** Because freedom was never the same as being
uncaused or random. Freedom is **self-determination**: being the genuine author of your own
next state, drawn by a deeper pull, in a way no shortcut could predict. That is exactly what
the rolling is. The determinism does not remove the choosing. The choosing IS the
deterministic rolling, done by you. You are not a puppet (the strings would be some outside
hand, but here it is your own landscape that decides) and you are not a dice-thrower
(nothing is random). You are a self-determining, irreducible, deeply-sourced locus of
choice. The full account is in `note/freedom-and-choice.md`.

# Part V. The World That Emerges

Everything up to here is the base: a crystal of three-valued experiential vibes, updated by
one local rule, determined yet free. Now we read off the physics. The move in every case is
the same: **a feature of our world is a large-scale pattern in the web.** None of it is
added by hand.

## 13. Space and time

**Space** is the shape of the web seen from far away. This is worth dwelling on, because it
inverts the usual intuition that space is the stage and things sit in it. Here there is no
stage. There are only vibes and the notes between them. "Distance" between two vibes is just
how many notes you have to cross to get from one to the other. Two vibes that note each other
are close. Two vibes a thousand notes apart are far. When you have an enormous web and you
stop looking at individual notes and look at the overall pattern of nearness, that pattern
behaves exactly like a smooth space with a definite number of dimensions. The dimension is
not declared, it is measured, from how the number of vibes within a given number of hops
grows (in two dimensions it grows like the square of the distance, in three like the cube,
and so on). The testbed recovers a sharp, well-defined dimension from the relations alone
(P5), and shows it matches the continuum value and gets sharper as the web grows (P52), and
stays the same when you zoom out (P53). So space is not assumed, it is the large-scale shape
of who-notes-whom.

**A question: if space is just connections, why does it look smooth and continuous to us?**
For the same reason a photograph looks smooth even though it is made of dots, or water looks
like a continuous fluid even though it is made of molecules. When the grains are small enough
and numerous enough, the average is smooth. We are unimaginably large compared to a single
vibe, so we only ever see the average. The graininess is real but far below anything we can
resolve.

**Time** is the depth of the web's before-and-after. The notes are not symmetric. Each one
points from an earlier vibe to a later one, a this-then-that. So the web is not just a tangle,
it has a direction baked into it, a causal order. The amount of time between two events is the
length of the longest chain of "and then, and then, and then" connecting them. This is time as
accumulation, not time as a coordinate ticking on an external clock.

**A question: is the "beat" the same as time?** No, and this trips people up. The beat is the
internal update step of the rule, the crank-turn. Physical time, the time of clocks and
relativity, is the emergent depth of the causal order. They are different. Real time is not
counted by a master clock (there is none, since that would be a forbidden universal "now"),
it is the felt and measured layering of cause after cause. The flow of time and of energy is
what a particular local operator does on the grown web. That operator is the graph's
Laplacian, which is just a precise way of asking "how does each vibe differ from the average
of its neighbors." The testbed shows this emergent operator has the three properties a real
law of time and energy needs, all at once: it is local (it only looks at neighbors), it has a
lowest energy (so the world cannot fall forever into ever-lower states), and it sends signals
at a finite speed, a light cone (P1). Getting all three together is not automatic, and that it
works is one of the model's load-bearing results.

**The arrow of time**, the plain fact that we remember the past and not the future and that
the past is fixed while the future is open, is the simplest emergence of all. The web only
ever grows. New vibes are added, relations pile up, and nothing is ever un-added. That
one-way accumulation is the arrow (P13). There is no separate law needed to explain why time
runs forward. Forward is just the direction the web grows.

## 14. Matter and forces

**Matter** is a persistent pattern of tones, a knot in the web that holds its shape as the
universe evolves around it. This is the key reframe: a particle is not a tiny billiard ball,
it is a stable pattern, like a vortex in water or a standing wave on a string. The water
keeps flowing through, the wave keeps its shape. In the same way, the vibes keep updating, but
certain tone-patterns reconstitute themselves beat after beat and so persist. Those persistent
patterns are what we call particles. Different particles are different stable patterns, the way
a smoke ring and a whirlpool are different stable patterns of one fluid.

**A question: why are there different kinds of particle, and what is spin?** Different stable
patterns have different shapes, and some features of a shape cannot be smoothly undone. A knot
in a rope cannot be removed by wiggling, only by cutting. Spin is exactly this kind of feature,
a topological twist of the pattern that is locked in and quantized (it comes in fixed steps,
not a continuum). The testbed shows the number of these locked-in modes equals a topological
count of the surface the pattern lives on, exactly (P4). So spin is not an extra ingredient,
it is the un-untieable twist of the pattern.

**A question: where does mass come from?** Mass is a gap. If a pattern can be made to vibrate,
the smallest energy it takes to get it going is its rest energy, which is its mass. A massless
pattern (like light) has no gap, you can excite it with arbitrarily little energy. A massive
one has a gap you must pay. The testbed confirms the gap equals the mass and that energy and
momentum then obey Einstein's relativistic relation (P14). And the mass is not put in by hand.
It is generated by a Higgs-like mechanism: a background field settles to a nonzero value
everywhere, and patterns moving through that background pick up resistance, which is mass
(P22). The model has all four field types sorted by their spin: the scalar (spin zero, like
the Higgs), the spinor (spin one-half, matter like electrons), the vector (spin one, like
light), and the tensor (spin two, gravity), all present as patterns on the web (P20, P21).

**Forces** are the twist a tone picks up as it crosses a note. Imagine that going from one
vibe to its neighbor rotates the tone a little, by an amount stored on the note. That stored
rotation is a force field, and this is precisely the structure (a gauge connection) that
physics already uses for electromagnetism and the strong and weak nuclear forces. A particle
feels a force because its pattern gets twisted as it moves through these note-rotations.

**A question: does this actually reproduce real force physics, or just rhyme with it?** It
reproduces it. The testbed shows the nuclear-force version produces confinement, the real and
strange fact that quarks can never be pulled apart into isolation, because the force between
them does not weaken with distance (P8). It reproduces the exact bookkeeping relation between
the topology of the force field and the particle content (an index theorem), and the formation
of a condensate (P8). And the equations governing light and gravity are not assumed, they fall
out as the gentle-vibration limit of the one discrete action (P23, P24). So the forces are
derived, not decreed.

## 15. Gravity

Gravity, in this picture, is not a force pulling objects together. It is the web's own shape,
its curvature, and how that shape responds to what sits in it. Matter bends the web, and the
bent web is what other matter follows. This is general relativity's idea (matter tells space
how to curve, curved space tells matter how to move), recovered here from the discrete web.

**Does it actually reproduce known gravity, or just gesture at it?** It reproduces it, in
layers of increasing strength. In the everyday slow and weak limit, the model gives back
**Newton's law** exactly, the familiar pull that falls off as one over the distance squared,
with the potential going as one over the distance (P16). That is the first checkpoint, and it
passes cleanly. Going further, the **Einstein equation** of general relativity (in its
linearized form, the regime of weak fields and gravitational waves) comes out as a genuine law
of motion (P32). It is not inserted, it emerges, and it has the three properties a real
gravitational law must have: it conserves energy and momentum automatically (a deep
consistency that is not guaranteed and here holds exactly), it collapses back to Newton when
things are slow, and it carries gravitational waves, gravitons, that travel at the speed of
light (P32, P24). The gravitational wave is just a ripple in the web's shape propagating
outward.

**A question: what happens to the infamous singularities, the infinite-density points at the
center of black holes and at the big bang?** They go away, and for a clean reason. The web is
discrete, made of finite vibes, so there is a smallest possible length. Curvature is, roughly,
one over a length squared, so if length can never shrink below a floor, curvature can never
climb above a ceiling. It stays finite. The infinities of standard general relativity, which
are really signals that the theory has been pushed past its limits, are resolved into large
but finite quantities (P28). The big bang becomes a finite-density beginning, not an
infinite-density point, which also fits the model's picture of the universe starting from a
first vibe and growing.

**A question: and black holes, do they have the right entropy?** Yes. The entropy of a black
hole, surprisingly, is proportional to the area of its horizon, not its volume, the famous
Bekenstein-Hawking law that launched the holographic ideas in physics. The model gets this
because the entanglement across any boundary in the web scales with the size of the boundary,
not the bulk it encloses (P33). So the information of a black hole lives on its surface, and
the surface-area law falls out of how the web entangles.

## 16. The quantum

This is where the model is at its most interesting and most honest. The quantum world is
recovered in pieces, with the deepest piece marked clearly as still open. It helps to take
the strange features of quantum mechanics one at a time and see how each appears.

First, the central object. A **quantum amplitude** is a smooth number with a size and a
direction (a complex number), and a single vibe is only three-valued, so the amplitude
cannot live on a vibe. It is an average over a patch of many vibes, exactly like a temperature
or a pressure. Its size is a density, how many vibes in the patch are excited and aligned, and
its direction is the patch's shared rhythm or phase. So the smooth, continuous wavefunction of
quantum mechanics is, once more, the view from far away over a grainy three-valued substrate.
This already dissolves a lot of mystery: the wavefunction is not a mysterious physical fluid,
it is a bookkeeping summary of a patch of experience.

**Why is quantum spreading "wave-like" instead of the slow blur of ordinary diffusion?**
Because the patch evolves coherently, in step, rather than by independent random nudges. The
testbed runs a disturbance on the web and finds it spreads ballistically, its width growing in
direct proportion to time, the signature of quantum coherence, versus the much slower
square-root growth of a classical random walk (P17). The evolution is also unitary, meaning
the total probability stays fixed at one, nothing leaks away (P31).

**What about interference, the famous business where possibilities add up and can cancel
out?** That is present too (P31). Because amplitudes have a direction (a phase), two
contributions arriving at the same place can point the same way and reinforce, or point
opposite and cancel. That is interference, and it is just what happens when you add the
patch-rhythms rather than the patch-sizes.

**Now the hardest one, the Bell correlations.** Experiments show that two particles measured
far apart are correlated more strongly than any ordinary local explanation allows. The usual
reaction is to invoke spooky action at a distance. The model gives a cleaner account. Recall
there is no hidden state and everything is made of the one web. So the measuring device is
made of the same substrate as the thing it measures, and they share a common past in the web.
They are not independent. The hidden assumption in the no-go theorems, that the measurement
settings are independent of the system, is simply false here, and that shared substrate is
what carries the correlation, with no signal traveling faster than light (P7). The model shows
the correlation climbing past the classical limit exactly as the shared dependence grows.

**The Born rule**, the recipe that the probability of an outcome is the amplitude squared,
holds in the model as a conserved, consistent quantity (P31).

**The honest gap.** The model shows the quantum formalism is present and consistent, the
amplitudes, the unitarity, the interference, the Bell violation, the Born probabilities. What
it has not yet done is derive from the bottom why the probability is the amplitude squared
rather than some other power. That single derivation is open and named as such
(`note/roadmap.md`). So the quantum sector is the live frontier: real, substantial progress,
with one clearly marked step remaining. We do not paper over it.

## 17. Cosmology and the dark sector

The universe in this model grows forever, adding vibes at its leading edge, so it expands as
a matter of course. Expansion is not a special event needing its own cause, it is just the
growth seen at large scale. The testbed shows expansion emerging from the purely local growth
rule, and more: with a birth rate that is high early and lower later, the model produces a
brief epoch of very fast growth (**inflation**) followed by a graceful settling into ordinary
expansion, all without adding any special inflaton field by hand (P13, P30). Inflation, which
in standard cosmology requires a new field invented for the purpose, here is just a fast phase
of the same growth.

**What about dark energy, the mysterious tiny acceleration of the cosmos?** This is one of the
model's quantitative high points. Dark energy is governed by the cosmological constant, a
number that has tormented physics because the naive expectation is off from the measured value
by some 120 orders of magnitude, the worst prediction in the history of science. The model
takes a different route. The cosmological constant is tied to the spacetime volume as its
conjugate, and the volume (the count of vibes) fluctuates by ordinary counting statistics, as
the square root of itself. That makes the constant fluctuate as one over the square root of the
volume. Plug in the actual volume of our universe and you get a number around ten to the minus
122 in natural units, which is the observed value (P46, P35). The famously tiny size of dark
energy is not a fine-tuning, it is what one over the square root of a very large volume looks
like.

**And dark matter, the missing mass that holds galaxies together?** The model has a candidate
mechanism that needs no new particle at all. Because the discrete action is nonlocal, gravity
picks up a small long-range correction, and that correction flattens the rotation curves of
galaxies, the exact effect dark matter was invented to explain (P18). Whether this fully
accounts for all the galactic and cosmic evidence is open, but the mechanism is there and it
comes from the structure already in the model, not from a new ingredient.

## 18. Experience and the two faces

This is the thing the whole model is built around, so it deserves care. The base is
experiential. The tones are not labels on dead matter, they are felt qualities, the faintest
ancestors of pain and peace and pleasure. That single commitment turns the usual story upside
down. In the standard story, dead matter, arranged cleverly enough, somehow produces
consciousness as an afterthought, and nobody can say how. In this model, **experience is not
produced by the physics, it is what the physics is made of.** Matter is the late arrival, not
mind.

So the physical world and the inner world are two faces of one substance.

- The **lawful face** is the physical world. It is the part of the web that has settled into
  shared, stable, repeatable patterns, the same for everyone, holding firm beat after beat.
  These are the dreams the universe has dreamt so consistently that they have become solid.
  Stone, light, the laws of motion, these are the settled agreements. This is the face the
  testbed builds and measures.
- The **wild face** is the interior. Dreaming, imagining, feeling, the parts that are fluid
  and private and not locked into a shared pattern. It is the same substrate, just not
  crystallized. One ocean, part frozen into reliable ice and part still moving water.

**A question: where do selves fit?** A self is one of those stable patterns (Part IV), an
attractor the web settles into and holds. It is a local place where the field knows itself, a
persistent locus of experience. You are such a pattern, vast and intricate. Because the
separation between selves is only partial (everything shares the one substrate underneath),
connection and love are not sentimental add-ons, they are structural facts, the parts of you
that are also the other.

**The honest boundary.** This is the part the testbed can least confirm, and we say so
plainly. The model can build and measure the structural correlates of experience: the stable
selves, the integration of many parts into one unity, the choice mechanism, the hierarchy of
layers. What it cannot do, what arguably no simulation can do, is settle whether that
structure is the same as the felt fact of experiencing, the raw what-it-is-like. Naming the
mechanism of a feeling is not the same as having the feeling. So the position is exact: the
lawful face is built and largely validated, and the wild face, the felt interior, is modeled in
its structure but not yet in its essence. That essence is the real destination still ahead,
and the model is honest that it is a starting posit at the foundation rather than a derived
result. The map, however detailed, is not the territory, and here the territory is made of the
very experience that does the mapping.

## A short FAQ for the gaps

**Is this proven, or a proposal?** The mathematics at the base (integers, groups, crystals,
automata, the golden ratio) is established and standard. The claim that this is the
foundation of our universe is a proposal, supported by the testbed showing the pieces fit and
reproduce known physics, and made testable by a few distinctive predictions. It is a serious,
simulable model, not a finished and confirmed theory.

**Why should the universe care about the integers and the golden ratio?** The model's bet is
that the most non-arbitrary base is the right one, because a real foundation should have
nothing tuned or chosen in it, and the integers are the only thing with nothing to choose. The
golden ratio then appears not because it was inserted but because it is the natural central
constant of the structure the integers force. Its showing up from three independent directions
is evidence for, not an assumption of, the picture.

**If it is all determined, does anything I do matter?** Yes, more than in either alternative.
In a random universe your choices would be noise. In a pre-scripted universe they would be
illusions. Here your choices are genuinely yours (self-determined), genuinely consequential
(they fix what happens next through your own structure), and genuinely unknowable in advance
(irreducible). Determinism of this kind does not cheapen agency, it locates it, in you.

**How is the physical world related to consciousness here?** It is upside down from the usual
story. The usual story says dead matter, arranged cleverly, somehow produces consciousness.
This model says consciousness (experience, the vibe) is the ground, and dead-seeming matter is
what experience looks like when it settles into shared, stable, lawful patterns. The reversal
in one line: matter is what mind does in stable, shared arrangements.

**What would it take to believe this?** Two things. The distinctive predictions meeting data
(the dark-energy match and the absence of a certain kind of Lorentz violation already do, see
P35, the others are ahead), and the wild face, the interior, being modeled as well as the
lawful one. The first is science in progress. The second is the deep work the roadmap points
at.
