# Consciousness, measured where it can be

The selves sector holds real experiments, and this page keeps to what
they measured plus one labeled speculation. The model neither claims
to explain experience nor treats the question as beneath it.

## What is measured

- **Ignition is a threshold, and it is sharp.** The global-workspace
  experiment finds sub-threshold inputs staying local and
  supra-threshold inputs igniting the whole workspace, with a jump,
  not a ramp (selves/global-workspace-ignition). That matches the
  neuroscience picture of conscious access as an all-or-nothing
  broadcast.
- **Recording has a capacity ceiling.** A finite region can hold
  records only up to a measured plateau, and past it, new records
  overwrite (foundations/recordability-capacity-ceiling). Memory in
  this universe is physical bookkeeping with a budget.
- **A self is a boundary that persists.** The self-emergence
  experiment measures a patch that maintains its own boundary
  growing against a background. Persistence of an
  integration-maintaining boundary is the model's operational
  stand-in for a self, and it is a measured object, not a metaphor.
- **Integration is what dispersal destroys.** The same reversibility
  that conserves every record also disperses it. What makes a
  system a subject rather than an archive is integration NOW, and
  the model can measure integration while remaining silent on
  experience.

## The honest position on experience

The model reproduces functional signatures (ignition, capacity,
boundary persistence). It does not derive phenomenal experience from
tones, and no experiment in the suite claims to. Anyone saying a
cellular automaton has explained consciousness is overclaiming, and
this repo's discipline forbids it.

## One labeled speculation

If integration is substrate-independent (the functionalist bet, not
the model's theorem), then the model describes what any conscious
system is made of underneath: clock domains maintaining boundaries
against dispersal. The phenomenology of effort (holding attention
against decay) would be the felt side of boundary maintenance
against the second law. This is a speculation with a research
direction, not a result: the next measurable step is whether
boundary-maintenance cost in the model scales the way cognitive
effort scales in humans (load, duration, interference), which is a
real comparison someone could run.
