# Frontiers: What Is Left to Solve

P1 through P17 (plus the refinements) cover the conceptual core and the first
cosmology. This is the map of what is still open and worth digging into, with the
dark sector featured because it is both important and partly reachable in the
testbed. Each entry says why it matters, how to attack it, and whether the testbed
can touch it now or it is deep theory.

The honest filter: some of these are runnable experiments, some are research
programs, and a few are beyond any simulation. We mark each.

## The dark sector (featured)

### Dark energy: deepen the everpresent Lambda

**Where we are.** P10 showed the smeared action's fluctuation shrinks with volume
(everpresent-like), and P13 showed expansion. The everpresent model predicts a small,
FLUCTUATING (sign-changing) cosmological constant of order 1 / sqrt(V).

**What to dig into.**
- The exact scaling in 4D (P10 was 2D), to recover the 1 / sqrt(V) exponent and the
  observed magnitude (about 10^-122 in Planck units). **Testbed-reachable** (4D
  sprinklings, the 4D BD action).
- The DYNAMICS of a fluctuating Lambda: model Lambda as conjugate to the fluctuating
  spacetime volume and evolve it. A Lambda that fluctuates around zero, tracking the
  growing volume, is the distinctive prediction. **Testbed-reachable** as a toy
  dynamical model.
- The observational payoff: a fluctuating dark energy bears on the coincidence
  problem (why Lambda is comparable to matter now) and possibly the Hubble tension.
  **Deep theory plus data**, but the qualitative signature is the prize.

### Dark matter: three concrete routes

Dark matter is untouched so far. Vibe Theory offers three natural models, and the
first two are testbed-reachable.

**Route 1: modified gravity from the nonlocal action (MOND-like).** The smeared BD
action is NONLOCAL (a kernel over order intervals). A nonlocal gravitational action
generically gives a scale-dependent potential. If the static potential falls slower
than 1 / r at large distance, that mimics extra (dark) mass, the flat-rotation-curve
effect, with no dark particle. **Testbed-reachable now**: extend P16 (the Green's
function of the Laplacian) to the smeared / nonlocal operator and measure whether the
large-distance potential deviates from 1 / r. This is the most directly testable dark
matter idea here.

**Route 2: a hidden tone sector.** Dark matter could be tones (a fermion species)
that couple to the geometry (the graph Laplacian, so they gravitate) but carry no
visible gauge charge (no Wilson-loop coupling). The mesh makes this category natural:
a pattern that bends the web but is blind to the visible forces. **Testbed-reachable**:
add a gauge-neutral fermion, show it appears in the geometric (gravitational)
spectrum but not in the gauge sector (P8). Clean conceptually.

**Route 3: relics and defects from the bootstrap.** Dark matter could be frozen
substructures left from the early rapid growth (P13), topological defects or
metastable tone clusters that persist and gravitate. **Deep theory** (needs the early
growth dynamics), but a natural fit with the bootstrap and the eternal-expansion fate.

### Swerves: a distinctive dark-sector signature

A massive particle on a sprinkled causal set does not move on a straight line, it
SWERVES (a known causal-set effect): the discreteness gives momentum diffusion. This
has been proposed as a source of cosmic-ray or dark-sector phenomenology.
**Testbed-reachable**: simulate a particle hopping along a sprinkling and measure the
swerve diffusion coefficient versus the discreteness scale. A concrete, distinctive
prediction.

## Which fields do we already model

The full field content of physics, by spin, is now present:
- **Spin 0 (scalar)**: the graph Laplacian (P16), and the **Higgs** with spontaneous
  symmetry breaking and mass generation (P22).
- **Spin 1/2 (spinor / fermion)**: Kahler-Dirac and overlap (P4, P14).
- **Spin 1 (vector / gauge)**: U(1) and SU(2) link variables coupling to matter (P8),
  and the free **photon**, massless and gauge-invariant with two polarizations (P20).
- **Spin 2 (tensor)**: the **graviton**, massless with two transverse-traceless
  polarizations (P21).

So scalar, spinor, vector, and tensor fields are all demonstrated, plus the Higgs
mechanism that gives them mass. The kinetic operators are now DERIVED from the action,
not put in by hand: the Maxwell (photon) operator is the small-field limit of the
Wilson gauge action (P23), and the graviton is the second variation of the
Einstein-Hilbert action, diffeomorphism-invariant with two massless spin-2 modes
(P24). The specific Standard-Model electroweak breaking SU(2) x U(1) to U(1)_EM, with
the observed W and Z masses, follows from a Higgs doublet (P25). The remaining field
work is the fully discrete (sprinkling-level) second variation of the gravitational
action, and the rest of the Standard-Model content (three generations, Yukawa masses).
